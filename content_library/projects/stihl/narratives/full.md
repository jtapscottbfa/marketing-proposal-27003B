# STIHL Incorporated — Electrical Design Program, Virginia Beach, VA

## Project Overview

The design team served as prime electrical engineering consultant for an industrial manufacturing complex in Virginia Beach, Virginia, delivering a sustained program of electrical systems engineering engagements from 2020 through 2025. The client operates a large-scale industrial plant producing outdoor power equipment, and the facility's electrical infrastructure required repeated evaluation, upgrade, and expansion as manufacturing operations evolved. Work was organized as a series of discrete engineering projects at the same campus, each addressing a specific system or building within the complex. The facility receives utility service from Dominion Energy at 34,500V primary, with step-down transformation to 480Y/277V for plant distribution.

## Scope

The program encompassed six documented engineering scopes:

**Switchboard MS3 and MS4 Replacement (BFA 20-003 / 21-036 / 21-036A, 2020–2021).** The primary engagement involved replacement of two existing main switchboards — MS3 and MS4 — in Mechanical Room C, each fed by a dedicated 2,500 kVA pad-mounted utility transformer. Scope included a formal short circuit and load flow study performed using SKM Power Tools software, producing a full equipment evaluation report, and subsequent preparation of construction drawings and Division 26 specifications for the replacement switchgear. The new switchboards were rated 4,000A, 480Y/277V, 3-phase, 4-wire, 100kA SCCR. A tap box and busway design for associated distribution was completed under BFA Project 21-036.

**Data Center MCB 3 and 4 Study (BFA 20-042, 2020).** A dedicated electrical study was performed for the facility's data center, addressing main circuit breakers 3 and 4. Specific study findings are not fully captured in the retained documents, but the engagement confirms the design team's role in evaluating critical power infrastructure across multiple buildings within the complex.

**IMM Accessories Power (BFA 21-014, 2021).** Electrical design services were provided for injection molding machine (IMM) accessories power. Construction drawings were produced as a final deliverable in July 2021. Specific load data and circuit details are in the drawing set.

**Guide Bar Plant Emergency Power (2022 / 2025).** Emergency power system design was developed for the Guide Bar Plant, with an initial electrical drawing set produced in July 2022 and a revised final drawing set issued January 2025. This engagement documents the design team's capacity for both initial design and as-built/updated deliverables for life-safety and mission-critical power systems within the manufacturing environment.

**Plant 5 / Warehouse 5 Switchgear Addition (BFA 23-005, 2024).** A new 5,000A, 480/277V, 3-phase, 4-wire main circuit breaker switchboard (SWBD-W5) was designed for Plant 5 and Warehouse 5. An electrical service and load calculation was performed, establishing a total demand of 3,191.4 kVA / 3,835.8A. The new switchboard serves multiple downstream panels including a main distribution panel, battery charger panels (BC3, BC4), EV charging panels (EV1, EV2), mechanical equipment panels (MECH1–MECH3), and DP9. Notable loads include electric vehicle charging infrastructure, battery chargers for industrial equipment, infrared heaters, unit heaters, and mechanical systems. Full Division 26 and Division 23 specifications were produced, along with electrical service calculations.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant for all scopes, self-performing electrical engineering design, short circuit and load flow analysis, construction document production (drawings and specifications), and delivery of engineering reports. The fee letter for the 21-036A switchgear replacement design documents reflects a design-for-bid scope (Section A only: design drawings and specifications), with construction administration, record drawings, and other post-bid services not included in that fee authorization. The multi-engagement pattern over five years reflects a repeat-client relationship in which the design team maintained working knowledge of the facility's existing electrical infrastructure across successive projects.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Under-rated protective devices.** The short circuit study for MS3 and MS4 identified multiple circuit breakers downstream of the new switchboards that were rated at 18kA interrupting capacity but were exposed to fault currents exceeding that rating under the new system configuration. Specifically, breakers for Panel LH, Panel LI (70A/3P), transformer feeds LL and LM (50A/3P), bus LV2 (70A), and Panels PBK1 and PBK2 (250A) all failed the evaluation. The study recommended immediate replacement of these devices. This finding is a direct outcome of the engineering analysis and illustrates the value of the short circuit study in surfacing latent safety risks prior to energizing new equipment.

**Service #4 at 100% transformer capacity.** Load analysis showed Service #4 loading at 2,537 kVA — equal to the 2,500 kVA transformer capacity when a 125% future-load growth factor is applied. The study flagged that Service #4 was fully loaded and had no headroom for additional load growth without transformer replacement or load rebalancing.

**Multi-building, multi-system coordination.** Serving a large industrial campus with active manufacturing operations required coordinating design work across mechanical rooms, warehouse buildings, data center facilities, and production areas while maintaining system reliability. The design team executed this across multiple sequential projects without a consolidated program management vehicle — each engagement stood alone while building on prior knowledge of the facility.

**EV infrastructure integration (Plant 5, 2024).** The Plant 5 switchgear addition was sized to accommodate EV charging infrastructure (panels EV1, EV2) as an emerging load category alongside traditional industrial loads, reflecting evolution of the facility's operational requirements.

## Outcomes

The program delivered replacement switchgear design for two 4,000A main switchboards serving the manufacturing complex, a quantified fault current and coordination analysis identifying immediate equipment hazards, a new 5,000A distribution switchboard supporting warehouse expansion and EV charging infrastructure, emergency power system designs for the Guide Bar Plant, and engineering support for data center and manufacturing equipment power across multiple facility buildings. All major deliverables — construction drawings, technical specifications, engineering studies, and load calculations — were completed and submitted as final documents. The engagement record spans from July 2020 through January 2025, with the most recent documented deliverable being the Guide Bar Plant Emergency Power final drawings.
