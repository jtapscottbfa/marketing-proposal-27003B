Mechanical engineering was the lead discipline on the West Point Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades project. The scope encompassed complete removal and replacement of the existing HVAC system serving the 20,491 GSF Virginia Army National Guard armory—a heavy timber facility with assembly, educational, and storage use groups—within a $967,000 not-to-exceed construction budget.

The mechanical design process began with a formal life cycle cost analysis using TRACE 700 energy modeling software calibrated to West Point, Virginia climate conditions (summer design 97°F DB, winter 19°F DB). The analysis evaluated multiple system alternatives, including variable refrigerant flow, ultimately supporting selection of rooftop variable air volume air handling units with energy recovery wheels, supply and return fans, and shut-off VAV terminal boxes with reheat hot water coils. Heating is provided by a propane-fired condensing boiler, with a propane service regulator upgrade included in scope.

Pathogen mitigation was a primary design driver per the client's post-COVID commander's intent. The mechanical system incorporates HVAC zoning configured to limit airborne particle migration between the facility's functional spaces, in-duct UV lighting, bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, and cold fusion air treatment technologies. A building automation system upgrade provides integrated humidity and temperature control across all HVAC zones, supporting the variable occupancy patterns of a weekend-drill armory.
