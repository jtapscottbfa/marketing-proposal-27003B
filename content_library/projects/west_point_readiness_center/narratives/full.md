# West Point Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades

## Project Overview

West Point Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades (VAFM PN 22-0016 / State PN 123-18578-013) is a complete replacement of the mechanical, plumbing, and associated electrical systems at the West Point Readiness Center, a Virginia Army National Guard armory located in West Point, Virginia (King William County). The facility is a 20,491 GSF heavy timber armory (Construction Type 4) encompassing assembly (6,798 SF), educational (7,363 SF), and storage (5,560 SF) use groups. The project was initiated by the Department of Military Affairs (DMA), Commonwealth of Virginia, under a qualifications-based state A/E services procurement.

## Scope

The project scope comprises full removal and replacement of the existing HVAC system within a $967,000 not-to-exceed construction budget, with design required to be completed within 210 days of notice to proceed. The design team was directed to address multiple performance objectives simultaneously: energy efficiency, indoor air quality, pathogen mitigation, and operational flexibility. The RFP identified pathogen mitigation—reflecting the client's post-COVID commander's intent for reconfigurable, high-filtration HVAC—as an explicit design requirement alongside standard energy and comfort objectives.

A life cycle cost analysis was performed using TRACE 700 energy modeling software calibrated to the West Point, Virginia climate (design conditions: summer 97°F DB, winter 19°F DB). Multiple system alternatives, including variable refrigerant flow, were evaluated. The analysis supported selection of rooftop variable air volume air handling units as the primary system, incorporating energy recovery wheels, supply and return fans, and dedicated outdoor air treatment. Terminal units are shut-off VAV boxes with reheat hot water coils. Heating is provided by a propane-fired condensing boiler; a propane service regulator upgrade is included in scope to support the new equipment load.

Pathogen mitigation technologies integrated into the mechanical design include: HVAC zoning configured to limit airborne particle migration between functional spaces; bipolar ionization; high-efficiency filtration; in-duct UV lighting; and cold fusion air treatment. A building automation system upgrade provides integrated humidity and temperature control management across the facility's zones. Minor associated electrical work and plumbing scope round out the project's systems coverage.

## Role

The firm served as prime consultant under a standalone design-bid-build contract issued by the Virginia Department of Military Affairs. As prime, the firm led all mechanical engineering design, conducted the LCCA and TRACE 700 energy analysis, and coordinated associated electrical and plumbing scope. The firm carried the project from scope development and system selection through construction documents; the 65% submittal was delivered in June 2023.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Pathogen mitigation as a primary design driver.** The armory's assembly and educational spaces required integration of multiple independent pathogen mitigation technologies within a single coherent air system—without producing a configuration too complex to maintain by National Guard facility staff operating on a weekend-drill schedule. The layered approach—VAV system with HVAC zoning, high-efficiency filtration, bipolar ionization, UV lighting, and cold fusion—provides redundant protection while remaining operable under standard BAS controls and maintainable by facility personnel.

**System selection within a constrained construction budget.** At $967,000 NTE for a 20,491 GSF complete HVAC replacement, budget discipline was central to system selection. The formal LCCA process evaluated VRF and other alternatives, providing a documented cost-benefit basis for the rooftop VAV system recommendation. Propane fuel for the condensing boiler was consistent with existing site infrastructure, though a service regulator upgrade was required to accommodate the new equipment.

**Mixed-use occupancy profile.** The facility's three use groups—assembly, educational, and storage—have distinct occupancy patterns typical of a National Guard armory on a weekend-drill schedule, with large portions of the building vacant for extended periods. The VAV system with zone-level temperature and humidity control addresses this variable occupancy profile, enabling portions of the building to be set back without affecting other zones.

## Outcomes

The project is complete. The design delivered an HVAC replacement system consistent with the DMA's performance objectives—energy-efficient, pathogen-resilient, and operationally maintainable—within the 210-day design schedule and $967,000 construction budget. The life cycle cost analysis provided a documented basis of system selection satisfying Virginia state procurement requirements for A/E services.
