Mechanical engineering was the primary discipline for this project. The scope centered on design of a replacement chilled water system for Building 143 (Dietetics) at the Tuscaloosa VA Medical Center, encompassing replacement of failing cooling towers, associated central plant equipment, and an existing R-22 refrigerant chiller. The mechanical team was responsible for HVAC design and central plant engineering, addressing both the chilled water system and air distribution systems serving the building.

The existing R-22 chiller represented a regulatory compliance driver: R-22 production in the United States was phased out after 2010 under the Montreal Protocol, making replacement with a compliant modern refrigerant system the required approach. The mechanical scope included developing design submissions at 35%, 65%, 95%, and 100% completion milestones, preparing construction cost estimates at each stage to comply with the $900,000 construction funding limitation required under FAR 52.236-22, and conducting site investigations to support design development. Construction period services included four site visits, one final inspection, and submittal reviews, ensuring the installed chilled water and HVAC systems conformed to design intent.
