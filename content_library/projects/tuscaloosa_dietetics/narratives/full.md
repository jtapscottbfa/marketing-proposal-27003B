## Project Overview

Building 143 (Dietetics) at the Tuscaloosa Veterans Affairs Medical Center (VAMC) in Tuscaloosa, Alabama housed a chilled water system whose central plant equipment — including cooling towers and a chiller operating on the phased-out R-22 refrigerant — had reached the end of serviceable life. The Department of Veterans Affairs, acting through Network Contracting Office (NCO) 7 out of Atlanta, procured architect-engineer (A/E) design services under a 100% Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB) set-aside contract (Contract No. 36C24722R0026, VA Project No. 679-22-104, NAICS 541330) to design the replacement of these systems.

## Scope of Services

The A/E scope required the design team to develop specifications and drawings for the repair or replacement of the chilled water system components in Building 143, including the failing cooling towers, associated mechanical equipment, and the R-22 chiller. Contracted deliverables included:

- **Design submissions** at 35%, 65%, 95%, and 100% completion milestones, each with associated specifications and construction drawings
- **Construction cost estimates** prepared at each submission milestone, required to remain within the $900,000 construction funding limitation per FAR 52.236-22
- **Site investigation services** to support design development
- **Construction period services (CPS)** comprising four site visits, one final inspection, and submittal reviews

The design period was contractually set at 120 calendar days from notice to proceed, with an A/E completion target of FY2022. Disciplines in scope included mechanical engineering (HVAC and central plant), plumbing engineering (domestic water), fire protection engineering (wet sprinkler and fire alarm), and electrical engineering (power distribution).

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant under this design-bid-build delivery. The contract was procured under the Veterans First Contracting Program (VAAR 852.219-10) as a 100% SDVOSB set-aside, with the requirement that at least 50% of the labor cost be performed by the prime consultant's own employees. The prime consultant was responsible for coordinating all disciplines and delivering the complete A/E package to the VA.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**R-22 Refrigerant Compliance.** The existing chiller used R-22 refrigerant, a hydrochlorofluorocarbon (HCFC) whose domestic production was phased out in the United States after 2010 under the Montreal Protocol. Replacement of an R-22 unit requires careful selection of a compliant modern refrigerant and coordination of associated regulatory requirements — matters addressed through the system replacement design developed under this contract.

**Design Within Funding Limitations.** The solicitation invoked FAR 52.236-22 (Design Within Funding Limitations), requiring the design team to deliver a construction-ready package within the $900,000 construction cost ceiling. Construction cost estimates prepared at each submission milestone served as the ongoing control mechanism, allowing scope and specification adjustments before the 100% submission to keep the design within the funding limit.

**Multi-Discipline Coordination in a Healthcare Facility.** The project engaged four engineering disciplines — mechanical, plumbing, fire protection, and electrical — requiring coordinated design across systems in an active medical center building. Submission milestones at 35%, 65%, 95%, and 100% provided structured review points for VA coordination and interdisciplinary conflict resolution.

## Outcomes

Design services for the chilled water system replacement were delivered under a 120-calendar-day contractual design period, targeting completion in FY2022. The project was awarded and executed under a 100% SDVOSB set-aside, supporting the VA's Veterans First Contracting Program objectives. Construction period services — including four site visits, one final inspection, and submittal reviews — extended the design team's involvement through the construction phase, ensuring constructed systems conformed to design intent.
