The electrical engineering scope on the NIT Pier 3 South power feeder project encompassed the complete medium-voltage distribution chain from the Dominion Energy 34.5 kV overhead utility service to the 480Y/277-volt secondary service point at the pier, at Norfolk International Terminals in Norfolk, Virginia.

Design elements included a #1/0 ACSR aluminum conductor utility tap on the existing 19.9 kV/34.5 kV, three-phase, four-wire overhead line; a 600-ampere/35 kV S&C Alduti-Rupter gang-operated sectionalizing switch (Cat. No. SCE137364R3E) for manual isolation; 60-ampere fused cutouts with 27 kV surge arrestors; and a 2,500/2,800-kVA (OA/FA) pad-mounted transformer stepping 34,500 volts down to 480Y/277 volts, three-phase, 60-hertz. The conduit distribution system extended approximately 635 linear feet along the pier deck face in 6-inch rigid pipe conduit supported on stainless steel banding and treated timber blocking at four-foot intervals, transitioning to concrete-encased 6-inch steel conduit in the below-grade approach section with 36-inch cover. An L-shaped junction box served the 90-degree bend at the transformer end.

Electrical design responsibilities included utility coordination with Dominion Energy regarding the pole terminal connection, review and markup of the client's site electrical plan, and production of a complete sealed single-sheet design drawing (E-501, Final Submittal, August 2021). The work drew on power distribution and utility substation sub-discipline expertise within the constraints of an active marine pier environment.
