## Project Overview

The NIT Pier 3 South – Power Feeder Serving Pier project provided a dedicated medium-voltage electrical service to Pier 3 South at Norfolk International Terminals (NIT), a commercial marine cargo terminal operated by Talton Marine Terminals, LLC in Norfolk, Virginia. The project introduced a complete medium-voltage distribution circuit — from a tap at the Dominion Energy 19.9 kV/34.5 kV overhead distribution system, through approximately 635 linear feet of pier-mounted conduit, to a new pad-mounted transformer at the pier end — to supply the pier's 480-volt electrical distribution system. The design firm served as prime consultant, providing electrical engineering design for this standalone infrastructure project. The design was completed as a final submittal in August 2021 (project no. 21-037) under a design-bid-build delivery structure.

## Scope

The scope was limited to electrical power distribution infrastructure; no mechanical, plumbing, fire protection, or architectural work was included. The primary design elements were:

**Utility service tap.** A new tap on Dominion Energy's 19.9 kV/34.5 kV, three-phase, four-wire overhead distribution line via two existing utility poles at the east end of the pier. The connection used #1/0 ACSR aluminum conductor. Design included coordination with Dominion Energy to confirm the suitability of the designated utility pole terminal for the service drop.

**Sectionalizing switch.** A 600-ampere, 35 kV S&C Alduti-Rupter gang-operated switch (Cat. No. SCE137364R3E) providing a manual isolation point downstream of the utility tap.

**Fused cutouts and surge protection.** 200-ampere cutouts fused at 60 amperes, with 27 kV surge arrestors, providing overcurrent and transient overvoltage protection ahead of the transformer.

**Pad-mounted transformer.** A 2,500/2,800-kVA OA/FA rated (3,500 kVA FFA) pad-mounted transformer — 34,500-volt three-phase primary stepping down to 480Y/277-volt, three-phase, 60-hertz secondary — for the pier's distribution system.

**Conduit system.** 6-inch steel conduit in concrete encasement from the utility pole run to the pier edge (Section B), transitioning to 6-inch rigid pipe conduit surface-mounted on the pier deck face for approximately 635 linear feet (Section A). The surface-mounted run used stainless steel banding straps and treated timber blocking at four-foot intervals. An L-shaped junction box at the transformer end accommodated the 90-degree directional change and cable terminations.

## Role on Project

The design firm was the prime consultant and sole engineering discipline on the project, providing full electrical engineering design services. The single-sheet final drawing set (sheet E-501) was prepared and sealed in-house by the project engineer, who also served as drafter, with an in-house checker providing quality review.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Utility coordination under construction schedule pressure.** A critical path item was confirming with Dominion Energy that the designated utility pole terminal could be used for the new service drop. This coordination requirement was explicitly documented on the design drawing — "Contractor to verify with Dominion if Power Pole Terminal #2 can be used for drop" — and was annotated on the client drawing review markup at the pole location. The open item was carried forward into the contract documents for contractor verification rather than treated as a pre-design resolved condition, ensuring the confirmation requirement was not lost during construction.

**Marine environment conduit installation.** Routing approximately 635 linear feet of medium-voltage conduit along an active marine pier required a system suited to the marine environment: corrosion-resistant stainless steel banding, treated timber blocking to isolate conduit from the concrete pier deck surface, and concrete encasement with 36-inch cover in the underground approach section. The design addressed the transition between the below-grade concrete-encased section and the deck-face surface mount at the pier edge.

**Coordination with recent dredging activity.** The project site area at Pier 3 South was dredged on June 1, 2021, to a depth of 36.0 feet — approximately six weeks before the client site plan date of July 7, 2021. The conduit routing and burial depths were designed with awareness of this recent grade disturbance, with the underground approach section providing the required 36-inch cover above the concrete encasement.

## Outcomes

The project produced a complete medium-voltage power feeder design, submitted as a final set in August 2021. The design provides Pier 3 South with a dedicated 480-volt supply derived from the Dominion Energy 34.5 kV distribution system, sized and protected for the pier's electrical loads with service entrance equipment, overcurrent protection, and surge protection. The conduit infrastructure — approximately 635 linear feet of pier-mounted conduit plus the concrete-encased below-grade approach — was designed for durability in the marine service environment. Contract value, construction timeline, and final project cost are not captured in the available source documents.
