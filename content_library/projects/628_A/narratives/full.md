# Building 6-28A Title II Services — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

Building 6-28A — the Dynamometer Building — is a new approximately 10,000 SF steel pre-fabricated facility at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA), a Department of Defense installation at 2835 Harvey Point Road, Hertford, North Carolina. The building is conceived as a sister structure to the adjacent Building 6-28 of similar size and program, and houses a machine shop, vehicle maintenance area, dynamometer testing area, compressed air system, and dedicated communications outbuilding.

The project's origins date to 2017, when a now-defunct engineering firm completed the original design. After a lengthy delay in releasing the project for construction solicitation, the government discovered that the original engineer of record had ceased operations — leaving a completed but aging and partially outdated design package with no active engineering firm of record to support it. Boeing Aerospace Operations (BAO), acting as the prime contractor to the U.S. Government under a Hunter Government Prime Contract, issued a competitive Invitation to Bid (ITB MP-11042022-6-28ATitle2) in November 2022 soliciting a qualified A&E firm to step into that role.

## Scope of Services

BFA was engaged as the prime A&E consultant and engineer of record for all project disciplines. The scope had two integrated components:

**Design Update and Re-Certification.** Within 90 calendar days of Notice to Proceed, BFA reviewed the entire 2017 design package, incorporated a comprehensive list of government-directed changes, coordinated with as-built drawings from a separate 6 Area Electrical Upgrade project completed in 2022, and delivered both a 100% Design Package for client review and a final Stamped/Certified Approved-for-Construction (AFC) drawing and specification set. Changes addressed included: architectural plan revisions (outbuilding pad locations, removal of roof skylights, vestibule relocation, addition of a climate-controlled communications outbuilding, removal of the dynamometer pit in favor of a surface-mount configuration); structural modifications (new concrete pad for DOAS-1 and VEX-1); extensive electrical updates (new 800A main distribution panel, medium-voltage concrete-encased duct bank routing from existing utility pole to the building, emergency lighting revisions, LED lighting to current base standards, car lift electrical); mechanical updates (DOAS-1, packaged terminal AC units, high-pressure dynamometer fume exhaust blower, compressed air system); and fire safety, life safety, IT/communications, and intrusion alarm rough-in additions.

**Title II Construction-Phase Services.** Spanning the project's 365-calendar-day period of performance, BFA provided the full suite of Title II services: onsite pre-bid conference participation, pre-bid contractor question responses, post-award submittal review and log management (updated bi-weekly to BAO), periodic discipline-specific onsite inspections with written compliance reports, review of contractor weekly progress reports, commissioning oversight with pass/fail commissioning compliance report, red-line drawing review, and as-built drawing preparation based on contractor-provided red-lines.

## Key Technical Challenges and Resolutions

**No Active Engineer of Record.** The defining challenge of this project was the absence of an active EOR. BFA stepped into a completed 2017 design from another firm without the benefit of project history, design calculations, or continuity of personnel. The team conducted a comprehensive review of the full design package and coordinated it against field conditions and subsequent infrastructure changes (the 6 Area Electrical Upgrade) to confirm the design basis before issuing AFC documents.

**Obsolete and Inconsistent Electrical Design.** A major electrical area upgrade had been completed in 2022 after the original design was completed in 2017. BFA resolved numerous electrical conflicts: deleted obsolete drawing notes, updated medium-voltage service routing to reflect actual infrastructure, upgraded the main distribution panel from its original size to 800A, updated lighting to current base LED standards, and coordinated the new concrete-encased duct bank and manhole sequence tying the new building to the main switchboard MSB-1 at building 6-53.

**Equipment Selection Changes.** During design development, the original DOAS unit (Valent VPRE 212-18J-30e-C-5DE) was substituted with an Addison PROH-720-E8XL, as documented in BFA's March 2023 Pre-Final Mechanical Equipment Change Log. This reflects the design update and equipment availability rationalization process BFA led as EOR.

**Secure Facility Constraints.** HPDTA is a DoD installation with strict access controls: background checks for all personnel, cell phone and personal electronic device restrictions, no photography, no wireless devices. BFA coordinated all site visits — for pre-bid conference, periodic inspections, and commissioning oversight — within these constraints.

## Disciplines

The project engaged nine design disciplines: architecture, structural engineering, civil engineering, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, plumbing engineering, fire protection engineering, telecommunications engineering, and security/intrusion alarm engineering (rough-in). BFA served as the engineering prime coordinator across all disciplines, consistent with the SOW's requirement that the A&E firm "perform as the engineering prime coordinator for all other disciplines for all Title II service activities."

## Outcomes

BFA delivered a complete re-certified 100% AFC design package, enabling competitive solicitation of the construction contract. Through the Title II phase, BFA supported the construction effort from pre-bid through final commissioning and as-built closeout, ensuring the original design criteria were maintained or exceeded in the constructed building. The project is recorded as complete.
