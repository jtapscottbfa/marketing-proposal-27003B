The design team provided prime architect-engineer services for the replacement and upgrade of Substation DS-20 at a Navy deperming station in Portsmouth, Virginia. The deperming station is a specialized naval facility that reduces the magnetic signature of ships; Substation DS-20 supplies electrical power to that range infrastructure. The project scope encompassed replacement of the existing step-down transformer and secondary switchgear, modernization of substation protection systems for 34.5 kV and 13.8 kV distribution equipment, shoreline stabilization via a new steel sheet pile bulkhead, and code-compliance improvements to bring substation devices up to current flood standards.

The substation occupies approximately 3,000 SF of fenced compound on private property leased by the government adjacent to the Elizabeth River, accessed through an adjacent industrial site — an unusual arrangement for a NAVFAC project that informed both the civil and geotechnical design approach. Foundation design utilized helical piles and cast-in-place concrete mat foundations and equipment platforms suited to the waterfront soil conditions. UFC 4-151-10 general criteria for waterfront construction governed the bulkhead and shoreline work.

Services were delivered under NAVFAC IDIQ A-E contract N40085-20-D-0005, Task Order ST 17-1605 (NAVFAC eProjects Work Order 1582345), with an Estimated Construction Cost of $3,000,000 against a program amount of $3,522,000. The Final Basis of Design was issued June 21, 2021 for a Design-Bid-Build construction contract package. Construction completion was estimated for June 30, 2022.
