Civil engineering scope on this project addressed the site design, shoreline stabilization, and regulatory compliance requirements for a Navy substation compound on the Elizabeth River in Portsmouth, Virginia. The roughly 3,000 SF fenced compound sits on private property leased by the government adjacent to the river — a site arrangement that combined waterfront construction requirements with the standard civil scope elements of a substation replacement.

The principal civil deliverable was the design of a new steel sheet pile bulkhead to replace the existing deteriorated shoreline structure. Bulkhead design followed UFC 4-151-10 general criteria for waterfront construction, the applicable Department of Defense standard for naval waterfront facilities. The design incorporated marine mammal protection considerations and addressed the tidal and jurisdictional permitting requirements associated with in-water work on the Elizabeth River, coordinating across four regulatory authorities: the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE), the Virginia Marine Resources Commission (VMRC), the Virginia Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ), and the Local Wetlands Board.

Site civil scope also included grading and drainage within the substation compound, fencing, erosion and sediment control measures, and coordination with the structural and geotechnical teams on the compound's physical constraints. The civil design managed the intersection of flood-code compliance requirements — driving platform and equipment elevations — with the site's waterfront setting.

The civil scope was completed through the Final Basis of Design issued June 21, 2021, and incorporated into the Design-Bid-Build construction contract package.
