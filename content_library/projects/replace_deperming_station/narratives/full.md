# Replace Deperming Station Substation DS-20

## Project Overview

This project involved prime architect-engineer design services for the replacement and modernization of Substation DS-20 at a Navy deperming station in Portsmouth, Virginia. A deperming station is a specialized naval facility that reduces the residual magnetic signature of ships — a passive degaussing operation critical to fleet survivability. Substation DS-20 supplies the high-voltage electrical power required to drive the deperming range equipment. The substation had reached the end of its service life and required comprehensive replacement of both the primary transformer and secondary protection and distribution systems.

The project site is located near the end of West Norfolk Road in Portsmouth, Virginia, on private property leased by the government adjacent to the Elizabeth River. The roughly 3,000 SF fenced substation compound is accessed by traversing an adjacent industrial property — an atypical site arrangement for NAVFAC work that carried direct implications for utility coordination, geotechnical access, and construction logistics.

Services were delivered under NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic IDIQ A-E contract N40085-20-D-0005, Task Order ST 17-1605 (NAVFAC eProjects Work Order 1582345, BFA Project #20-075). The program amount was $3,522,000 with an Estimated Construction Cost of $3,000,000.

## Scope of Services

The A-E scope encompassed civil, structural, electrical, and geotechnical engineering across a Design-Bid-Build construction contract package. Primary design elements included:

- **Electrical:** Replacement and upgrade of the existing DS-20 step-down transformer and secondary switchgear. Modernization of substation protection systems for 34.5 kV and 13.8 kV distribution equipment, including grounding and lightning protection. The design corrected a client-specification error during development: the SAES had called for a single-phase 50 kVA service transformer, which the Basis of Design revised to a three-phase 75 kVA, 34,500 V Delta – 208Y/120 V three-phase unit consistent with the facility load and code requirements.
- **Structural:** New foundation systems for substation equipment pads and platforms, utilizing helical piles and cast-in-place concrete mat foundations suited to the waterfront soil conditions. Cast-in-place concrete equipment platforms designed to current flood-code elevations.
- **Civil:** Site grading, drainage, and fencing within the 3,000 SF compound. Shoreline stabilization via replacement of the existing deteriorated bulkhead with a new steel sheet pile bulkhead designed to UFC 4-151-10 general criteria for waterfront construction. Erosion and sediment control measures and compliance with tidal permitting requirements (USACE, VMRC, DEQ, and Local Wetlands Board jurisdictions).
- **Geotechnical:** Subsurface investigation and foundation recommendations for the waterfront site. A topographic survey covering approximately 0.42 acres was performed to establish existing conditions across the substation compound and adjacent access corridor.

The Final Basis of Design was issued June 21, 2021. A Design-Bid-Build construction contract package was prepared for competitive procurement, with construction completion estimated by June 30, 2022.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime A-E consultant under the NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic IDIQ contract, providing all engineering disciplines in-house or under direct coordination. The prime consultant was responsible for integrating electrical, structural, civil, and geotechnical scopes, managing the NAVFAC design review process, and ensuring compliance with applicable UFC criteria and federal environmental and waterway permitting requirements.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Waterfront site on leased private property.** The substation is sited on privately held land leased by the government, with access routed through a neighboring industrial facility. This created coordination complexity for subsurface investigation access, utility confirmation, and construction logistics that is atypical for installations on government-owned military land. The design team addressed this through careful topographic and geotechnical surveying scoped to cover the compound and access corridor, and through explicit documentation of access constraints in the project record.

**Flood-code compliance for energized substation equipment.** Bringing existing substation devices up to current flood codes — while maintaining operational continuity of electrical service to the deperming range — required equipment platform designs at flood-compliant elevations and careful coordination of equipment replacement sequencing. Cast-in-place concrete platforms and mat foundations were designed to place new switchgear above the applicable design flood elevation.

**Shoreline stabilization under tidal permitting.** The Elizabeth River frontage required a new steel sheet pile bulkhead under UFC 4-151-10 and required coordination with multiple jurisdictional authorities (USACE, VMRC, DEQ, Local Wetlands Board). The design team incorporated marine mammal protection considerations and waterfront construction criteria into the civil scope, navigating the multi-agency permit environment as part of the DBB package.

**Client-specification discrepancy corrected in design.** The SAES scope documents specified a single-phase 50 kVA service transformer; the design team identified this as inconsistent with the facility load requirements and the three-phase nature of the distribution system. The Basis of Design formally corrected the specification to a three-phase 75 kVA unit (34,500 V Delta – 208Y/120 V), preventing a procurement error that would have required a change order during construction.

## Outcomes

The Final Basis of Design was completed and issued June 21, 2021, establishing the technical basis for a Design-Bid-Build construction package with an Estimated Construction Cost of $3,000,000. Construction completion was targeted for June 30, 2022. The design modernized a critical piece of naval infrastructure supporting the deperming mission at Norfolk, addressed documented equipment deterioration, and brought the facility into compliance with current flood and waterfront construction standards.
