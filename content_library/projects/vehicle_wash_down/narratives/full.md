# Vehicle Washdown Area Survey and Design — Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The design team provided architectural and engineering survey and design services for a new vehicle washdown facility at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA), a Department of Defense installation located at 2835 Harvey Point Road in Hertford, North Carolina. Services were delivered to Boeing Aerospace Operations (BAO), the facility operations contractor and contracting entity, on behalf of HPDTA and the US Government. The project was executed under BFA project number 23-018 with a 580-calendar-day period of performance covering site survey, design, and Title II construction phase services.

The project replaced the former Building 5-2 — a demolished warehouse — with an approximately 3,862-square-foot, one-story masonry vehicle washdown building. A three-bay concept was studied at the pre-35% submittal stage, presenting two configurations: Option 1 with one large automatic vehicle wash bay, one small automatic car wash bay, and one manual degreasing bay; and Option 2 substituting a second manual bay for the large automatic wash to reduce cost. The design advanced to 95% completion as a two-bay facility — one automatic wash for small and medium vehicles and one manual wash and degreasing bay — with construction cost estimated at approximately $4.47 million and an estimated construction duration of 365 days.

## Scope

The design team's scope followed a three-phase process defined in the Statement of Work: (1) a site survey to gather existing conditions data, (2) a design recommendation report identifying anomalies and areas of concern, and (3) full construction document preparation. The SOW identified required engineering disciplines as architectural, civil, structural, mechanical, and electrical, with plumbing, fire protection, and environmental compliance carried through each discipline's scope.

The building is a one-story masonry structure with 4-inch brick veneer and 8-inch reinforced CMU bearing walls supporting light-gauge metal trusses and standing seam metal roofing. The exterior was detailed to match the established campus aesthetic at HPDTA. Two vehicle wash bays are served by paired 12-foot by 14-foot overhead coiling doors on each face. Interior wall and ceiling surfaces in wash bays were specified with moisture-resistant PVC panel systems; the mechanical room received furred-out metal stud framing, batt insulation, and gypsum board. All floor finishes are sealed concrete sloped to trench drains.

Civil scope included site grading and drainage design, stormwater management, erosion and sediment control, utility coordination, and site paving and access improvements to accommodate vehicles up to 80,000 pounds.

Plumbing design provided domestic cold water distribution with a 3-inch backflow preventer, non-freeze wall hydrants, floor drains and trench drain systems, oil-water separators serving each wash bay, three 1,500-gallon reclaim tanks, a 1,000-gallon grease interceptor, and a new wastewater lift station connection to the base wastewater treatment plant. Water metering was provided for both fresh intake and wastewater discharge monitoring. Compressed air piping was also included in the plumbing scope.

Electrical scope included a new medium-voltage service transitioned from aerial to underground at a new utility pole, a 750 kVA pad-mounted transformer, an 800-ampere 277/480V main distribution panel, and a 600-ampere 120/208V lighting and receptacle panel supplied through a 150 kVA dry-type step-down transformer. Wet-location Lithonia D-series bay luminaires and exterior wall pack lighting were provided throughout. A 48-strand single-mode fiber optic connection to Building 7-25 established telecom service to the new facility, and a warning/annunciator signal light system was installed on the exterior north face.

Mechanical scope addressed ventilation to dilute spray chemical fumes in the wash bays, equipment protection climate control, and electric unit heating.

Environmental scope addressed National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) compliance, petroleum, oil, and lubricant storage requirements, spill control planning, and stormwater permitting in coordination with the North Carolina Department of Environmental and Natural Resources. The project was subject to Davis-Bacon prevailing wage requirements under wage decision NC20220057.

## Role on Project

The design team served as the prime design consultant, responsible for all engineering disciplines and coordination with HPDTA, Boeing Aerospace Operations, and equipment vendors including the AUTEC Car Wash Systems car wash equipment supplier. Design was executed to Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC) standards applicable to each discipline, including UFC 3-301-01 (Structural Engineering), UFC 3-101-01 (Architecture), UFC 3-500-01 (Electrical), UFC 3-520-01 (Interior Electrical Systems), and UFC 4-211-01. The project was scoped as a Design-Bid-Build delivery, with construction intended to be bid on a lump sum competitive basis with a minimum of four qualified bidders.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Subsurface legacy conditions.** The building site coincided with the footprint of Building 5-2, a former warehouse demolished and removed above grade but leaving "utility pole" type wood pilings in place below grade. These pilings intersected the proposed foundation footprint. The structural design accommodated this condition by anticipating cut-off of the pilings approximately 4 to 5 feet below existing grade before placing new shallow spread footings, with final determination deferred pending civil site plan finalization and geotechnical investigation.

**Wastewater treatment at a high-wash-volume facility.** Vehicle wash operations generate significant wastewater laden with oils, greases, and sediment. The plumbing design addressed this through a layered treatment approach: oil-water separators per bay capturing wash effluent at source, reclaim tank systems for wash water recovery, a grease interceptor protecting the base sewer system, a sediment filtration system, and metered discharge to a new lift station connection at the base wastewater treatment plant. This multi-barrier design was required to satisfy both the facility's operational needs and HPDTA's environmental compliance requirements.

**Structural loads for heavy vehicle operations.** The facility was required to accommodate vehicles up to 80,000 pounds in the large wash bay. The structural slab-on-grade design provided an 8-inch reinforced concrete slab in the large bay, a 6-inch slab in the smaller bay, and a 4-inch slab in the equipment room and office, with thickened areas at tank locations. Load design was governed by the heavier vehicle loads rather than standard occupancy criteria.

**Two-option design at pre-35% stage.** The pre-35% submittal presented both a three-bay option and a two-bay option to HPDTA, with analysis showing the cost savings from Option 2 were likely less than 10% of total project cost given the building itself being the primary cost driver. HPDTA selected a configuration, and the design advanced to 95% completion on that basis.

## Outcomes

The design team advanced the project to pre-final (95%) design completion, producing a construction document package with an estimated construction cost of approximately $4.47 million. The design met UFC standards for both facility function and campus aesthetic integration. No fire suppression system was identified in the basis of design or cost estimate for this occupancy type; fire alarm status was not documented in available records. Construction was anticipated to begin October 2024, with the 365-day construction duration implying substantial completion in fall 2025.
