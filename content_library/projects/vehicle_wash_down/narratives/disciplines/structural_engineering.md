Structural engineering scope for the HPDTA vehicle washdown facility addressed foundation design, slab-on-grade design for heavy vehicle loads, and superstructure framing for a one-story masonry bearing wall building. The building is approximately 3,862 square feet in plan, with exterior and interior bearing walls of 8-inch reinforced CMU providing lateral resistance to wind and seismic loads. The roof structure consists of light-gauge metal trusses at 4-foot spacing anchored to masonry bearing walls with embedded hold-down clips, supporting metal decking and standing seam roofing.

Foundation design used shallow spread footings ranging from 2-foot by 12-inch to 2-foot-6-inch by 12-inch in section, sized for an assumed allowable bearing pressure of 2,000 PSF pending geotechnical confirmation. A significant subsurface challenge was the presence of legacy wood pilings from the demolished Building 5-2 within the new building's footprint. The structural approach was to cut these pilings off approximately 4 to 5 feet below existing grade before placing new footings, with final extents to be confirmed after civil site plan finalization and geotechnical investigation.

Slab-on-grade design was governed by vehicle weight requirements: an 8-inch reinforced concrete slab with #4 bars at 12-inch spacing each way in the large wash bay (80,000-pound vehicle design load), a 6-inch slab with #4 bars at 18-inch spacing in the smaller bay (20,000-pound design load), and a 4-inch slab with welded wire reinforcement in the equipment room. Design was performed to UFC 3-301-01, IBC, and 110 MPH wind speed (Seismic Design Category B, Site Class D).
