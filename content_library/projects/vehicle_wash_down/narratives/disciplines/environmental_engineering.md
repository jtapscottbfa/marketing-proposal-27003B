Environmental engineering scope for the HPDTA vehicle washdown facility addressed regulatory compliance requirements applicable to construction and operations at a Department of Defense installation in North Carolina. The Statement of Work dedicated a full section to environmental requirements covering National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) compliance, petroleum, oil, and lubricant (POL) storage, spill control and cleanup planning, and stormwater management.

NEPA compliance was required for the project as a federal action on a DoD facility, with the environmental scope encompassing the documentation and review processes required prior to construction authorization. Stormwater management requirements were governed by the North Carolina Department of Environmental and Natural Resources (NCDENR), including preparation of a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) and implementation of best management practices (BMPs) for erosion and sediment control during construction. The environmental scope was closely coordinated with the civil engineering discipline for site grading, drainage, and utility design.

Operational environmental compliance for the wash facility — including management of wash effluent, oil-water separation, grease interception, and metered discharge to the base wastewater treatment plant — was addressed through the plumbing engineering scope in coordination with environmental requirements. Spill control and cleanup procedures were specified as part of the project's environmental requirements to protect base soil and water resources consistent with HPDTA's environmental health and safety requirements for contractors. Hazardous material handling requirements applicable to the demolition of the Building 5-2 site and subsurface conditions were also within the environmental compliance boundary.
