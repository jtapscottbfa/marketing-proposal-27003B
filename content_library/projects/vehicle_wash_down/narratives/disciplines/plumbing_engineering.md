Plumbing engineering scope for the HPDTA vehicle washdown facility was one of the most complex and operationally critical elements of the project, addressing wash water supply, wastewater treatment and discharge, reclaim, and compressed air distribution for a high-volume vehicle wash operation on a DoD installation.

Domestic cold water service enters the building through a 3-inch backflow preventer protecting the base water supply. Non-freeze wall hydrants and hose bibs distribute water within the facility. Each wash bay is drained through floor drains and trench drain systems sloped to match car wash equipment provider requirements, with slab penetrations and trenching coordinated with the structural slab-on-grade design. An oil-water separator serves each wash bay, capturing vehicle wash effluent before it reaches the sanitary system. Three 1,500-gallon reclaim tanks collect and recirculate wash water, reducing fresh water consumption. A 1,000-gallon grease interceptor protects the downstream base sewer system from grease and oil carryover. Effluent that cannot be reclaimed is directed through a sediment filtration system and discharged via a new wastewater lift station connection to the base wastewater treatment plant. Both fresh water intake and wastewater discharge are metered for operational monitoring and environmental compliance reporting.

Compressed air piping distributed throughout the facility serves car wash equipment and manual cleaning operations. Plumbing design was coordinated with civil grading for site utility connections, with structural for trench drain and tank locations in the slab, and with environmental engineering for NEPA and spill control compliance.
