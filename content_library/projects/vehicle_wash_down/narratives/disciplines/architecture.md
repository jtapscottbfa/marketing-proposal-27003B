Architectural design for the vehicle washdown facility at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA) encompassed building envelope design, interior layout, material selection, and UFC code compliance for a new approximately 3,862-square-foot, one-story structure. The building replaced the site of the demolished Building 5-2 and was sited and detailed to match the established campus aesthetic — exterior walls consist of 4-inch brick veneer over 8-inch reinforced CMU, aligning the new facility with surrounding HPDTA structures. The roof system is a standing seam metal roof over metal decking supported by light-gauge metal trusses bearing on the masonry walls.

The program provides two vehicle wash bays — one automatic and one manual degreasing bay — plus a mechanical room housing car wash system equipment. Each wash bay is accessed through paired 12-foot by 14-foot overhead coiling doors. Interior wall and ceiling finishes in the wash bays are PVC panel systems selected for durability in a wet, chemical-exposure environment; the mechanical room is finished with metal stud furring, R-13 batt insulation, and gypsum board. All floor finishes are sealed concrete sloped to drain. A mezzanine in the manual bay accommodates the washing and degreasing of large service vehicles.

Design criteria included UFC 1-200-01, UFC 3-101-01, UFC 3-120-10, UFC 3-190-06, IBC 2018, and ICC A117.1-2009. The design team coordinated architectural scope with structural bay geometry, plumbing trench drain layouts, mechanical ventilation requirements, and electrical door and lighting rough-in throughout the construction document phase.
