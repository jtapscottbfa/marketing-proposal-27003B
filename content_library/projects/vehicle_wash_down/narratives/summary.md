The design team provided survey and full engineering design services for a new vehicle washdown facility at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA), a Department of Defense installation in Hertford, North Carolina. The project replaced the demolished Building 5-2 site with an approximately 3,862-square-foot, one-story masonry building housing two vehicle wash bays — one automatic wash serving small and medium vehicles and one manual wash and degreasing bay equipped with a mezzanine to accommodate large service vehicles and mechanical equipment. The facility was designed to UFC standards throughout, including UFC 4-211-01, and the exterior envelope was detailed to match the established campus aesthetic using 4-inch brick veneer over 8-inch reinforced CMU bearing walls with a standing seam metal roof.

Scope spanned architectural, structural, civil, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, and environmental disciplines. Structural design addressed legacy subsurface conditions — residual wood pilings from the prior building required cut-off to depth before new shallow spread footings could be placed. Plumbing design incorporated oil-water separators, reclaim tanks, a grease interceptor, and a new wastewater lift station connection to the base treatment plant, satisfying both facility operations and environmental compliance requirements. Electrical scope included a new medium-voltage service with a 750 kVA pad-mounted transformer and a 277/480V distribution system to serve car wash equipment, wet-location bay lighting, and a 48-strand single-mode fiber optic telecom connection. Construction cost was estimated at approximately $4.47 million at the 95% design level, with an estimated 365-day construction duration. Services were delivered for Boeing Aerospace Operations as the contracting entity on behalf of HPDTA and the US Government.
