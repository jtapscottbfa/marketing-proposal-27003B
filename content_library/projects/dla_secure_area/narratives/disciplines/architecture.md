The architectural scope for the DLA Secure Area Renovation centered on converting a raw, unfinished 650-square-foot interior — concrete floor, exposed metal deck ceiling, and exposed structural steel — into a hardened SCIF interior meeting ICS 705-1 and UFC 4-010-05 accreditation standards.

Partition design was driven by acoustic and physical security requirements. All partitions throughout the Secure Suite were specified and constructed to Sound Group 4 (STC 50 minimum) acoustic standards, satisfying the ICS 705-1 mandatory noise attenuation threshold for sensitive compartmented information processing spaces. SCIF-compliant interior doors were selected and detailed in coordination with the electronic security system, providing access-controlled entry at suite boundaries while maintaining the required acoustic rating.

RF foil shielding was integrated into wall and ceiling assemblies throughout the Secure Suite perimeter as a TEMPEST countermeasure, forming the RF barrier required under UFC 4-010-05 for classified information processing environments. This shielding was coordinated with mechanical, electrical, and telecommunications penetrations through the perimeter to avoid compromising the RF barrier integrity.

Interior finishes — flooring, acoustical ceiling, and wall surfaces — were specified to complete occupancy-ready environments appropriate for the VTC Room, IT/Server Room, and Common Open Office. The construction sequence was designed to maintain physical separation from the adjacent Command Control Center (CCC), which remained active and accessible throughout construction. The design team served as Designer of Record for the project, with the architectural discipline spanning the full interior renovation scope within the SCIF accreditation boundary.
