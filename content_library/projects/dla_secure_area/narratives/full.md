# Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) Secure Area Renovation

## Project Overview

The Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) Secure Area Renovation converted an unfinished 650-square-foot interior space in Building 45 at Naval Support Activity (NSA) Philadelphia, Pennsylvania into a fully accredited Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) Secure Suite. The facility was designed to meet Intelligence Community Standard ICS 705-1 and Unified Facilities Criteria UFC 4-010-05 — the governing federal standards for facilities used to store, process, and discuss sensitive compartmented information. The SCIF was configured as a Continuous Operation (24/7) type, housing three functional spaces: a Video Teleconference (VTC) Room, an IT/Server Room, and a Common Open Office area. Building 45 is a newly constructed facility, opened in November 2019, situated within a restricted fenced compound at NSA Philadelphia (4921 South Broad Street, Philadelphia, PA 19112). The contracting authority was NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic, with DLA Troop Support as the end-user occupant whose operations drove the project's security and operational requirements.

## Scope

The project encompassed full Design-Build renovation of a raw, unfinished space — concrete floor, exposed metal deck ceiling, and exposed structural steel beams — into a hardened, multi-discipline engineered SCIF. Principal scope elements by discipline:

**Architecture.** Interior partition construction throughout to Sound Group 4 (STC 50 minimum) acoustic standards, satisfying ICS 705-1 acoustic attenuation requirements. SCIF-compliant interior doors and interior finishes (flooring, ceiling, wall surfaces) to complete occupancy-ready spaces. RF foil shielding integrated into wall and ceiling assemblies throughout the Secure Suite perimeter as a TEMPEST countermeasure.

**Mechanical (HVAC).** A standalone packaged HVAC system sized for the Secure Suite, providing air distribution to all three functional spaces under continuous 24/7 operating conditions. The system was designed independently of the building's shared HVAC infrastructure to maintain the isolation required for SCIF accreditation. Building automation system (BAS) controls were incorporated for monitoring and operation.

**Electrical.** Dedicated electrical service panel (Panel EML1A) serving only the Secure Suite. Power line RF filters on supply conductors entering the SCIF boundary to prevent conducted signal emanation — a TEMPEST countermeasure required under UFC 4-010-05. Red/Black electrical separation isolating classified-signal circuits from unclassified loads. Interior lighting for all spaces. Emergency power provisions supporting the 24/7 continuity-of-operations requirement.

**Plumbing.** Domestic water and sanitary connections to serve the occupied suite.

**Fire Protection.** Modifications to the building's existing wet sprinkler system and fire alarm system to extend coverage and detection into the newly constructed SCIF spaces, integrated to support continuous occupancy.

**Telecommunications.** Structured cabling infrastructure — CAT6 copper and fiber optic cabling — serving the VTC Room and IT/Server Room, routed and terminated within the SCIF accreditation boundary per ICS 705-1 requirements.

**Electronic Security System (ESS).** Integrated access control system (ACS), intrusion detection system (IDS), and closed-circuit television (CCTV), designed to satisfy ICS 705-1 physical security requirements. Cybersecurity requirements for classified facility infrastructure were addressed across the ESS design and coordinated with the electrical Red/Black separation strategy.

## Role

The design team served as Designer of Record (DOR) under the prime Design-Build contractor — a role defined in the NAVFAC Design-Build contract as the licensed architect/engineer responsible for providing design services, holding professional stamping authority, and fulfilling quality assurance obligations for the design documents. All engineering and architectural disciplines were delivered under this DOR engagement, documented as BFA Project No. 20-084. The Basis of Design Final Submittal was completed April 12, 2021, marking completion of the design phase.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Operations continuity in an active restricted facility.** Building 45 houses the adjacent Command Control Center (CCC), a continuously active, restricted operations space whose activities could not be interrupted during construction. Temporary partition walls and controlled-access doors were installed to physically isolate the SCIF construction zone from the active CCC before renovation work began. Construction sequencing was managed to protect CCC operations throughout the project duration.

**SCIF accreditation across all disciplines simultaneously.** Converting a raw, open interior to ICS 705-1 accreditation standards required each discipline to be designed not in isolation but as a coordinated accreditation system. Acoustic requirements drove partition and door specifications. TEMPEST requirements drove both RF foil shielding in the architectural assemblies and the electrical Red/Black separation and RF filter design. Physical security requirements drove the integrated ACS/IDS/CCTV ESS design. Telecommunications routing and fire protection penetrations each required specific treatment at the SCIF perimeter to maintain acoustic and RF shielding integrity. Achieving accreditation required all disciplines to satisfy their respective requirements simultaneously within a single 650-square-foot envelope.

**Standalone systems within a shared building.** Because the SCIF must function as an isolated, controlled environment, both the HVAC and electrical systems were designed as independent, Secure Suite-dedicated systems. The mechanical standalone design prevented any shared ductwork pathways that could create acoustic or signal-leakage vulnerabilities at the SCIF boundary. The dedicated electrical panel and power conditioning isolated the suite's electrical infrastructure from the building's common distribution. Telecommunications and security cabling were similarly contained within the accreditation boundary.

**New construction with access restrictions.** The work took place inside a newly constructed building within a restricted fenced compound at NSA Philadelphia, with controlled access to both the installation and the building. Contractor personnel and the Designer of Record were required to meet security clearance requirements specified in the RFP, and the classified facility context imposed coordination requirements with the accrediting authority throughout the design process.

## Outcomes

The completed design delivered a fully accredited SCIF Secure Suite of approximately 650 square feet at NSA Philadelphia, supporting continuous 24/7 operation for DLA Troop Support. The design addressed the full ICS 705-1 and UFC 4-010-05 requirements across all seven disciplines within the project scope — acoustic, TEMPEST, physical security, mechanical isolation, and cybersecurity — within a compact interior renovation footprint and an active restricted-access facility.
