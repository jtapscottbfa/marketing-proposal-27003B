# Additional Cooling for Rooms 109 & 110 of F Wing, CEBAF Center — Jefferson Lab

## Project Overview

The design team provided architectural-engineering design services for supplemental cooling in two rooms (F109 and F110) of the F-Wing of CEBAF Center, Building 12, Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab), located at 12000 Jefferson Avenue, Newport News, Virginia. Jefferson Lab is a U.S. Department of Energy Office of Science user facility operated by Jefferson Science Associates (JSA), LLC. The work was procured under Task Order #5 of JSA master contract 21-C0683 (Purchase Order 21C0683005), with the design team serving as prime consultant for A-E services. The design RFQ was issued September 26, 2022; the purchase order was issued October 13, 2022; NTP was received October 14, 2022. Final design was delivered on April 10, 2023.

The project responds to a documented need for supplemental cooling capacity in two electronics-intensive laboratory support rooms that the building's base HVAC system could not adequately serve. Per the project narrative on drawing M-001, "the project consists of providing additional cooling for Rooms 109 & 110 of F-Wing, CEBAF Center, Jefferson Lab. Equipment was sized as per the owner's scope-of-work with an additional 10% capacity."

## Scope of Work

The design scope encompassed complete engineering design services for a two-room supplemental cooling installation, including:

- **60% design package** and **100%/final construction documents** with all drawings, specifications, cost estimate, equipment selection data, and project schedule — delivered as a single combined final submittal package per JLab's standard format.
- **Full specification set** covering Division 01 (general requirements), Division 23 (HVAC), and Division 26 (electrical).
- **Construction cost estimate** with markups (bond, contingency, overhead, profit), yielding an estimated construction cost of approximately $40,361 against a $50,000 program budget.
- **Project schedule** projecting an approximately eight-calendar-day construction window (May 22–29, 2023), encompassing HVAC/plumbing, electrical, DDC, and closeout activities.

The mechanical system design specifies two Daikin 18 MBH wall-mounted indoor split-system air handlers (model FTXM18VVJU — one per room, designated AHU-1 in F109 and AHU-2 in F110) paired with two outdoor air-cooled condensing units (model RXM18VVJU, designated ACCU-1 and ACCU-2) mounted on new concrete pads outside the F-Wing. Condensate drainage is provided via 3/4-inch PVC condensate drain piping. The design also includes the removal of an existing portable cooling unit and associated ductwork in Room F110, with ceiling tile patching at the penetration.

The electrical design provides power to both condensing units from existing 225A, 208Y/120V panelboard RP1G via two new 2-pole circuit breakers (15A and 12A) added to the panelboard. Two new 30A, 2-pole, non-fused NEMA 4X disconnect switches are provided outdoors at each condensing unit. Conduit routing transitions from 3/4-inch EMT to LFMC at the exterior wall penetration, with a sleeve-and-seal detail per the drawing standard.

## Role on Project

The design team served as **prime consultant** responsible for the complete A-E scope. Services included: scope development, equipment selection and sizing (mechanical and electrical), construction documents production, construction cost estimating, and specification writing. Post-design construction services (RFI response, submittal review) were included in the fee. Key personnel included the designer of record and cost estimator. The project was issued as a qualifications-based selection under the existing JSA on-call A-E contract.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Federal laboratory environment and DOE procurement structure.** Jefferson Lab's contracting structure (DOE as ultimate owner; JSA as operator and contracting entity) required navigating DOE accounting codes, JSA purchase order formats, and JLab drawing standards — including a seven-role JLab approval signature chain on each drawing sheet. The drawing and specification package met JLab's combined deliverable format (drawings, specifications, cost estimate, equipment data, and schedule in one PDF submission).

**Equipment sizing with capacity margin.** The owner's scope-of-work defined cooling loads for the two rooms, and the design added a 10 percent capacity margin above the stated scope to account for operational growth in the electronics load. Daikin 18 MBH split systems were selected and documented with full equipment selection data in the final submittal.

**DDC integration with existing controls infrastructure.** The new split-system units were specified to integrate with the facility's existing BACnet DDC network administered by Johnson Controls. New thermostats were set 2°F above the existing room thermostats (new set points: 75°F; existing: 73°F) to define control priority hierarchy and prevent contention between the supplemental and base HVAC systems.

**Constrained construction schedule.** The projected construction window of approximately eight calendar days (May 22–29, 2023) required sequencing HVAC/plumbing, electrical, and DDC commissioning activities within the facility's operational constraints as an active national laboratory.

## Outcomes

The design team delivered complete 100 percent/final construction documents for the two-room supplemental cooling installation on April 10, 2023, within the A-E fee of $7,915 and the $50,000 program construction budget. The estimated construction cost at final submittal was approximately $40,361 — within budget with margin. The eight-sheet drawing set (cover, mechanical legend/narrative, demolition plan, mechanical new work plan, mechanical details, mechanical schedules and controls, electrical legend/schedules/details, and electrical floor plan) and accompanying specifications provided a complete, bid-ready package. The design is complete; construction was not yet awarded per project documents.
