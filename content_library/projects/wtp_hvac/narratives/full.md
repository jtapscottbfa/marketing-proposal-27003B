## Project Overview

The Gloucester SWTP HVAC project addressed aging and failing HVAC systems at two critical process buildings within Gloucester County's Surface Water Treatment Plant (SWTP) complex at 8929 Reservoir Ridge Road, Gloucester County, Virginia. The existing equipment serving both the Reverse Osmosis (RO) Plant Server Room and the Water Treatment Plant (WTP) Lab required replacement to maintain reliable environmental control in spaces housing sensitive water treatment instrumentation and laboratory operations. Gloucester County engaged BFA as prime consultant to develop the replacement HVAC design and a complete procurement specification package for public competitive bid under local competitive (IFB) procedures.

## Scope

The project comprised two independent but co-bid scopes:

**Scope 1 — RO Plant Server Room.** Removal and disposal of the existing 10-ton HVAC unit at the Reverse Osmosis Plant. Installation of a replacement split system: Trane TWA120044 heat pump (outdoor unit) and Trane TWE12044 air handler (indoor unit), served by 3-phase 460V electrical service. Equipment was selected to match existing system capacity and spatial footprint while replacing aging components in the temperature-sensitive Server Room environment.

**Scope 2 — WTP Lab.** Disconnection and abandonment of the existing HVAC system serving the Water Treatment Plant Lab. Installation of a new 4-ton multi-port mini-split heat pump (Trane/Mitsubishi MXZ-SM48) with two 2-ton ceiling cassette indoor air handling units (Trane/Mitsubishi PLA-A24EA8) and a branch selector box (PAC-MKA32BC). The scope also included outside makeup air introduced through 6-inch intake ducts fitted with 16×8 flanged storm blade wall louvers and volume dampers, balanced to 150 CFM per ASHRAE 62.1. High-voltage electrical wiring from the abandoned system was rerouted to serve the new mini-split outdoor unit, with new breakers provided as required. Pre-construction coordination with the County Engineer was specified for duct routing and makeup air discharge location.

## Role

BFA served as prime consultant, responsible for the full HVAC design and specification for both scopes. BFA developed the complete Scope of Work, HVAC technical specifications, equipment selection and sizing criteria, installation requirements, testing and balancing criteria, and contractor coordination protocols. This work was issued as Addendum No. 3 to Gloucester County's Invitation for Bids — a 100% design submittal dated June 28, 2024 — which replaced the Scope of Work and Price Page from the original bid documents. The project was procured as a standalone design-bid-build contract under local competitive bidding with low-bid award.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Active water treatment facility constraints.** Both scopes were located within operating water treatment facilities where interruptions could affect public water supply. The specification addressed this through detailed equipment removal sequencing, equipment selection matched to existing system footprints where feasible, and explicit pre-construction coordination requirements with the County Engineer. The ceiling cassette configuration selected for the WTP Lab (mini-split with branch box) allowed staged installation with minimal disruption to the lab ceiling plane.

**Makeup air compliance in a constrained space.** The WTP Lab scope required outside makeup air compliant with ASHRAE 62.1 in a building with limited routing options — the specification references a 3rd-floor slab context that constrains duct paths. BFA's design specified 6-inch duct runs with storm blade wall louvers and volume dampers balanced to 150 CFM, with an explicit requirement for pre-construction coordination between the contractor and County Engineer to verify exact routing before installation. This pre-construction coordination requirement transferred routing risk appropriately while ensuring ASHRAE compliance was not compromised.

**Two-scope specification under a single bid document.** BFA structured Addendum No. 3 as a single IFB covering both the RO Plant and WTP Lab scopes with separate line items on the bid price page. This allowed Gloucester County to evaluate and award both scopes together while maintaining clear cost separation between facilities. The scope update incorporated into Addendum No. 3 (scope updated May 15, 2024; addendum issued June 28, 2024) reflects design refinement through the procurement phase, resulting in a clean 100% submittal package.

## Outcomes

BFA delivered a 100% HVAC design and procurement specification supporting Gloucester County's replacement of end-of-life HVAC equipment at both the RO Plant and WTP Lab. The county's desired construction completion was June 30, 2024. Given the small scope, the elapsed time since the 2024 bid documents, and the completeness of the procurement package, the project is assessed as complete. Specific contract award and final construction values are not available in the source documents, which reflect the pre-award solicitation phase.
