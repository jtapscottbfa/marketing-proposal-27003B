# NIH Parking Lot — Demolition of Buildings 18, 18T, 32 & 32T and Construct Surface Parking Lot

## Project Overview

This project repurposed a portion of the NIH Bethesda Campus at 9000 Rockville Pike, Bethesda, Maryland. Four aging single-floor research buildings — Buildings 18, 18T, 32, and 32T, totaling 30,400 GSF combined — were demolished, and the cleared site was redeveloped as a paved surface parking lot. The project was executed under NIH Contract C101656 for the NIH Office of Research Facilities, Division of Design and Construction Management, using a Design-Build delivery methodology. The project targeted construction completion by December 31, 2021.

The approximately 0.75-acre site at the southern end of the NIH Bethesda Campus required careful integration of new electrical infrastructure to support safe and secure continuous use of the new parking facility.

## Scope

The scope of design services was focused entirely on exterior electrical systems. The Electrical Basis of Design (BOD) addressed the following:

- **Parking lot LED lighting:** New energy-efficient LED light fixtures distributed across the parking lot to support safe, secure nighttime operations. Fixture selection was specified on Sheet E-501 of the drawing set (fixture schedule).
- **200A exterior panelboard:** A new 200A exterior panelboard was designed adjacent to the existing Transformer AT-3, providing a dedicated distribution point for the parking lot lighting circuits and the gate booth load.
- **Gate booth power:** A new power supply circuit was designed to serve the new parking lot gate booth, integrated into the 200A panelboard.
- **Distribution infrastructure:** Underground distribution via Schedule 40 PVC conduit (minimum 1½-inch diameter), minimum #6 AWG copper conductors with THHN/THWN insulation rated at 600V. Equipment grounding conductors were specified on all feeder and branch circuits; all lighting poles were grounded per code.

The SOW directed the design team to "provide energy-efficient utilities and lighting to support safe and secure continuous use of parking lot," which drove the LED fixture selection and the continuous-duty rating of all distribution infrastructure.

## Role on Project

The electrical engineer served as a subconsultant on the Design-Build team delivering NIH Contract C101656. The electrical scope was entirely self-contained — no other MEP disciplines were involved in the BFA-authored portion of the project, as the surface parking lot required no HVAC, plumbing, or fire protection systems. The electrical engineer was responsible for the complete Electrical Basis of Design, from system architecture through conductor sizing and grounding specifications. The BOD final submittal was issued December 1, 2021.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Federal campus integration — connecting to existing infrastructure.** The new parking lot electrical system was not a freestanding utility installation; it required tapping an existing on-campus transformer (AT-3) and installing a new exterior panelboard that would integrate with NIH's campus electrical infrastructure. The BOD addressed this by specifying the panelboard location adjacent to the existing transformer and sizing the 200A service to accommodate both the lighting loads and the gate booth, with capacity for future expansion implicit in the 200A service rating.

**Site-only project with no building context.** The project is atypical for A/E practice in that it involves no building — the deliverable is a surface parking lot. This constrained the electrical scope to site lighting and site electrical distribution only, with no interior electrical systems. The design challenge was ensuring the exterior lighting layout and distribution infrastructure met NIH's operational requirements (continuous use, safety, security) within the available 0.75-acre footprint.

**Design-Build delivery with A/E BOD responsibility.** On a Design-Build contract, the basis of design is often developed collaboratively between the A/E and the contractor. The electrical BOD was issued as a standalone Final Submittal, indicating the A/E component was responsible for a complete, contractor-independent technical basis that the construction phase could execute against.

## Outcomes

The Electrical Basis of Design final submittal was completed and issued December 1, 2021. The desired construction completion date was December 31, 2021. The project delivered a new surface parking lot on the NIH Bethesda Campus with complete exterior electrical infrastructure: LED site lighting, a 200A exterior distribution panel, and gate booth power — all designed to meet NIH requirements for energy efficiency and secure, continuous operations. Contract value and actual construction completion date are not documented in the available source record.
