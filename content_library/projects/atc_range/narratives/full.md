# ATC Firing Range — Indoor Firing Range #2 Renovation
## Advanced Training Center, Harpers Ferry, WV | U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)
### BFA Project No. 22-018 | Basis of Design: January 2023

---

## Project Overview

Bowman Foster & Associates (BFA) served as prime engineering consultant for the renovation and alteration of Indoor Firing Range #2 (Room 134) at the CBP Advanced Training Center (ATC) in Harpers Ferry, West Virginia. The CBP ATC is an active federal law enforcement training installation, and Range No. 2 required comprehensive renovation to bring it into alignment with the configuration and performance of the adjacent Indoor Range No. 3. BFA delivered coordinated structural, architectural, and electrical engineering design, with HVAC scope coordinated through a specialty range ventilation subconsultant.

---

## Existing Conditions and Design Challenge

Range No. 2 presented a well-worn interior characteristic of long-service indoor ballistic ranges. The existing ceiling system used a series of baffles set at 30 degrees from horizontal, with control lighting positioned at gaps aligned with the different firing lines. The baffle structure consisted of steel frame supports attached to the existing roof structure carrying ¼-inch or 3/8-inch thick ballistic steel plates, with 1-inch thick Tectum acoustical ceiling panels on 7/8-inch furring. The walls were precast concrete panels clad with 2-inch Tectum acoustical panels on furring with acoustic batt. The floor was polished exposed concrete. The existing building was fully sprinklered.

The renovation challenge was three-dimensional: the ceiling geometry had to be fundamentally reconfigured (from angled baffles to a horizontal ballistic steel plate ceiling), the acoustical treatment had to be renewed throughout, projectile damage had to be repaired, and both the lighting and target systems had to be replaced — all within an active CBP installation and to the exacting standard set by the adjacent operational Range No. 3.

---

## Scope of Work

### Architectural
The architectural scope governed the core renovation: deconstruction of the angled ceiling baffle system and its support structure, followed by installation of a horizontal ballistic steel plate ceiling suspended from the existing roof structure, new Tectum acoustical ceiling panels on furring, and replacement of all wall Tectum acoustical panels. The scope also included repair of projectile-related damage to the floor slab surface — concentrated at the front of the bullet trap — and to the concrete wall panels at the ends of the shooting lanes adjacent to side walls. All exposed surfaces were to be repainted upon completion.

### Structural
The structural scope provided the engineered underpinning for the new ceiling system. Steel angles were designed for attachment to the bottom chords of the existing steel joists to support the new cable hangers that carry the horizontal ballistic steel plate ceiling. Additional steel angles were specified to form the sloped baffle panels. Where the attachment locations fell more than 6 inches from joist bottom panel points, the existing joists required reinforcement. The structural design was developed in compliance with DoD UFC 1-200-01, IBC 2018, IEBC 2018, and ASCE 7-16.

### Electrical
The electrical scope replaced the existing lighting fixtures with new fixtures matching Range No. 3: KIM AFL13 Medium Halogen and Metal Halide Floodlights and Galco 371DST Heavy-Duty Strobe Warning Lights in both red and blue, utilizing existing switch configurations where possible. The scope also addressed the full replacement of the existing target system with a new Action Target system, including location and rough-in for control panels, cabinets, and server boxes. Electrical design was governed by ANSI C2, IEEE 142, NFPA 70, UFC 3-500-10N, and UFC 3-520-01. EMT conduit with compression fittings was specified for interior non-hazardous locations; all conductors were copper THHN/THWN rated at 600 volts, with a separate green-insulated equipment-grounding conductor on all feeder and branch circuits.

### Mechanical
Mechanical (HVAC) design for the range ventilation system was coordinated with Range Ventilation Design, Inc. (Mike Bott), a specialty subconsultant. The electrical design was required to incorporate mechanical scope prior to construction documents, ensuring integrated coordination between the ventilation and electrical systems.

## Role and Delivery

BFA served as prime consultant, responsible for the coordinated multi-discipline engineering design package (structural, architectural, and electrical) and for the subconsultant coordination interface with the specialty range ventilation designer. The project was procured under a federal contract vehicle and the design was in prefinal Basis of Design stage as of January 2023.
