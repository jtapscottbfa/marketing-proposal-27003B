# Structural Engineering — ATC Firing Range Renovation
## Advanced Training Center, Harpers Ferry, WV | CBP | BFA Project No. 22-018

BFA's structural engineering scope on the CBP ATC Indoor Firing Range #2 renovation centered on designing the engineered steel support system for a new horizontal ballistic steel plate ceiling — a technically demanding task within an existing structure not originally configured for this ceiling geometry.

The existing roof system used steel joists, and the new ceiling required steel angles bolted to the bottom chords of those joists to carry cable hangers supporting the horizontal ballistic plate ceiling assembly. Additional steel angles were designed to form the sloped baffle panels that are a functional requirement of an indoor firing range ballistic environment. A key engineering challenge was attachment point location: where bolts fell more than 6 inches from joist bottom panel points, the existing joists required reinforcement to carry the added loads without overstressing the chord or panel point connections.

Structural design criteria followed the DoD unified facilities criteria framework — UFC 1-200-01 (DoD Building Code) — along with IBC 2018, IEBC 2018, and ASCE 7-16 for minimum design loads. The IEBC governed the approach to modifications of the existing structure, ensuring that reinforcement and new attachments were evaluated against both new construction standards and existing-building provisions.

This scope reflects BFA's experience with the intersection of structural renovation work and specialized ballistic/protective building systems in secure federal facilities, where structural decisions directly affect the ballistic integrity of the finished installation.
