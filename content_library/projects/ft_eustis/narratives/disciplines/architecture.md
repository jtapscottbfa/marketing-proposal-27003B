Architectural scope on this project centered on a comprehensive interior renovation of 23 restrooms within McDonald Army Health Center Building 576 — a 134,984 square foot, three-story military outpatient clinic constructed in 1962. The design team served as architect-engineer of record under a design-build task order, subconsulting to the construction prime and responsible for a complete Basis of Design and construction documents for all architectural elements.

Demolition of existing finishes to substrate formed the starting point for each restroom, with hazardous material abatement incorporated into the demolition scope to address materials potentially present in a building of this age. Replacement finishes encompassed ceramic tile flooring and wall tile, acoustical ceiling systems, and painted and wallcovered surfaces. Interior partitions and drywall or plaster repairs were included where required to prepare substrates for the new finish systems. Toilet partition systems were replaced in full, and interior doors, frames, and door hardware were replaced throughout the 23-restroom scope.

All architectural work was integrated with concurrent MEP system upgrades across the same spaces, requiring close coordination across disciplines to sequence work within the active facility. Design was developed under the active-facility constraint of a continuously operating outpatient clinic, requiring coordination with the Defense Health Agency and facility management to plan phased construction across the restroom suite while maintaining clinic operations throughout.
