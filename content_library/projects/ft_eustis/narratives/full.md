# Upgrade Restroom Finishes and Fixtures — McDonald Army Health Center, Building 576, Fort Eustis, VA

## Project Overview

McDonald Army Health Center (MAHC), Building 576, is a 134,984 square foot three-story outpatient clinic on Fort Eustis Army Base in Virginia. Originally constructed in 1962 with a single-story addition in 1973, the facility serves as the primary ambulatory care center on post for active-duty personnel and their families. The building carries an estimated plant replacement value of $137.34 million, reflecting the depth of building systems investment and the facility's criticality to the installation's healthcare mission.

By FY22, the restroom facilities throughout Building 576 required comprehensive capital renewal. Original finishes had exceeded their service life, and the aging fixture inventory — manual-operation faucets, flush valves, and conventional lighting — no longer met current standards for infection control, water efficiency, or energy performance in a healthcare setting. The project was funded and procured as CLIN 0003 of the FY22 Eastern Region Miscellaneous Projects solicitation (W912DY22R0073), an 8(a) sole-source set-aside task order issued by USACE Huntsville Engineering and Support Center (contract code W912DY).

## Scope

The renovation addressed 23 restrooms across Building 576 — twenty-two on the first floor and one on the second floor. The project was delivered under a design-build contract structure, with the AE team subconsulting to the construction prime and responsible for a complete multi-discipline Basis of Design and construction documents.

**Architectural scope** encompassed demolition of existing finishes to substrate, hazardous material abatement where required given the building's age, and full replacement of all interior finish elements: ceramic tile flooring and wall tile, acoustical ceiling systems, toilet partition systems, and interior doors with frames and hardware. Drywall/plaster repairs, painting, and wallcovering completed the finish package.

**Plumbing scope** replaced all fixtures with motion-activated, battery-operated models — motion-activated battery-operated faucets at lavatories and motion-activated battery-operated flush valves at toilets and urinals. Existing domestic water supply and sanitary drain piping were retained as serviceable; new service connections were provided only where fixture replacements required changes to rough-in dimensions.

**Electrical scope** installed LED lighting fixtures throughout the 23 restrooms — wall-mounted, recessed, and ceiling-mounted types — with occupancy-sensor controls (wall-switch and ceiling-mounted). Existing conduit and conductors were retained and reused where feasible. No new emergency power infrastructure was required.

**Mechanical scope** modified or replaced HVAC systems and controls as needed to serve the reconfigured restroom areas, including relocation of thermostats and temperature sensors per revised floor plans. Testing, adjusting, and balancing (TAB) was required upon completion, performed by an AABC-, NEBB-, or TABB-certified firm.

**Fire protection scope** addressed fire alarm horn/strobe requirements in the affected restrooms per applicable code, with fire suppression addressed per UFC 3-600-01 (DoD fire protection design standard).

## Role on Project

The design team served as architect-engineer of record on a subconsultant basis to the design-build prime contractor. Deliverables included a complete multi-discipline Basis of Design and construction documents covering architecture, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and fire protection for the 23-restroom renovation scope. The AE team also coordinated with the Defense Health Agency and facility management throughout the design process to address utility disruption planning and construction sequencing within the active facility.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Active healthcare facility operations.** Building 576 remained in continuous operation as a military outpatient clinic throughout design and construction. The contractual requirement to coordinate with DHA and facility management to "minimize and coordinate required utility disruptions" shaped the design approach at every discipline. Phased renovation sequencing across the 23 restrooms was the primary strategy for preserving continuous clinic access during construction.

**Aging building stock and substrate unknowns.** A facility built in 1962 and carrying a $137.34M plant replacement value presents inherent substrate uncertainty. Deteriorated finish conditions, potential hazardous materials from original construction, and service infrastructure that may not match existing drawings are common realities in buildings of this vintage. The scope included hazardous material abatement within the demolition package as a proactive measure, and the design-build structure allowed for responsive adjustment between design development and field conditions.

**Balancing system replacement against reuse.** The project's design approach explicitly prioritized serviceable-reuse over wholesale replacement where appropriate: existing electrical conduit and conductors were retained and reused, and existing water supply and drain piping remained in place with new connections only where required. This approach reduced material cost, minimized construction disturbance, and shortened the duration of utility outages in an occupied healthcare building — while still achieving the water-efficiency and energy-efficiency performance targets central to the project's justification.

## Outcomes

The project delivered upgraded restroom finishes and fixtures across all 23 restrooms of McDonald Army Health Center Building 576, modernizing a critical military medical facility to current standards for healthcare finishes durability, infection control, water efficiency, and energy performance. Motion-activated plumbing fixtures and LED lighting with occupancy-sensor controls replaced aging manual systems throughout the renovation scope. The design-build delivery structure, with the AE team integrated as subconsultant to the construction prime, enabled coordinated design-construction sequencing within the constraints of a continuously operating outpatient clinic. The project was completed under FY22 appropriations as part of the USACE Huntsville Eastern Region Miscellaneous Projects task order.
