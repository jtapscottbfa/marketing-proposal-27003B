Mechanical engineering services provided HVAC system design for the SCIF renovation of Building 2611, addressing both the diverse thermal zone requirements of the facility and the stringent TEMPEST electromagnetic security constraints imposed by ICD 705.

The design addresses seven distinct thermal zones within the approximately 3,900 square-foot facility: two 7.5-ton rooftop heat pump units with electric backup heat serving the mission control rooms (MCR-1 and MCR-2); a 4-ton rooftop heat pump with electric backup heat serving reception and restroom areas; a 4-ton rooftop heat pump with electric backup heat serving the server room workstation area; a 10-ton ceiling-mounted computer room air conditioning (CRAC) unit serving the high-density server rack room; a 1.5-ton wall-mounted mini-split system serving the processing room; and a 3.5 MBh unit heater with wall exhaust fan serving the electrical room. Testing, adjusting, and balancing (TAB) was included in the mechanical scope.

The defining technical constraint for the mechanical design was compliance with ICD 705 TEMPEST requirements at all HVAC penetrations of the SCIF perimeter. All ductwork crossing the secure space boundary incorporates man-bars (physical barriers within duct runs), waveguides, and dielectric breaks to prevent electromagnetic signal leakage or intrusion through mechanical pathways. This requirement governed duct routing and penetration detailing across the facility and required close coordination with architecture, electrical, and fire protection disciplines to resolve perimeter crossing locations.
