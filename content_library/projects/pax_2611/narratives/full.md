# Renovation and Security Upgrades to Building 2611, NAS Patuxent River

## Project Overview

Building 2611 at Naval Air Station Patuxent River is a single-story, approximately 3,900 square-foot concrete masonry/brick veneer structure originally constructed in 2000. Before this project, the building served as a radar operations and range tracking facility. The project renovated the building to function as a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) — also referred to as a Special Access Program Facility (SAPF) — compliant with ICD/ICS 705v1.5.1 and TEMPEST electromagnetic security requirements, in support of Naval Air Warfare Center Aircraft Division (NAWCAD) mission operations.

The project was issued as a design-build construction contract through an 8(a) sole-source solicitation (N0042125R0027) by NAWCAD at NAS Patuxent River, with a government-stated construction magnitude in the $6 million to $6.5 million range. The 212-day construction phase ran from May 2025 through March 2026.

## Scope of Work

The renovation scope encompassed comprehensive interior demolition and full reconstruction to SCIF/SAPF standards within the existing building envelope. The exterior shell — CMU/brick veneer walls, steel joist roof framing, and modified bitumen roofing — was retained as the structural and envelope substrate for the conversion.

**Architecture.** Existing interior partitions, finishes, and systems were fully demolished. New SCIF-compliant wall construction was installed throughout, with new interior spaces including two mission control rooms (MCR-1 and MCR-2), server rack and workstation areas, a processing room, a reception area, and support spaces. The existing co-ed restroom was reconfigured into separate ADA-compliant men's and women's facilities. New interior finishes and a suspended acoustic tile ceiling system were installed throughout. Door frames, thresholds, and openings were detailed to meet ADA and SCIF physical security requirements. A new detached CMU/stucco electrical room addition was constructed to house emergency power equipment.

**Mechanical (HVAC).** The HVAC design provides zoned conditioning for the distinct thermal environments within the facility: two 7.5-ton rooftop heat pump units with electric backup heat serving MCR-1 and MCR-2; a 4-ton rooftop heat pump with electric backup heat serving reception and restroom areas; a 4-ton rooftop heat pump with electric backup heat serving the server room workstation area; a 10-ton ceiling-mounted computer room air conditioning (CRAC) unit serving the server rack room; a 1.5-ton wall-mounted mini-split system serving the processing room; and a 3.5 MBh unit heater with wall exhaust fan serving the electrical room. Testing, adjusting, and balancing (TAB) was included.

**Electrical.** A new 360 kW, 120/208V, 3-phase 4-wire diesel generator on an elevated reinforced concrete slab, a 1200A 208V 4-pole automatic transfer switch, and a 20-minute online double-conversion uninterruptible power supply (UPS) at 208V input/output provide emergency and power-conditioned supply to the facility. Interior lighting was designed to a 40 foot-candle target throughout with LED recessed fixtures, occupancy sensors, and dimmers on all circuits. LED exit and emergency lighting were provided. Red rotary and flashing beacon lights were specified to warn uncleared personnel. A Class II lightning protection system with four down conductors and three ground rods at each building corner was designed and installed, along with a surge protection device on the main service and a grounding system designed to 5 ohms or less.

**Plumbing.** The existing co-ed restroom was replaced with separate ADA-compliant men's and women's facilities. New fixtures include three water closets with automatic flush valves, one urinal with automatic flush valve, two wall-hung lavatories with automatic faucets, one kitchen sink, one mop sink, and one ADA-compliant drinking fountain with bottle filler. A 6,000-watt electric water heater was provided with a thermostatic mixing valve and expansion tank. New domestic water and sanitary drainage rough-in required concrete slab cutting for new underground piping, while the existing water service, meter, and backflow preventer were retained.

**Fire Protection.** The existing fire alarm system was demolished and replaced with a new addressable fire alarm and mass notification system. The new system includes one addressable fire alarm control panel (FACP) with voice evacuation and battery backup, a retained DMP digital communicator panel, and a Federal Signal UVRI-B radio transmitter with antenna. Mass notification components include three programmable text message signs. Detection and notification devices include four manual pull stations, twelve smoke detectors, six duct detectors, five self-amplifying speakers for secure spaces, seven alert strobes, and three weatherproof exterior speakers. The existing building falls below the threshold requiring wet-pipe fire suppression under UFC 3-600-01; no sprinkler system was installed.

## Role on Project

The design team served as the architect-engineer subconsultant to the design-build contractor, which held the prime construction contract with the government. The design team prepared the Basis of Design and design documentation across all disciplines — architecture, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and fire protection — in support of the contractor's design-build delivery. The contractor was responsible for producing construction drawings (IFC documents) and delivering the completed facility. The final Basis of Design submittal was completed in October 2025.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**TEMPEST and SCIF electromagnetic security compliance across all disciplines.** The dominant technical challenge was designing every MEP system to satisfy ICD 705 TEMPEST requirements simultaneously. SCIF electromagnetic security imposes constraints that cross discipline boundaries: HVAC ductwork at secure perimeter penetrations required man-bars, waveguides, and dielectric breaks to prevent signal leakage or intrusion through mechanical pathways; electrical conduits at the secure boundary required dielectric breaks; fire alarm circuits crossing the SCIF perimeter required dielectric breaks; and all conduits and electrical boxes within the secure area required RF treatment and surface mounting. Coordinating these requirements across architecture, mechanical, electrical, and fire protection disciplines during design was a central coordination effort.

**Emergency power for mission-critical classified operations.** The facility required uninterrupted power continuity to support classified operations. The solution — a large diesel generator with automatic transfer switching and an online double-conversion UPS — provides layered protection against grid outages. The electrical room addition was designed as a physically separate structure to house this equipment with appropriate separation from the classified interior.

**Adapting a 25-year-old building for SCIF standards.** The existing building (constructed in 2000, designed by The Benham Group as a radar operations facility) required full interior demolition to achieve SCIF compliance. The exterior CMU/brick veneer shell, steel joist framing, and modified bitumen roof were retained and evaluated as the structural and envelope substrate, while all interior construction was replaced to meet current standards.

## Outcomes

Building 2611 was converted from a legacy radar operations facility into a SCIF-compliant environment supporting NAWCAD mission operations at NAS Patuxent River. The project was delivered under design-build using an 8(a) sole-source procurement vehicle within the government-stated $6 million to $6.5 million construction magnitude range. The 212-day construction phase was completed in March 2026. Awarded contract value and post-occupancy performance data are not available in the project source record.
