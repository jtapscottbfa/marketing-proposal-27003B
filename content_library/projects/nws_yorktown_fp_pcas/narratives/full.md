## Project Overview

Building 476 at Naval Weapons Station Yorktown (WPNSTA Yorktown), Yorktown, Virginia, is a single-story, 157,260-sf wood-framed warehouse with Concrete Masonry Unit (CMU) siding and a metal roof. The building is divided into eight separate bays, with offices and restrooms provided for tenant use within portions of the building. Its occupancy classification is Moderate-Hazard Factory Industrial Occupancy, Group F-1 — a classification the project did not alter.

The firm served as prime consultant providing Post Construction Award Services (PCAS) for NAVFAC on project e-Projects number 1751889. PCAS is the standard NAVFAC construction-phase A-E services delivery mode: the firm provided construction-phase oversight — submittal review, RFI response, and field observation — while construction was performed by a separate general contractor. Delivery was design-bid-build.

## Scope

The construction scope addressed fire suppression and life safety deficiencies across Building 476 through three interconnected work elements.

**Fire Suppression — Bay 8.** The wet pipe fire sprinkler system in Bay 8 was replaced in full. New notification appliances and initiating devices were installed in Bay 8. Existing sprinkler systems in Bays 1–7 were not replaced under this project and are not connected to the new water supply.

**Fire Alarm and Mass Notification — Building-wide.** A new combined fire alarm and mass notification system was installed throughout all eight bays of Building 476. In Bays 1–7, new monitoring components were integrated into the existing fire alarm zone circuits. The building-wide scope of the fire alarm and mass notification work reflects life safety requirements applying to the entire 157,260-sf structure regardless of the bay-limited suppression scope.

**Fire Pump and Underground Distribution.** A new fire pump was added to serve all eight bays via new underground distribution piping. The new water supply infrastructure was sized to support the upgraded suppression system in Bay 8 and provides the distribution backbone for the building.

## Role

The firm acted as prime consultant under a PCAS contract with NAVFAC. The multi-discipline team encompassed fire protection engineering, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, plumbing engineering, and structural engineering. In the PCAS role, the team reviewed contractor submittals, responded to requests for information, and conducted field observations to verify conformance with the construction documents. Design of record was by others; the firm's engagement was construction-phase quality assurance and technical oversight.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Partial-scope fire suppression in an occupied multi-tenant industrial warehouse.** The project replaced the sprinkler system in Bay 8 only while seven bays retained their existing systems. Coordinating a partial fire suppression replacement in a 157,260-sf multi-tenant Group F-1 facility — where new fire pump and underground distribution infrastructure must serve the whole building while new and legacy suppression systems coexist — requires careful management of system interfaces, pressure coordination, and protection continuity during construction. The PCAS team's role included scrutiny of submittals and field conditions at the interface between new and existing systems, where design-document intent and contractor execution are most likely to diverge.

**Building-wide fire alarm integration with legacy zone circuits.** Installing a new combined fire alarm and mass notification system across all eight bays while integrating — not replacing — existing fire alarm zone circuits in Bays 1–7 introduced compatibility and coordination complexity. The existing zone circuit integration required the electrical and fire protection disciplines to verify through submittal review and RFI resolution that the new system correctly incorporated legacy detection infrastructure and that the mass notification system met NFPA and DoD installation requirements throughout.

## Outcomes

The project delivered upgraded fire suppression capability in Bay 8, a new fire pump with building-wide underground distribution infrastructure, and a modernized combined fire alarm and mass notification system across all eight bays of Building 476. The Group F-1 occupancy classification was maintained throughout construction. The firm completed its PCAS engagement as prime consultant to NAVFAC.
