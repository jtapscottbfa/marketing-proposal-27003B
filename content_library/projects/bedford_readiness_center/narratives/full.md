## Project Overview

The Bedford Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades (DMA Project VAFM PN 22-0013; BFA Project 22-051) provided complete HVAC system replacement for the Bedford Readiness Center, a Virginia National Guard facility in Bedford, Virginia. The project was initiated by the Virginia Department of Military Affairs (DMA) through a Request for Fee Proposal issued in August 2022, with a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,127,000.

The project replaced an aging HVAC system — including all ductwork and piping estimated at 50 or more years old — with modern equipment incorporating enhanced indoor air quality (IAQ) features. The design addressed both the facility's basic thermal conditioning needs and heightened infectious disease prevention requirements through the integration of bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV and visible lighting treatments, cold fusion technology, and other emergent IAQ technologies.

## Scope

The design scope encompassed complete HVAC system replacement, including:

- Removal and replacement of all HVAC units per CPSM life cycle cost analysis
- Removal and replacement of all 50+ year old ductwork and piping
- Integration of infectious disease prevention components: bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV lighting, visible lighting, cold fusion, and other emergent technologies
- Building Automation System (BAS) upgrade including humidity and temperature controls
- HVAC zoning to reduce potential migration of airborne particles
- Electrical support for new HVAC equipment, including new circuits and panel work as required
- Supporting architectural, structural, and electrical component replacement or repair as required

A/E services included comprehensive site investigation (floor plan dimensions, fire wall locations, available areas for wall penetrations, suitable unit locations), geotechnical investigation as required, detailed cost estimates at each submission milestone, DEB working drawing submittals for building permit, and full construction administration services. Design deliverables were produced at 35%, 65%, and 100% completion.

## Role on Project

The firm served as prime A/E consultant under a design-bid-build delivery structure. The contract was issued directly by the Virginia Department of Military Affairs and procured through CPSM qualifications-based A/E selection. The design team provided mechanical and electrical engineering services for the project. Scope included agency coordination with DEQ, DEB, SFMO, and other applicable state and local agencies, with the A/E responsible for all associated review fees except those incurred from DEB and SFMO.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Aging infrastructure replacement.** The facility's HVAC infrastructure was estimated at 50 or more years old. Rather than selective replacement, the project required complete system teardown — removing all existing units, ductwork, and piping. This created coordination challenges within an active military facility: the design addressed interim conditions, ceiling system removals and subsequent reinstallation, and construction sequencing required to support continued facility operations where possible.

**Infectious disease prevention integration.** Beyond standard HVAC replacement, DMA required integration of contemporary IAQ technologies — bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV and visible lighting treatments, cold fusion, and other emergent technologies — into a facility not originally designed for these systems. The design incorporated these technologies within the replacement system while satisfying CPSM compliance requirements and accommodating the facility's existing electrical and structural constraints.

**Electrical system complexity.** The existing electrical infrastructure serving the facility's HVAC equipment consisted of two separate service feeds: a 400A, 240/120V, 1-phase, 3-wire main distribution panel (MDP) in the boiler room, and a separate 240 Delta/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire service at a north-end power pole serving pad-mounted mechanical equipment and two rooftop units. A site electrical investigation prior to design found that panel schedules for multiple legacy panelboards distributed throughout the facility may not have been accurate, requiring field verification before finalizing electrical design. The electrical scope addressed new circuits for all replacement HVAC equipment, removal of existing wiring back to panel, and temporary support and reinstallation of existing lighting fixtures in all areas where ductwork removal required ceiling systems to be taken down.

**Multi-agency coordination.** As a state military facility, the project required review and approval coordination with DEQ, DEB, SFMO, and other applicable state and local agencies. The DEB review process required a building permit submittal at the working drawing phase, with the A/E bearing responsibility for required corrections across review cycles and for all applicable agency review fees other than those assessed by DEB and SFMO.

## Outcomes

The design reached 100% completion through a 100% Basis of Design (BOD) dated October 25, 2023, within a contractual design period of 210 days from NTP. The design was performed in accordance with CPSM requirements and addressed DMA's full program — complete HVAC replacement incorporating modern indoor air quality systems in an active National Guard readiness center. Construction was separately bid under DMA's CPSM procurement process within a 3–4 month window following final design approval, with a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,127,000.
