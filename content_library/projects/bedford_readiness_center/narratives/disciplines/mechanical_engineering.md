The mechanical engineering scope for the Bedford Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades encompassed complete HVAC system replacement for the Virginia National Guard facility in Bedford, Virginia. The work addressed an aging system — with all ductwork and piping estimated at 50 or more years old — requiring full removal and replacement rather than selective upgrades.

The mechanical design incorporated an enhanced indoor air quality (IAQ) approach consistent with the owner's infectious disease prevention requirements. New system components included bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV lighting, visible lighting, cold fusion technology, and other emergent IAQ technologies integrated into the replacement HVAC equipment. The design also included an upgraded Building Automation System (BAS) with humidity and temperature controls, and HVAC zoning configured to reduce potential migration of airborne particles between facility spaces.

The mechanical engineering effort was conducted within a design-bid-build delivery structure, with the firm serving as prime A/E consultant for the Virginia Department of Military Affairs. Design services included a comprehensive site investigation to determine existing conditions — floor plan dimensions, fire wall locations, available areas for wall penetrations, and suitable unit locations — prior to finalizing system layout. The project carried a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,127,000 and reached 100% design completion in October 2023.
