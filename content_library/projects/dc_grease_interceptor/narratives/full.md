# NSF Kitchen Grease Interceptor — Washington VA Medical Center

## Project Overview

The NSF Kitchen Grease Interceptor project (VA Project 688-22-003) involved the engineering design and construction administration for installation of grease interceptor infrastructure serving the Nutrition and Food Service (NSF) kitchen at the Washington VA Medical Center, 50 Irving Street NW, Washington, DC 20422. The kitchen is situated on the First Floor, B Wing of the medical center. The project was procured as a VOSB set-aside task order under solicitation 36C24522R022, with an estimated construction cost in the range of $100,000 to $250,000.

## Scope of Work

The technical scope covered two engineering disciplines: plumbing and electrical. Three 30-GPM grease interceptors were designed for installation in the NSF kitchen: one connected to the existing kitchen pulper and two connected to commercial sinks already equipped with garbage grinders. Type "L" copper piping was specified to connect each interceptor to its corresponding kitchen equipment and to route discharge to the existing floor sinks. The electrical scope provided three new 20A single-pole circuits originating at the existing Panel "K" in Room 1C-116, with 20A GFI receptacles in cast boxes installed at each grease interceptor location. Conduit was specified as EMT with copper conductors insulated to THHN/THWN standards.

Design criteria spanned federal and local standards: UFC, ICC, IPC, and ASPE publications; VA Plumbing Design Manual (May 2021); OSHA regulations; NFPA; District of Columbia Building Code; DC Water GI Regulation; ANSI C2 (National Electrical Safety Code); NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code); and the Department of Veterans Affairs Electrical Design Manual.

A/E services encompassed construction documents, construction administration, and post-construction services including record drawings. The contractual performance period was 30 calendar days from issuance of notice to proceed.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime A/E consultant under a federal qualifications-based selection. The contract vehicle was the VA's A/E IDIQ task order program. The project was a VOSB set-aside, requiring verification through the VA's Center for Verification and Evaluation (CVE) and listing on VetBiz. The team was responsible for the full engineering scope — no sub-consultants are identified in the available source documents.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Regulatory compliance in an active federal healthcare facility.** The work involved modifications to an operating kitchen in a functioning VA medical center. Design criteria required compliance with both federal VA standards (VA Plumbing Design Manual, VA Electrical Design Manual) and District of Columbia regulatory requirements (DC Water GI Regulation, DC Building Code) simultaneously. The Basis of Design addressed this layered compliance environment explicitly, citing each applicable standard and confirming the grease interceptor sizing (30 GPM per interceptor) against DC Water requirements for kitchen grease loading.

**Integration with existing electrical infrastructure.** The electrical design extended power from existing Panel "K" serving Room 1C-116, which carried both 480Y/277V and 208Y/120V three-phase services. The 20A/1P circuits and GFI receptacles required careful coordination with the existing panel configuration to confirm breaker slot availability and voltage alignment. The design specified the applicable existing service and branch circuit characteristics to allow the contractor to confirm field conditions during construction.

**Grease interceptor selection for mixed equipment types.** The NSF kitchen contained two distinct categories of grease-generating equipment — a pulper (food waste processor) and commercial sinks with garbage grinders — each with different flow and waste-load characteristics. The design addressed this by providing three discrete interceptors sized at 30 GPM each, rather than a single combined unit, to maintain independent maintenance and operational flexibility for the kitchen.

## Outcomes

The Basis of Design was completed and submitted as a final deliverable on October 13, 2022. The A/E scope was carried through construction documents, construction administration, and post-construction record drawings within the contracted 30-calendar-day performance period. The project was executed under a federal VOSB set-aside in compliance with VA acquisition regulations. Construction cost remained within the $100,000–$250,000 magnitude bracket established in the solicitation.
