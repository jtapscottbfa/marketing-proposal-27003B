# Building 10 – Room 9N104 – Cellular and Molecular Therapeutics Laboratory Renovation
## Long-Form Project Narrative

---

### Project Overview

Bowman, Foster & Associates (BFA) served as prime design-builder for the renovation of Room 9N104, a 211-square-foot single-module wet laboratory on the 9th floor of Building 10 — the National Institutes of Health's Mark O. Hatfield Clinical Research Center, part of the NIH Clinical Center Complex (CCC) in Bethesda, Maryland. The project was executed under Work Request C109043 for the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI), with oversight by NIH's Office of Research Facilities (ORF), Division of Design and Construction Management (DDCM).

The Cellular and Molecular Therapeutics Branch (CMTB), led by Dr. John F. Tisdale, performs advanced molecular biology research aimed at curing sickle cell disease through bone marrow repair and replacement strategies. The branch had been assigned Room 9N104 years earlier but lost the space to the NIH Office of the Director; abatement and partial demolition had been started and then halted. When the room was re-assigned to CMTB, they engaged ORF to execute a full design-build renovation restoring and upgrading the space to meet active laboratory standards for a staff scientist and two postdoctoral researchers.

---

### Scope of Work

**Architectural.** The renovation encompassed complete interior fit-out of the 211 SF room. BFA designed and delivered a new 1-hour fire-rated door with half-light panel (relocated to improve lab layout), a new aluminum-framed, thermally-broken, insulated-glazing window per NIH Building 10 standards (Kawneer basis-of-design, GSA Window Performance Condition 3B blast-resistance criteria), and window blinds. Flooring was fully replaced with vinyl composition tile (VCT). Wall finishes were prepared and painted. The ceiling remained open (exposed), consistent with the NHLBI program requirements. Fixed metal casework with 1"-thick black phenolic resin countertops and backsplashes was installed on three walls, including an under-counter flammable storage cabinet, adjustable-height bench areas, and wall-mounted black phenolic shelving (four rows, 12"–18" deep). Three adjustable-height workstations (29"–36" range) accommodated both seated and standing configurations. Government-furnished equipment (undercounter freezers, undercounter refrigerators, thermal cycler, reciprocating shaker, and micro centrifuges) was installed within the casework footprint.

**Mechanical / HVAC.** The existing induction unit and associated piping were demolished. A new 36 Series horizontal ceiling-mounted induction terminal unit was designed and installed, connected to the building's existing chilled water and hot water systems. The new unit was equipped with DDC controls integrated into the building BAS, meeting NIH DRM requirements. Laboratory compressed air and vacuum dispense turrets (two sets, bench-mounted) were installed to support the molecular biology workflows. Pre-construction TAB readings confirmed a blockage in the existing duct system that had been reducing supply airflow to approximately 35 CFM; the new system restored design airflow to 132–139 CFM. Mechanical calculations (TRACE 700) confirmed 0.5-ton cooling load and -4.7 MBh heating load for the 218 GSF conditioned space.

**Electrical.** Existing lights, receptacles, and data outlets were demolished. New work included six Lithonia FML4W 48" LED pendant-mounted wrap fixtures (4000K, 5000 lm each), nine Snite LED 18" under-shelf task lighting units, and 120V wire-mold raceways along lab benches with quad outlets in knee spaces and a dedicated outlet under the window sill countertop. Data/telecom conduit with outlets was provided at each of the three workstations plus under the window sill. Circuits were fed from existing panels 1264A36 and 1264A38, located in the 9th-floor corridor. Voltage drop calculations confirmed 0.64% and 0.15% for each feeder circuit — well within the 3%/5% design criteria. Lighting photometric calculations confirmed an average of 87.7 footcandles across the room, with task lighting providing up to 185 FC at the bench level.

**Plumbing.** Existing eyewash, domestic water, and gas piping were demolished back to mains. New work included an 18×15" epoxy resin sink, counter-mounted WaterSaver autoflow deck-mounted eyewash with GS-Plus spray heads and a WaterSaver FAP3600 thermostatic tempering valve (ASSE 1071 certified, preset 85°F), and Chicago Faucets gooseneck faucets with foot-pedal controls and ceramic quarter-turn cartridges. Compressed air and vacuum supply connections were provided via Chicago Faucets deck-mounted needle valve assemblies. Drainage was connected to the existing building sanitary system.

### Project Context and Challenges

Working within the Building 10 Clinical Center Complex imposed substantial procedural and logistical requirements beyond a typical lab renovation. BFA and its construction partner (Rima Construction LLC) coordinated compliance with the following NIH-specific requirements:

- **Construction Risk Assessment (CRA)** and Interim Life Safety Measures (ILSM) under NIH FDM Section 3-10, addressing infection control, air quality, noise, vibration, and emergency procedures in an active occupied hospital building.
- **The Joint Commission (TJC) Environment of Care** standards and annual training requirements for all contractor personnel.
- **NIH Division of the Fire Marshal (DFM)** as the Authority Having Jurisdiction — design approval, in-progress close-in inspections, and final certification prior to occupancy were all required. Fire Marshal approval was obtained (signature dated 4/19/21 on drawing G-001).
- **Building 10 Contractor Single Point of Entry Program (SPEP)** — all contractor personnel entered and signed in daily through Room B2-A17A at the Level B-2 loading dock.
- **No material staging space** — the room's position in the middle of an occupied 9th-floor corridor meant all materials had to be transported through active hospital spaces with no lay-down area.
- **Utility shutdowns** required advance coordination with ORF's Division of Facilities Operations and Maintenance (DFOM).
- **Davis-Bacon Act** prevailing wages applied throughout construction.

The design followed NIH's Design Requirements Manual (DRM, 2016 edition with revisions), Facility Development Manual (FDM), applicable IBC/IMC/IPC 2018 editions, NEC 2020, NFPA 1, 13, 45, 70, 72, 99, 101, and FGI Guidelines. Three formal design review submissions (65%, 95%, and Final) were completed, with the Final Submittal package dated April 7, 2021.

---

### Role on Project

BFA served as prime design-builder — holding the prime contract directly with NIH/ORF and providing both the architect-engineer design team and construction oversight through a joint design-build entity with Rima Construction. BFA prepared all design documents, basis-of-design documentation, BOD submissions at each milestone, specifications, and construction administration deliverables. The design team covered architecture, plumbing, mechanical, and electrical engineering across the full scope. The construction contractor was responsible for field execution, quality control, and all NIH safety program compliance.

---

### Outcomes

The project delivered a fully equipped, code-compliant wet laboratory for three CMTB research personnel, supporting molecular biology techniques including DNA/RNA work, plasmid construction, and PCR. The final design submittal was accepted April 7, 2021, with scheduled construction completion July 31, 2021. The project was completed within the 276-calendar-day estimated period of performance. No change orders are documented in the source record.
