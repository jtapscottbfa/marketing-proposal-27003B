# Mechanical Engineering — Building 10 Room 9N104 Renovation

BFA's mechanical engineering scope covered HVAC design and laboratory utility systems for the 211 SF Cellular and Molecular Therapeutics laboratory renovation at NIH Building 10.

The primary mechanical work involved replacement of a failed existing induction unit and associated controls. Pre-construction TAB readings by the contractor (Rima Construction) confirmed the existing supply duct was delivering only 35 CFM due to a blockage in the duct or unit — far below the design flow. BFA designed and specified a new Carrier 36 Series horizontal ceiling-mounted induction terminal unit (model 36SH30KR31, 40" length) connected to the building's existing chilled water (45°F EWT, 0.70 GPM) and hot water systems. Cooling capacity: 9,524 BTU/hr total; heating capacity: 7,052 BTU/hr. New DDC controls were integrated into the building BAS, replacing the disconnected pneumatic controls found on the existing unit. Design supply airflow of 132–139 CFM was restored.

BFA also designed the lab compressed air and vacuum dispense system: two bench-mounted turret sets (Chicago Faucets 980-WS909AGVCP needle valve assemblies) connected to the building's existing lab gas distribution systems.

Mechanical load calculations were performed using TRACE 700 (dataset dated 02/04/2021), confirming 0.5-ton peak cooling load and -4.7 MBh heating load for the 218 SF conditioned space based on Washington, D.C. climate data and NIH DRM room occupancy/equipment assumptions. Final TAB reporting was required prior to punch list and final acceptance.

All work was designed in compliance with the NIH Design Requirements Manual (DRM), NIH Facility Development Manual (FDM), IMC 2018, NFPA 45 (laboratory chemicals), and NFPA 99 (healthcare facilities).
