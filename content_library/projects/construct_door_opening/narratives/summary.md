The design team provided architecture and engineering services for an interior renovation of Building 9023 at Fort Gregg-Adams (formerly Fort Lee), Virginia, under task order PB00003-4J for the US Army Directorate of Public Works. The project centered on constructing a new door opening through an existing masonry wall, requiring structural assessment of the masonry system and a coordinated suite of interior improvements. Work encompassed selective demolition of existing partitions, flooring, ceiling systems, casework, and a modular cubicle system; construction of new metal stud partitions with gypsum board (approximately 40 LF); installation of three solid-core wood doors with metal frames; an aluminum transaction window; new carpet tile and VCT flooring; acoustical ceiling tile; and Level 4 interior painting. HVAC systems were modified and tested and balanced to serve the reconfigured layout, electrical outlets and lighting were repositioned, and two separate existing fire alarm and mass notification systems were consolidated into a single integrated system. A defining condition of the project was that soldiers continued training in Building 9023 throughout the construction period, requiring careful work sequencing and contractor access coordination to maintain an active military training environment. The project was delivered under a design-bid-build method, with 65%, 95%, and 100% IFC construction document submittals; record drawings confirming as-built conditions were issued in June 2025.
