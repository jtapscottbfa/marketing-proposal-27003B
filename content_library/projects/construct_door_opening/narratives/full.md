# Construct Door Opening — Building 9023, Fort Gregg-Adams

## Project Overview

The design team provided architecture and engineering services for the renovation of Building 9023 at Fort Gregg-Adams (formerly Fort Lee), Virginia, under task order PB00003-4J issued by the US Army Fort Gregg-Adams Directorate of Public Works (DPW). The project was executed under a design-bid-build delivery method; the design team produced construction documents at 65%, 95%, and 100% IFC milestones with final as-built record drawings completed in June 2025. Building 9023 is a single-story facility used for military training and administrative support functions. Bowman Foster & Associates Consulting Engineers served as the project design firm, with Pyramid Company LLC preparing the record drawings.

## Scope of Work

The project scope centered on constructing a new 3'-0" × 7'-0" door opening through an existing masonry wall within Building 9023. The work extended to a broader interior renovation encompassing: selective demolition of existing partitions, flooring, ceiling grid and tile, casework, and a modular cubicle system; construction of new metal stud partitions with 5/8" gypsum board and sound batt insulation (approximately 40 LF); installation of three solid-core wood doors with metal frames; installation of an aluminum transaction window; new carpet tile and VCT flooring; acoustical ceiling tile replacement; and Level 4 interior painting in Sherwin-Williams SW6119 Antique White. The reconfigured floor plan created a new Corridor 114 and enlarged Multi-Purpose Room 110. Interior finishes included Roppe 700 series wall base and new 24 oz 2'×2' carpet tiles to match existing.

Mechanical scope included modification of the existing HVAC system to serve the reconfigured interior, with test and balance required upon completion (SOW §23 05 00). Electrical scope included reworking power outlets at demolished and new wall locations and repositioning existing lighting fixtures to align with the new layout (SOW §26 01 00). Fire protection scope involved consolidating two separate existing fire alarm and mass notification systems into a single integrated system, with system design drawings provided across sheets F-101 through F-104 (SOW §28 46 11).

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant, providing architecture, structural, mechanical, electrical, and fire protection engineering services. Services included full construction document preparation through 100% IFC, design review coordination, and documentation of as-built conditions. The Davis-Bacon wage determination applicable to the project was VA20230170 (01/13/2023, VA170 Building).

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Occupied facility constraint.** The SOW explicitly required that "Soldiers will continue training in the building during construction." This constraint shaped the design documentation and project specifications to enable phased or sequenced construction activities, with contractor access restricted to accommodate an active military training environment throughout the work period. Work sequencing, dust containment, and phasing requirements were addressed in the construction documents.

**Masonry wall opening.** The primary task — constructing an opening through an existing masonry wall — required structural assessment of the existing wall system to evaluate load paths above the proposed 3'-0" × 7'-0" opening and to design appropriate support conditions to maintain structural integrity of the wall above the new door. The demolition plan documents the masonry removal scope and preparation requirements for the new opening.

**Dual fire alarm and mass notification system consolidation.** Building 9023 contained two separate fire alarm and mass notification systems. The project design consolidated these into a single integrated system, involving fire alarm control panel coordination, device layout, and compliance with NFPA standards and applicable Army fire protection requirements. The fire alarm and MNS design is documented across four drawing sheets (F-101 through F-104).

**Installation naming transition.** Source documents reflected both "Fort Lee" (the pre-2023 installation name, retained in older drawing title block standards) and "Fort Gregg-Adams" (the current official installation name). Design coordination accounted for this naming transition throughout the project record.

## Outcomes

Construction was completed and record drawings were issued in June 2025, with the title sheet dated 1 June 2025 and as-built conditions confirmed on the demolition plan sheet as of 11 June 2025. The completed renovation delivered a reconfigured single-story interior with new circulation paths, updated finishes throughout, a consolidated and code-compliant life safety system, and a new masonry wall opening — all executed within an active military training facility without interruption to installation operations.
