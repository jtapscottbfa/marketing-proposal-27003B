## Project Overview

The HRSD Boat Harbor Treatment Plant project delivered electrical engineering design services for a wastewater treatment facility operated by Hampton Roads Sanitation District (HRSD) in Norfolk, Virginia. HRSD is a regional public authority responsible for wastewater collection and treatment across the Hampton Roads metropolitan area. The engagement — project 23-011A — was a single-discipline electrical scope, with the design firm acting as prime consultant in direct contract with the owner. The engineer of record's professional seal appears on the issued drawing, dated September 7, 2023.

## Scope

The electrical design scope encompassed the facility's complete medium-distribution and low-voltage power distribution system, documented on a single issued electrical one-line diagram (Sheet E-601, "Electrical One-Line Diagram"). Key elements of the design include:

- **Main service:** A 1,250-ampere, 480-volt, three-phase main service entrance sourced from Dominion Energy, designed at 65 kAIC interrupting capacity, with a current transformer (CT) cabinet and main enclosed circuit breaker at the service point.
- **Pump distribution:** Six dedicated pump feeders — Pumps 1 through 6 — each rated at 125A/480V/3-phase with individual enclosed circuit breakers, serving the treatment plant's primary wastewater pumping equipment.
- **Dewatering:** A 400A/480V/3-phase feeder to the facility's dewatering system.
- **Crane circuits:** Two 300A/480V/3-phase circuits serving crane hoists at the facility (likely north and south or primary and secondary crane positions).
- **Facility distribution feeders:** A 125A/480V North Signal Distribution Load (NSDL) feeder and a 300A/480V North WLSI feeder address additional facility process loads.
- **Step-down transformer and panel distribution:** Two 225 kVA transformers step 480V down to 208/120-volt service. Downstream, a 900-ampere, 208/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire inside load center and a 600-ampere, 208/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire north panel serve building-level lighting and convenience loads.
- **Wire trough distribution:** A wire trough at the head of the main bus provides organized conductor routing from the service entrance.
- **Conductor sizing:** Main service conductors specified as three sets of 3-600 KCMIL with one #1/0 AWG ground in a four-inch conduit per Construction Note F; branch circuit conductors range from 350 KCMIL down to #1/0 AWG depending on load.

No architectural, mechanical, civil, or structural disciplines are represented in the project record. This was a single-discipline electrical engagement.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Multi-branch, mixed-voltage distribution coordination:** The one-line diagram reflects a complex, multi-branch power distribution system serving both 480-volt process loads (six pumps, dewatering, cranes) and 208/120-volt facility loads through two step-down transformer pairs. Designing selective coordination across this distribution tree — with a 65 kAIC main and enclosed branch breakers — required careful fault-current analysis to maintain treatment plant operational continuity in the event of a downstream fault.

**Active coordination at drawing issue:** The drawing contains a "WIRE HOLD" annotation on the main bus, indicating a pending coordination item at the time of drawing issue, and a partial panel designation ("NORTH H WLS?") that reflects an in-progress label resolution — likely an abbreviation for a North Headworks Wet Level Station or similar treatment process designation. These notations are characteristic of design-phase coordination with a complex treatment plant facility where process naming conventions evolve through the design.
