Mechanical engineering formed the core discipline for this project. The scope encompassed the complete removal of existing ceiling-mounted air handling units and ductless split systems serving the 2,734 SF Cardiac Catheterization Lab in Building 500, Hunter Holmes McGuire VA Medical Center, and their replacement with a system configuration appropriate for OR-class catheterization suite environments.

The base bid design specified a Daikin variable refrigerant flow/variable refrigerant volume (VRF/VRV) system for the administrative, storage, and control room areas of the suite. The catheterization procedure suites — which require HVAC performance equivalent to surgical operating rooms — were served by two new roof-mounted air handling units at N+1 redundancy, configured for 100% outside air supply with a dedicated exhaust fan system. The N+1 arrangement ensures that no single equipment failure interrupts procedure capability. A bid deduct alternate was also designed, substituting three roof-mounted AHUs for the OR suites and retaining an existing ceiling-mounted unit for the lower-acuity office and control room spaces, providing procurement flexibility for the VA.

Controls for the replacement systems, including the Daikin I-Touch pad for the VRF/VRV platform, were integrated with the facility's existing Johnson Controls Metasys building automation system (DDC). This preserved centralized monitoring and scheduling capability without requiring replacement of the existing BAS infrastructure.
