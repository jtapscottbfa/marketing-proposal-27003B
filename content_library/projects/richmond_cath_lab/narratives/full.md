# AE Replace Cath Lab HVAC — Hunter Holmes McGuire VA Medical Center

**VA Project No. 652-22-123 | Richmond, Virginia | ECC $750,000**

---

## Project Overview

Hunter Holmes McGuire VA Medical Center in Richmond, Virginia operates two cardiac catheterization lab suites in single-story, steel-framed building additions on the north side of the main hospital complex, Building 500. The cath labs together encompass 2,734 SF and house specialized interventional procedure environments with HVAC requirements equivalent to operating suites — stringent pressure relationships, air change rates, filtration, and humidity control are integral to infection prevention and equipment operation in these spaces.

The existing HVAC infrastructure — ceiling-mounted air handling units and ductless split systems installed above the ceiling plane and on the roof — had reached the end of its service life and no longer reliably met the environmental standards required for active cardiac catheterization procedures. The Department of Veterans Affairs, through its Network Contracting Office 6, issued a qualifications-based solicitation (Contract No. 36C24622R0033) for architect-engineer services to design a full HVAC replacement for both the east and west cath lab additions. The contract was set aside 100% for Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB) firms under the VA's Veterans First Contracting Program (VAAR 852.219-10).

---

## Scope

The AE scope covered schematic through construction document design for the complete HVAC replacement, plus construction period services and HVAC commissioning. The design program addressed both a base bid configuration and a bid deduct alternate, giving the VA flexibility in procurement based on appropriated funds.

**Base bid:** Full removal of all existing HVAC equipment. Replacement systems included:
- A Daikin VRF/VRV system serving the administrative, storage, and control room areas of the cath lab suite
- Two new roof-mounted air handling units at N+1 redundancy serving the OR-class catheterization suite environments, configured for 100% outside air supply with a dedicated exhaust fan system
- Integration of VRF/VRV controls via the Daikin I-Touch interface, tied to the existing Johnson Controls Metasys building automation system (DDC)

**Bid deduct alternate:** Retain the existing ceiling-mounted unit for offices and control room; substitute three roof-mounted AHUs for the OR suites in lieu of the N+1 two-AHU configuration.

Electrical scope covered new disconnects, fuses, and circuit breakers for all replacement HVAC equipment, with new copper THHN/THWN conductors in EMT conduit. To minimize disruption to an active hospital facility, new HVAC equipment was fed from nearby panelboards designed to allow cath lab power outages independently from the rest of the building.

Fire protection and life safety scope included coordination for the fully sprinklered systems to remain functional or be integrated through construction, with infection control fire barrier provisions during phasing. Heat detection was planned for tie-in to the building fire alarm during any period when sprinklers might be temporarily compromised. A registered fire protection engineer was specified to provide design and code compliance review.

Structural scope addressed any penetrations, equipment pads, or roof-framing modifications required to support the new roof-mounted AHU equipment on the existing single-story steel-framed structures.

The design team also served as the HVAC Commissioning Authority per SOW §6.0, providing commissioning services for all new HVAC systems in lieu of a VA-hired third-party commissioning agent.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Occupied healthcare facility and system continuity.** The cath labs were understood to be active procedure spaces, making HVAC outage management a critical design consideration. The electrical design specifically routed new HVAC equipment connections to nearby panelboards to permit controlled outages limited to the cath lab suites, limiting disruption to the broader hospital. Infection control fire barrier provisions were incorporated into the construction sequencing to maintain life safety compliance through phasing.

**OR-class environmental requirements with aging infrastructure.** Cardiac catheterization suites require HVAC performance comparable to surgical operating suites. The existing ceiling-mounted units and split systems could not be cost-effectively rehabilitated to meet these requirements. The solution — two roof-mounted AHUs at N+1 redundancy with 100% outside air supply — provides the reliability, filtration, and air-change capability the space program demands, while the N+1 configuration ensures no single equipment failure disrupts procedure capability.

**Procurement flexibility via bid alternate structure.** The SOW and design both accommodate a base bid and a bid deduct alternate, reflecting the VA's need to match scope to available appropriations. The alternate retains the existing ceiling-mounted unit for lower-acuity spaces and adds a third roof-mounted AHU for OR suite coverage. Providing both options in a single design package allows the VA to award within budget without requiring a redesign.

**Controls integration with existing BAS.** The replacement systems — including the Daikin VRF/VRV platform — required integration with the existing Johnson Controls Metasys building automation system via the Daikin I-Touch interface. This preserved the facility's centralized DDC monitoring capability without requiring a wholesale Metasys replacement.

**Commissioning as AE responsibility.** Rather than relying on a separate VA-engaged commissioning agent, the AE team accepted the HVAC commissioning authority role. This consolidated accountability for system performance verification with the design team that specified the equipment, improving coordination during functional performance testing.

## Outcomes

The design team delivered a schematic design narrative in August 2022 (VA Project No. 652-22-123). The project record reflects design completion status. The design established a full technical basis for the cath lab HVAC replacement, including both the base bid and bid deduct alternate scopes, at an estimated construction cost of $750,000.

Construction award date and construction completion are not documented in the available source record. The AE design period was structured for 212 calendar days from Notice to Proceed, with construction period services contracted for a further 485 calendar days following construction award.
