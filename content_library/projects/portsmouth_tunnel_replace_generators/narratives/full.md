## Project Overview

The ERC DTT Portsmouth West Vent Building Emergency Generator Replacement was a multi-discipline infrastructure project serving Elizabeth River Crossings (ERC) — the public-private partnership entity operating the Elizabeth River Downtown Tunnel toll road in Hampton Roads, Virginia. The project replaced the existing standby emergency diesel generator at ERC's Portsmouth West Vent Building, a four-level, 44,983-SF tunnel ventilation support facility at the west portal of the Downtown Tunnel. The West Vent Building provides critical fan power, life safety, and tunnel systems support; continuous operation of the facility throughout construction was a hard constraint on the design.

The project was administered under Virginia Bureau of Capital Outlay Management (BCOM) project code 501-B0501-015, using Virginia DGS CO-7 General Conditions for design-bid-build delivery. The 100% final design package — specifications, drawings, cost estimate, and equipment submittals — was issued November 5, 2020.

## Scope

The primary design scope was a complete emergency power system replacement encompassing the following elements:

- New Caterpillar 3512C standby diesel generator, 1,500 kW / 1,875 kVA, 480V three-phase four-wire wye, 60 Hz, 1,800 rpm, 0.8 power factor, with a 48-hour subbase fuel tank rated for ASTM D975 Grade 2-D S15 diesel
- New 4,000A main switchgear lineup (MDPE) at 480Y/277V with two 800A circuit breakers, one 400A circuit breaker, and one integrated 600A automatic transfer switch
- Two ASCO Series 300 automatic transfer switches: one 800A/4-pole open-transition unit (catalog H03ATSB30800NGXL) and one 400A/4-pole open-transition unit (catalog J03ATSB30400NGXL), rated 480V/60Hz
- New underground conduit ductbank from the exterior generator pad to the building's power distribution system
- Reinforced concrete mat foundation and elevated slab platform for the new generator, approximately 32 feet by 12.5 feet, designed to support 115,000 lbs of generator equipment
- Abandonment of the existing emergency generator, underground fuel storage tank, day tank, exhaust ducts, supply/exhaust ducts, and associated fuel piping

Approximately 8,418 SF of building area was affected by the project scope across the first and second floors of the existing four-level facility.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Maintaining continuity of service in an active tunnel facility.** The Portsmouth West Vent Building supports the Downtown Tunnel's continuous operations, including tunnel ventilation fans, life safety systems, and critical electrical loads. An unplanned outage during construction would constitute an operational safety event. BFA developed a detailed 19-step construction phasing sequence — codified on drawing E-001 — that sequenced underground conduit installation, switchgear placement, ATS connection, generator commissioning, fuel transfer from the existing underground tank to the new subbase tank, and abandonment of the legacy equipment. The phasing was designed so that at no point in the construction sequence were active life safety loads left without a transfer path. Each step was sequenced to restore live power distribution before the next transition was initiated.

**Floodplain siting with a 115,000-lb generator.** The project site is within the Elizabeth River floodplain, with a base flood elevation of 8.00 feet. The new generator, weighing 115,000 lbs, required a substantial structural foundation capable of carrying that load while also meeting flood resilience requirements. The structural and civil design teams elevated the generator platform slab to +7.50 feet, placing the generator bottom at elevation 11.00 feet — 3 feet above the base flood elevation. The foundation was designed as a reinforced concrete mat slab, approximately 32 feet by 12.5 feet and 1 foot thick, supported on 8-inch concrete walls, with allowable soil bearing capacity of 1,000 psf per geotechnical investigation by GeoEnvironmental Resources, Inc. The foundation was engineered to Seismic Design Category D, Site Class D, and 125-mph (3-second gust) design wind per the 2015 Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

**Equipment interface and manufacturer coordination.** The Caterpillar 3512C required anchor bolt layout coordination with the generator support frame manufacturer (Chillicothe Metal Company, per structural notes) and generator control wiring for the autostart circuit. The fuel system design called for draining, polishing, and transferring diesel from the existing underground storage tank to the new generator's 48-hour subbase tank — sequenced within the phasing plan to ensure fuel availability was maintained during the generator commissioning step.

## Outcomes

The 100% final design submittal was issued November 5, 2020, delivering a complete, permit-ready construction document package. The package included full specifications (Division 1 through Division 26), the six-sheet drawing set, equipment submittals for the Caterpillar 3512C and ASCO Series 300 transfer switches, geotechnical report, and a construction cost estimate of $1,260,260.48. The design provided a code-compliant, flood-resilient standby power system capable of sustaining the Downtown Tunnel's emergency power loads for a minimum of 48 hours without fuel resupply — meeting ERC's operational continuity requirements for the active tunnel concession.
