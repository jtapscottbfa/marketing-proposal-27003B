# Coastal Precast HRBT Technopref — Electrical Systems Study

**Client:** Coastal Precast Systems  
**Location:** Cape Charles, Virginia  
**Project No.:** 21-005  
**Completion:** February 2021  
**Engagement Type:** Electrical Engineering Study (study-only; no design or construction phase)

---

## Project Overview

Coastal Precast Systems operates an industrial precast concrete manufacturing plant in Cape Charles, Virginia, on the Eastern Shore of the Chesapeake Bay. The facility serves as a fabrication site for precast structural elements supplied to the Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel (HRBT) expansion — a major regional transportation infrastructure program. The plant's electrical distribution system supports heavy industrial loads including large welding stations, production panel distribution, a quality-control circuit, and facility lighting.

In early 2021, an electrical systems study was commissioned to characterize the plant's power distribution system, evaluate existing protective device adequacy, and establish arc flash incident energy levels and PPE program requirements across the facility. The study was completed and issued as a Final Submittal on February 26, 2021.

---

## Scope

The electrical systems study comprised four integrated analytical tasks:

**Short-Circuit Analysis.** The fault current available at each node of the electrical distribution system was calculated. The system is served by a 1,500 kVA utility transformer. The main distribution panel (MDP) is rated 800A at 480V with a 22 kA interrupting rating (Square D). Distribution extends from the MDP to multiple downstream panels: a 200A WEST panel, a 400A N EAST panel, a 300A S EAST panel, and a 100A lighting panel — all at 480V. Two 250A branch circuit breakers serve dedicated welding loads (L WELD #1, L WELD #2) at 200A load draw each. A 15A energy and demand monitoring meter is also installed. Step-down transformers (a 75 kVA unit serving the quality-control panel at 240V, and three 25 kVA units — RP1, RP2, RP3 — serving 240V downstream panels fed from the N EAST, S EAST, and WEST panels respectively) extend the distribution system to 240V secondary circuits.

**Protective Device Coordination Study.** The time-current characteristics of each protective device in the distribution system were analyzed to confirm proper selective coordination — ensuring that a fault at any point in the system trips only the nearest upstream device without cascading to the main or upstream breakers unnecessarily.

**ANSI Equipment Evaluation.** Each protective device and panel in the distribution system was evaluated against ANSI ratings for the fault currents calculated in the short-circuit analysis. All equipment — Square D throughout — passed the ANSI equipment evaluation under the calculated fault levels.

**Arc Flash Evaluation (IEEE 1584-2018 / NFPA 70E / OSHA).** Incident energy levels were calculated at each switchgear and panel location in accordance with IEEE 1584-2018. Arc flash PPE category levels were assigned: Level 0 (no PPE required) at the 480V MDP, lighting panel, N EAST panel, and S EAST panel; Level 3 (arc flash suit required) at the downstream 240V panels and the 480V WEST panel. These results form the PPE program basis for personnel working on or near energized electrical equipment at the facility.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Heavy welding loads in an industrial distribution system.** The facility's electrical system carries two large welding loads (200A each) served by 250A breakers at 480V — loads that drive fault current and coordination demands distinct from a conventional commercial or institutional distribution system. The short-circuit and coordination analyses accounted explicitly for these loads and their protective devices.

**Mixed voltage levels and transformer cascade.** The distribution system spans two voltage levels (480V and 240V) through multiple step-down transformers of varying kVA ratings. Arc flash calculations required separate analysis at each voltage level and transformer secondary, as impedance through the transformers materially affects available fault current and incident energy at 240V nodes. The step-down transformer cascade was the primary driver of the Level 3 arc flash designations at the downstream 240V panels, where lower fault current extends clearing time and elevates incident energy.

No SOW, contract, or BOD document exists in the project record. All scope and system facts in this narrative are drawn directly from the study deliverables.

## Outcomes

- All protective devices in the Coastal Precast Systems HRBT plant distribution system passed the ANSI equipment evaluation under the calculated fault levels, confirming adequacy of the existing Square D switchgear for the plant's load profile.
- Arc flash incident energy levels were characterized at every switchgear location, providing the facility with a complete IEEE 1584-2018 compliant arc flash evaluation and the PPE program basis required under NFPA 70E and OSHA.
- A Final Submittal study report and analytical one-line diagram were issued February 26, 2021, providing Coastal Precast Systems with documentation suitable for maintenance program use and regulatory compliance.
