Electrical engineering services for this engagement consisted of a comprehensive electrical systems study for an industrial precast concrete manufacturing plant in Cape Charles, Virginia. The study — the sole contracted deliverable — addressed four analytical tasks: short-circuit analysis, protective device coordination, ANSI equipment evaluation, and arc flash evaluation.

The facility's electrical system is served by a 1,500 kVA utility transformer feeding an 800A, 480V main distribution panel (Square D, 22 kA interrupting rating). Distribution extends to multiple 480V production and lighting panels, two 200A welding loads, and a quality-control circuit. Step-down transformers (75 kVA and three 25 kVA units) serve 240V secondary panels. The short-circuit analysis calculated available fault current at each node; the coordination study confirmed that protective devices are selectively coordinated throughout the system. All Square D equipment passed the ANSI equipment evaluation under calculated fault levels.

The arc flash evaluation was performed in accordance with IEEE 1584-2018, NFPA 70E, and OSHA requirements. Incident energy levels were calculated at each switchgear location. Results ranged from Level 0 (no PPE required) at the primary 480V distribution panels to Level 3 (arc flash suit required) at the downstream 240V panels and the 480V WEST panel — the latter driven by the lower fault current and extended clearing times on the transformer secondary circuits. The study was issued as a Final Submittal in February 2021, providing the facility's PPE program basis and arc flash labeling documentation for ongoing maintenance operations.
