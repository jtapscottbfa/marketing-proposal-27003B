## Project Overview

Honest Pastures is a commercial food service facility located in Virginia Beach, Virginia. The project involved the renovation of the building's kitchen exhaust system under contract C106551; at least one amendment (Amendment 01) was issued during the engagement. Final mechanical drawings were issued July 22, 2021, and the project is considered complete based on the "FINAL.DWGS" designation of the drawing set.

The design deliverables consisted of two mechanical drawings: Sheet M-101 (Floor Plan — New Work) and Sheet M-102 (Roof Plan — New Work), both at 1/4" = 1'-0" scale. The design firm served as prime consultant, with design and project engineering performed by staff engineer RLW (drawn by and project engineer) and checked by RW.

## Scope

The project scope was a targeted mechanical renovation focused on kitchen exhaust and make-up air, with the following components:

**Type 1 Kitchen Exhaust Hood:** CaptiveAire Model 5/30, 10'-½" wide × 5'-0" deep × 29-½" tall, rated at 1,100 CFM, requiring 208V 3-phase electrical service. The hood was positioned above the new commercial cooking line on the ground floor.

**Rooftop Exhaust Fan:** Dayton Model 4YF16, approximately 1,800 CFM, replacing the existing rooftop fan. The project was scoped as an "Exhaust Fan Renovation" per the contract description on file.

**Make-Up Air Handling Unit:** CaptiveAire Model A1G10, 2,000 CFM variable speed, located on the roof and connected to the exhaust hood via two 10" × 22" square-to-round duct transitions. The intake was positioned 10'-0" from the exhaust fan discharge to prevent short-circuit recirculation.

**Gas Commercial Cooking Equipment:** Three pieces of new gas-fired equipment positioned under the exhaust hood:

- ATOSA USA countertop hotplate/stove, Model ATSP-2448-1 (48" W × 30" D × 10.5" H, 4 burners, 64,000 BTU total)
- ATOSA USA gas griddle, Model ACHP-2 (24" W × 28" D, 60,000 BTU total)
- Blodgett gas double-deck convection oven, Model DF-100 DBL (3-phase, 60 Hz, 6.0A)

The building is single-story; the roof structure is metal decking and metal joists. Gas rough-in scope was not explicitly addressed in the mechanical drawings, and no gas piping drawings were included in the available documentation. Electrical connection requirements for the exhaust hood and convection oven are noted in the construction notes; no electrical discipline drawings were produced within this drawing set.

## Role on Project

The design firm served as prime design consultant and produced the complete mechanical design package. No subconsultants are identified in the available project documents. The scope of services documented in the available record is design-phase production (construction drawings); construction administration services are not documented.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Exhaust and make-up air balance.** A Type 1 kitchen exhaust system requires careful balancing of exhaust and make-up air to maintain hood capture velocity and building pressurization. The design resolved this by pairing the approximately 1,800 CFM exhaust fan with a 2,000 CFM variable-speed make-up air unit, providing a slight positive-pressure buffer at the hood while allowing variable-speed adjustment to match operating conditions. The make-up air intake was located 10'-0" from the exhaust discharge per general notes on Sheet M-102 to prevent short-circuiting.

**Coordination of equipment electrical loads.** The exhaust hood requires 208V 3-phase power and the convection oven draws 3-phase 60 Hz 6.0A. These electrical connection requirements are noted in the mechanical construction notes on Sheet M-101. No electrical discipline drawings were part of the design package available in the project record; the electrical coordination would have been handled separately or by others.

**Compact two-sheet design package.** The complete scope was documented in two drawing sheets at medium complexity — a floor plan identifying four tagged new-work items and a roof plan showing two rooftop equipment items with connection detail. This compact documentation format is efficient for a small focused renovation but leaves no room for equipment schedules, specifications, or detailed notes beyond the construction note callouts.

## Outcomes

The design was completed and issued as a final drawing set on July 22, 2021. The project — kitchen exhaust fan renovation including a new Type 1 hood assembly, replacement rooftop exhaust fan, variable-speed make-up air unit, and new commercial gas cooking equipment — was fully documented in the two-sheet mechanical design package. The building's single-story metal joist structure was accommodated in the roof equipment placement and noted in the general notes. Contract financial data, construction schedule, and construction outcomes are not present in the available project record.
