# Warehouse Machine/Welding Shop Ventilation

## Project Overview

The Warehouse Machine/Welding Shop Ventilation project delivered complete design services for a targeted ventilation system upgrade at a US Customs and Border Protection facility in Harpers Ferry, West Virginia (BFA Project #24-059). The project addressed documented deficiencies in the air quality environment of an existing machine/welding room within the facility's warehouse building—a space used for active welding and machining operations that require controlled exhaust of metal fumes, combustion byproducts, and airborne particulates. The existing condition, an exterior wall-mounted exhaust fan, was inadequate for the room's ventilation demands, and the project replaced it with a purpose-designed dedicated mechanical ventilation system. The 100% design submittal was completed November 22, 2024, under a design-bid-build delivery method. The design team served as prime consultant for all design disciplines on the project.

## Scope of Work

The design scope encompassed mechanical and electrical engineering systems for the welding room ventilation upgrade within the existing warehouse building.

**Mechanical.** The mechanical design introduced a new inline exhaust fan rated at 1,500 CFM to actively remove welding fumes and airborne contaminants from the work area, discharging through a new gooseneck termination. A new dedicated outside air unit was designed to supply controlled makeup air, with the system configured to maintain negative pressure within the welding room relative to adjacent spaces—preventing contaminant migration and protecting occupants and equipment in the broader facility. New supply ductwork and diffusers completed the air distribution system within the room. The demolition scope included removal of the existing exterior wall-mounted exhaust fan, which was superseded by the new inline system.

**Electrical.** The electrical design provided power infrastructure for the replacement ventilation equipment. Demolition of the existing exterior wall-mounted exhaust fan and its associated wiring and conduit—traced back to the existing distribution panel—was included to eliminate legacy infrastructure conflicts. New power connections were designed for the replacement inline exhaust fan and the new outside air unit, specified with EMT conduit and compression fittings, copper conductors with THHN/THWN 600V insulation, and motor circuits for equipment rated at 1/2 HP and larger served from 208V or 480V three-phase, 60 Hz power.

The project was procured as a standalone federal contract under a design-bid-build delivery method.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant, providing all mechanical and electrical engineering services for the project from design through the 100% submittal milestone. No sub-consultants or other disciplines are identified in the available source documentation.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Ventilation system performance for welding operations.** The central technical challenge was designing a ventilation system adequate for the air quality demands of active welding and machining within an occupied federal facility. Welding operations produce metal fumes, gases, and particulates that require dedicated source exhaust and sufficient makeup air to sustain negative pressure and prevent contaminant migration. The design addressed this with a paired exhaust-and-makeup-air approach: a 1,500 CFM inline exhaust fan paired with a dedicated outside air unit, sized and configured to maintain negative pressure within the room relative to adjacent areas.

**Legacy system conflicts.** An existing exterior wall-mounted exhaust fan was present but inadequate for the room's ventilation demands. Rather than leaving the existing system as a partial complement to the new equipment, the design incorporated full demolition of the existing fan and its electrical circuits. This approach eliminated the risk of redundant or conflicting exhaust pathways and allowed the new ventilation system to be designed, balanced, and operated as a single coherent system.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a complete 100% design package for a purpose-designed ventilation system upgrade at a federal law enforcement support facility. The design replaced inadequate existing infrastructure with a dedicated system configured for the safety and air quality demands of machine and welding operations, incorporating negative-pressure control to protect adjacent occupied spaces. The 100% submittal was completed in November 2024.
