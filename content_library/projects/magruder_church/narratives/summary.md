Building 2707, the York River Presbyterian Church — locally known as Magruder Church — is a small historic chapel at Camp Peary (Armed Forces Experimental Training Activity/AFETA), Williamsburg, Virginia. Constructed in 1887 with a front belfry tower added in 1894, the wood-frame, single-story structure rests on a red brick pier foundation and is a contributing resource to the Magruder Village Historic District (DHR ID#099-5240). The project was issued under a Firm Fixed Price contract and tasked the design team with two distinct objectives: preserving the church from further decay (CLIN 001) and rehabilitating it to a functional state suitable for events and occasional religious use (CLIN 002). Design and construction document services were delivered across an estimated 190 calendar days from notice to proceed.

Rehabilitation scope encompassed MEP systems replacement throughout the building — replacement of ten electric baseboard heating units with programmable thermostat controls, a full electrical rewire including panelboard circuit extension for new parking lot lighting, and plumbing upgrades limited to a single cold-water restroom. Architecture led historic preservation coordination, ensuring new systems were introduced without compromising the building's 1887 fabric. Disciplines engaged included architecture (with a historic preservation emphasis), mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, and plumbing engineering. The CLIN 002 construction documents were issued for construction in October 2022, representing a complete MEP rehabilitation package for this modest but historically significant federal chapel facility.
