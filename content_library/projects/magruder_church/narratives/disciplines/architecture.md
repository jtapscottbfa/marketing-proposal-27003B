Architecture on the Magruder Church project centered on historic preservation and rehabilitation services for Building 2707 — a small, one-story, one-room chapel built in 1887 at Camp Peary (AFETA), Williamsburg, Virginia. The building is a contributing resource to the Magruder Village Historic District (DHR ID#099-5240) and was determined not eligible for the National Register of Historic Places, placing it under a preservation framework that governed all design decisions.

The architectural scope encompassed two contract line items under a Firm Fixed Price federal contract (RFP 2020-011): CLIN 001 (Preservation), covering base preservation design and construction documents at 30%, 100%, and IFC milestones; and CLIN 002 (Rehabilitation), covering restoration to a functional state for events and occasional religious use, at 30%, 60%, 100%, and IFC milestones. The dual intent — arresting further decay of the 1887 wood-frame structure while enabling modern habitability — required coordination across MEP disciplines to ensure new systems were introduced without compromising the building's historic fabric: drop-siding cladding, beaded corner boards, tin-shingle front-gabled roof, and red brick pier foundation with infill.

The architectural sub-discipline is classified as `architectural_historic`, reflecting the preservation emphasis of the engagement. The design team operated under historic preservation constraints appropriate to a contributing resource on an active Department of Defense installation, where access controls and federal procurement requirements added coordination complexity.
