Mechanical engineering on the Magruder Church CLIN 0002 Rehabilitation addressed the replacement of an aging electric baseboard heating system serving the historic chapel at Camp Peary (AFETA), Williamsburg, Virginia. The scope was explicitly bounded to heating only — no cooling system was introduced, consistent with the building's occasional-use occupancy as a chapel for events and religious services.

The rehabilitation replaced ten electric baseboard heaters (BB-1 through BB-10) throughout the main chapel volume and alcoves with new floor-mounted units, each sized to the zone it serves — capacities ranging from 396 to 2,900 BTUH, operating at 120V with wattages from 375W to 1,000W. A single fin tube heater supplemented the baseboard array. New 7-day programmable thermostats replaced the existing thermostat devices, providing occupied/unoccupied setback capability: an occupied heating setpoint of 70°F and an unoccupied setback of 65°F. Outdoor design conditions were established at 17°F winter dry-bulb and 91°F / 77°F MWB summer (summer conditions noted on schedule; no cooling system installed). Indoor winter design temperature: 65°F.

Controls documentation (sheet M-701) specified a controls points list: zone temperature monitoring (analog input, alarm, trend, graphic display), heating stage 1 output (binary output), heating setpoint tracking (alarm, trend, graphic display), and schedule via software loop. One-stage electric heating control, with the low-zone setpoint monitored as an alarm condition. The design represents a straightforward like-for-like replacement of electric resistance heating in a small historic building, with the addition of programmable setback capability to improve energy management for an intermittently occupied facility. Construction documents were issued at IFC level in October 2022.
