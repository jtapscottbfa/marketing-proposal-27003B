# Building 2707, York River Presbyterian Church (Magruder Church) — Preservation and Rehabilitation

## Project Overview

Building 2707, York River Presbyterian Church — familiarly known as Magruder Church — is a small one-story, one-room chapel located at Camp Peary, the Armed Forces Experimental Training Activity (AFETA) in Williamsburg, Virginia. Completed in 1887 and expanded with a front belfry tower in 1894, the structure features a wood frame structural system clad with drop siding and beaded corner boards, resting on a red brick pier foundation that has been infilled with matching brick. The chapel is three bays wide by three bays deep, with a narrow apse extension at the rear and a central belfry projecting from the front. A front-gabled roof covered with tin shingles completes the historic exterior profile.

The building is a contributing resource to the Magruder Village Historic District (DHR ID#099-5240), which has been determined not eligible for inclusion in the National Register of Historic Places. Its status as a contributing historic resource governed the design approach throughout: interventions were constrained to preserve the building's character while restoring it to functional habitability.

## Scope

The project was structured as a Firm Fixed Price A/E design contract under RFP 2020-011 (AFETA), organized into two Contract Line Items. CLIN 001 (Base Bid) covered Preservation Design & Construction Development Services, including 30%, 100%, and IFC document deliverables. CLIN 002 (Bid Option) covered Rehabilitation Design & Construction Development Services, including 30%, 60%, 100%, and IFC deliverables. The available project record documents the CLIN 002 Rehabilitation scope.

The project intent was explicitly dual: "(1) to preserve the church from further decay; (2) restore the Church to an acceptable level for events, functions and occasionally use as its original intended purpose (i.e. Church)." The performance period was estimated at 190 calendar days from notice to proceed, with NTP anticipated in May/June 2020 and overall design completion targeted for November/December 2020.

The full MEP rehabilitation scope encompassed: replacement of all electric baseboard heating units with new programmable-thermostat-controlled units; a full electrical rewire of the building with extension of the parking lot lighting circuit; and plumbing rehabilitation confined to a single cold-water restroom alcove. Architecture coordinated historic preservation constraints across all MEP disciplines, ensuring new system installations were compatible with the building's wood-frame fabric.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Historic structure constraints.** Working within an 1887 wood-frame chapel on a DoD installation introduced design constraints characteristic of sensitive historic rehabilitation work. The building's pier foundation with infill, load-bearing wood frame walls, and tin-shingle roof limited penetration locations and new system routing. The design scope was bounded accordingly: existing piping and electrical infrastructure were reused where feasible (existing cold-water piping retained; existing panelboard 'A' retained in place), and new systems were selected for minimal footprint — floor-mounted baseboard heaters rather than ducted systems, surface-mounted conduit where required.

**MEP scope sizing for occasional-occupancy use.** The church's program — events and occasional religious services — drove a deliberately minimal MEP footprint. No cooling system was introduced; heating-only operation with night setback was deemed appropriate for the building's use pattern. Plumbing was confined to a single restroom with two fixtures (flush-tank water closet and wall-hung lavatory) on domestic cold water only, with no hot water equipment. This restraint in scope reflects a coherent design philosophy: provide what is needed for safe habitability without over-intervening in the historic structure.

**Full electrical rewire in a small historic building.** Despite the modest building area, the electrical rehabilitation required full replacement of all existing wiring. The challenge was executing a complete rewire — disconnecting and replacing circuits for all baseboard heaters, removing flood light wiring, and installing new parking lot lighting — while saving and reinstalling the building's historic light fixtures (incandescent pendant above the entry door, wall-mounted incandescent fixtures inside) to preserve interior character. The existing 125A, 240V/120V, single-phase panelboard was retained and extended with a new circuit for parking lot lighting.

**Drawing set versioning without revision markings.** The CLIN 0002 drawing set was issued in two revisions: 10/07/2022 (six sheets) and 10/14/2022 (four sheets, partial re-issue). The re-issued sheets lacked revision clouds or delta markings, making it impossible from the documents alone to identify what changed between revisions. The design team managed the construction document package through two closely-spaced issuances to the federal client.

## Outcomes

The CLIN 0002 Rehabilitation construction documents were issued for construction (IFC) in October 2022. The drawing set — six sheets covering plumbing plans, mechanical floor plans, mechanical schedules and controls, electrical legend and schedule, electrical site plan, and electrical demolition — constitutes a complete MEP rehabilitation package for the historic chapel. The temporal gap between the originally anticipated design completion (November/December 2020) and the IFC issuance date (October 2022) indicates approximately two years of project duration beyond the original 190-day estimate; the available record does not document the reason for this interval.

No post-construction closeout documents were present in the available file set. Project status is inferred as complete on the basis of the IFC drawing issuance. Contract value and construction cost data are not available in the source record.
