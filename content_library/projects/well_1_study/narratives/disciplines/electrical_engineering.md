Electrical engineering was the sole discipline engaged on the Gloucester County Well No. 1 Electrical Coordination Study. The scope of electrical engineering services encompassed all phases of the coordination study: field survey of the existing electrical distribution system serving Well Pump No. 1 at the Raw Water No. 2 Treatment Plant; development of a computational power system model in SKM Power Tools; short circuit analysis to quantify available fault currents at each distribution bus; equipment evaluation of all overcurrent protective devices against ANSI Fault Analysis criteria; and time current curve (TCC) development with resulting protective device setting recommendations.

The distribution system under study extends from the utility service — a 1,500 kVA, 34,500–480Y/277 V transformer with a three-phase short circuit contribution of 2,528 amperes — through a 3,000-ampere main switchboard and a 600-ampere Panel DP-1, to a 100 HP / 460 V well pump motor and an emergency generator transfer switch arrangement. The electrical analysis determined that full selective coordination of the overcurrent protective devices is not achievable for the installed system configuration; compromised coordination, achievable through recommended instantaneous trip settings consistent with ANSI/NEMA MTS-2019, is the appropriate and code-supported outcome. NEC 2023 provisions confirm that the two 600-ampere circuit breakers in series are not required to be selectively coordinated. All equipment passed the ANSI Fault Analysis equipment evaluation. The study was completed June 2024.
