# Room Renovation, Bldg. 20840 — Wright-Patterson AFB, OH

This project involved the design-build renovation of three rooms (W133L, W133K, and W133G) within Building 20840 at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, Ohio — the Major General Harry G. Armstrong Complex, home of the 711th Human Performance Wing and the United States Air Force School of Aerospace Medicine. The 457,182 SF, four-story research and medical support facility was constructed in 2011 and carries a plant replacement value estimated at $221 million. The renovation converted the subject rooms into functional laboratory and associated support spaces serving the Airman Systems Directorate and aerospace medicine research mission.

The scope encompassed full interior demolition, hazardous materials abatement, and reconstruction of architectural finishes — including new partitions, flooring, ceilings, doors, and signage — as well as complete MEP systems coordination across all three rooms. Mechanical work included selective HVAC modifications with new air grilles and diffusers, an upgraded air valve serving the laboratory space, and the installation of a new stainless steel snorkel exhaust duct system with government-furnished equipment to serve laboratory fume control. Electrical work included new emergency power, desktop raceways, GFCI circuitry, and lighting revisions. Plumbing included new laboratory sinks with eye wash fixtures and tempered water connections. Fire alarm and mass notification components were relocated to accommodate the new room configuration, with NICET Level 4 drawing approval required for the relocation work.

The project was procured as CLIN 0004 within a multi-location FY22 Eastern Region Miscellaneous Projects task order (Solicitation W912DY22R0073) issued by the US Army Engineering and Support Center, Huntsville. Delivery was design-build under an 8(a) sole-source set-aside.
