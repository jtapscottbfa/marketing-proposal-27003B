# Room Renovation, Bldg. 20840 — Wright-Patterson AFB, OH
## Long-Form Project Narrative

---

### Project Overview

Building 20840 at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base — the Major General Harry G. Armstrong Complex — is a 457,182 square foot, four-story research and medical support facility constructed in 2011 with an estimated plant replacement value of $221 million. The building serves as the home of the United States Air Force School of Aerospace Medicine and the Airman Systems Directorate, both components of the 711th Human Performance Wing located on Wright-Patterson AFB in Dayton, Ohio.

This project renovated three rooms in Area B of Building 20840 — Rooms W133L, W133K, and W133G — converting the existing spaces into functional laboratory and associated support areas aligned with the Wing's research and aerospace medicine mission. The work was procured as CLIN 0004 within Solicitation W912DY22R0073, a multi-location FY22 Eastern Region Miscellaneous Projects design-build task order issued by the US Army Engineering and Support Center, Huntsville (USACE HNC). The solicitation was structured as an 8(a) sole-source set-aside issued 13 September 2022 under the Small Business Administration's 8(a) Business Development Program.

---

### Scope

The renovation scope encompassed full interior demolition of the three rooms, hazardous materials abatement, and complete reconstruction of architectural finishes and MEP systems. The work was governed by UFC 4-510-01 Design: Military Medical Facilities and NFPA 99 Health Care Facilities Code, reflecting the 711th HPW's dual research and medical support mission.

**Architectural:** Interior demolition was performed throughout all three rooms, including removal of existing wall partitions, finishes, casework, and associated equipment. Hazardous materials abatement was included in the scope. New construction included interior partitions, drywall, flooring, ceilings, doors and frames, painting and wall coverings, and signage. Casework and laboratory equipment provisions were coordinated with government-furnished equipment.

**Mechanical:** The existing HVAC system was largely retained. New work was limited to selective modifications including replacement air grilles and diffusers in W133L and W133K, and an upgraded air valve in W133L to serve the renovated laboratory space at 385 CFM. In W133G, a new 3-inch stainless steel duct system was installed to serve a government-furnished laboratory snorkel (model MOVE-X ME Type 1500-75 / FUMEX MET 1500-75), complete with an 8-inch Phoenix Controls air control valve in the snorkel ductwork.

**Electrical:** New electrical work included a 208V emergency power outlet connected to panel L1E-A, a desktop electric raceway system along the south and east walls providing seven receptacle positions, and GFCI duplex receptacles distributed across multiple wall locations. Lighting revisions included relocation of a fixture in the W133K/W133L vestibule and rewiring of all lighting in that area to the vestibule switch.

**Plumbing:** Existing domestic cold and hot water piping was reused throughout all three rooms. New plumbing fixtures included a stainless steel laboratory sink with a gooseneck faucet and eye wash wand with a tempered water connection in W133L/K, and a chemical-resistant epoxy/resin sink with a hand-held eye wash in W133G. Existing nitrogen and compressed air lines were abandoned in place and capped.

**Fire Protection:** The fire alarm and mass notification system was relocated off a demolished wall in W133G to accommodate the new room configuration. NICET Level 4 fire alarm drawing approval was required for the relocation.

---

### Role on Project

The design team participated as subconsultants under the design-build delivery structure. The 8(a) sole-source set-aside procurement mechanism required the prime contractor to be a Small Business Administration-certified 8(a) firm; the design team contributed engineering services across architecture, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and fire protection disciplines in support of the prime's design-build delivery.

### Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Occupied facility coordination.** Building 20840 is an active research and medical support facility. The 711th Human Performance Wing's normal duty hours are 0700–1600, Monday–Friday, requiring construction activities to be scheduled and sequenced to minimize disruption to ongoing Wing operations. Contractor coordination with Wing personnel was required throughout the construction phase.

**UFC and NFPA 99 compliance in a laboratory renovation context.** The project required design compliance with UFC 4-510-01 Design: Military Medical Facilities and NFPA 99, which impose specific requirements on room layout, ventilation, plumbing fixture specifications, and fire alarm/mass notification systems in medical and research environments. The laboratory snorkel system and eye wash fixture designs were developed to meet these requirements while accommodating government-furnished equipment.

**NICET Level 4 fire alarm coordination.** Relocating the fire alarm and mass notification system off a demolished wall in W133G required NICET Level 4-stamped fire alarm drawings. This introduced a specialty coordination requirement within the design-build delivery that had to be managed as part of the overall design package.

**Multi-CLIN solicitation structure.** The WPAFB room renovation was one of six line items within a broader multi-location solicitation covering facilities at Wright-Patterson AFB OH, Fort Meade MD, Fort Eustis VA, Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst NJ, and Shaw AFB SC. The WPAFB task had to be scoped and priced as a discrete deliverable within the larger task order framework.

**Liquidated damages exposure.** The contract included liquidated damages at $560.00 per calendar day of contractor delay, requiring careful schedule management throughout the design and construction phases.

---

### Outcomes

The project delivered a renovated laboratory and support suite within an active Air Force research and medical facility, serving the aerospace medicine and Airman systems research mission of the 711th Human Performance Wing. The design-build approach integrated architectural and MEP systems design within a unified delivery structure, with compliance to military medical facility standards and occupied-facility constraints governing the approach throughout.
