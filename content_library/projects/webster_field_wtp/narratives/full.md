# B8175 Modify Wastewater Treatment Plant — Construct Pitched Roof Over Drying Bed
**Webster Field Annex, St. Inigoes, Maryland**
**Client: Naval Facilities Engineering Command – Washington (NAVFAC Washington)**

---

## Project Overview

Webster Field Annex is a Naval Air Systems Command (NAVAIR) field support facility located in St. Inigoes, St. Mary's County, Maryland. The facility operates a Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP) that includes an array of sludge drying beds — open concrete basins used to dewater sludge solids as part of the wastewater treatment process. Prolonged exposure to precipitation was degrading the drying process efficiency and potentially affecting effluent quality. The Navy identified a requirement to protect the drying beds with a permanent overhead structure while preserving the open-air, unenclosed nature of the beds to maintain operational function.

The design team was engaged as prime consultant under NAVFAC MACC contract N40080-19-D-0003 — a NAVFAC Multiple Award Construction Contract (MACC) vehicle — to design a permanent open pitched-roof shelter covering all three drying beds. The project received BFA internal project number 1613723.

---

## Scope of Work

The design scope encompassed all structural and civil engineering necessary to deliver a permanent open shelter structure over the three existing sludge drying beds. The designed structure consists of:

- A prefabricated metal-plate-connected wood truss roof system, spanning approximately 78'-7.5" in width and 38'-0" in depth, covering the three drying beds in a single contiguous structure.
- A 4:12 roof pitch, providing a ridge height approximately 16'-5" above the existing concrete slab at the ridge line, with a minimum eave height of 14 feet above the slab.
- Pressure-treated southern pine (SPIB rules, MC ≤15%) framing members, including king trusses at interior column lines, rafters at maximum 24" on center, and 8×8 pressure-treated lateral braces at each post.
- Laminated veneer lumber (LVL) beams — (5)-1¾"×18" LVL along each column line — providing the primary horizontal support for the truss assembly.
- 12×12 pressure-treated timber posts bearing on new cast-in-place concrete piers, each founded on 4'-6" × 4'-6" × 1'-0" spread footings designed for a maximum allowable soil bearing pressure of 2,000 PSF per geotechnical investigation by Bob Taylor Engineering, Inc.
- Asphalt shingle roofing on 3/4" roof sheathing with continuous aluminum gutters on both sides of the ridge.
- An entirely open structure — no side walls, enclosures, or MEP systems.

Design was governed by NAVFAC Unified Facilities Criteria including UFC 1-200-01, UFC 3-301-01, UFC 3-110-05, and UFC 3-201-01, alongside IBC 2018, ASCE 7, and AWC NDS-2015.

## Role on Project

The firm served as prime consultant providing structural engineering design services. The structural engineer of record was Marcos Freeman, PE (Maryland PE License 841286). Geotechnical investigation was performed by Bob Taylor Engineering, Inc., whose boring logs and soil bearing recommendation were incorporated into the structural foundation design. The project was delivered as design-bid-build, with the design team responsible for producing the contract documents used to solicit and award the construction contract.

---

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Open shelter structure within a federal NAVFAC delivery framework.** Designing an open canopy structure within NAVFAC's UFC-governed design standards requires the same rigor applied to enclosed buildings — load combinations, connection detailing, geotechnical compliance — without the benefit of an enclosed building's inherent lateral and diaphragm continuity. The design achieved lateral stability through 8×8 pressure-treated diagonal braces at each post location and a plywood diaphragm at the roof plane, with Simpson Strong-Tie H55 hurricane clips on all trusses to meet the 105 MPH ultimate design wind speed (Exposure C) requirement.

**Geotechnical constraints and existing infrastructure.** The structure had to be integrated with the existing concrete slab-on-grade, existing 8" CMU stem walls, and an existing concrete light pole pier — all of which were designed to remain. New isolated concrete piers and spread footings were sized and placed to avoid conflicts with existing foundations while achieving the required soil bearing and load transfer. Bearing capacity was limited to 2,000 PSF per the geotechnical report, which the footing design accommodated.

**Scope addition via bilateral modification.** The project was originally scoped and priced at $395,612 for the construction contract (CLIN 0001). A bilateral modification subsequently added the full pitched-roof-over-drying-beds scope, increasing the construction contract value to $750,487 — a scope addition of $354,875 — and extending the construction period of performance by 363 calendar days to a revised contract completion date of June 2, 2022. The design documents supporting this modification — specifications and a seven-sheet drawing set — were issued as a final submittal in March 2022.

---

## Outcomes

The project delivered a permanent, code-compliant weather protection structure over the three active sludge drying beds at the Webster Field Annex WWTP. Construction was completed under the revised contract by June 2022. The final construction contract value was $750,487. All design work was performed under NAVFAC's UFC standards, with the structural design stamped by a licensed Maryland professional engineer. The project demonstrates experience with NAVFAC MACC delivery, outdoor utility infrastructure improvement, and the design of open structural systems in a federal military installation context.
