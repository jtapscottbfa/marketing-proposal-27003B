Structural engineering constituted the primary and lead discipline on this project. The design team developed the complete structural system for a permanent open pitched-roof shelter covering three sludge drying beds at the Webster Field Annex Wastewater Treatment Plant — a structure with no enclosing walls, no mechanical or electrical systems, and direct exposure to outdoor environmental loads.

The structural design encompasses prefabricated metal-plate-connected wood truss framing spanning approximately 78'-7.5" × 38'-0" in plan. Trusses are pressure-treated southern pine (SPIB grading rules, MC ≤15%) spaced at maximum 24" on center, supported on king trusses at interior column lines, which in turn bear on laminated veneer lumber (LVL) beams ((5)-1¾"×18" LVL per column line). Lateral stability is provided by 8×8 pressure-treated diagonal braces at each post and a roof plywood diaphragm, with Simpson Strong-Tie H55 hurricane clips at all truss-to-beam connections. The minimum eave height is 14 feet above the existing concrete slab; the roof is pitched at 4:12.

Foundation design involved new cast-in-place concrete isolated piers and spread footings (4'-6" × 4'-6" × 1'-0") sized for a maximum allowable soil bearing pressure of 2,000 PSF per geotechnical investigation. 12×12 pressure-treated timber posts transfer loads from the framing to the piers. Existing CMU stem walls, the existing slab-on-grade, and an existing concrete light pole pier were all designed to remain in place. Design complied with UFC 3-301-01, IBC 2018, ASCE 7, and AWC NDS-2015. Ultimate design wind speed was 105 MPH, Exposure C; Risk Category I; Seismic Design Category A. Marcos Freeman, PE (Maryland PE License 841286), served as structural engineer of record.
