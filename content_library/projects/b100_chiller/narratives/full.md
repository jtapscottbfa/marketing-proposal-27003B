# Building 100 Chiller Replacement — NAS Oceana
## Full Project Narrative

### Project Overview

The firm served as prime architect-engineer under NAVFAC Contract No. N40085-20-D-0005, an Indefinite Quantity A-E services contract for mechanical and electrical projects across Naval Facilities Engineering Command installations. The project — eProjects Work Order No. 1656492, FY2020 — required preparation of a complete Design-Bid-Build construction package for the replacement of two rooftop air-cooled chillers at Building 100, Naval Air Station Oceana, Virginia Beach, Virginia. The estimated construction cost was $424,707. Construction procurement was structured as an SBA 8(a) set-aside.

### Facility Context and Mission-Critical Requirement

Building 100 at NAS Oceana houses mission-critical radar equipment whose continuous operation depends on uninterrupted chilled water cooling. The building's primary cooling source is a central chiller plant in Building 119, which operates two 450-ton water-cooled chillers with 100 HP variable-frequency-drive centrifugal pumps distributing chilled water via direct-buried copper piping. As backup for those primary chillers, Building 100 previously relied on two 30-ton air-cooled chillers mounted on the low roof of the building's first-floor electrical room, supported on a W8 steel frame atop four 10-inch concrete piers.

Through site investigation and interviews with facility operators, the design team determined that the two existing backup chillers had become operationally unreliable — at the time of survey, isolation valves were closed and neither unit was operating. Replacement of the entire backup system was required to restore the intended N+1 redundancy posture protecting radar operations.

### Scope of A-E Services

The firm performed a comprehensive Site Engineering Investigation (SEI) prior to design development, documenting existing system architecture for both Building 100 and Building 119, interviewing Air Operations and NAVFAC Shops and DDC personnel, and consulting with the Trane ESPC operator. Survey findings were consolidated in a Final SEI Summary Report submitted 45 days after task order award, in advance of the Prefinal (100%) design submittal.

Design disciplines engaged on the project included mechanical, electrical, structural, and architectural.

**Mechanical.** The new backup system consists of a single air-cooled chiller sized appropriately for the radar system's thermal loads, replacing the two unreliable 30-ton units. The new chiller is configured with absolute priority to supply the radar system whenever the Building 119 chiller plant is unavailable, and can supply the shared campus loop when radar system protection is not required. The design includes new chilled water distribution piping and a controls package fully integrated with the existing Trane Energy Savings Performance Contract. Existing in-line centrifugal pumps (Pump #11 and #12) in the second-floor mechanical room, operating in parallel at an assumed 72 GPM (2.4 GPM/ton), were addressed in the piping and pump design. A crane access and placement plan was developed as part of the construction documents, with coordination of FAA airspace requirements given the equipment's proximity to the flight line.

**Electrical.** The electrical design addressed demolition of the two existing chiller circuits back to Panel "PJ" — a 400A MLO, 208Y/120V, 3-phase GE panelboard in mechanical room 207 — including removal of the existing 175A circuit breakers, disconnect switches, and conductors. A new circuit was designed from Panel "PJ" for the replacement chiller, including a new disconnect switch and conductors. The design also required assessment of Panel "PJ"'s age and condition to determine whether the existing panelboard could safely accept new circuit breakers or required replacement. Coordination with the government was required for any work involving the building's main electrical distribution system in the first-floor electrical room, where an Onan Bypass Transfer Switch and an adjacent emergency generator already provided building-wide emergency power backup.

**Structural.** The existing W8 steel support frame for the chillers was assessed. Load verification confirmed that the existing four 10-inch concrete piers align with cast-in-place concrete walls bearing to foundation, avoiding additional loading on the roof slab. The existing steel framing carried surface rust and required cleaning and repainting. A structural load letter sealed by a licensed professional engineer was specified for the construction contractor, verifying existing roof capacity for the new chiller. Additionally, an existing 50-inch steel pipe guardrail on the south side of the roof did not comply with current fall protection requirements and did not extend around the full roof perimeter. The design replaced it with a new code-compliant 42-inch guardrail embedded into the concrete roof slab around the entire perimeter, in accordance with UFC 3-301-01, OSHA Fall Protection Guidelines, and OPNAVINST 5100.23-CH 13.

**Architectural.** Rooftop work included repair of the modified bituminous membrane at the existing chiller curbs, replacement of flashing and sealant at the concrete curbs, and repainting of all existing curbs. Abandoned rooftop piping penetrations through the building's cast-in-place concrete walls were sealed and re-insulated in accordance with manufacturer-recommended standard details. A two-year installer warranty was required for all roof patch work and new membrane flashing.

### Project Delivery

The design was procured under a Design-Bid-Build delivery method within NAVFAC's IQ A-E contract framework. The design schedule required final design completion within 150 cumulative calendar days after task order award, with a phased submittal sequence (SEI report at day 45, Prefinal at day 74, Interim Final at day 129, Final at day 150). Post-Construction Award Services — including shop drawing review, field consultation during construction, and participation in the pre-construction conference and PAK meeting — were included in the task order scope.
