# Mechanical Engineering — Building 100 Chiller Replacement, NAS Oceana

The mechanical scope centered on the design of a new single air-cooled backup chiller system replacing two unreliable 30-ton rooftop units that had served as the N+1 redundant cooling source for Building 100's mission-critical radar equipment. The design team performed a Site Engineering Investigation (SEI) to characterize both the existing Building 100 backup system and the primary chilled water plant in Building 119 (two 450-ton water-cooled chillers with 100 HP VFD pumps), establishing the thermal loads, flow requirements, and control interfaces necessary to size and configure the replacement system.

The new chiller was designed with absolute operational priority for the radar system: whenever the Building 119 chiller plant is unavailable, the backup chiller automatically engages to protect radar operations. When radar cooling is not required, the chiller can supplement the shared campus loop. New distribution piping and pump configuration addressed the existing parallel in-line centrifugal pump arrangement (Pump #11 and Pump #12) operating at an assumed 72 GPM.

Controls integration was a technically demanding element of the scope. The existing building chilled water systems operate under an Energy Savings Performance Contract administered by Trane; the new chiller's sequence of operations and DDC controls were required to be fully compatible and integrated with that system. A crane location plan was also developed as part of the construction documents, coordinating placement adjacent to the flight line and addressing FAA airspace requirements for equipment lift operations.
