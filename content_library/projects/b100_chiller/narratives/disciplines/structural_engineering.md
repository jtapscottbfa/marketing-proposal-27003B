# Structural Engineering — Building 100 Chiller Replacement, NAS Oceana

The structural scope addressed two distinct elements: verification of the existing roof support system's capacity for the replacement chiller and replacement of a non-compliant fall-protection guardrail.

The existing chillers were supported on a W8 steel frame bearing on four 10-inch concrete piers. Survey confirmed that the piers align with load-bearing 10-inch cast-in-place concrete walls that extend to foundation, meaning the new chiller's weight transfers directly to the existing structural wall system without imposing additional load on the roof slab. The existing steel framing showed surface rust and was specified for cleaning and repainting. Because the project constituted a direct unit replacement, structural reinforcement was not anticipated; the design documents required the construction contractor to provide a structural load letter sealed by a licensed professional engineer verifying that existing roof capacity is sufficient for the new unit.

The more substantial structural design element was the guardrail replacement. An existing 50-inch steel pipe guardrail on the south side of the low roof did not meet current fall protection requirements — height, extent, and attachment method were all deficient. The proximity of the replacement chiller to the roof edge made full-perimeter fall protection mandatory. The design replaced the existing guardrail with a new 42-inch compliant guardrail embedded into the 10-inch concrete roof slab and extending around the entire roof perimeter, designed in accordance with UFC 3-301-01 (Structural Engineering), UFC 3-310-04 (Seismic Design), ASCE 7-10, OSHA Fall Protection Guidelines, and OPNAVINST 5100.23-CH 13 (Navy Safety and Occupational Health Program Manual — Fall Protection).
