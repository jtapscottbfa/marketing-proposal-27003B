# LPD NNS Welding Services

## Project Overview

LPD NNS Welding Services is an electrical power distribution project supporting welding operations at Newport News Shipbuilding (Huntington Ingalls Industries, NNS) in Norfolk, Virginia. Newport News Shipbuilding is one of the nation's primary constructors of US Navy surface combatants and amphibious warfare vessels, including Landing Platform Dock (LPD) class ships. The project involved engineering design of a purpose-built electrical service distribution system to deliver high-current welding power to active shipyard work locations. Design was issued under BFA project number 24-052, dated August 27, 2024, under Virginia professional engineer seal.

Source documentation for this project record consists of a single electrical detail sheet (drawing E-501, DETAILS). Project-level information including contract value, construction schedule, owner contact, and procurement method is not captured in available source documents.

## Scope of Work

The scope encompasses the complete design of an outdoor industrial electrical power distribution system from medium-voltage primary service to point-of-use welding receptacles at seven service locations. The system was designed as a ground-up installation appropriate for shipyard pier-side or open-yard environments, using construction methods suited to outdoor industrial service — NEMA 3R enclosures, exterior-rated panel mounting structures, and PVC conduit on galvanized strut supports.

**Primary Service and Transformation.** The system originates at a 34.5kV overhead three-phase, four-wire primary service. A fused cutout rated at 60A (USLD) and 27kV surge arrestors provide primary overcurrent and transient protection. Power is stepped down through a pad-mounted distribution transformer (MU12, 34,500/480-277V, 4000/3kVA rating, 85kV insulation class) to utilization voltage.

**Main Switchboard.** Transformation feeds a 4,000-ampere, 277/480V, three-phase, four-wire main switchboard in a NEMA 3R enclosure on a concrete pad, incorporating multiple 800-ampere main breakers and 400-ampere branch breakers with spare positions for future loads.

**Distribution to Service Racks.** From the main switchboard, ten runs of 4-inch conduit — including two spare conduits — route to seven service rack locations across the work area. The conduit rack is constructed with 4-inch PVC conduit on galvanized strut supports from grade.

**Service Rack Assemblies.** Each service rack consists of a 400-ampere, 277/480V, three-phase, four-wire panelboard mounted on exterior plywood backboards supported by 4×4 salt-treated wood posts. Branch breakers at each rack feed a combination of 200-ampere and 100-ampere, three-phase, four-wire receptacles of the RS (Russell Stoll or equivalent) pattern, providing the high-current connections required for industrial welding equipment. The design notes that certain rack locations may use owner-furnished transformers.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**High-Current Industrial Load at Shipyard Scale.** Welding service receptacles draw sustained high current — each 200-ampere, three-phase outlet represents substantial continuous electrical demand. The design addresses this through careful sizing of the transformer, main switchboard ampacity, branch circuit protection, and conduit fill: a 4,000-ampere main switchboard with 10 distribution conduit runs to 7 service locations ensures adequate capacity and redundancy for multi-shift shipyard operations.

**Outdoor and Industrial Environment.** The installation is outdoor or open-yard at an active shipyard, requiring NEMA 3R enclosures for weather protection, pressure-treated structural materials for service rack posts, and exterior-rated construction methods throughout. The conduit system uses PVC on galvanized strut supports — appropriate for a corrosive coastal industrial environment.

**Medium-Voltage Primary Service Integration.** Taking primary service at 34.5kV rather than at utilization voltage places transformer selection, protection coordination, and primary-side disconnecting means in scope, adding engineering complexity beyond a typical low-voltage distribution project. The design specifies appropriate surge protection (27kV arrestors), fusing, and insulation class (85kV) for the primary service environment.

**Expandability.** Two spare conduit runs between the main switchboard and the service rack distribution area, combined with spare breaker positions in the main switchboard, provide documented capacity for future service rack additions without major re-engineering.

## Outcomes

Engineering design for the LPD NNS Welding Services electrical distribution system was completed and issued under Virginia PE seal as of August 27, 2024 (BFA project 24-052). The design documents a complete medium-voltage-to-point-of-use power distribution system for shipyard welding service at seven locations, including primary service protection, pad-mounted transformation, main switchboard, conduit distribution, and panelboard assemblies with industrial welding receptacles.
