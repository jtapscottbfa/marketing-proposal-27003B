# Building 1816 Replace and Upgrade Exhaust Systems
## Naval Weapons Station Yorktown, Yorktown, Virginia

---

### Project Overview

Building 1816 at Naval Weapons Station Yorktown (WPNSTA Yorktown) is the depot-level maintenance facility for the MK-48 torpedo — one of the U.S. Navy's primary submarine-launched weapons systems. The building was originally constructed in 1976 and encompasses 48,373 gross square feet. Three rooms within the facility (Rooms 101, 102, and 103) are used for active torpedo defueling and refueling operations with Otto Fuel II, a propellant whose primary constituent is propylene glycol dinitrate (PGDN), representing approximately 75 percent of the fuel by volume. Otto Fuel II is highly toxic and volatile; its handling generates airborne contaminants that require continuous, reliable exhaust ventilation and strict negative-pressure control (0.05–0.10 in. w.g.) to prevent fume migration into adjacent occupied spaces.

By the project's initiation, the existing HVAC and exhaust infrastructure supporting these rooms had reached the end of its service life. Aging rooftop air handling units, undersized exhaust fans, deteriorating ductwork, and an inadequate heating system collectively compromised the building's ability to maintain the ventilation performance required under applicable Navy and OSHA standards for hazardous industrial occupancies. Replacement was required to restore safe, code-compliant operations in a facility of direct operational significance to the fleet.

---

### Scope of A-E Design Services

The A-E scope covered mechanical, electrical, fire alarm, structural, and environmental disciplines across a renovated footprint of 3,014 square feet within the existing 48,373-square-foot building.

**Mechanical Systems.** The core scope was replacement of the three rooftop air handling units serving the fuel-handling areas: AHU-14, AHU-15, and AHU-16. New ductwork was designed for AHU-14 and AHU-15 to accommodate the replacement units and revised airflow routing. All fourteen exhaust fans serving these spaces were replaced: EF-8 through EF-21. A supplemental boiler (1,624 MBTU) with a dedicated 162 GPM circulation pump was added to address heating capacity limitations. The existing hot water pump P-1, which had been serving at 280 GPM, was replaced with a new unit sized for 500 GPM to match the upgraded system requirements. The design also included a DDC controls upgrade to provide integrated monitoring and control of the upgraded exhaust and air handling systems.

**Electrical Systems.** Electrical scope supported the mechanical equipment replacements, including power distribution to new AHUs, exhaust fans, the supplemental boiler, and the replacement pump. Motor control and grounding and lightning protection requirements were addressed for the new equipment.

**Fire Alarm.** Modifications to the existing fire alarm system were required to account for the new equipment and ductwork configurations in the affected spaces.

**Structural.** Structural assessment of existing roof conditions was performed to determine reinforcement requirements for new rooftop equipment. Bar joist reinforcement and dunnage frames were designed to support the replacement AHUs and exhaust fans at their new locations and weights.

**Environmental — Hazardous Material Abatement.** Pre-design environmental investigation identified hazardous materials to be abated in connection with the HVAC work. Asbestos-containing materials (ACM) were confirmed in HVAC roof curb flashing (2–3% Chrysotile) and presumed in HVAC duct mastic. Lead, cadmium, and chromium were identified in paint samples. PCB-assumed ballasts and mercury-containing lamps were present in existing fluorescent fixtures. Abatement specifications and design coordination were included in the design package.

---

### Role on Project

The firm served as prime A-E consultant under a NAVFAC IDIQ task order. The scope encompassed full design services from project initiation through final design and construction document preparation, with design submittals at Design Development (35–50%), Pre-Final (100%), and Final Design. The construction contract package was prepared as a Design-Bid-Build (DBB) package for separate competitive procurement. Construction was to be procured via SBA-8(a) MACC set-aside using a low-price technically acceptable (LPTA) methodology. The contract vehicle was NAVFAC IDIQ Contract No. N4008520D0005, project RM 22-0238 (e-Project 1697374), administered by NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic / WPNSTA Yorktown.

---

### Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Hazardous Occupancy Ventilation Compliance.** The most significant technical challenge was designing HVAC and exhaust systems that maintain mandatory negative pressure in three active Otto Fuel II handling rooms. These spaces cannot operate at positive or neutral pressure — any lapse in exhaust capacity allows toxic fumes to migrate. The design response included replacing all fourteen exhaust fans and the three rooftop AHUs as an integrated system, incorporating DDC controls to enable continuous monitoring and alarm capability, and sizing the supplemental boiler and circulation pump to ensure the heating system can sustain airflow even under cold-weather conditions when fresh-air intake loads are highest.

**Hazardous Material Discovery and Abatement Coordination.** The presence of ACM in HVAC roof curb flashing and presumed ACM in duct mastic required abatement sequencing to be coordinated with the mechanical demolition and replacement work. Pre-design sampling confirmed the ACM locations (updating earlier pre-survey assumptions regarding floor tiles and pipe insulation), allowing the construction documents to specify abatement work precisely and in advance of equipment replacement. Lead-based paint in the affected areas and PCB ballasts in existing lighting fixtures were similarly incorporated into the abatement scope to minimize re-mobilization.

**Structural Reinforcement for Rooftop Equipment Replacement.** The existing bar joist roof structure required analysis and reinforcement to accommodate replacement rooftop AHUs and exhaust fans, which may have differed in weight and configuration from the original equipment installed in 1976. Structural dunnage frames and bar joist reinforcement were designed to allow installation without compromise to roof structural integrity.

**Occupied Facility Operations.** Building 1816 is an active torpedo maintenance facility. The design package had to sequence the replacement work in a manner compatible with continued facility operations where possible, given that the ventilation systems serve areas with active hazardous material handling.

---

### Outcomes

The project produced a complete Design-Bid-Build construction contract package for competitive procurement via SBA-8(a) MACC set-aside. The design replaced end-of-life HVAC and exhaust infrastructure in a Navy depot-level maintenance facility where ventilation performance is directly tied to the safety of personnel handling highly toxic materials. Construction contract cost and project completion date are not captured in the available source record.
