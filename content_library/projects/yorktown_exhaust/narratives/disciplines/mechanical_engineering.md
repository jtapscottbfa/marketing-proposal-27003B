Mechanical engineering was the lead discipline for Building 1816 Replace and Upgrade Exhaust Systems at Naval Weapons Station Yorktown, a NAVFAC A-E project for the depot-level MK-48 torpedo maintenance facility. The project scope centered on the replacement and upgrade of the building's HVAC and exhaust infrastructure serving three rooms where Otto Fuel II — a highly toxic torpedo propellant — is handled during active defueling and refueling. These rooms require continuous negative-pressure ventilation (0.05–0.10 in. w.g.) to prevent migration of toxic fumes to adjacent occupied spaces, making system performance a critical life-safety and operational requirement.

The mechanical scope included replacement of three rooftop air handling units (AHU-14, AHU-15, and AHU-16), fourteen exhaust fans (EF-8 through EF-21), and new ductwork for AHU-14 and AHU-15 to match the replacement units and revised airflow routing. To address heating system limitations, a supplemental boiler (1,624 MBTU) with a dedicated 162 GPM circulation pump was added. The existing hot water pump P-1 was replaced with a new unit sized for 500 GPM, up from the original 280 GPM, to serve the upgraded system. A DDC controls upgrade provided integrated monitoring and control of the exhaust fans and air handling units. The mechanical design was coordinated with structural modifications for rooftop equipment support and with hazardous material abatement requirements for ACM-containing HVAC roof curb flashing and duct mastic encountered during pre-design investigation.
