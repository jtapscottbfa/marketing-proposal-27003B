Environmental engineering services at Building 1816, Naval Weapons Station Yorktown, centered on pre-design hazardous material investigation and abatement design in connection with the HVAC and exhaust system replacement. Because the mechanical scope required removal and replacement of rooftop equipment, roof curb penetrations, and interior ductwork in a 1976-era building, pre-design sampling was conducted to identify and characterize hazardous materials that would be disturbed during construction.

Analytical results confirmed asbestos-containing material (ACM) in HVAC roof curb flashing (2–3% Chrysotile) and identified HVAC duct mastic as presumed ACM (the suspect material was not accessible for sampling). Lead, cadmium, and chromium were detected in paint samples from affected areas. PCB-assumed ballasts and mercury-containing lamps were identified in existing fluorescent light fixtures within the project scope. Abatement design and specifications were incorporated into the construction document package, with abatement sequencing coordinated to precede mechanical demolition and equipment replacement activities.

The environmental investigation superseded earlier pre-survey assumptions documented in the SAES — which had listed ACM floor tiles, sealant, and pipe insulation as presumed — replacing them with actual survey-confirmed findings. This evolution from presumed to confirmed ACM locations is a standard pattern in NAVFAC pre-design investigations and represents the design basis used for the final construction documents.
