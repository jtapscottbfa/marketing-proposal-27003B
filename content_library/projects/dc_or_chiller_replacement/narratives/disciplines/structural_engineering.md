The structural engineering scope on this project centered on a comprehensive Roof Fall Protection Design Analysis for Building 1 and Building 6 at VA Medical Center, Washington D.C. The analysis identified all roof edge and elevation-change locations requiring guardrail installation to satisfy IBC 2024 Section 1015 guardrail requirements, OSHA Standard 1926 / 29 CFR, VA Technical Information Library criteria, and ASCE 7-22 minimum design load standards. The analysis also produced general fall protection construction document sheets for use by subsequent designers and contractors performing work near fall-risk areas within the scope boundary.

A critical component of the analysis was an existing structural assessment of the roof systems across the scope area. All roof structures within Buildings 1 and 6 were confirmed to be 10-inch thick reinforced concrete slabs — likely originally designed to accommodate potential future vertical expansion — with calculated allowable live loads exceeding 100 pounds per square foot. This finding established that the existing structural systems could support required fall protection railing installation loads without structural augmentation across the full scope area.

Three roof membrane system types were documented and evaluated as part of the site conditions assessment: ballasted aggregate systems, TPO single-ply membrane, and EPDM synthetic rubber membrane. The analysis quantified approximately 7,300 linear feet of required railing across a complex, multi-wing hospital campus roof plan encompassing approximately 15 or more distinct numbered railing zones, with crane staging areas identified for installation access. Final submittal for the fall protection analysis was dated March 3, 2025.
