Mechanical engineering on this project encompassed industrial process piping and fuel system design for the replacement aboveground storage tank installation serving the Perry Point VA Medical Center boiler plant.

The core mechanical scope was the design of the fuel distribution system connecting three new 35,000-gallon double-wall horizontal aboveground storage tanks to the facility's four boilers. New fuel fill piping was specified as double-wall ASTM 53 steel with insulation and aluminum jacket. Dedicated fuel supply and return lines were designed for each boiler connection. The existing fuel oil pumping system and tank fill pumping system were evaluated and determined to remain in service, requiring mechanical coordination for interface with the new tank arrangement.

New ancillary fuel system components included a permanent fuel polishing system to maintain fuel quality within the storage tanks, a fuel water separator, and tank flow meters with remote readout capability to the boiler plant control room — enabling operators to monitor consumption and inventory from a central location without physical inspection of each tank.

Tank instrumentation design included an automatic tank gauge and leak detection system specified to comply with Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) requirements using an NWGLDE-approved detection method. Cathodic protection for the new tanks was designed as part of the mechanical-civil interface scope, coordinated with the electrical grounding system.

The three-tank arrangement, replacing a single 150,000-gallon vessel, required mechanical piping design that maintained operational flexibility — allowing individual tanks to be isolated for maintenance or inspection without interrupting fuel supply to the boiler plant.
