# Replace 150K Gallon Fuel Tank at Boiler Plant — Perry Point VA Medical Center

**Client:** VA Maryland Healthcare System – VISN5
**Location:** Perry Point VA Medical Center, Perry Point, MD
**Contract:** NRM Project No. 512A5-20-302 / Task Order 512A5-20-308
**Procurement:** Federal IDIQ, SDVOSB set-aside (RFP 36C24520R0091)
**Construction Budget:** $800,000
**Design Performance Period:** 120 calendar days from kickoff

---

## Project Overview

The Perry Point VA Medical Center boiler plant operated on a single 150,000-gallon aboveground fuel oil storage tank — a single-wall vessel with an earthen containment berm that no longer met current environmental and safety standards for secondary containment. The Department of Veterans Affairs, VISN5 Network Contracting Office 5, issued a task order under an A/E IDIQ contract to design its replacement. The project was set aside for Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Businesses (SDVOSBs), and the design contract was executed under NRM Project No. 512A5-20-302.

The physical security context added complexity beyond a standard tank replacement: the site required evaluation under the VA's Physical Security and Resiliency Design Manual (PSRDM) blast analysis methodology. The tank farm's proximity to facility buildings 315 and 321 necessitated blast mitigation design as an integral element of the civil and structural scope.

## Scope of Services

The A/E design encompassed all disciplines required to decommission the existing fuel system and commission its replacement:

**Tank system and fuel piping.** The design specified three prefabricated, double-wall, 35,000-gallon horizontal aboveground storage tanks to replace the single 150,000-gallon tank. New fuel fill piping was designed as double-wall ASTM 53 steel with insulation and aluminum jacket. Dedicated fuel supply and return lines were designed to serve all four boilers. The existing fuel oil pumping system and tank fill pumping system were retained. New systems included a permanent fuel polishing system, an MDE-approved automatic tank gauge and leak detection system (NWGLDE method), a fuel water separator, and tank flow meters with remote readout to the boiler plant control room. Cathodic protection and tank grounding were incorporated.

**Civil and containment.** The existing 150,000-gallon single-wall tank and its earthen containment berm were demolished. In their place, a reinforced concrete secondary containment pad was designed to retain 110 percent of the volume of one 35,000-gallon tank plus the volume of a seven-day, 25-year storm event — meeting MDE requirements for aboveground petroleum storage. A site retention and containment wall completes the secondary containment system.

**Structural / blast mitigation.** PSRDM analysis informed the design of 12-inch CMU blast mitigation screen walls on the north and west sides of the new tank farm. This blast wall design was driven by standoff distance constraints relative to occupied facility structures.

**Electrical and site lighting.** New site lighting was designed by relocating the existing underground lighting circuit. Tank grounding was provided as a coordinated electrical-civil scope item.

**Environmental and industrial hygiene.** The design included a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) and erosion and sediment control plan. Industrial hygiene services addressed soil contamination assessment in the vicinity of the existing tank, and a lead paint sampling and disposal plan was prepared for materials associated with the existing tank and infrastructure.

**Fire protection.** Fire protection engineering was included in the design team's discipline coverage for this project; specific fire protection scope items are not detailed in the available source record.

## Role on Project

The firm served as prime A/E consultant under a VA IDIQ task order set aside for SDVOSBs, delivering multidisciplinary design services across civil, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, structural, environmental, and fire protection disciplines. The design was executed under a 120-calendar-day performance period from the kickoff meeting.

A prior design contract (NRM 512A5-18-302) for the same physical project had been executed by a separate firm under a change order to the Central Emergency Power Distribution project. The 2020 contract (512A5-20-302) represented a new design procurement, with the prior-phase design narrative available as reference context. The A/E team was responsible for producing a full construction document package suitable for Design-Bid-Build procurement.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Three-tank replacement for one.** The 150,000-gallon single-wall tank was replaced not with a single equivalent vessel but with three 35,000-gallon double-wall units. This configuration reduced individual vessel size to within prefabricated tank availability, simplified secondary containment geometry, and enabled a phased shutdown sequence that maintained boiler fuel supply during transition.

**Secondary containment sizing at a constrained site.** MDE regulations required the containment pad to hold the full volume of the largest single tank plus a storm volume surcharge. The site is space-constrained within an active medical center campus, requiring careful geometric coordination between the containment pad, the three-tank arrangement, blast walls, and access for fuel delivery.

**PSRDM blast analysis and CMU screen walls.** VA physical security requirements obligated a PSRDM standoff analysis for the tank farm relative to occupied buildings. The resolution — 12-inch CMU blast mitigation screen walls on the north and west elevations — was developed to achieve required standoff equivalence within the available site footprint, a structural design challenge embedded in what would otherwise be a straightforward civil project.

**CUI design narrative from prior phase.** A design narrative from the 2018 predecessor contract was marked Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI). The blast analysis calculations and standoff distance data in that document informed the current project's structural scope while requiring appropriate handling protocols for sensitive physical security information.

**Environmental baseline at an active fuel site.** The existing tank site presented potential soil contamination and lead paint conditions requiring industrial hygiene assessment and a disposal plan before and during demolition — a regulatory prerequisite for MDE permit compliance and construction contractor bid packaging.

## Outcomes

Design services were delivered within the 120-calendar-day performance period. The completed design produced a construction package for a three-tank double-wall fuel storage system with full secondary containment, blast-compliant screen walls, integrated leak detection, and an updated site fuel distribution network serving the Perry Point boiler plant's four boilers. The construction budget was $800,000. Final construction contract value and completion date are not captured in the available project record.
