## Overview

Riverport Welding is a private industrial welding facility located in Newport News, Virginia (project no. 24-053). The facility operates as an industrial manufacturing environment with high-density welding machine loads, requiring a purpose-designed electrical power distribution system scaled to serve concentrated, high-ampacity welding operations. The design firm served as engineer of record for the electrical design, with construction documents sealed by a Virginia-licensed professional engineer (PE no. 0998004).

## Scope

The scope of work was electrical engineering, focused on the facility's power distribution from the utility interface through to individual welding machine circuit positions. No mechanical, plumbing, fire protection, architectural, or civil scope is reflected in the available documentation.

The electrical scope encompassed:

- **Utility interface and transformation.** Tap from an existing private 3.4/6kV, three-phase, four-wire overhead utility through three 100-ampere fused cut-outs (fused at 20A) and three 6kV surge arresters, feeding three pole-mounted 167 kVA step-down transformers (3,463/6,000–120/240V, 2.8% impedance; 501 kVA aggregate). The transformer bank converts the medium-voltage private utility supply to 120/208V for building distribution.
- **Service entrance.** 1,200-ampere, 120/208V, three-phase, four-wire service entrance with an exterior-mounted NEMA 3R main service breaker (1,000A continuous, 100,000 AIC interrupting capacity).
- **Primary distribution.** Wire trough backbone distributing from the service entrance through two 200-ampere and two 600-ampere enclosed circuit breakers to downstream panelboards.
- **Welding machine panels.** Four 600-ampere, 120/208V, 30-circuit MLO panelboards dedicated to welding machine power. Panels E and F were new construction; Panels 1 and 2 were existing. Each panel provides fifteen two-pole, 60-ampere circuit positions, each with a dedicated feeder (3 no. 4 conductors and 1 no. 8 ground in 1-1/2-inch conduit) to individual welding machines.

The construction documents consist of a single electrical sheet (E-501, "Details — One Line Diagram"), a complete one-line diagram representing the full electrical distribution system from utility to welding machine circuit level.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Medium-voltage private utility interface.** The facility's electrical supply derives from a private 3.4/6kV overhead utility rather than a standard public utility service. This required a custom transformer bank design — three pole-mounted 167 kVA units with a 3,463/6,000V primary winding configuration — along with medium-voltage protective equipment appropriate to the supply voltage class: fused cut-outs (100A with 20A fuses) and 6kV surge arresters at each transformer tap point.

**High-ampacity welding machine load density.** Industrial welding operations impose high, intermittent current draws concentrated across many simultaneous circuits. The distribution design addresses this through dedicated 60-ampere, two-pole circuit positions per welding machine with individual feeder conductors per circuit, a 600-ampere MLO panelboard architecture that limits load concentration at each panel, and a 1,200-ampere service entrance sized for the aggregate demand across all welding machine positions.

**Fault current coordination.** The combination of a medium-voltage utility source and a 501 kVA transformer bank produces significant available fault current at the service entrance. The main service breaker is rated for 100,000 AIC, appropriate for the fault current exposure at this service voltage and transformer impedance.

**Integration of new and existing distribution.** Two new welding machine panels (E and F) were designed alongside two existing panels (1 and 2) at the same voltage and ampacity class, extending the facility's distribution capacity while maintaining a consistent panel architecture.

## Outcomes

The project delivered signed and sealed electrical construction documents for the Riverport Welding facility's power distribution system. The design provides up to 60 individual welding machine circuit positions across four dedicated 600-ampere panels, sourced from a 501 kVA transformer bank fed by the site's private medium-voltage utility. The 1,200-ampere service entrance accommodates the aggregate welding machine load demand with layered overcurrent and fault-current protection from the medium-voltage tap through the service entrance.
