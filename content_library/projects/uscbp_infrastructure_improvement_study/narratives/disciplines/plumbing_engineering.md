The plumbing engineering scope in the CBP Advanced Training Center Infrastructure Improvements Analysis addressed four utility systems: potable water, fire suppression, irrigation, and sanitary sewer — covering the full range of campus water and waste infrastructure across the 116-acre facility.

Potable water was supplied by Jefferson Utilities via an 8-inch water main, with a water softening treatment system located at the campus pump building and an average campus demand design of approximately 40,000 gallons per day. Fire suppression shared the 8-inch potable water main, with a minimum required fire flow of 500 gallons per minute. The irrigation system consisted of a 4-inch loop (partially installed at the time of the study), with the campus grey water system documented as non-functional — a gap flagged in the study's improvement recommendations. Sanitary sewer was collected and treated on-site at a 40,000-gallon-per-day wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) constructed during the Phase 5 campus expansion, served by a central pump station with a 45,000-GPD capacity and 8-inch gravity collection mains. The study developed two wastewater improvement options: expansion of the existing on-site WWTP (estimated at $177,620) and connection to an off-site WWTP (estimated at $219,415). Water treatment system improvements were estimated at $191,102. Recommended improvements across all four systems were incorporated into the phased capital program spanning FY2022 through FY2042.
