# U.S. CBP Advanced Training Center — Infrastructure Improvements Analysis

## Project Overview

The U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Advanced Training Center (ATC) in Harpers Ferry, West Virginia is a 116-acre federal campus supporting law enforcement training for CBP personnel — encompassing dormitories, a dining facility, administrative buildings, a tactical training center, an indoor and outdoor firearms range, a land border training facility, medical services, a lodge and conference center, and associated site infrastructure. As the campus neared the end of an earlier expansion phase (Phase 5), CBP identified the need for a comprehensive infrastructure improvements analysis to evaluate existing utility systems and develop a capital improvement roadmap aligned with the campus's long-range development program.

The Infrastructure Improvements Analysis (IIA) was performed under a contract held by IntegriWard, LLC, with GORDON Engineering and the design firm serving as the primary A/E subconsultants. The 95% submission of the IIA was delivered in May 2021, following a 180-day period of performance from Notice to Proceed. The study served as the technical foundation for CBP's planning and programming of capital infrastructure investments at the ATC campus through FY2042.

## Scope of Work

The IIA evaluated six major campus utility systems against the 25-year build-out program defined in CBP's 2017 Area Development Execution Plan (ADEP):

**Potable Water.** The campus received water supply from Jefferson Utilities via an 8-inch main, processed through a water softening system at the campus pump building. Average daily demand was approximately 40,000 gallons per day. The study assessed distribution capacity and recommended improvements to support projected facility additions.

**Fire Suppression.** Fire protection shared the 8-inch potable water infrastructure, with a minimum required fire flow of 500 gallons per minute. System adequacy was evaluated against the campus expansion load.

**Irrigation.** The campus included a 4-inch irrigation loop, partially installed at the time of the study. The grey water reclamation system was documented as non-functional, and the study addressed restoration and completion of the irrigation infrastructure.

**Sanitary Sewer.** Campus sanitary sewer was collected by an 8-inch gravity system and treated on-site at a 40,000-gallon-per-day (GPD) wastewater treatment plant (WWTP), originally constructed as part of the Phase 5 expansion. A central pump station provided 45,000 GPD capacity. The study developed two future WWTP options: expansion of the on-site plant ($177,620) or connection to an off-site WWTP ($219,415).

**Electrical Distribution.** The campus received a 12.5kV, 3-phase overhead electrical service from Potomac Edison (First Energy), entering via a circuit paralleling US Route 340 along the northern campus boundary. A critical finding was that this circuit was at or near its rated capacity — a constraint that materially limited the campus's ability to absorb additional load from planned new facilities. The existing underground distribution infrastructure comprised more than 63 power manholes and handholes documented across the campus, connected by an extensive ductbank network.

**Communications.** Campus communications were served by Comcast (coaxial cable and fiber) and Frontier/Verizon (copper, analog telephone). Potomac Hall served as the primary campus communications distribution hub. The underground communications infrastructure included a network of manholes (minimum 12' L × 8' W × 8' H) and handholes connecting all major campus buildings.

The study identified 26 infrastructure-affecting capital projects — re-prioritized from the original 28 projects in the 2017 ADEP to reflect current CBP facility priorities — scheduled across five fiscal year windows: FY2022, FY2027, FY2032, FY2037, and FY2042. Total projected construction cost for the improvement program was $4,502,466 (design-bid-build basis, Davis-Bacon wages). Water treatment system improvements were separately estimated at $191,102.

## Role on Project

The design firm served as subconsultant to GORDON Engineering, which held a subconsultant agreement under IntegriWard, LLC's prime contract with CBP. The firm's documented scope on the IIA covered electrical power distribution and communications systems analysis, including field verification of the campus underground ductbank infrastructure, assessment of the existing electrical service capacity constraints, and development of phased electrical and communications improvement recommendations. The firm is identified on the IIA report cover as a named preparer alongside GORDON, and firm representative Ian Peralta presented the electrical and communications systems overview at the 50% submission review. The final deliverable package included a seven-sheet electrical and communications drawing set — electrical site plans (ES-101, ES-102) and manhole/handhole detail sheets (E-501 through E-505) — prepared by the firm in conjunction with GORDON.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Electrical Capacity Constraint.** The single most significant infrastructure finding was that the existing 12.5kV Potomac Edison circuit serving the campus was at or near its rated capacity. With 26 additional facility projects planned over the 25-year horizon, electrical service capacity was a binding constraint on the campus's growth path. The study quantified the shortfall against the projected demand profile and incorporated electrical service upgrade projects into the earliest fiscal year windows (FY2022) to unblock near-term campus development.

**Integrating Infrastructure Analysis Across All Utility Systems.** The IIA required simultaneous assessment of six infrastructure systems across a complex 116-acre campus with mixed-vintage construction and a partially complete Phase 5 expansion. Correlating existing conditions across potable water, fire suppression, irrigation, sanitary sewer, electrical, and communications — each served by different providers and agencies — into a single coherent capital improvement program required careful sequencing and prioritization. The phased approach, structured around ADEP fiscal year windows, provided the organizing framework.

**ADEP Re-Prioritization.** The 2017 ADEP contained 28 candidate projects. The IIA analysis determined that several of these had no infrastructure improvement impact under current CBP priorities and were excluded from the capital cost program. The remaining 26 projects were re-numbered to reflect the updated CBP priority order, ensuring the cost estimate and improvement schedule aligned with the client's current planning intent rather than the original ADEP sequence.

## Outcomes

The IIA delivered a complete existing-conditions assessment of all six campus utility systems, a phased infrastructure improvement program for 26 capital projects, a detailed cost estimate totaling approximately $4,502,466 in projected construction costs across FY2022–FY2042, two wastewater treatment alternatives with comparative cost data, and a seven-sheet electrical and communications infrastructure documentation drawing set. The 95% submission was delivered in May 2021. The study provided CBP with the technical basis for programming infrastructure investments to support the full 25-year ATC campus build-out, with immediate-term actions in the FY2022 window addressing the critical electrical capacity constraint.
