Electrical engineering was the primary technical discipline on this project, encompassing complete design of two diesel emergency standby generators and their integration into the existing electrical service infrastructure at the Southwest Virginia RRC campus in Abingdon, Virginia.

Generator 1, rated at 500kW, provides full-building backup coverage for the main building and storage building. The supporting electrical design includes a 500kVA transformer, a 3,000A automatic transfer switch (ATS) and circuit breaker, and underground electrical ductbank and conduit routing to the existing main distribution panel. Generator 2, rated at 150kW, serves the administration building through a 112.5kVA transformer and 400A ATS and circuit breaker with a matching ductbank connection.

Both generators are configured with integral diesel sub-base fuel tanks sized for a minimum 72-hour runtime at full load, and each installation includes a BAS (Building Automation System) tie-in for monitoring and operational integration. Fuel type analysis was conducted for both units; diesel was selected as the basis of design over natural gas based on significant cost differentials — particularly for Generator 2, where the natural gas alternative carried a greater than three-to-one capital cost premium over the diesel configuration.

Outline electrical specifications and equipment selection documentation were produced as part of the 100% Working Drawing Submittal Basis of Design in December 2022. The electrical scope also coordinated with structural elements (concrete pads, maintenance platforms) and civil elements (underground ductbank routing and site utilities) to deliver a fully integrated generator installation design.
