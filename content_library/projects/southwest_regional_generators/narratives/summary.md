The Southwest Virginia RRC Modernization – Construction Back-Up Generators project delivered electrical engineering design for two diesel emergency standby generators at the Southwest Virginia Readiness and Reserve Center (RRC) in Abingdon, Virginia. The facility is owned and operated by the Virginia Department of Military Affairs (Virginia Army National Guard) and consists of three campus buildings totaling approximately 92,058 GSF. The design team served as subconsultant to RRMM Architects (prime A/E) under Virginia DMA Project No. VAFM PN 22-0028.

Generator 1 (500kW) provides full-building backup power for the main building (~72,862 GSF) and storage building (~4,400 GSF) through a 500kVA transformer and 3,000A automatic transfer switch. Generator 2 (150kW) provides coverage for the administration building (~14,796 GSF) through a 112.5kVA transformer and 400A automatic transfer switch. Both generators are diesel-fueled with integral sub-base tanks sized for 72-hour runtime. Supporting design elements include concrete equipment pads, galvanized steel maintenance platforms with stairs and railings, BAS tie-in, and underground electrical ductbank and conduit connecting each generator to the existing electrical service infrastructure. Fuel type analysis confirmed diesel as the basis of design, yielding significant cost savings over natural gas alternatives — particularly for Generator 2, where the natural gas option carried more than a three-to-one cost premium.

Design was completed through the 100% Working Drawing Submittal in December 2022 under a 90-day design schedule. The project was procured through Virginia's qualifications-based selection process and delivered via design-bid-build.
