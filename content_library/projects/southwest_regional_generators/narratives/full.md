# Southwest Virginia RRC Modernization – Construction Back-Up Generators

## Project Overview

The Southwest Virginia RRC Modernization – Construction Back-Up Generators project provided electrical engineering design services for two diesel emergency standby generators at the Southwest Virginia Readiness and Reserve Center (RRC) in Abingdon, Virginia. The facility is owned and operated by the Virginia Department of Military Affairs (Virginia Army National Guard) and consists of three structures on two adjacent parcels at 26460 and 26502 Newbanks Road: a main building of approximately 72,862 GSF, a storage building of approximately 4,400 GSF, and an administration building of approximately 14,796 GSF — totaling approximately 92,058 GSF of served facility space.

The project was executed under Virginia DMA Project No. VAFM PN 22-0028. The design team served as subconsultant to RRMM Architects (prime A/E, RRMM Project No. 20188-03), contributing electrical engineering design services and equipment selection (BFA Project No. 19-020A). The project was procured through Virginia's qualifications-based selection (QBS) process and structured for design-bid-build construction delivery.

## Scope of Services

The project scope centered on complete electrical engineering design for two diesel generators sized to provide total-building backup power coverage for all three campus structures.

**Generator 1 (500kW)** was sized and specified to serve the main building and storage building at full load. Supporting electrical infrastructure includes a 500kVA transformer, a 3,000A automatic transfer switch (ATS) and circuit breaker, and an underground electrical ductbank with conduit runs connecting the generator to the existing main distribution panel. An integral diesel sub-base fuel tank was sized for a minimum 72-hour runtime. Ancillary elements include a concrete equipment pad, a galvanized steel maintenance platform with stairs and railings for code-compliant access, and BAS (Building Automation System) tie-in for monitoring and operational integration.

**Generator 2 (150kW)** was sized to serve the administration building at full load. Supporting electrical infrastructure includes a 112.5kVA transformer, a 400A automatic transfer switch and circuit breaker, and an underground ductbank and conduit connection to the existing electrical service. The same ancillary elements apply: diesel sub-base tank (72-hour runtime), concrete equipment pad, galvanized steel maintenance platform with stairs and railings, and BAS tie-in.

Outline specifications covering electrical, concrete, metals, earthwork, and utilities construction scopes were prepared as part of the Basis of Design. A 100%-design cost estimate was produced by OACCI (Osmundson & Associates Cost Consultants, Inc.) using RS Means 2022 pricing, with the project structured for competitive lump-sum bid by at least four contractors.

## Role on Project

The design team functioned as a subconsultant to RRMM Architects, providing electrical engineering and equipment selection services for the generator installations. RRMM Architects held the prime A/E contract with the Virginia Department of Military Affairs and was responsible for overall project coordination and BOD authorship. The design team's scope was focused and equipment-centric: selecting generator equipment, specifying the full supporting electrical and ancillary infrastructure, and producing documents through the 100% working drawing submittal stage.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Budget pressure.** The RFP established a not-to-exceed construction budget of $750,000. The independent 100%-design cost estimate (OACCI, November 30, 2022) projected a total estimated construction cost of approximately $1,029,334 — approximately $279,334 above the owner's stated NTE budget, a variance of roughly 37%. The NTE figure was set at the program stage in July 2022; the estimate reflects the full cost of the specified 100%-design installation. How the project team resolved this gap — through value engineering, scope adjustment, or additional funding authorization — is not documented in the available source records. This budget alignment challenge is a relevant past-performance data point for projects where program budgets are established before detailed engineering is complete.

**Fuel type optimization.** The BOD evaluated diesel and natural gas fuel configurations for each generator. Natural gas alternatives carried a significantly higher capital cost: approximately $617,500 versus $537,000 for Generator 1 (a ~15% premium), and approximately $407,500 versus $128,000 for Generator 2 (a greater than three-to-one premium). Diesel was selected as the basis of design for both generators, providing substantial cost savings while meeting the 72-hour runtime operational requirement for an active National Guard readiness center.

**Compressed design schedule.** The RFP specified a 90-day design period from notice to proceed, targeting design completion by November 18, 2022. The 100% Working Drawing Submittal 1 was dated December 1, 2022 — approximately two weeks past the stated target. The minor schedule variance is consistent with the technical coordination required for two generator installations at an active military facility, including underground utility conflict assessment and integration with existing electrical service infrastructure.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a complete Basis of Design — including outline specifications, equipment cut sheets, and an independent 100%-design cost estimate — for two diesel backup generators providing total-facility emergency power coverage at an active Virginia Army National Guard readiness center. Design documents were completed through the 100% Working Drawing Submittal in December 2022. Construction start and completion dates are not documented in the available project records; given the project's age and the completion of the 100%-design submittal, the installation is presumed to be complete. The design provides the Southwest Virginia RRC with continuous backup power capability across all three campus buildings, ensuring mission readiness continuity for National Guard operations in the region.
