Fire protection engineering scope on this project is the primary technical discipline, centered on full replacement of an end-of-life Halon 1301 suppression system in the basement server room (Room 003, approximately 1,817 SF raised-floor area) of Building D323 at Naval Support Facility Indian Head. The existing system — 1982-vintage Kidde KPH-60 cylinders and a Fire Lite Sensiscan 1000 control panel — is demolished and replaced with a pre-engineered NFPA 2001-compliant clean agent suppression system. P.E.-stamped conceptual design calculations (Jemmel A. Pursoo, PE, May 2025) specify approximately 39 total cylinders for inert gas or Novec 1230 protection at 30% design concentration, covering both the under-raised-floor plenum and the above-floor room volume of the combined 1,980 SF scope area.

Room integrity is a key deliverable: a fan pressurization leak test per NFPA 2001 is required prior to system acceptance, and a pressure relief vent is designed at the wall between Corridor H002 and Vestibule V002 for a 0.06 atm / 136 psf differential. A pre-award enclosure integrity test by Harris Fire Protection (May 2025) established a reference hold time of 12.13 minutes at the 7-ft design height for Novec 1230 at 4.5% concentration.

Fire detection scope includes a new Fire Alarm Releasing Control Panel with Very Early Warning Smoke Detection under the raised floor, above-floor detectors, EPO switch supervision, CRAC unit relay interfaces, connection to the building main FACP (System 3, first-floor lobby), and a radio transmitter link to the Emergency Operations Center in Building D327 via AES radio mesh network. Halon cylinder disposition follows DoD ozone-depleting substance procedures via DLA Distribution Richmond.
