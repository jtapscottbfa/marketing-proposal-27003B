# Melwood Splashpad

## Project Overview

The Melwood Splashpad (BFA project 24-002) is an outdoor interactive water feature at Melwood's facility in Nanjemoy, Maryland, developed as Phase II of a two-phase aquatic facility expansion. Phase I addressed the electrical systems of the existing in-ground pool complex; Phase II introduced a new splash pad — a ground-level interactive water feature — east of the existing facility, along with the pool recirculation and water treatment equipment required to operate it. The project is a recreational aquatic program for a private institutional client, delivered via design-bid-build under a standalone project contract.

The design was completed at 100% on February 5, 2024, producing a four-sheet electrical construction drawing set. Construction status is not captured in available source records.

## Scope of Work

The electrical engineering scope encompassed two work packages: new power distribution and equipment connections within the existing pool equipment building to serve the Phase II splash pad features, and site electrical routing from the equipment building to the splash pad area.

**Pool equipment building.** The equipment building houses four programmatic rooms — a Chemical Room, Filter Room, Storage and Guard Room, and Pump Pit. New Phase II loads added to Panelboard 'A' include:

- Feature Filtration Pump: 480V, 3Ø, 7.5HP, 9.7A
- Feature Pump: 208V, 3Ø, 10HP, 28.7A
- Feature Timer Panel: 120V, serving the splash pad's feature activation control
- UV Sterilizer and Ozone Feeder: 120V
- Motor starters for pool pumps
- CO2 Controller and Chemical Controller: 120V (existing to remain)

The drawings also address demolition scope: removal of all corroded electrical distribution equipment, retention of feeders and circuits for reuse, and replacement of corroded equipment enclosures with 304 stainless steel throughout.

**Site electrical.** A 1-inch empty conduit was routed from the equipment building to the splash pad area, providing the pathway for the feature bollard's power and control wiring. Final connection requirements for the feature bollard were designated for electrical contractor coordination with the manufacturer.

**Existing infrastructure retained.** The 750 kVA pad-mounted utility transformer and the 208/120V, 3Ø service disconnect were retained. Panelboard 'A' (100A MCB, 208/120V, 3Ø, 4-wire, surface-mounted Cutler-Hammer POW-R-LINE) was retained and reused, with Phase II loads connected to available circuits.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant and engineer of record for the electrical scope. The drawing set was produced by Bowman Foster & Associates, Inc., with AJR as designer/engineer of record and MED as checker. The scope covers electrical engineering only; no architectural, structural, civil, mechanical, or plumbing drawings for the splash pad facility appear in the available source record, indicating separate design consultants or owner-furnished documents for those scopes.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Aquatic environment durability.** Pool equipment rooms present a harsh environment for electrical enclosures due to chemical off-gassing (chlorine, CO2, ozone) and sustained humidity. The design addressed this by specifying 304 stainless steel enclosures for all new equipment and directing replacement of corroded existing equipment with LMI or PVC materials where practical. This approach extends service life and reduces maintenance exposure in a chemically aggressive space.

**Integration with existing service infrastructure.** Phase II work had to integrate with an existing electrical service — 750 kVA transformer, 208/120V service disconnect, and Panelboard 'A' — without requiring a service upgrade. New loads were designed to fit within available panel capacity, with new circuits added for Phase II equipment while existing circuits for the original pool equipment were retained and reused.

**Deferred feature bollard specification.** The site plan directs the electrical contractor to coordinate final power and control connection requirements for the feature bollard with the manufacturer after bid. The design provided a 1-inch empty conduit to the field and a coordination note. This is a standard approach for proprietary water feature elements whose final specifications are not resolved at the 100% design milestone.

## Outcomes

The project produced a complete 100% electrical construction drawing set for the Melwood Splashpad Phase II, issued February 2024. The documents cover new water feature power distribution, pool equipment room electrical modifications, demolition of corroded equipment, and site conduit routing to the splash pad. Contract value and construction schedule data are not available in the source record.
