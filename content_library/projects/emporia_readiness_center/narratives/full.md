## Project Overview

The Emporia Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades was a Virginia Department of Military Affairs (DMA) project for complete HVAC system replacement at the Emporia Readiness Center, located at 410 Courtland Road, Emporia, Virginia. The facility is a one-story brick readiness center in Greensville County, serving the Virginia National Guard. The project was initiated by a Request for Fee Proposal (State PN 123-18578-020 / VAFM PN 22-0023) issued by DMA's NGVA-FMO-AE office on May 5, 2022.

The project was driven by a distinctive design mandate from the outset. DMA's commander's intent, transmitted with the RFP, explicitly framed the effort not as a response to a single pathogen but as the creation of "flexible and reconfigurable capabilities" to address the broadest possible range of future biological threats. The intent rejected the risk of "planning to fight the last war" — acknowledging that the humidity level optimal for mitigating COVID-19 spread might be different from what the next pathogen requires, and that every controllable HVAC parameter should be maximized for adaptability. This framing shaped the scope, the system selection, and the BAS configuration throughout design.

## Scope

The project encompassed complete replacement of the readiness center's HVAC infrastructure under a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,407,000, delivered via design-bid-build with a 210-day contractual design schedule from Notice to Proceed.

**Mechanical systems** formed the primary scope:

- Complete removal and replacement of all HVAC units, evaluated in accordance with CPSM life-cycle cost analysis
- Analysis of existing ductwork, followed by removal and replacement or extensive cleaning as field conditions warranted
- Analysis of existing piping, followed by removal and replacement or extensive cleaning as field conditions warranted
- Installation of a pathogen mitigation technology array: bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV lighting, visible lighting, and cold fusion technology
- Building Automation System (BAS) upgrade with humidity and temperature control across a wide operational range, explicitly sized for pathogen adaptability rather than a fixed setpoint
- HVAC zoning to reduce potential migration of airborne particles between building zones

**Electrical** scope comprised minor associated electrical work as required to support the mechanical system replacement.

The A/E services scope extended beyond design production. It included comprehensive investigative services to characterize existing conditions — floor plan dimensions, fire wall locations, available areas for wall penetrations, and suitable locations for new units. Soil borings and geotechnical investigation were required as needed. The A/E was responsible for detailed cost estimates at each design submission phase, DEB working-drawing submittal and correction, and full construction administration services. DMA specified a bidding window of approximately three to four months following final design approval, with bidding documents to be provided in electronic form and as full- and half-size hard copy sets.

## Role on Project

The A/E firm served as prime consultant for full design services, encompassing mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, investigative services, and construction administration. Coordination with multiple state and local regulatory agencies was required — DEQ, DEB, SFMO, and any other applicable reviewing authorities — for all required reviews, approvals, and associated fees. The A/E was responsible for all review fees except those incurred through DEB and SFMO, and was required to resolve DEB comments at the working-drawing phase; fees for any reviews beyond the second round were to be deducted from the A/E's design fee.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Pathogen-adaptive design mandate.** The project's defining challenge was translating DMA's commander's intent — broad, future-oriented pathogen adaptability — into a specific HVAC system and controls configuration. Rather than designing for a single pathogen's optimal conditions, the approach layered multiple mitigation technologies (bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV lighting, visible lighting, cold fusion) with a BAS programmed for wide-range humidity and temperature control. HVAC zoning provided an additional layer of containment by limiting cross-zone particle migration. The design philosophy prioritized controllability and operational range over optimization for any single scenario.

**Existing-conditions investigation.** With limited available as-built documentation at the time of RFP (the primary reference HVAC drawing for Building 9-4 was transmitted via DoD SAFE separately and a copy in the project file is corrupt and unreadable), a comprehensive investigative phase was incorporated into the A/E scope before design could proceed. This investigation was intended to establish floor plan geometry, fire wall locations, penetration feasibility, and viable unit placements from first principles.

## Outcomes

Source documents capture the project at the RFP and pre-award stage. Design was engaged at a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,407,000 with a 210-day design timeline under design-bid-build delivery. Specific design contract award value, final construction cost, and project completion date are not captured in the available project record.
