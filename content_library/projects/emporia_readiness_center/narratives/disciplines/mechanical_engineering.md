Mechanical engineering was the primary design discipline on the Emporia Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades, a complete HVAC system replacement for a Virginia National Guard readiness center in Emporia, Virginia, with a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,407,000.

The mechanical scope encompassed removal and replacement of all HVAC units evaluated through CPSM life-cycle cost analysis, analysis of existing ductwork and piping with replacement or extensive cleaning based on field conditions, and a comprehensive BAS upgrade providing wide-range humidity and temperature control. The BAS configuration was a deliberate design choice driven by DMA's commander's intent: rather than targeting a single pathogen-optimal setpoint, the system was designed to operate across a broad range of humidity and temperature conditions to remain effective against future, unknown pathogens. HVAC zoning was incorporated to limit cross-zone migration of airborne particles.

A layered pathogen mitigation array was installed alongside the replacement mechanical systems: bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, UV lighting, visible lighting, and cold fusion technology. The combination of active and passive technologies was intended to provide the widest possible array of mitigation measures.

Prior to design, the mechanical scope included comprehensive investigative services to characterize existing conditions — floor plan dimensions, fire wall locations, feasible wall penetration areas, and suitable unit placement locations. The project was delivered under a design-bid-build method with a 210-day contractual design schedule from Notice to Proceed, including full construction administration services.
