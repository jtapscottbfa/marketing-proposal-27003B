# ERC DTT Norfolk East Vent Building — Emergency Generator Replacement

## Project Overview

The ERC Downtown Tunnel (DTT) Norfolk East Vent Building Emergency Generator Replacement (BFA Project No. 20-014A, BCOM Project Code 501-B0501-014) is an engineering design project for Elizabeth River Crossings (ERC), the private concessionaire operating the Downtown Tunnel and Midtown Tunnel under Norfolk Harbor. The East Vent Building, located at 114 Ligon St, Norfolk, Virginia, houses the mechanical and electrical systems that ventilate the twin bores of the Downtown Tunnel. An existing 400 kW emergency generator inside the building's first floor had reached the end of its serviceable life and was undersized relative to current and projected tunnel ventilation loads. The project objective was to design a code-compliant replacement generator system of substantially greater capacity, sited and elevated to withstand the site's significant flood hazard.

The design scope encompasses three integrated disciplines — electrical, structural, and civil — and progressed through a multi-round review by the Virginia Department of General Services / Division of Engineering and Buildings (DGS/DEB), reaching REFINAL submittal status in March 2026.

## Scope

**Electrical.** The core electrical scope replaces the existing 400 kW generator with a new Caterpillar 3512C diesel-engine generator set rated at 1500 ekW / 1875 kVA (480Y/277V, 3-phase, 60 Hz, standby). The generator includes a sub-base diesel fuel tank sized for 48 hours of continuous operation at 100% load. New distribution infrastructure comprises a 4000A main switchboard, an emergency distribution panel (EMDP) inside the building, and two ASCO Series 300 automatic transfer switches — one 800A/4P unit and one 400A/4P unit — providing automatic and manual load-transfer capability to tunnel loads. The design redistributes emergency power to eight 100 HP tunnel jet fans (four per eastbound bore, four per westbound bore), each served by an ATS with bypass-isolation capability for maintenance access. New EMDP panels also feed the existing EMDP panel arrangement, preserving compatibility with the existing distribution architecture. The existing 400 kW generators are disconnected but remain in place.

**Structural.** Supporting a 115,000 lb generator set at an elevation of +15.30 ft — dictated by floodplain requirements — required a purpose-designed elevated support structure and deep foundation. The structural solution is a steel support frame composed of W8 wide-flange columns and beams, HSS 4×4 columns, and C12×20.7 channel stringers, stabilized by X-bracing in two bays. The frame bears on a 2-ft thick reinforced concrete mat foundation approximately 32 ft × 12.5 ft in plan (~400 sf), with the bottom of the foundation at -12.0 ft below existing grade. A galvanized steel bar-grating stair and landing with code-compliant handrails provides access to the elevated generator platform. Seismic restraint vibration isolators are specified for generator mounting. Design codes: ASCE 7-2022, ACI 318-22, AISC 360-2016; seismic site class D. The structural engineer of record was Don Broyles, P.E. of Broyles and Associates, PC, serving as subconsultant.

**Civil.** The project site is located in FEMA Flood Zone AE with a base flood elevation of 8.0 ft and is additionally designated as a sea-level rise inundation area under the City of Norfolk Flood Plain and Coastal Hazard Overlay (FPCH-O), which establishes a required design flood elevation of 12.0 ft. The civil scope includes site planning for the new outdoor generator enclosure adjacent to the existing vent building, routing of new underground electrical conduit, and coordination of a topographic site survey by Hoggard/Eure Associates. Floodplain compliance documentation was prepared, submitted, and approved by the City of Norfolk Floodplain Administrator prior to final submittal.

## Role on Project

The design firm served as prime consultant, holding the design contract with ERC and directing a team of subconsultants: Broyles and Associates, PC for structural engineering, and Hoggard/Eure Associates for civil/survey. The prime role encompassed electrical engineering design, civil engineering, code compliance documentation, and design-review coordination through Virginia DGS/DEB. Scope discussions with ERC began in May 2020; the design progressed to REFINAL submittal by March 2026.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Flood Zone AE siting.** The most significant design constraint was the site's flood exposure. With a FEMA BFE of 8.0 ft and a City of Norfolk required design flood elevation of 12.0 ft — on a site also mapped as a sea-level rise inundation zone — placing a 115,000 lb generator and its sub-base fuel tank at or near grade was not feasible. The design response was to elevate the generator floor to +15.30 ft via the custom steel support frame, providing 7.3 ft of freeboard above BFE and 3.3 ft above the FPCH-O design flood elevation. The City of Norfolk Floodplain Administrator issued a compliance letter (Tristian Barnes, March 5, 2026) confirming the project meets Section 3.9.7 FPCH-O requirements.

**Multi-round DGS/DEB review.** The project was submitted through Virginia's DGS/DEB review process — unusual for a private-owner project but applicable given the public-private partnership concession context. Review comments across multiple cycles addressed building data tabulation, structural observation requirements, floodplain permitting, CO-6b special inspection documentation, and stair handrail compliance under the 2021 VCC. All comments were resolved by the REFINAL submittal date of March 5, 2026. The stair design was revised per a direct conversation with DEB architectural reviewer Bill Pisa on that same date, reflecting real-time resolution of the final outstanding item.

**Capacity step-change and load compatibility.** Replacing a 400 kW generator with a 1500 ekW unit — a near-4× capacity increase — required full replacement of the emergency distribution architecture, including the main switchboard and transfer switches, rather than a simple generator swap. The new generator's operating weight (115,000 lbs) made a new engineered foundation and elevated support structure necessary; the existing building could not support the unit at grade or on the existing floor slab.

**Code update mid-design.** The design originated in 2020 and incorporated a code-base update to the 2021 Virginia Construction Code (VCC) during the design period, requiring rechecks of life-safety details including stair handrails and structural observation requirements.

## Outcomes

The design reached REFINAL submittal status in March 2026 with DGS/DEB review complete and floodplain compliance confirmed by the City of Norfolk. The full submittal package includes a complete project manual (DGS general conditions, Division 1 through Division 26 specifications, geotechnical report, and equipment submittals for the generator and transfer switches) and a 12-sheet drawing set covering general, civil, structural, and electrical disciplines. The project was issued for bid upon completion of the review cycle; construction had not yet begun at the time of the final submittal. The project demonstrates experience in heavy electrical infrastructure design for transportation tunnel operations, flood-hazard-constrained site design, and coordination of public-agency review processes on behalf of private infrastructure operators under public-private partnership concession agreements.
