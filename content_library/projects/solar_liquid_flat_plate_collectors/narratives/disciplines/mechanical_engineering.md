Mechanical engineering provided the core technical discipline expertise underlying this federal criteria development engagement for NAVFAC. The project's subject matter — solar liquid flat plate and evacuated tube collector systems for solar water heating — is fundamentally a mechanical engineering domain, encompassing thermal collection, heat transfer, fluid handling, and system performance for active solar thermal installations in federal facilities.

The mechanical engineering scope on this project was exercised through criteria writing rather than project-specific design: the discipline contributed technical knowledge of solar thermal system types, operating parameters, component requirements, applicable performance standards, and integration with building mechanical systems. This knowledge underpinned the consolidation of two existing UFGS sections into a single, technically current specification governing solar water heating equipment across Department of Defense facilities.

The merged specification addressed current technologies for both flat plate and evacuated tube solar collector types — two distinct thermal collection approaches with different performance characteristics, applicable standards, and installation requirements. Ensuring the consolidated UFGS accurately reflected current industry standards, energy and performance requirements, and applicable HVAC and thermal system criteria for each system type was a central mechanical engineering contribution. The building systems taxonomy for this project identifies HVAC systems as within scope, reflecting the thermal and mechanical systems integration dimension of solar water heating criteria.

Mechanical engineering participated in a five-phase iterative submittal process, with technical review by Tri-Service agencies (NAVFAC, USACE, AFCEC, USMC) and NASA at each phase.
