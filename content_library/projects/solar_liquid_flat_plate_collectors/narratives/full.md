# UFGS 48 14 13.00 20: Solar Liquid Flat Plate and Evacuated Tube Collectors
## Federal Criteria Development — NAVFAC Atlantic

### Project Overview

This engagement was a federal A-E professional services project for the Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command (NAVFAC), executed under IDIQ A-E contract N6247019D5013. The project's deliverable was a technical document — a revised and merged Unified Facilities Guide Specification (UFGS) — rather than a designed or constructed facility. The UFGS is the primary vehicle through which the Department of Defense communicates design and construction requirements to facility designers and contractors across the military services; developing and maintaining UFGS criteria is a specialized federal A-E service distinct from project-specific design work.

The specific task was to merge two existing UFGS sections — UFGS 48 14 13.00 20 (Solar Liquid Flat Plate and Evacuated Tube Collectors) and UFGS 22 33 30.00 10 (Solar Water Heating Equipment) — into a single consolidated specification section, while updating its technical content to reflect current technologies, current industry standards, and current energy and performance requirements applicable to solar thermal systems in federal facilities.

### Scope of Work

The scope of work, as defined in the Statement of A-E Services, encompassed the following elements:

- **Specification merge:** Consolidate UFGS 48 14 13.00 20 and UFGS 22 33 30.00 10 into a single UFGS section covering solar water heating equipment, resolving any conflicts or redundancies between the two source documents.
- **Content update:** Update the merged specification to incorporate current technologies, applicable industry standards, and current energy and performance requirements for solar liquid flat plate and evacuated tube collector systems.
- **Comparison and coordination matrix:** Develop a matrix comparing and coordinating content between the two source specifications (referenced in the SOW as the "Comparison/Coordination Matrix Example" per Attachment B).
- **CCR resolution:** Review and resolve outstanding Criteria Change Requests (CCRs) from the Criteria Management System (CMS), which had accumulated against the source specifications.
- **Option 1 (base contract option):** Merge UFGS 23 03 00.00 20 (Basic Mechanical Material and Methods) into the consolidated document as a further integration task.
- **Deliverable formats:** Produce the final UFGS in Word, SpecsIntact (.sec), and PDF formats. The specification is intended to serve as a design and construction document for federal facility designers and contractors, and must include applicable requirements for accessibility, antiterrorism, security, sustainability, corrosion protection and control, and safety.

The project was structured as a five-phase iterative submittal process, with formal review milestones at Kickoff Meeting, 35% Concept Outline Submittal, 65% Progress Submittal, Pre-Final Draft Submittal, and Final Submittal. Each phase incorporated technical review and coordination feedback from all reviewing agencies.

### Role on Project

The firm served as prime consultant under the NAVFAC IDIQ A-E contract, holding the prime contractual relationship with NAVFAC and leading all aspects of criteria development, specification drafting, CCR resolution, and multi-service review coordination. Disciplines engaged on the project included mechanical engineering, plumbing engineering, fire protection engineering, and specifications writing — reflecting the multi-system technical scope of solar water heating equipment criteria, which spans thermal collector systems, domestic hot water integration, and safety and suppression considerations as applicable.

### Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Multi-service review coordination.** The UFGS program is a Tri-Service and inter-agency effort: formal technical reviewers on this project included NAVFAC, USACE (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers), AFCEC (Air Force Civil Engineer Center), USMC, and NASA. Coordinating iterative review cycles across five agencies — each with potentially distinct installation priorities, existing practices, and technical positions — required disciplined submittal management and clear disposition of reviewer comments at each phase. The five-phase submittal structure in the SOW was designed to manage this coordination incrementally.

**CCR backlog resolution.** The scope explicitly included review and resolution of outstanding Criteria Change Requests accumulated in the CMS system against the source specifications. CCRs represent documented unresolved technical issues, conflicts with current standards, or identified outdated requirements. Resolving CCRs prior to and during the merge was a prerequisite to producing a defensible, current consolidated specification.

**Technology currency for solar thermal systems.** Solar water heating technology — including both flat plate and evacuated tube collector types — has evolved significantly. Updating the merged specification to reflect current product types, applicable industry standards (SRCC, ASHRAE, applicable ASTM and ISO standards as applicable), and current energy performance requirements required substantive technical judgment in mechanical engineering and plumbing engineering disciplines.

### Outcomes

The project is complete. The deliverable was a consolidated UFGS section on Solar Water Heating Equipment (UFGS 22 33 30.00 10) incorporating the merged content of the two source specifications, updated technical requirements, resolved CCRs, and applicable coordination with UFGS 23 03 00.00 20 per Option 1 scope, produced in Word, SpecsIntact, and PDF formats for distribution and adoption across the NAVFAC, Army, Air Force, Marine Corps, and NASA facility programs. The completed specification provides design and construction guidance for federal solar water heating installations, addressing both flat plate and evacuated tube collector system types.
