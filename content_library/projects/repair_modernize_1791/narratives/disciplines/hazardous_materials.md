Hazardous materials scope for Building 1791 at Joint Base Andrews encompassed identification, characterization, and abatement specification for the full range of regulated materials present in the 23,773 SF facility prior to renovation. The building's 60-year industrial operating history produced a complex hazmat profile requiring investigation across multiple material categories.

Asbestos-containing materials (ACM) identified in the facility included residual window frame sealant, black mastic, window glazing putty, pitch pockets, and steam pit gaskets — materials consistent with construction and maintenance practices of the 1960s through 1990s. Lead-containing paint (LCP) was identified on interior CMU walls and structural steel elements, with concentrations of lead, cadmium, and chromium evaluated through laboratory analysis of paint chip samples. Mercury-containing fluorescent lamps were identified in the former x-ray room (T8 fixture type). PCB-containing fluorescent ballasts were identified or assumed present throughout the facility in fixtures not labeled "No PCBs." Two PCB-rated electrical transformers were identified on-site with confirmed PCB content below 1 ppm. The former x-ray room was found to have lead-lined walls and doors, requiring special handling for demolition and disposal.

Abatement work was organized across three specification sections: 02 82 00 (Asbestos Remediation), 02 83 00 (Lead Remediation), and 02 84 16 (Handling of Lighting Ballasts and Lamps Containing PCBs and Mercury). The hazardous materials assessment and abatement specification coordinated directly with the architectural demolition scope and construction phasing, as abatement clearance in each area was required before renovation construction could proceed. The investigation and specification work enabled the Design-Build contractor to manage regulatory compliance and worker protection requirements throughout the construction period.
