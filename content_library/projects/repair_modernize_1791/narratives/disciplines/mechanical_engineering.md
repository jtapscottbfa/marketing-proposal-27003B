Mechanical engineering scope for Building 1791 at Joint Base Andrews encompassed the complete removal and replacement of all HVAC systems in the 23,773 SF industrial and administrative facility. The replacement system was designed to address the building's demanding mix of occupancies — welding shop, paint booth, sanding and fabrication rooms, NDI operations, and general office areas — each presenting distinct ventilation and temperature control requirements.

Six air handling units were provided. AHU-1 serves the welding room as a 100% outside air unit with a chilled water cooler and gas heat, incorporating a dust collector with recirculation and an explosion-proof variable frequency drive to manage hazardous airborne contaminants generated by welding operations. AHUs 2 through 5 are constant-volume units with energy recovery wheels, chilled water coils, and gas heat, serving the general building zones. AHU-6 is a dedicated split system for the Communications and Electrical rooms. The paint booth is served by a purpose-designed AHU with VFDs on both supply and exhaust fans, configured to NFPA 33 requirements for spray finishing operations.

Chilled water is generated by two chillers: one serving the main building AHUs with a buffer tank, 30% glycol loop, and expansion tank, and a second dedicated to the paint booth AHU. Supplemental electric unit heaters provide heating in the Riser, Mechanical, Communications, and Electrical rooms. A central dust and fume collection system with six-inch point-of-use extractors and VFD serves the welding shop. A dedicated ventilation system for the Dirty/Sanding Room addresses airborne particulates from aluminum, steel, titanium, polyurethane paint, and epoxy primer operations. Design criteria were referenced to Andrews Air Force Base, Maryland weather data.
