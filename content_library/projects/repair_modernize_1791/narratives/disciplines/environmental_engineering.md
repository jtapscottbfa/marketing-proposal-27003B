Environmental engineering scope for Building 1791 at Joint Base Andrews consisted of a pre-construction hazardous materials investigation to characterize the building's existing conditions prior to renovation and to establish the abatement scope required for safe construction activities. The investigation was conducted as part of the design-build scope and informed the specification of remediation work packages incorporated into the construction documents.

The investigation methodology included: collection and laboratory analysis of bulk samples of suspect asbestos-containing materials (ACM); collection and analysis of paint samples to determine lead, cadmium, and chromium concentrations; visual inspection of a percentage of fluorescent light fixtures for the presence of PCB-containing ballasts and mercury-containing lamps; and a visual inspection for other mercury- and PCB-containing components throughout the building.

The environmental investigation identified regulated materials requiring remediation across multiple material categories, documented in three specification sections: 02 82 00 (Asbestos Remediation), 02 83 00 (Lead Remediation), and 02 84 16 (Handling of Lighting Ballasts and Lamps Containing PCBs and Mercury). The investigation findings informed the construction phasing and access sequencing for the renovation, as hazmat abatement in each zone was prerequisite to general construction work. The building's 60-year industrial history — including former x-ray operations and decades of painting and coating activities — produced a layered and varied hazmat profile that the environmental scope had to characterize with sufficient specificity to support contractor compliance. Specific abatement quantities, clearance air monitoring requirements, and disposal protocols are carried in the specification sections; those details are not captured in the available source record at the project data level.
