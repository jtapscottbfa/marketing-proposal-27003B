# Repair Modernize MXG Building 1791 — Joint Base Andrews, MD

## Project Overview

Building 1791 at Joint Base Andrews (JBA), Maryland is a single-story, 23,773 SF industrial and administrative facility for the Air Force Maintenance Group (MXG). Constructed in 1966 with a 1993 addition, the building supports a range of aircraft maintenance industrial operations including welding, fabrication, water jet cutting, lathe and grinding, non-destructive inspection (NDI/x-ray), paint booth work, penetrant inspection, JOAP lab, and associated office and conference functions. The facility is classified as mixed-use Group B (Business) and Group F-1 (Moderate Hazard Industrial) occupancy per IBC, with Type IIB construction and a maximum height of 36 feet.

The project was procured under Design-Build contract AJXF 16-1507 by the 316th Civil Engineering Squadron (316 CES/CENMP) at Joint Base Andrews. The contractual performance period was 465 calendar days following Notice to Proceed: 100 days for design completion and 365 days for construction.

## Scope of Work

The renovation encompassed all major building systems and disciplines across the full approximately 23,773 SF footprint.

**Architecture.** Architectural scope covered demolition of incomplete prior construction, new interior partitions, roofing repair and replacement, replacement of doors, frames, hardware, and windows, new finishes throughout (drywall, tile, acoustical ceilings, flooring, painting), toilet partitions and accessories, and compliance with current IBC and DoD accessibility requirements. The mixed industrial and administrative occupancy required careful zone separation in the finish and life safety design.

**Structural.** Structural engineering was included in the design team's scope in support of the renovation, addressing the existing masonry CMU structure and miscellaneous metals associated with the renovation scope. Specific structural modifications supported the HVAC equipment replacement and civil improvements.

**Mechanical / HVAC.** The project required complete removal and replacement of all HVAC systems. The replacement design provided six air handling units (AHU-1 through AHU-6) tailored to the building's mixed uses: AHU-1 serves the welding room as a 100% outside air unit with a chilled water cooler and gas heat, incorporating a dust collector with recirculation and an explosion-proof variable frequency drive to address hazardous air contaminants. AHUs 2 through 5 are constant-volume units with energy recovery wheels, chilled water coils, and gas heat serving the general building zones. AHU-6 is a split system for the Communications and Electrical rooms. The paint booth is served by a dedicated AHU with VFDs on both supply and exhaust fans, designed to NFPA 33 requirements. Chilled water is provided by two chillers — one for the main building AHUs (with buffer tank, 30% glycol loop, and expansion tank) and one dedicated to the paint booth AHU. Supplemental heating in utility spaces is provided by electric unit heaters. A central dust and fume collection system serves the welding shop with six-inch point-of-use extractors and VFD control. The Dirty/Sanding Room has a dedicated ventilation system sized for airborne particulates from aluminum, steel, titanium, polyurethane paint, and epoxy primer operations.

**Electrical.** The full electrical service was replaced, including demolition and replacement of existing pad-mounted transformers, primary switch gear, and all associated wiring. The new service is anchored by a 1.5 MVA transformer rated 13,200–480Y/277V, a 1,600A 480/277V service-rated switchboard, a 225 KVA step-down transformer (480V:120/240V), and a 150 KVA step-down transformer (480V:120/208V). Panelboards at 480/277V, 208/120V, and 240/120V serve the building loads. Interior lighting was replaced throughout with LED fixtures compliant with ASHRAE 90.1 and UFC 3-530-01, with occupancy sensors throughout. Surge protection devices (SPDs) were provided at service and distribution equipment. Dedicated circuits were provided for motorized roll-up doors. An arc flash hazard analysis was performed per NFPA 70E.

**Plumbing.** New domestic cold and hot water supply piping was installed in copper tubing (Type L above grade, Type K below grade), with sanitary waste, vent, and drain in Schedule 40 PVC below the floor slab and cast iron no-hub above. Two natural gas-fired instantaneous (tankless) water heaters were installed outdoors. A duplex rotary-screw compressed air system was provided serving 18 service bays, three tire-changing machines, and a tool room. Two low-pressure breathing air compressors (minimum 5 cfm each, with dual 3M face masks) support operations requiring supplied-air. An emergency eyewash and shower station was installed per applicable safety codes. Natural gas distribution piping was provided in black carbon steel Schedule 40.

**Fire Protection.** A complete new wet-pipe sprinkler system was designed per UFC 3-600-01 and NFPA 13, with hazard classifications ranging from light to ordinary to extra hazard depending on zone. The paint booth sprinkler is designed to extra hazard requirements per NFPA 33, with an isolation valve. A new Post Indicator Valve was placed 40 feet from the building, and a new fire department connection was provided. A new fire hydrant was added on G Street, tied into the existing water main. The fire alarm system is an addressable intelligent system combining voice evacuation and mass notification per UFC 4-021-01 and NFPA 72, with RF transceiver connectivity to the Monaco D21 Central Monitoring Center. Duct smoke detectors were provided per NFPA 90A. Carbon monoxide detection was installed in all rooms with fuel-fired equipment.

**Civil.** Site civil work included a new stoop at the southwest rear of the building, a new fire hydrant on G Street tied to the existing 8-inch watermain, a new fireline connection from Arnold Avenue with new Post Indicator Valve, a new 2-inch water service to the building, and a new 6-inch sanitary sewer main connection on the west side. The existing sanitary connection on the southwest side was abandoned. Curb and gutter at utility installation areas were replaced, and the existing concrete drive at the northwest corner was repaired.

**Environmental / Hazardous Materials.** A pre-construction hazardous materials investigation was conducted to identify, sample, and characterize suspect materials: asbestos-containing materials (ACM) including residual window frame sealant, black mastic, window glazing putty, pitch pockets, steam pit gaskets; lead-containing paint (LCP) on interior CMU walls and structural steel; mercury-containing fluorescent lamps in the former x-ray room; PCB-containing fluorescent ballasts (assumed unless labeled); two PCB-rated electrical transformers; and lead-lined walls and doors in the former x-ray room. Abatement work was specified under three divisions: 02 82 00 (Asbestos Remediation), 02 83 00 (Lead Remediation), and 02 84 16 (Handling of Lighting Ballasts and Lamps Containing PCBs and Mercury).

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant across all engineering disciplines on behalf of the Design-Build team, authoring the Basis of Design as a formal contract deliverable to the 316 CES. Design responsibility spanned architecture, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, civil, environmental, and hazardous materials disciplines. No LEED certification was required for this project per SOW Part II §II.J.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Completing abandoned prior work.** A previous renovation contract had been awarded and started but was terminated before completion. The building was in a partially demolished state — with incomplete demolition, partially constructed walls, and scope gaps — when this contract commenced. The design team was required to assess the as-left condition of the prior work, identify remaining demolition not completed by the prior contractor, and design the renovation around what had and had not been done. The SOW directed the new Design-Build contractor to complete all remaining demolition shown in the reference drawings and to address scope gaps created by the prior termination.

**Industrial ventilation for hazardous operations.** The welding shop, paint booth, sanding room, and NDI operations each presented distinct industrial hygiene requirements. AHU-1's 100% outside air configuration with explosion-proof VFD and centralized dust/fume collection, the dedicated paint booth AHU with NFPA 33-compliant sprinkler protection, and the Dirty/Sanding Room ventilation system each required coordinated mechanical, electrical, and fire protection design to address the specific airborne hazard profiles of each zone.

**Hazardous materials in an operational industrial building.** The building's 60-year history of industrial use produced a layered hazmat profile including multiple ACM materials in different locations and conditions, lead paint on structural steel and CMU, PCB-containing equipment, and a decommissioned x-ray room with lead-lined construction. Abatement sequencing had to be coordinated with the renovation phasing to allow safe access for construction activities.

**Governing document conflict — LEED.** SOW Part I required LEED-certifiable design while SOW Part II explicitly stated LEED Certification is not required. The two-part SOW's governing hierarchy clause (Part II governs where contradictions exist) resolved this conflict, directing the design to meet the intent of sustainable design without pursuing formal certification.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a fully modernized 23,773 SF Maintenance Group facility at Joint Base Andrews capable of supporting the full range of aircraft maintenance industrial operations. All major building systems were replaced: HVAC, electrical service, plumbing, compressed air, fire suppression, fire alarm and mass notification, and site utilities. Hazardous materials from the building's 60-year operational history were identified and abated. The renovation addressed the incomplete prior work and delivered a code-compliant, operationally ready facility to the 316th Civil Engineering Squadron within a 465-calendar-day contract period.
