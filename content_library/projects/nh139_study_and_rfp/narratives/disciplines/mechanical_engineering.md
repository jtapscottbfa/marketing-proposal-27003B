Mechanical engineering was the lead technical discipline for the HVAC and mechanical systems component of both project phases. During the 100% Study phase (completed December 2023), the team conducted a field investigation of all HVAC systems serving the 78,800-square-foot, 1964-vintage facility at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads. The assessment covered two rooftop air-cooled chillers — a 140-ton Trane RTAC140 (end-of-life, recommend replacement) and an 80-ton Trane CGAM (recently replaced, retain) — along with North and South penthouse mechanical rooms each housing air-handling units and hydronic heating equipment. The North penthouse hot-deck/cold-deck AHU, over 20 years old, was recommended for replacement; the South penthouse AHU, recently replaced, was designated for retention. Dual-duct variable-air-volume terminal boxes with pneumatic controls throughout the occupied floors were assessed as end-of-life, with a recommendation to transition to single-zone electric-reheat VAV units as part of the broader capital renewal program. Split-system VAV AHUs serving sensitive data spaces on the second floor and computer room air-conditioning units operating on R-22 refrigerant were also documented and assessed for replacement priority. The study addressed the building's pneumatic-to-DDC controls transition, with an air compressor in the North penthouse supporting the legacy system. For the Design-Build RFP phase (November 2024), mechanical engineering contributed performance and prescriptive specifications for HVAC scope included within the authorized P-1401 replacement package.
