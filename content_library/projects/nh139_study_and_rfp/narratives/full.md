## Project Overview

Building NH-139 at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads, Norfolk, Virginia is a 78,800-square-foot, multi-story command facility constructed in 1964 as the Atlantic Fleet Intelligence Center. Over its sixty-year service life, the building has housed and continues to house multiple high-priority Navy tenant commands — including the Navy Naval Information Warfighting Development Center (NIWDC), the Joint Regional Intelligence Center (JRIC), Cruise Missile Support Activity Atlantic (CMSA LANT/NGA), United States Fleet Forces Command (USFF), and a Defense Intelligence Agency Joint Staff element. The facility "serves the Atlantic Fleet by facilitating various President of the United States (POTUS) directed operations within a versatile space designed for diverse missions." Its continuous operational status and sensitive tenant mix impose extraordinary constraints on system outages, survey access, and construction sequencing.

By the early 2020s, the building's mechanical and electrical infrastructure had reached or exceeded its design service life across multiple systems. The 1979-vintage Westinghouse switchgear, the 38 kV primary service switch, both substation transformers, the North penthouse air-handling unit, and the pneumatically controlled dual-duct VAV distribution system had all been flagged as high-risk aging assets. NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic (NAVFAC MIDLANT) contracted the prime A-E under IDIQ contract N40085-20-D-0005 to address this condition through two linked task orders: first, a condition assessment study to inventory and prioritize all aging systems across the facility; and second, a Design-Build RFP to execute the highest-priority replacement scope under authorized FY22 MILCON funding.

## Scope of Services

The A-E scope spanned two distinct eProject task orders under a single Statement of A-E Services.

**HVAC and Electrical Systems Study (eProject 1692530; 100% submission December 22, 2023).** The team conducted a systematic field investigation of all mechanical and electrical systems across the building's first and second floors, two mechanical penthouses (North and South), rooftop equipment areas, and the electrical equipment yard and Quonset hut. The study inventoried and assessed condition for the full complement of mechanical and electrical assets: two rooftop air-cooled chillers (Trane RTAC140 at 140 tons, end-of-life; Trane CGAM at 80 tons, recently replaced, retain); North and South penthouse AHUs serving discrete command zones (North AHU hot-deck/cold-deck configuration, over 20 years old, recommend replacement; South AHU recently replaced, retain); dual-duct VAV terminal boxes with pneumatic controls throughout occupied spaces (recommend replacement with single-zone electric-reheat VAV); split-system VAV AHUs serving sensitive data spaces in Rooms 156, 157, 161, 168, and second-floor data rooms 211, 214, 215, and 219; and computer room air-conditioning units in Rooms 122, 127, 113, and 113A operating on R-22 refrigerant (near end-of-life, recommend replacement). On the electrical side: 34.5 kV primary service through SF6 switch SW-233 (38 kV rated, end-of-life); 1,000 kVA and 750 kVA substation transformers (end-of-life); Switchgear 1A (Westinghouse, 3,000 A / 208V / 3-phase, 1979, end-of-life, recommend replacement); Switchboard Main 2 (Westinghouse, 2,000 A / 208V, 1989, in Quonset hut, end-of-life); Switchboard 3 (Cutler Hammer Power-R-Line, 1,600 A / 208V, excellent condition, retain); Generators 1 and 2 (Caterpillar, 600 kW each, Room 158B, good condition, retain); Generator 3 (Caterpillar, 600 kW, Quonset hut, recommend replacement); UPS units 1 and 2 (500 kVA each, Room 199A, to be replaced); UPS 3 (250 kVA, Room A116, good condition, retain); and approximately 30 panelboards across the building. The study produced a retain-or-replace recommendation for each system and a rough-order-of-magnitude estimate of approximately $25.8 million for comprehensive building-wide system renewal.

**Design-Build RFP (eProject 1726324; Final DB RFP November 6, 2024).** Drawing on the study findings, the team prepared a complete Design-Build solicitation package for FY22 MILCON P-1401 — the authorized replacement scope targeting the building's backup power generation infrastructure. The RFP covered replacement of Generator 3 (primary scope), Bid Option i for additional generator scope, replacement of UPS units 1 and 2 in Room 199A, Switchgear 1A and associated distribution panels designated for replacement, and PLC replacement. The package included performance and prescriptive technical specifications, DB RFP design development drawings across architectural, mechanical, electrical, fire protection, and structural disciplines, and building requirements (Gross Area: 34,000 SF for the specific replacement scope footprint). The authorized MILCON program amount was approximately $3,930,000, with an estimated construction cost of $3,600,000 — representing a subset of the $25.8M capital need identified in the study.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Continuously occupied operationally sensitive facility.** Building NH-139 houses multiple intelligence and warfighting commands that imposed significant access and scheduling constraints on field investigation. The study team "close coordination with the government was established to ensure that the surveyed spaces were unoccupied and secure during the inspection" — a non-trivial logistical requirement for a facility operating continuously under sensitive-use conditions.

**Mixed-vintage infrastructure with no uniform replacement schedule.** The building contains equipment ranging from 1979-vintage switchgear to recently replaced boilers and AHUs, with components at widely varying stages of their service life. The study developed a retain-or-replace recommendation for each system based on condition assessment, estimated remaining service life, and operational criticality — distinguishing recently replaced equipment (South penthouse AHU, Trane CGAM chiller, South penthouse boilers, Camus Dynaforce condensing boiler) from end-of-life assets (Switchgear 1A, SW-233, both substation transformers, North penthouse AHU, CRAC units on R-22, pneumatic VAV boxes) and from equipment in good operating condition despite age (Generators 1 and 2, UPS 3, Switchboard 3).

**Stratified funding: authorized MILCON vs. total identified capital need.** The study identified approximately $25.8 million in capital investment needed for comprehensive system renewal; the authorized MILCON P-1401 funded approximately $3.9 million for a prioritized subset. The RFP was scoped accordingly, with competitive bid options (Generator 3 replacement as Bid Option i; Switchboard Main 2 as Bid Option ii) structured to maximize use of available appropriations without overstepping funded scope.

**Pneumatic-to-DDC controls transition.** The building's HVAC distribution operated on a pneumatic control system with an air compressor in the North penthouse. The study addressed the controls transition as part of the broader HVAC renewal scope, recommending replacement of dual-duct pneumatic VAV terminal boxes with single-zone electric-reheat VAV units as part of the comprehensive renewal program identified for future capital funding.

## Outcomes

The 100% HVAC/Electrical Systems Study was delivered to NAVFAC MIDLANT on December 22, 2023, providing a complete condition inventory, retain-or-replace matrix, and $25.8 million ROM capital plan for Building NH-139's aging infrastructure. The Final Design-Build RFP for P-1401 was delivered on November 6, 2024, completing the prime A-E's contracted scope. As of the final A-E deliverable date, the DB solicitation was positioned for award; construction award and start date are not confirmed in the available project record.
