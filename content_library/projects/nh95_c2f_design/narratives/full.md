# C2F NH95 Renovations — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The C2F NH95 Renovations is a Design-Bid-Build A-E services task order issued by Naval Facilities Engineering Command (NAVFAC) MIDLANT for the renovation of portions of Building NH-95 at Naval Station Norfolk (NSA Hampton Roads), Norfolk, Virginia. The project serves the US Second Fleet (C2F) Command and is funded under FY24 Special Project RM24-2031 (eProjects Work Order 1797165) at a program budget of $39 million. The work spans the first and second floors of Building NH-95, replacing aging MEP infrastructure and upgrading the facility to meet current operational, energy, and security requirements for an active naval command.

Building NH-95 is located at NSA Hampton Roads and houses C2F operational and support functions. The renovation addresses the full MEP and low-voltage systems envelope of the occupied command facility, incorporating mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, telecommunications, and security disciplines. Hazardous material abatement — ACM floor tiles, sealant, pipe insulation, lead paint, and PCB ballasts — was required throughout the renovation zones as a precondition to systems work.

## Scope

**Mechanical.** The renovation reconfigures the existing dual-duct variable-air-volume (VAV) system to serve renovated areas on both floors (Areas 6, 8, 10, and 11). The former Data Center computer room air-conditioning (CRAC) units are demolished; the vacated data center space is transitioned to standard occupancy and served by a new VAV system. New direct digital controls (DDC) are provided for all dual-duct VAV boxes, coordinated with the building's existing Automated Operations Center (AOC). Specialty mechanical work includes a 36-inch range exhaust hood (≤390 CFM) for the Admiral's cook staff galley, an exhaust fan for the IT closet in Area 11, and a 3 kW electric wall heater at the building entry for Area 11. Design conditions are: cooling 91°F DB / 76°F WB (1% design); heating 20°F DB; interior set points 78°F DB / <50% RH (cooling) and 68°F (heating).

**Electrical.** All existing electrical conduit, wiring, disconnect switches, and lighting fixtures on both floors are demolished and replaced. The existing 480Y/277V and 208Y/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire electrical services are retained. New LED lighting is provided throughout at 3500K color temperature and 80 CRI minimum, with occupancy/vacancy sensor controls in common areas, bathrooms, and offices. Emergency and exit lighting with 90-minute battery backup is installed. New and reconfigured panelboards serve general power and lighting circuits. Dedicated 20A circuits are provided for office equipment; GFI outlets are installed in kitchen and restroom areas; 208V, 3-phase connections are provided for HVAC equipment; and power poles and whips serve modular furniture workstations.

**Plumbing.** New restroom facilities are constructed, including water closets, lavatories, and bi-level water coolers. The cook staff galley receives a two-compartment sink and mop sink with domestic water and sanitary connections. Domestic water distribution uses copper tubing (ASTM B88, Type K/L/M); sanitary drainage uses cast iron piping. Electric water heaters (UL 174, 4.5 kW dual elements) serve domestic hot water loads. Insulation includes mineral fiber on domestic hot water piping and cellular glass on domestic cold water piping.

**Fire Protection.** The fire protection scope requires a survey of the existing fire suppression system including water flow testing of the available supply, a survey of the existing mass notification system, and a survey of any clean agent suppression systems in the areas of work. Fire suppression plans and fire alarm/mass notification system plans are included as required design package deliverables.

**Telecommunications.** All existing secure and non-secure communications systems are demolished and replaced. New fiber and copper telecommunications infrastructure is provided for data, voice, and video services. An Equipment Facility/Point-of-Presence (EF/POP) room is designed per Protected Distribution System (PDS) guidelines with ICD 705 Type A wall construction for the secure enclosure. The telecommunications scope also encompasses approximately 36,000 square feet of two LR-167 and LR-168 relocatable facilities associated with the project.

**Security.** An Electronic Security System (ESS) is designed throughout, including perimeter sensors, CCTV coverage at the primary entrance, card reader with keypad access control, and a Panel Control Unit (PCU) with backup power. Controlled area design requirements apply per NAVFAC standards. The facility is a restricted military area permitting no alien personnel on site.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime A-E consultant, delivering discipline-specific Bases of Design across all six engineering disciplines and producing a Design-Bid-Build construction package. The SAES (Statement of A-E Services) was administered by NAVFAC MIDLANT under IDIQ Contract N4008520D005. Construction procurement is sole-source. Source documents confirm BFA letterhead on the mechanical BOD, consistent with the design team holding direct prime responsibility to NAVFAC.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Data Center decommissioning and space conversion.** The existing Data Center CRAC cooling units are demolished rather than repaired, reflecting a complete change of use for the space. The VAV extension required careful coordination with the existing dual-duct system architecture and the building AOC to avoid disruption to adjacent occupied spaces during construction.

**ICD 705 secure enclosure design.** The EF/POP room requires physical construction meeting ICD 705 Type A wall standards — a controlled-area specification governing electromagnetic shielding, penetrations (dielectric sleeves), and construction sequencing in an active naval facility. This is a technically demanding requirement that goes beyond standard telecom closet design and drives both the telecom and security engineering scopes.

**Hazardous material abatement in an active command facility.** ACM floor tiles, sealant, pipe insulation, lead paint, and PCB lighting ballasts are present throughout the renovation zones. Abatement must be sequenced with MEP demolition activities, and the active military environment imposes personnel access restrictions (US-persons only), which affects contractor pool selection and construction sequencing.

**Dual-scope electrical BOD.** The Electrical Basis of Design document encompasses two distinct scopes: the NH-95 building electrical renovation and a separate telecommunications scope for the LR-167 and LR-168 relocatable facilities. The relationship between these scopes — whether the relocatable facilities are within the same building footprint or separate structures — is not fully resolved in the available source documents, and the 36,000 SF figure appears in the telecom scope rather than the primary renovation scope. This is noted as a record-level flag for human review.

**Active military installation access constraints.** The work area is a US-persons-only military facility, restricting the contractor workforce. All design deliverables, specifications, and construction procurement must account for this constraint.

## Outcomes

The project is classified as design complete. The design team delivered Bases of Design across all MEP and low-voltage disciplines, with a construction package structured for Design-Bid-Build sole-source procurement. Construction contract award date, construction start, and construction completion date are not captured in the available source documents; the SAES notes the estimated construction completion date as TBD. Design fee and construction contract value were not established at the time of source documents, with the SAES stating that the fee would be negotiated at time of task order issuance. The $39 million figure represents the program budget (Program Amount) per the SAES.
