# NOSC Orlando SEPA Repairs — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The NOSC Orlando SEPA Repairs project addressed physical security deficiencies at the Navy Operational Support Center (NOSC) Orlando, a multi-story, multi-tenant facility within the Armed Forces Reserve Center at Naval Support Activity (NSA) Orlando, located at 9500 Armed Forces Reserve Drive, Orlando, Florida. Following the July 2015 terrorist attack on the Chattanooga, Tennessee, NOSC — in which four Marines and one Navy sailor were killed — the Department of the Navy conducted a systematic security vulnerability evaluation across 21 NOSCs and developed a standardized program of "Security Engineering Program Assistance" (SEPA) repairs to address the identified deficiencies. The Orlando NOSC was among the installations selected for this programmatic upgrade effort.

The facility serves Navy, Marine Corps, and Army Reserve tenant units across a three-floor complex of interconnected buildings — designated the Wilson, Taft, and Smedley Buildings — with active Navy and USMC operational spaces occupying the first and second floors. The third floor was unoccupied at the time of the survey and was identified as slated for demolition under a separate contract. Multi-branch tenancy created scope boundaries that followed Navy-occupied spaces and Navy perimeter interests.

## Scope of Work

The project was issued as a design-build solicitation by NAVFAC Southeast Capital Improvements (solicitation N6945022R0107), with work placed against IDIQ contract N69450-13-D-0010, Work Order 1560522. The construction magnitude stated in the solicitation was between $1,000,000 and $5,000,000, and the contractual performance period was 180 days after award. Procurement was executed as a Section 8(a) direct award under the SBA Partnership Agreement.

The scope addressed four primary ATFP improvement categories:

**Vehicle barrier and entry control.** The two existing cantilevered sliding vehicle gates at the primary ECPs off Dowden Road were demolished and replaced with new K-rated cantilevered sliding gates — a 24-foot gate at the west ECP and a 20-foot gate at the east ECP. Existing gate pedestals at both locations were also removed. A K-rated manual drop-arm gate was added at the rear loading dock access point. Along the north perimeter fence, K-rated anti-ram cable reinforcement was installed across approximately 100 linear feet at the west run and approximately 420 linear feet at the east run, providing cable barrier coverage along the north perimeter.

**Pedestrian access control.** Two ingress/egress pedestrian turnstiles replaced the existing man gate at the primary facility entry, integrated with new CAC card readers and intercom stations at each position.

**Electronic security system (ESS) modernization.** The access control system (ACS) was fully replaced with a Hirsch/Identiv DIGI*TRAC Model 8 controller platform running the Velocity software system, with CAC-compatible scramble keypad readers (uTrust TS Government) installed at all exterior access doors, vehicle gates, and turnstiles. The video surveillance system (VSS) was upgraded with five PTZ IP dome cameras positioned on the building perimeter and one fixed IP dome camera at the quarterdeck exterior, connected to a new 16-channel hybrid DVR/NVR (Speco D16HT, 8TB). Security communication (SCS) Aiphone multi-tenant speakerphone stations were installed at all gate and turnstile positions, linking to monitoring locations distributed across the Wilson, Taft, and Smedley Buildings and the supply bay. The ESS headend equipment was housed in an equipment rack in Room S109 Telecom, with the security system workstation located in the Unit Commons (Room W205A).

**Glazing and interior door security.** Fragmentation retention film — 3M Ultra Night Vision S25, 8-mil, applied with mechanical attachment — was installed on all exterior windows and glazed doors throughout the facility to mitigate glass fragmentation hazard. Selected interior doors received deadbolt and upgraded security hardware installations to provide shelter-in-place capability for building occupants.

Supporting electrical infrastructure included new branch circuits from normal power panel L1-11 (208Y/120V) for the gate operators and turnstiles, plus a dedicated 20-ampere circuit for the security headend rack. Conduit runs for the ESS infrastructure extended up to approximately 566 feet from the electrical room to the more distant gate locations.

## Role on Project

The project was procured as a design-build contract under the Section 8(a) program with NAVFAC Southeast as the contracting authority. A licensed design firm served as designer of record under the design-build prime contractor, providing engineering and construction document services as a subconsultant. The design team covered architecture, civil engineering, structural engineering, electrical engineering, security engineering, and telecommunications/low-voltage engineering disciplines.

A comprehensive field site survey was conducted over two days in December 2022, covering architectural, structural, civil, security, and electrical conditions throughout the facility and at the perimeter. The site survey documented existing conditions relative to the 2018 pre-final reference design package that had been incorporated into the RFP, confirming scope, updating product specifications, and establishing the current-conditions baseline for the design-build execution.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**2018 reference design updated to current standards.** The design-build RFP incorporated a 2018 pre-final design package — originally prepared by LS3P Associates (architecture), ADC Engineering (civil), Newcomb & Boyd (electrical/security), and MMSA (structural) — as a reference for the design-build contractor. The RFP explicitly required that all building codes, UFC criteria, and design standards be updated to currently applicable requirements. The 2022 site survey validated existing conditions and captured changes relative to the 2018 reference, including updated product selections (e.g., camera resolution improvements from 2MP to 8MP specifications reflecting the evolution of available IP camera products over the four-year gap).

**K-rated barrier integration at an occupied operational installation.** Installing K-rated vehicle barrier foundations, anti-ram cable anchors, and gate operator infrastructure at an active, occupied military facility required designing new barrier systems that met vehicle crash-test rating requirements while integrating into the existing perimeter fence configuration and site utilities.

**Long conduit runs for ESS infrastructure.** The site layout required conduit runs of up to 566 feet from the panel room to the more distant gate locations. Electrical design included voltage drop calculations for the longest runs to confirm conductor sizing kept drop within the 3% threshold, using #1/0 conductors for the west gate run (556–566 feet) and #1 conductors for the east gate run (416–442 feet).

**Multi-branch federal facility.** The NOSC is a Navy tenant operating within an Army-owned Armed Forces Reserve Center, with Navy, Marine Corps, and Army Reserve units sharing the building. Security upgrade scope boundaries were defined by Navy-occupied spaces and Navy perimeter responsibilities, with coordination required across the multi-branch tenant structure inherent to the Armed Forces Reserve Center facility type.

## Outcomes

The project delivered the complete ATFP upgrade package prescribed by the Navy's post-Chattanooga NOSC security evaluation program to the Orlando facility: modernized K-rated vehicle barriers at all entry control points, a fully integrated electronic security system combining access control, video surveillance, and intercom/communication subsystems, fragmentation-resistant glazing protection across the facility exterior, and interior shelter-in-place hardware. The design-build delivery method and 8(a) procurement allowed NAVFAC Southeast to move the project through the acquisition process efficiently. Project status is consistent with completion given the solicitation date (2022) and 180-day contractual performance period, though formal completion documentation is not present in the source record.
