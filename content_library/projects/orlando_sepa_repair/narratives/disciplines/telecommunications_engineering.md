Telecommunications engineering on the NOSC Orlando SEPA Repairs project covered the structured cabling and low-voltage infrastructure that supported the electronic security systems installed throughout the facility and at the perimeter. The scope was concentrated in the structured cabling sub-discipline and was closely integrated with the security engineering design.

The telecommunications scope included the design and installation of cable pathways — conduit systems, pull boxes, and termination infrastructure — for the access control system (ACS), video surveillance system (VSS), and security communication (SCS) subsystems. A dedicated equipment rack was installed in Room S109 Telecom to house the security headend, including the ACS controller, hybrid DVR/NVR, and associated networking and power equipment. The security system workstation in the Unit Commons (Room W205A) was connected to the headend via the structured cabling infrastructure.

Conduit routing for the security systems extended from the headend room to the perimeter gate and turnstile locations, with runs ranging from approximately 416 to 566 feet to the more distant gate positions — a layout driven by the separation between the interior headend and the facility's perimeter entry control points. Pull boxes were distributed at intervals along the conduit routes to facilitate cable installation across the long runs. Seven monitoring locations across the Wilson, Taft, and Smedley Buildings and the supply bay, as well as the exterior camera mounting positions on the building perimeter, were served by the telecommunications cabling infrastructure.

The telecommunications scope supported the security systems as an integrated low-voltage infrastructure effort rather than as a standalone structured cabling program; no separate voice or data cabling program was part of this project.
