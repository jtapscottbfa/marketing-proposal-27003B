# MVLA Existing Climate Control Infrastructure

## Project Overview

The firm served as prime consultant on the MVLA Existing Climate Control Infrastructure project for the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association (MVLA), the private organization responsible for the preservation and operation of George Washington's Mount Vernon. The project was located at the Donald W. Reynolds Museum and Education Center, 3200 Mount Vernon Memorial Highway, Alexandria, Virginia 22309 — a museum and educational facility on the historic Mount Vernon estate. The firm's project number is 25-013; the final drawing set was issued April 22, 2025.

## Scope

The project scope comprised two related phases: an existing conditions survey of active climate control piping serving the museum's conservation display cases, followed by the design of a new copper pipe distribution system to extend and improve conditioned supply coverage to approximately 18–19 climate-controlled display cases on the first floor.

The existing infrastructure — a 3-inch copper pipe supply from a lower-level conservation machine (a precision humidity and temperature control unit) — terminates at the upper edge of the first-floor gallery. The design documented and extended this supply with:

- New 2-inch copper distribution mains routed through first-floor gallery corridors
- New ¾-inch copper branch lines serving individual display case clusters
- Manual ball valves at the technical compartment beneath each display case (per 2018 IMC, 2021 IMC, 2018 IPC)
- 2×2×2-inch copper tees at branch takeoffs
- 12×12 wall-mounted access panels at key connection points
- Flexible silicone tube final connections from the copper piping through the wall to each individual display case

Gallery spaces served include named exhibit areas corresponding to the museum's interpretive program (Be Washington, Revolutionary War, Making the American Army, Coalition Builder, Mount Vernon, and others). The design scope intentionally excluded the building's primary HVAC infrastructure — a VAV-based air distribution system serving the general museum environment — which was outside the firm's engagement.

Design documents were issued as a 2-sheet set: a title sheet (G-001) with vicinity and location mapping, and a first-floor HVAC plan (M-101) at 3/32"=1'-0" scale, including a display case connection detail showing the silicone tube final connection from copper pipe through the wall to the display case technical compartment. All new and existing pipe above ceiling is specified to be copper.

## Role on Project

The firm acted as prime consultant, directly engaged by the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association. A Virginia-licensed Professional Engineer (VA PE License No. 048025) stamped and sealed the drawing set. The firm produced all design documents, including project location and vicinity mapping on the title sheet and the complete first-floor mechanical design plan with associated details and construction notes.

The design was developed in coordination with the MVLA's conservation consultant team, which had previously prepared a draft existing-conditions survey (September 2024) that documented display case locations, existing active climate infrastructure routing, and the interface with the lower-level conservation machine. A reference mechanical plan from a concurrent Education Center renovation project provided contextual information on the building's primary VAV-based HVAC system.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Conservation-grade precision in a museum environment.** Conservation-grade display case climate control requires a level of precision and reliability beyond standard building HVAC. The design provides individual isolation capability at each display case via a manual ball valve in the technical compartment, permitting servicing or replacement of a single case connection without disrupting adjacent cases or the wider distribution system. The use of flexible silicone tube final connections accommodates minor dimensional variation between the rigid copper distribution and each display case's inlet interface.

**Routing in an occupied, culturally sensitive gallery.** The first-floor gallery is an active museum with named exhibit halls. New copper distribution mains were routed through gallery corridors following paths identified in the pre-design survey, minimizing new wall penetrations and preserving the integrity of the exhibit environment. Wall-mounted access panels were incorporated to support future maintenance access without requiring repeated intrusion into finished gallery spaces.

**Integration with pre-existing supply infrastructure.** The existing 3-inch copper supply from the lower-level conservation machine was the fixed supply source. The new distribution design branches from this termination point and works within the constraints of the existing system without requiring modification to the conservation machine or its primary supply run.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a final two-sheet drawing set in April 2025, providing construction-ready documents for extending climate-controlled supply to approximately 18–19 display cases throughout the Donald W. Reynolds Museum and Education Center first floor. Design complies with the 2018 International Mechanical Code, 2021 International Mechanical Code, and 2018 International Plumbing Code. The project demonstrates the firm's capability in specialty mechanical engineering for historic museum facilities, including survey, existing conditions documentation, and precision distribution system design in occupied, culturally significant spaces.
