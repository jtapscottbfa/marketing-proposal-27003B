The firm provided mechanical engineering services for the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association (MVLA) at the Donald W. Reynolds Museum and Education Center at George Washington's Mount Vernon in Alexandria, Virginia. The project scope encompassed an existing conditions survey of active climate control infrastructure and the design of a new copper pipe distribution system to serve conservation-grade, climate-controlled display cases throughout the museum's first floor.

Beginning with a pre-design survey performed in coordination with the MVLA's conservation consultant team, the firm documented the existing 3-inch copper supply from a lower-level conservation machine and developed a design distributing conditioned supply to approximately 18–19 display cases via new 2-inch copper distribution mains and ¾-inch copper branch lines routed through the gallery corridors. Each display case connection terminates with a manual ball valve in the technical compartment beneath the case and a flexible silicone tube final connection, providing both individual isolation and a clean interface to each case. The design specifies 2×2×2-inch copper tees at branch takeoffs and 12×12 wall-mounted access panels to support long-term maintainability.

The final two-sheet drawing set (title sheet and first-floor HVAC plan) was issued in April 2025, stamped by a Virginia-licensed Professional Engineer, and designed in compliance with the 2018 and 2021 International Mechanical Code and the 2018 International Plumbing Code. The project represents a specialty application of mechanical and plumbing engineering in a sensitive historic museum context, requiring precision distribution system design integrated with existing building infrastructure without disrupting the primary VAV-based HVAC serving the general museum environment.
