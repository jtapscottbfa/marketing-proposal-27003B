The firm provided mechanical engineering services for the MVLA Existing Climate Control Infrastructure project at the Donald W. Reynolds Museum and Education Center, George Washington's Mount Vernon, Alexandria, Virginia. The scope centered on the survey and design of a conservation-grade climate control distribution system serving approximately 18–19 display cases on the museum's first floor.

Mechanical engineering work encompassed documentation of the existing active climate control infrastructure — including the existing 3-inch copper supply terminating from a lower-level conservation machine — and the design of a new distribution system extending conditioned supply from that source to each display case location throughout the first floor galleries. The design specifies new 2-inch copper distribution mains routed through gallery corridors, ¾-inch copper branch lines at each display case cluster, and flexible silicone tube final connections at each case. Manual ball valves installed in the technical compartment beneath each display case provide individual isolation, and 12×12 wall-mounted access panels preserve long-term maintainability of the system. All new and existing pipe above ceiling is copper. Construction notes reference the 2018 International Mechanical Code and 2021 International Mechanical Code. The drawing set was stamped and sealed by a Virginia-licensed Professional Engineer (VA PE License No. 048025), with the final issue dated April 2025.

The project reflects the firm's experience with specialty mechanical engineering in historic, occupied museum environments — where precision humidity and temperature control for artifact preservation must be provided without disrupting museum operations or the separate VAV-based HVAC system serving the building's general environment.
