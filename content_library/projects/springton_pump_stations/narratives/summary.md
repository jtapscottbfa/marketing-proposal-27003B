The design team provided mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (MEP) engineering services as subconsultant to Pinnacle Group Engineering, Inc. for the City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities' Pump Station #290, located in the Springton at Grassfield residential neighborhood of Chesapeake, Virginia. The project is the design of a new municipal sanitary sewage pump station to serve the developing community, with service areas extending approximately 3,500 feet from both Pump Station #289 and Pump Station #290.

The station is a two-level structure with an upper-level pump room and control room and a subgrade wet well (bottom elevation -15.30, finish floor elevation +12.90). The pumping system centers on Gorman Rupp VS6A60-B self-priming pumps driven by 60-horsepower, three-phase motors operating through variable frequency drives. A bubbler-type liquid level control system housed in a NEMA 12 panel governs pump sequencing, supplemented by a submersible level transducer and multiple high-level float switches. Emergency power continuity is provided through a portable generator receptacle and manual transfer switch. The mechanical scope covers ventilation of the wet well and control room using exhaust fans, motorized dampers, and a unit heater. Sanitary piping connects the 10-inch PVC gravity influent sewer to the wet well and routes pump discharge to a 10-inch ductile iron force main.

The design team prepared mechanical drawings (M-1 through M-4), electrical drawings (E-1 through E-4), and Division 43 (Electrical and Controls) and Division 44 (Mechanical Equipment) specifications conforming to City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities standards. Documents represent the 35% design submittal transmitted to Pinnacle Group Engineering in February 2026; design was ongoing at the time of this milestone.
