# Springton Pump Station — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The design team served as MEP subconsultant to Pinnacle Group Engineering, Inc. (prime engineer and engineer of record) for the City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities' Pump Station #290, located in the Springton at Grassfield residential neighborhood of Chesapeake, Virginia. The project is the design of a new municipal sanitary sewage pump station to serve the developing community. A GIS service-area map established 3,500-foot service buffers from both Pump Station #289 and Pump Station #290, confirming the scope of the new station within the Springton at Grassfield drainage area. All MEP design documents produced by the design team focused on Pump Station #290.

The project is being delivered as a design-bid-build project for the City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities, with all materials, equipment, and work required to conform to City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities construction specifications and standards for water and sanitary sewer facilities, as well as the City Public Facilities Manual.

## Scope

The MEP design scope spans three integrated disciplines across a two-level structure: an upper-level pump room and control room at grade (finish floor elevation +12.90) and a subgrade wet well (bottom elevation -15.30).

**Mechanical.** Ventilation design for the pump room and wet well addresses the corrosive atmosphere and confined-space requirements of the station. The ventilation system comprises a wall-mounted exhaust fan (Greenheck AFR-20 or equal, 500 CFM, 0.7-inch static pressure, direct drive, 1/4 HP, 120V) for the wet well exhaust path, a second exhaust fan for the control room, motorized dampers (Belimo VCD-23, 24-volt actuator) with a normally-closed sequence interlock, backdraft dampers, and extruded aluminum stationary discharge and intake louvers (LV-1, 24×24 inches; LV-2, 24×36 inches). A single-stage electric unit heater (UH-1, Markel/Dayton or equal, 1/4 HP, 120V) serves the control room for cold-weather operation. The motorized dampers are held in the closed position when the exhaust fan is de-energized; upon fan energization the dampers open to the full-open position. A stilling well assembly (NEMA 4X stainless steel enclosure, 6-inch Schedule 80 PVC pipe) houses the level transducer signal cable and integrates with the mechanical floor plan. Mechanical drawings cover legend and abbreviations (M-1), site plan (M-2), HVAC floor plans for both levels (M-3), and detail drawings for the stilling well junction box, exhaust fans, unit heater, louvers, and motorized damper mounting (M-4).

**Electrical.** The power distribution system is served by Dominion Energy Virginia utility service, routed underground to the pump station building. A main distribution panel (MDP-5) distributes power to the two 60-horsepower, three-phase pump motors (Gorman Rupp VS6A60-B) through variable frequency drives, enabling speed-modulated pump operation across the wet well operating range. Emergency power continuity is provided through a portable generator receptacle (42 ampere, 3-pole/4-wire) connected to a manual 4-pole double-throw safety switch (lockable in all positions); a dedicated generator pad is located south of the pump station building. A surge protective device is included on the electrical distribution system.

The pump control system uses a bubbler-type liquid level control system (air-purging type) housed in a NEMA 12 panel, with a submersible level transducer in a 6-inch Schedule 80 PVC stilling well providing continuous wet well level measurement. Three high-level float switches (high level, backup high level, and hi-hi) provide redundant level alarm points. Check valve limit switches (Allen Bradley 802T-H or equal) on each pump confirm valve status. A phase monitor protects against phase loss, reversal, and low-voltage conditions. A remote terminal unit (RTU) transmits alarms and telemetry from the station.

Interior lighting provides LED ceiling fixtures (Phoenix Lighting, 17W, 5600K, 120V, surface ceiling) in the pump room and control room. Exterior lighting uses LED wall-pack fixtures (Hubbell Outdoor Geopak TRP1, 28.2W, 3000K, 120/277V, with integral photocell) at the building perimeter. An arc flash hazard analysis — including time-current coordination study and short-circuit equipment evaluation based on actual installed equipment — is specified as a contractor deliverable, with results required as part of the 50% design phase submission. Electrical drawings cover legend and abbreviations (E-1), site plan (E-2), lighting floor plans for both levels (E-3), and power floor plans for both levels (E-4).

**Plumbing.** The sanitary piping system connects the 10-inch PVC gravity influent sewer (invert elevation -6.30) to the circular wet well. From the wet well, two Gorman Rupp VS6A60-B pumps (6-inch discharge, 8-inch suction, 12.38-inch impeller; design point 1,250 RPM, 355 GPM at 139-foot TDH) draw down the wet well and discharge through 8-inch ductile iron flanged piping, through gate valves and check valves, to a 10-inch ductile iron force main (discharge centerline elevation +8.20). A PVC bubbler tube and bell terminating in the stilling well serve the bubbler-type level control system. Ancillary plumbing includes a 3/4-inch hose bib with reduced-pressure zone backflow preventer, a 4-inch floor drain with P-trap in the pump room, and a sump pump (Zoeller Model 147, 1/2 HP, 115V, submersible) where indicated by plans.

Specifications provided with the 35% submittal include Division 43 (Electrical and Controls) and Division 44 (Mechanical Equipment), conforming to City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities standard pump station specifications. The Division 43 specification covers control panels, the bubbler level control system, transfer switch, generator receptacle, lighting, and the sequence of operation. Division 44 covers flooded suction pumps, self-priming pumps, submersible pumps, motors, the sump pump, and ventilation fans.

## Role on Project

The design team served as MEP subconsultant to Pinnacle Group Engineering, Inc. (E. Rodney Flores, PE, Lic. No. 31038, engineer of record). The transmittal of MEP drawings and specifications from the design team to Pinnacle Group Engineering established this subconsultant relationship. The prime engineer was responsible for the overall pump station design, structural design, civil site design, and final engineer-of-record stamp; the design team's responsibility was the complete MEP scope described above. The 35% submittal package — eight drawing sheets (M-1 through M-4 and E-1 through E-4) and two specification divisions — was transmitted to Pinnacle Group Engineering in February 2026 for review and coordination.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Wet well depth and ventilation-control integration.** The station's two-level configuration spans approximately 28 feet from the wet well bottom (-15.30) to the pump room finish floor (+12.90). Ventilating the subgrade wet well — which presents a corrosive, potentially toxic-gas environment — while maintaining energy efficiency in the control room required a coordinated sequence of operation linking the motorized damper positions directly to exhaust fan status, ensuring that the intake path is not open to the exterior when ventilation is not active.

**VFD addition to in-progress design.** During active design review at the 35% stage, variable frequency drives were identified as a required addition to the pump motor control scheme. Annotations on detail sheets PS-4 and PS-5 directed the design team to add VFDs, which are reflected in the electrical power floor plan (E-4) as VFD-3 and coordinated with the control panel design.

**Design-review coordination at 35% stage.** The 35% submittal represents an early design milestone with active open items between the design team and the engineer of record. A design review annotation on the pump room detail drawings noted a potential change in pump discharge capacity from the current design basis (Gorman Rupp VS6A60-B at 355 GPM at 139-foot TDH) to a higher capacity (700 GPM, noted in reviewer comment); this change was not yet incorporated into the 35% documents. Additional open items included final equipment locations for pump control panel, surge protection device, remote terminal unit, and other equipment on detail sheets PS-4 and PS-5. These items are characteristic of early design stage development and were documented for resolution at subsequent design milestones.

**Arc flash requirements.** City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities requirements and standard pump station specifications require the contractor to perform an arc flash hazard analysis — including a time-current coordination study and short-circuit equipment evaluation — based on the actual equipment installed and as-built cable routing. The design team coordinated this requirement across both the mechanical and electrical drawing sets, with both sets carrying the same arc flash note establishing the contractor's obligation and the 50% phase submission deadline for the analysis.

## Outcomes

At the 35% design milestone, the design team delivered a coordinated MEP package — eight drawing sheets and two specification divisions — to Pinnacle Group Engineering in February 2026. The package established the station's HVAC, power distribution, controls, emergency power, lighting, and sanitary piping systems at an early design level sufficient for prime engineer review and owner comment. Design was ongoing at the time this milestone package was produced, with subsequent milestones (50%, 65%, 95%) expected to resolve open items identified during the 35% review.
