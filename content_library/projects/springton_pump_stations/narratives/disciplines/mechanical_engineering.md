Mechanical engineering services for Pump Station #290 in the Springton at Grassfield neighborhood of Chesapeake, Virginia covered ventilation and HVAC design for the station's two-level structure: the upper-level pump room and control room at grade, and the subgrade wet well.

The mechanical scope addressed the corrosive and potentially hazardous atmosphere of the wet well, requiring continuous exhaust ventilation with a controlled intake sequence. The system comprises a wall-mounted exhaust fan (Greenheck AFR-20 or equal, 500 CFM, 0.7-inch static pressure, direct drive, 1/4 HP, 120V) for the wet well exhaust path, a second exhaust fan for the control room, motorized dampers (Belimo VCD-23, 24-volt actuator) on the intake and exhaust sides, backdraft dampers, and extruded aluminum stationary discharge and intake louvers (LV-1, 24×24 inches; LV-2, 24×36 inches). A single-stage electric unit heater (Markel/Dayton or equal, 1/4 HP, 120V) serves the control room for cold-weather operation. The sequence of operation holds motorized dampers in the normally-closed position when the exhaust fan is de-energized; upon fan energization the dampers open to the full-open position before the fan reaches operating speed.

A stilling well detail — NEMA 4X stainless steel enclosure with 6-inch Schedule 80 PVC pipe and 1/2-inch signal cable routing — houses the level transducer for the pump control system and integrates with the mechanical floor plan layout. Mechanical drawings (sheets M-1 through M-4) include a legend sheet, site plan, HVAC floor plans for both levels, and detail drawings covering the stilling well junction box, exhaust fans, unit heater, louvers, and motorized damper mounting assembly. The mechanical legend sheet also carries the arc flash hazard analysis note establishing contractor obligations for the time-current coordination study.
