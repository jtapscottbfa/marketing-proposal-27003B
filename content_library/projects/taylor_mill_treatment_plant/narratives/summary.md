The Taylor Mill Treatment Plant (TMTP) is a 10-million-gallon-per-day (MGD) drinking water facility owned and operated by the Northern Kentucky Water District (NKWD) and originally constructed in 1953. The engineering team, serving as prime consultant in association with Cornwell Engineering Group, provided evaluation, design, and construction phase services for a multi-element capital improvement program with an anticipated construction budget of $1,615,000. Primary scope elements included replacement of the ultraviolet (UV) disinfection system, installation of a new powdered activated carbon (PAC) feed system, evaluation and replacement of high-service pump no. 3, and evaluation of filter valves and actuators. Supplemental scope items added during the RFP clarification process included concrete repair work in the treatment plant tunnel and repairs to the clarifier launder troughs.

Mechanical and electrical design encompassed new ventilation equipment for the chemical building to maintain safe air exchange within NEC-classified hazardous areas associated with the PAC feed environment, demolition and replacement of extensive legacy chemical feed motor and control equipment, new UV system electrical and control infrastructure, explosion-proof LED lighting throughout Class I/II Division 2 areas, panelboards, grounding and bonding, and integration with the plant's SCADA system. Delivery was design-bid-build through a locally competitive, qualifications-based procurement. Construction was scoped to occur within the plant's seasonal maintenance window to minimize disruption to potable water production at the 70-year-old facility.
