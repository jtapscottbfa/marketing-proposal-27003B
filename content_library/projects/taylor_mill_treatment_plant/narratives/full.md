# Taylor Mill Treatment Plant — UV Disinfection and PAC Feed System, Pump #3, and Filter Valve & Actuator Upgrades & Improvements

## Project Overview

The Taylor Mill Treatment Plant (TMTP), located in Taylor Mill, Kentucky, is a 10-million-gallon-per-day (MGD) drinking water treatment facility owned by the Northern Kentucky Water District (NKWD). Originally constructed in 1953, the plant serves as a primary production facility for the district. This project addressed a series of aging and underperforming process systems that required evaluation, replacement, or rehabilitation to maintain reliable potable water production at the facility.

## Scope

The engineering team delivered evaluation, design, and construction phase services for four primary project elements:

1. **UV disinfection system replacement** — removal and replacement of two UV reactor trains (A1 and A2) in the main treatment building, including demolition of the existing UV uninterruptible power supply (UPS) system and battery bank located in the inter-building tunnel, and installation of a new UV controller on the first floor of the main treatment building.

2. **Powdered activated carbon (PAC) feed system installation** — replacement of an extensive array of legacy chemical feed equipment in the chemical building, including polymer feed valve motors, filter aid polymer feed pumps, hybrid coagulant transfer pump, carbon mixer, carbon screw feeder, PAC feeder pumps, and associated control panels, along with installation of new PAC feed system electrical infrastructure and ventilation.

3. **High-service pump no. 3 evaluation and replacement** — scope included evaluation and design services for pump no. 3 in the main treatment building's high-service pump area.

4. **Filter valve and actuator evaluation** — evaluation of existing filter valves and actuators as part of the overall plant improvement program.

Supplemental scope items confirmed through the RFP clarification process included concrete repairs in the treatment plant tunnel and repairs to the clarifier launder troughs.

Construction documents consist of a four-sheet final submittal drawing set (one mechanical plan, three electrical plans) and fifteen specification sections spanning mechanical ventilation and electrical systems. The anticipated construction budget was $1,615,000.

## Role

The engineering team served as prime consultant in association with Cornwell Engineering Group, providing mechanical and electrical engineering design and construction phase services. BFA's role as engineer of record is confirmed by the firm's name and Newport News, Virginia address appearing on all four drawing title blocks, alongside a Kentucky professional engineer seal. The project was procured through a locally competitive, qualifications-based selection process under a standalone project contract using the design-bid-build delivery method.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**NEC-classified hazardous areas in the chemical building.** The chemical building contains areas designated under the National Electrical Code as Class B/Division 2 (combustible dust, associated with powdered activated carbon handling) and Class I/Division 2 (flammable gas/vapor) environments. All new electrical equipment installed in these zones required explosion-proof ratings. New LED luminaires specified for the chemical building are rated Class I Division 2 and Class II Division 2 (LE551, 4-ft, 64W, 4000K). The mechanical ventilation design — a new roof-mounted exhaust fan and motorized intake louver — was coordinated with the hazardous area boundaries to maintain adequate air exchange within the classified spaces.

**Operational continuity in an active water treatment facility.** Construction was scoped to occur within NKWD's seasonal maintenance shutdown window, running nominally from Labor Day through Memorial Day (approximately nine months), to minimize disruption to water production. Electrical general notes on the construction drawings expressly prohibited any disruption to the Fort Thomas supply line serving pumps 1, 2, and 5 during construction outages, reflecting the plant's continuous production obligations to its service area.

**Parallel capital project coordination.** NKWD was concurrently executing a separate project to replace emergency generators and electrical service at the treatment plant site. The project team designed around the existing electrical infrastructure while accommodating the scope of NKWD's parallel generator replacement effort, avoiding scope conflicts and ensuring the two projects' electrical work would be compatible at project completion.

**Working in a 1953 facility.** The chemical building and main treatment building represent original-vintage construction, with existing systems dating to multiple eras of modification. Electrical demolition scope documented in the construction drawings reflects the complexity of removing layered legacy systems — including polymer feed valve controls, carbon mixer controls, polymer sump pump controllers, and the UV UPS system — before installing new infrastructure.

## Outcomes

Final design documents were completed in May 2023, with the four-sheet final submittal drawing set representing completion of the design phase for the TMTP upgrade program. Upon completion, the Taylor Mill Treatment Plant received a replaced UV disinfection system, a new PAC feed system, improved high-service pump infrastructure, and evaluated filter valve and actuator conditions — collectively extending the service life and operational reliability of a facility that had been in continuous service since 1953. The project was completed within an anticipated construction budget of $1,615,000.
