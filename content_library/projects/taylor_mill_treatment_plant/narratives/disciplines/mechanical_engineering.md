Mechanical engineering scope on the Taylor Mill Treatment Plant project focused on ventilation design for the chemical building, a facility area carrying NEC-classified hazardous area designations associated with the powdered activated carbon (PAC) feed environment. The primary deliverable was design of a new roof-mounted exhaust fan and motorized intake louver working in tandem to provide fresh air supply and exhaust for the PAC-related space: exhaust fan EF-1 (Greenheck G-095-VG, 690 CFM, 0.5 in. ESP, 1/6 HP, 115V direct drive, roof-centrifugal) and motorized intake louver L-1 (20 × 20 inches, steel) serving Room 106. Existing dust collector exhaust fans (1 HP each), a unit heater, and a motorized door within the chemical building were evaluated and retained in place.

Mechanical specifications authored for the project provided a complete construction document package for the ventilation improvements: common motor requirements for HVAC equipment (spec section 230513), hangers and supports for HVAC piping and equipment (230529), testing, adjusting, and balancing for HVAC systems (230593), control dampers (230923.12), metal ducts (233113), and HVAC power ventilators (233423). TAB requirements ensure the ventilation system meets design airflow in the as-built configuration.

The ventilation design addressed the challenge of maintaining code-compliant air exchange within a working chemical building that remained in partial service during the plant's seasonal maintenance shutdown window, with the classified hazardous area boundaries informing both equipment selection and layout.
