# Building 285 Demolish Three Smoke Fans — NAS Oceana

## Project Overview

Building 285 at Naval Air Station Oceana in Virginia Beach, Virginia contained three aging smoke exhaust fans — designated F-14, F-15, and F-16 — installed in second-floor mechanical rooms. Each fan was a 30-inch-diameter axial unit rated at 20,000 CFM and weighing approximately 540 pounds. With these systems reaching end of serviceable life and no longer needed in their current configuration, NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic (PWD Oceana FEAD, PM&E PRA211/214) initiated a design-build project to permanently remove all three fans along with their associated mechanical, electrical, and architectural systems, and to restore the affected building envelope and interior finishes to a finished condition.

The project was procured as a design-build Request for Proposal (eProjects WO# 1703410) and is identified internally as BFA Project #24-004. The design team served as Designer of Record (DOR), producing a multi-discipline Basis of Design that reached Interim Final submittal status in February 2025.

## Scope of Work

The project scope touched four engineering and design disciplines across a two-story mechanical room stack in Building 285.

**Mechanical.** The primary scope item was the complete demolition of fans F-14, F-15, and F-16, including their mounting hardware, duct connections, and appurtenances. Each fan fed a ductwork system that ran from the second-floor mechanical room down through the first-floor ceiling, totaling approximately 180 SF of duct plus 105 SF of duct to each louver per fan. Duct silencers — 30-inch-diameter, 20,000-CFM-rated sound attenuators — were also removed in their entirety, along with all associated hangers and structural support hardware. All demolished items were removed from government property per the contract requirements.

**Electrical.** All electrical systems serving the three fans were demolished back to source. This included power conductors, conduit, raceway, junction boxes, disconnect switches, and the associated transformer(s). Demolition extended from each fan unit back to the source panel to ensure the building electrical system was left in a clean, safe condition without abandoned or deenergized circuits in place.

**Architectural.** Each fan was connected to the building exterior through a 72-inch by 30-inch exterior louver. Following fan removal, the three louver openings were infilled with 8-inch CMU construction, with 1-inch EPS insulation board and a vinyl copolymer aggregate finish applied over a reinforcing fabric system to match the existing EIFS exterior finish. Interior work included removal of mechanical ceiling grilles and replacement of all acoustical ceiling tiles disturbed by the demolition activities.

**Structural.** The ductwork serving each fan penetrated the second-floor concrete structure through approximately 42-inch-diameter floor openings. After duct demolition, these openings required structural infill to restore floor continuity. The design addressed each opening with a 3¼-inch reinforced concrete slab bearing on 11-gauge light-gauge metal plate supported by HSS 2×4 structural tube framing connected to existing ring plates embedded in the concrete. Each infill was designed for 50 PSF live load or a 1,000-pound concentrated point load, and finished flush with the surrounding floor surface.

## Role on Project

The design team served as Designer of Record under a design-build contract with NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic. This role encompassed all design disciplines — mechanical, electrical, structural, and architectural — under a single design package, with coordination responsibility across all disciplines and the requirement to produce construction-ready documents meeting NAVFAC UFC and UFGS standards.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Multi-discipline coordination across a constrained vertical stack.** The three fan systems each occupied a continuous vertical path from second-floor mechanical room through the first-floor ceiling to the building exterior. Demolition had to be sequenced and designed so that mechanical, electrical, architectural, and structural work were self-consistent: duct removal drove the floor opening locations; floor opening infill design drove the structural approach; and louver removal drove the exterior envelope restoration. The BOD coordinated these dependencies explicitly, with each discipline section accounting for the constraints imposed by the others.

**Restoring the building envelope to match existing EIFS.** Louver infill with CMU and EIFS on an existing military facility requires matching the existing exterior finish system in both material and appearance. The design specified a vinyl copolymer aggregate finish over reinforcing fabric on 1-inch EPS insulation board as the vehicle to achieve a finish consistent with the building's existing EIFS, without requiring full replacement of surrounding panels.

**Structural floor opening infill without heavy construction access.** Infilling three concrete floor openings in an occupied mechanical room, flush with the existing floor and to a structural load rating, required a design that could be installed without a heavy concrete pour above. The light-gauge metal plate on HSS tube framing solution, with a 3¼-inch reinforced slab placed on top, allowed the infill to be built from above using manageable structural components that could be brought into the space through existing access paths.

## Outcomes

The project produced an Interim Final Basis of Design in February 2025, representing substantially complete design documentation suitable for construction procurement under the design-build delivery. The design fully resolved the multi-discipline scope — mechanical removal, electrical demolition, exterior louver infill, and structural floor restoration — in a coordinated package meeting NAVFAC design standards.
