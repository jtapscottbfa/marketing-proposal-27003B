## Structural Engineering — Building 285 Demolish Three Smoke Fans, NAS Oceana

Structural engineering scope on this project addressed the concrete floor openings left in the second-floor mechanical room after demolition of the fan ductwork. Each of the three duct runs penetrated the second-floor concrete structure through an opening approximately 42 inches in diameter, sized for the 30-inch duct with clearance. Following duct demolition, these openings required engineered infill to restore structural continuity and provide a finished floor surface suitable for continued use of the mechanical room.

The design solution for each opening consisted of a 3¼-inch reinforced concrete slab placed on 11-gauge light-gauge metal plate, supported by HSS 2×4 structural tube framing connected to existing ring plates embedded in the surrounding concrete structure. This approach allowed the infill to be constructed from above without requiring access from below or heavy forming equipment, using components that could be brought into the mechanical space through existing access paths. Each infill was designed to carry 50 PSF live load or a 1,000-pound concentrated point load — consistent with the occupancy requirements of a mechanical room floor — and finished flush with the surrounding existing floor surface to avoid trip hazards and maintain level equipment access. The structural design was coordinated with the mechanical and architectural scopes, with infill locations determined by the duct centerline positions established in the mechanical demolition documents.
