# Partial Renovation — The Way, The Truth, and The Life Church
## Portsmouth, Virginia | BFA Project 24-034

### Project Overview

The Way, The Truth, and The Life Church occupies a tenant space at 4608 Deep Creek Blvd, Portsmouth, Virginia 23702. The congregation undertook a partial renovation of that space to replace aging, non-code-compliant restroom facilities with new mens and womens restroom suites and to reconfigure an adjacent office area (described in architectural documents as a future Pastor's Study). The project was designed and documented under a design-bid-build delivery model, with Bondurant Associates Engineering & Architecture serving as architect of record (Bondurant job number 2302) and a separate MEP engineer providing mechanical and plumbing engineering services as subconsultant (internal project number 24-034). The building is slab-on-grade, single-story construction. No gross square footage is recorded in the available source documents; the renovation area encompasses two restroom suites with a connecting corridor, and is characteristically small in scope consistent with a tenant-improvement engagement.

### Scope of Work

**Architectural.** Bondurant Associates produced architectural drawings covering demolition of the existing interior partitions and plumbing fixtures, new interior partition layouts creating mens and womens restroom suites, new suspended ceiling system with access panels and light fixture locations, door and hardware schedules (two new interior doors with hollow metal frames), ADA toilet accessory elevations (grab bars, tissue holders, paper towel dispensers, mirrors, baby-changing station), and construction details for new-to-existing slab connections and slab trench patches. Four partition types with varying heights were specified, braced per code to underside of bar joists. General notes required continuous sealant beads, vinyl base, and fire extinguishers per local code. The City of Portsmouth, Virginia has jurisdiction; construction codes are Virginia statewide codes.

**Mechanical.** The mechanical scope was limited to restroom exhaust ventilation. Two Greenheck inline cabinet exhaust fans were designed and selected for the new restroom suites: EF-1 (Greenheck CSP-A390, 220 CFM, 0.5 ESP, 1,035 RPM, 115V/1-phase, 1.42 FLA) serving the mens restroom, and EF-2 (Greenheck CSP-A250, 200 CFM, 0.47 ESP, 1,000 RPM, 115V/1-phase, 0.56 FLA) serving the womens restroom. Each fan discharges through ductwork routed at the ceiling level, with wall-mounted gravity dampers at the exhaust discharge points and manual balancing dampers within the duct runs. Fans are controlled by occupancy sensors and are provided with dedicated disconnect switches. No HVAC supply or heating equipment was within the MEP subconsultant's scope; existing building systems were presumed to provide general space conditioning.

**Plumbing.** The plumbing scope encompassed full replacement of domestic water and sanitary systems within the renovation area, including selective demolition of all existing fixture connections, supply piping, and sanitary piping to the points indicated on the demolition drawings. The existing 40-gallon gas-fired domestic water heater was designated to remain. New domestic cold and hot water distribution was designed with 1/2-inch branches to lavatories and 3/4-inch branches to the shower, with top-mounted centerset faucets at 0.5 GPM flow rate. A hot water recirculation loop was provided — RCP-1, an inline pump rated at 5 GPM and 0.5 HP, 120V/1-phase — to ensure hot water delivery to the renovated fixtures without excessive draw-off delay. The new sanitary waste and vent system used 2-inch to 4-inch piping routed to the existing sanitary lines, with 2-inch floor drains, 4-inch floor clean-outs, and a vent through roof (VTR). New fixtures included flush-tank water closets (one ADA-accessible floor-mounted and one standard floor-mounted), a flush-valve wall-mounted urinal, counter-mounted lavatories, and a floor-mounted shower. Below-slab DWV rough-in was required throughout, executed via saw-cut trenches in the existing slab-on-grade with patched slab per Bondurant's architectural construction details.

### Role on Project

The MEP engineering firm served as subconsultant to Bondurant Associates, the architect of record. The MEP subconsultant was responsible for the full mechanical and plumbing engineering design — drawing production, equipment selection (Greenheck fan selections documented in pre-submittal cut sheets), and coordination of MEP systems with the architectural partition and ceiling layout. MEP progress drawings (sheets PD101, P-101, and M-101) were produced as a separate drawing package, issued July 25, 2024, referencing the Bondurant Associates architectural set for mounting heights and partition details.

### Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Below-slab plumbing in existing construction.** Routing new sanitary waste piping in an existing slab-on-grade building required coordinated slab saw-cutting and patching. Bondurant Associates addressed this directly in the architectural construction details on sheet A1.2, providing specific typical details for new-to-existing slab connections at trench locations. The plumbing design was developed to minimize trench extent while satisfying code-required slopes and cleanout access.

**Hot water delivery to remote fixtures.** In a small renovation within an existing building, the distance from the retained water heater to the new fixtures risked unacceptable hot water wait times. A recirculation loop with an inline pump (RCP-1, 5 GPM) was incorporated into the domestic water design to maintain circulating hot water availability at the fixtures without enlarging the water heater or relocating it.

**Dual-set coordination between architect and MEP subconsultant.** The architectural and MEP drawing packages were produced by different firms under separate numbering systems (Bondurant job 2302 vs. BFA 24-034) and issued on separate timelines (architectural set initial issue March–April 2024; MEP progress drawings July 2024). Cross-referencing between the sets was managed through notation on the MEP drawings directing the contractor to the architectural set for mounting heights, and through the architectural slab-trench details that explicitly documented the below-slab plumbing trench conditions.

### Outcomes

The project produced complete, coordinated construction documents for the renovation of two restroom suites and an adjacent office area for a private religious tenant, with full mechanical exhaust ventilation and a complete new plumbing system. Both restroom suites received ADA-accessible fixture configurations. The plumbing system included a recirculating hot water loop, low-flow fixtures at 0.5 GPM, and a complete new sanitary waste and vent system tied to the existing building sanitary. The mechanical system delivered code-compliant restroom exhaust ventilation with occupancy-sensor control and gravity-damper isolation at both suites. The project is complete.
