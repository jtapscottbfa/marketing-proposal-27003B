# ICC Building 7 Range Hood Installation — Prosperity Eating Disorders and Wellness Center

## Project Overview

This project delivered mechanical, electrical, and fire protection engineering design for the installation of a commercial kitchen range hood within an existing behavioral health and wellness facility in Norfolk, Virginia. The work was a focused first-floor tenant improvement at ICC Building 7, supporting the operational needs of the Prosperity Eating Disorders and Wellness Center — a healthcare occupancy providing eating disorder treatment and wellness programming. The final construction document set comprised two sheets (a cover sheet and a combined MEP plan) issued February 3, 2022.

## Scope of Work

The engineering scope was narrow and highly coordinated across three disciplines within a single room:

**Mechanical:** Selection and placement of a Greenheck Model GRR5 fire-ready range hood (30-inch width, minimum 500 CFM exhaust capacity, factory-supplied self-contained fire suppression system, NFPA 101 compliant) in the Demo Kitchen, room 115. The exhaust system consisted of a 12-inch diameter duct routed through existing cabinets and the exterior building wall, discharging through a spun aluminum wall cap assembly with backdraft damper and bird screen to prevent re-entrainment. A wall cap detail was prepared showing the penetration configuration.

**Electrical:** Power distribution modifications to support the new appliance included a new NEMA 5-15R receptacle (115V, 1A) for the range hood control panel and evaluation of the existing NEMA 14-50R receptacle serving the existing range — relocation of that receptacle may have been required to serve the new hood. A new 15A/1P circuit breaker was added at space 22 of the existing Panel 2, a 225A, 208/120V, three-phase, three-wire surface-mounted panelboard (Square D Type NQOB). Existing Panel 1 and Panel 2 remained in service. The panel schedule for Panel 2 was documented on the drawings.

**Fire Protection:** Fire protection integration was achieved through the range hood's factory-supplied integral suppression system, which is self-contained and compliant with NFPA 101. A new fire alarm pull station was provided and connected to the hood per the manufacturer's instructions. The design coordinated the suppression system actuation with the building's fire alarm circuit.

The engineering team served as prime consultant. Romdhya Kumar held the engineer of record role (Virginia PE License 0402465820). The project was designated with internal number 22-007 and was issued as a CHA (Construction Administration phase) drawing set, indicating that services were provided in connection with the construction phase at this building.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Exhaust routing within an occupied tenant space.** The exhaust duct needed to penetrate the exterior wall of an existing building in a small kitchen footprint. The design resolved this by routing the 12-inch duct through the cabinet space before the wall penetration, minimizing exposed duct in the kitchen interior and maintaining clearances.

**Multi-system coordination in a single drawing.** The combined MEP sheet (ME101) consolidated mechanical, electrical, and fire protection scope onto a single plan at 1/4"=1'-0" scale. This required precise delineation of each system's scope within a compact room, with construction notes (eight notes total) clarifying sequence of operations and responsibility boundaries.

**Healthcare facility context.** The project was sited within an occupied behavioral health facility. The NFPA 101 compliance requirement for the range hood reflects the life safety classification applicable to healthcare occupancies, and the fire alarm pull station integration ensures the suppression system actuation is tied to the building's alarm infrastructure.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a complete, permit-ready MEP drawing set for the range hood installation, issued February 3, 2022. The design coordinated mechanical exhaust, electrical power distribution, and fire protection across three engineering disciplines within the scope of a single kitchen room in an existing healthcare facility. No contract value, construction schedule, or program budget data were available in the source record; the project is documented as complete in the project record.
