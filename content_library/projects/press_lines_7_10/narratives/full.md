## Overview

Extrusion Press Line Repairs – Press Lines 7 and 10 is a repair and renovation design effort at Naval Support Facility (NSF) Indian Head, Indian Head, Maryland. The project addresses two adjacent extrusion press line facilities: Press Line 7 (Buildings 561, 568, 567, 1233, 1232, and 1722) and Press Line 10 (Buildings 564, 569, 1234, and 1725), operated by the Naval Surface Warfare Center Indian Head Division (NSWC IHD). Client of record is NAVFAC / Public Works Department South Potomac (NAVFAC eProjects 1748574 and 1750151). Design services were provided as prime consultant, with the project following a design-bid-build delivery method.

The press lines are active explosive processing facilities housing below-grade press bays and above-grade control rooms, warming oven buildings, and ancillary support structures. Buildings are predominantly reinforced concrete and concrete masonry construction of mid-20th century vintage. All buildings at both press lines are within a designated historic district at NSF Indian Head, subject to Section 106 consultation with the Maryland State Historic Preservation Officer (SHPO). "All buildings in this project are part of the historical district and subject to SHPO consultation."

## Scope

Design scope encompasses structural and electrical engineering across ten buildings at two press line clusters.

**Structural.** Existing-conditions assessment of the press line building stock, with repair recommendations, as the basis for construction documents. Roofing replacement on the warming oven buildings (Buildings 567, 568, 569 for Press 10; counterparts for Press 7), which have historic wood-framed roof systems (2-inch by 8-inch rafters at 16 inches on center) on CMU and reinforced concrete walls. French drain installation behind below-grade retaining walls at the press bay control rooms (Buildings 561 and 564) to address moisture infiltration — 4-inch slotted PVC underdrain with gravel, filter fabric, and polyethylene sheeting behind the retaining wall face, coordinated with temporary removal and reinstallation of adjacent steam and ventilation lines. Pressure-treated wood walkway construction at both press lines: 4-by-4 posts at approximately 92 inches on center, 2-by-10 joists, 2-by-6 decking, 3-foot clear width, safety yellow top rail. Interior finishes, miscellaneous metals, exterior improvements, and demolition of existing mechanical equipment in press bays are included in construction scope.

**Electrical.** Complete replacement of existing electrical panels and service infrastructure across both press line building clusters. New 100-ampere underground electrical service with concrete duct bank serving warming oven building groups (Buildings 567, 568, 569 and their Press 7 counterparts). New disconnect, meter, and surge protection device at the utility pole. New 120/240-volt panelboard in Building 561. LED area lighting throughout the press bays, engineered to Class 1, Division 1, Groups C and D (flammable gases and vapors) and Class 2, Division 1, Groups E, F, and G (combustible dusts) hazardous location classifications per NEC, with explosion-proof fixture and conduit-seal assemblies. New LED wall pack fixtures for exterior illumination at building perimeters (non-explosion-proof). Secondary ground girdle system — #6 bare solid copper wire throughout affected buildings. New rigid conduit runs on walkways for lighting and future control wiring. Lightning protection system: remove existing, salvage, and reinstall with grounding continuity testing per OP-5 and NFPA 780.

Additional construction scope across both press lines includes hazardous materials abatement (asbestos-containing board ceilings in warming oven buildings and lead-based paint throughout), floor drain cap and fill at control rooms, water line repair at floor penetrations, and backflow prevention device inspection and certification at Buildings 561 and 564.

Both press lines carry a construction period of performance of 335 calendar days, with estimated project costs of $100,000 to $500,000 per press line.

## Role

The design team served as prime consultant providing structural engineering, electrical engineering, and overall design coordination for both press line renovation packages. Performance Work Statements were finalized by NAVFAC in April 2024, establishing the construction scope and requirements. Structural basis-of-design documents reached the 65% submittal milestone in February 2025. The design is structured for design-bid-build delivery, with construction to be executed by a separate NAVFAC construction contractor.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Hazardous location electrical in active explosive processing facilities.** "B564 is a mostly thick concrete structures used for processing of explosive materials." The press bays are classified as hazardous locations (Class 1, Division 1, Groups C and D; Class 2, Division 1, Groups E, F, and G) due to explosive material processing operations. All electrical system replacement and lighting design within press bay environments required engineering to NEC hazardous location requirements, including explosion-proof fixtures, conduit sealing, and compatible grounding. This constraint governed equipment selection, installation sequence, and conduit routing for new electrical infrastructure throughout the press bay buildings.

**Hazardous materials abatement.** Asbestos-containing board ceilings in the warming oven buildings (identified in historic drawings from Appendix C of the PWS documents) and lead-based paint throughout the building stock were identified as existing conditions requiring abatement prior to renovation work. Both PWS documents lead with abatement requirements before describing other repair scope, indicating abatement is the primary schedule and cost driver. Abatement scope was fully integrated into the construction performance requirements.

**Historic district and SHPO compliance.** All buildings at both press lines are within the designated historic district at NSF Indian Head and subject to mandatory Section 106 consultation with the Maryland SHPO, and to CWAP-documented historic resources conditions requiring a formal consultation process. Design scope development and material selection must balance historic preservation requirements against the technical demands of an active explosive-processing industrial environment.

**Moisture infiltration in below-grade press bays.** The press bay control rooms at Buildings 561 and 564 feature below-grade concrete construction with retaining walls subject to hydrostatic moisture intrusion. The structural design response includes a French drain system behind the retaining walls, with temporary removal and reinstallation of adjacent steam and ventilation lines required to access the wall face for drain construction. Floor drain cap and fill at control rooms and water line repair at floor penetrations address complementary moisture-management items at slab level.

**Parallel design for two press lines under a single design effort.** Press Lines 7 and 10 are nearly identical in scope but involve distinct building clusters and separate NAVFAC eProjects numbers. The design team developed shared standard details (walkway, French drain, hazardous location lighting) applicable to both lines while tailoring electrical service configurations to each building cluster's specific panel and service requirements.

## Outcomes

As of February 2025, the project is in active design at the 65% structural basis-of-design submittal stage. The design effort is advancing toward construction document completion for two press line renovation packages that will repair and modernize aging electrical infrastructure, address moisture infiltration at below-grade press bays, replace deteriorated roofing on historic wood-framed warming oven buildings, abate hazardous materials, and restore code-compliant hazardous-location lighting across the historic explosive processing building clusters at NSF Indian Head.
