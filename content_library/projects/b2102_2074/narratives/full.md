# Full Narrative — Building 2102 and Building 2074 SCIF/Secured Facility Conversion, NAS Patuxent River

## Project Overview

Bowman Foster & Associates (BFA) provided mechanical and plumbing engineering design services for the conversion of two existing buildings — Building 2102 and Building 2074 — at Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland, into accredited secured and classified facilities for the Naval Air Warfare Center Aircraft Division (NAWCAD). The project was executed under the authority of the NAWCAD Infrastructure, Safety & Environmental (ISE) Department and completed as a Final Basis of Design submittal dated April 7, 2023 (BFA project 22-047).

The two buildings, both single-level CMU structures, served different classified use requirements. Building 2074 required full ICD-705 SCIF accreditation — a rigorous construction standard set by the intelligence community governing acoustics (Wall Type A, Sound Group 4, STC-50 ceiling), electromagnetic shielding (60 dB ultra-radiant RF foil per Chapter 3, CNSSAM TEMPEST/1-13), physical security (man trap, hollow metal doors meeting RF requirements, access control, non-conductive breaks and grounding), and telephony isolation (CNSSI 5002). Building 2102 was converted to an "Open Secret" classified operational workspace with comparable power, environmental, and communications infrastructure, but to a below-SCIF standard that permitted an open industrial ceiling aesthetic, sound-deadening wall treatment, frosted windows, and an oversized rear garage door.

## Scope of Work

BFA's design scope covered mechanical and plumbing engineering for both buildings, within a broader multi-discipline construction package that also encompassed electrical, fire alarm, communications, and interior finishes work.

**Mechanical:** The existing HVAC systems — split-system heat pumps, motorized outside air dampers, and wall-mounted 7-day programmable thermostats — were removed entirely from both buildings. Replacement systems consisted of new split-system heat pumps with electric supplemental heat, new spiral ductwork hung below the ceiling (without insulation in conditioned spaces), and a motorized outside air louver sized to meet ASHRAE 62.1-2007 ventilation requirements. HVAC design conditions were established for the Pax River climate: outside cooling design of 91°F dry bulb / 80°F wet bulb, outside heating design of 19°F dry bulb, with interior admin-area conditions held at 78°F dry bulb / 50% relative humidity (cooling) and 70°F dry bulb (heating). Design criteria referenced NAVFAC Planning & Design Policy, UFC 3-410-02N, IMC-2010, and applicable ASHRAE standards.

**Plumbing:** The scope included complete removal of existing restroom fixtures — flush-tank water closets, wall-mounted lavatories, and water heaters — from both buildings, reflecting the conversion from general-purpose office occupancy to mission-focused classified workspace. Kitchenette plumbing was added at both buildings to support operational personnel: refrigerator, sink, and millwork with upper and lower storage.

## Role on Project

BFA served as the mechanical and plumbing engineering design consultant, producing the Basis of Design document that established system selection, design criteria, equipment specifications, and applicable standards. The construction was governed by a NAWCAD-issued Scope of Work requiring the contractor to develop design-build construction drawings at 30%, 60%, and 100% completion milestones with government review at each stage — a design-management approach typical of secure-facility construction under intelligence community standards.

## Key Challenges and Technical Context

Converting operational office buildings to ICD-705-compliant SCIFs introduces MEP constraints that do not exist in conventional construction. All HVAC penetrations through SCIF boundary walls must be treated for acoustic and RF leakage — duct penetrations require baffles or attenuators, and all electrical conduit entries require appropriate hardening. The spiral ductwork approach (below ceiling, no insulation) was selected to minimize ceiling penetrations and simplify coordination with the 6-sided SCIF box geometry.

The power requirements for both buildings were highly specialized: 400Hz 3-phase AC and 28VDC conversion/generation systems (supporting avionics test and mission-critical equipment) with power tracks running along all four walls and outlet circuits sized for both standard 60Hz and specialty frequencies. Building 2074 required 13 such specialty power connections; Building 2102 required 9. These power systems are characteristic of naval aviation test and operations facilities and drove much of the electrical coordination required.

Building 2102's rough cooling load estimate (~330,000 BTU/h, approximately 27.5 tons) was flagged as preliminary, underscoring the project's basis-of-design stage at the time of BFA's deliverable.

## Outcomes

The Final Basis of Design for mechanical and plumbing systems was delivered on April 7, 2023. The project demonstrates BFA's capability to execute MEP engineering for intelligence community accredited facilities — navigating the intersection of NAVFAC design standards, UFC design criteria, ICD-705 SCIF construction requirements, TEMPEST countermeasures, and the specialized power and environmental needs of naval aviation test and operations environments.
