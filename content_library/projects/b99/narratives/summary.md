Building 99 is a three-story, 27,120 SF dormitory at Pituffik Space Base (formerly Thule Air Base), Greenland, originally constructed in 1959. The project is a full renovation — procured as design-build by the US Army Corps of Engineers, New York District, on behalf of the 821st Air Base Group (USAF/Space Force) — providing 54 remodeled apartment modules (18 per floor) for E5/E6 NCO personnel. Construction magnitude is estimated between $10M and $25M, with a 960-calendar-day performance period from notice to proceed.

The engineering design team served as MEP subconsultant to the prime AE joint venture (Jacobs / Ewing Cole). Scope encompassed mechanical and plumbing engineering for one of the most extreme-climate US military installations: design outdoor temperatures of -40°C (-40°F) shaped every system selection. Mechanical design centers on a dedicated outdoor air energy-recovery AHU (Exhausto VEX5080, condensation-rotor energy recovery at 77.4% temperature efficiency) with a 171.81 kW glycol-water pre-heat coil and a 66.35 kW defrost coil, fed by a dual hydronic heating loop system drawing 85°C district heating water from the base utility plant. Per-room hydronic radiators with individual thermostatic control serve all 54 dwelling units. Plumbing design covers domestic cold and hot water service, hot water generation via three SWEP B85 plate heat exchangers (102 kW combined) with 500 L storage tanks, full fixture complement for 54 units, and sanitary drainage through the building's utility crawl space — all exterior and under-floor piping pre-insulated and dual heat-traced per UFC 3-130-05 Arctic construction requirements. The 100% design submission was completed in December 2025, with drawing revisions issued through February 2026.
