# Repair Dormitory Building 99 — Pituffik Space Base, Greenland

## Project Overview

Building 99 is a three-story, approximately 27,120-square-foot dormitory at Pituffik Space Base (formerly Thule Air Base), Greenland. Originally constructed in 1959, the building has reached the end of its useful service life as configured and requires a comprehensive renovation to provide eighteen remodeled apartment modules per floor — fifty-four total — for E5/E6 non-commissioned officers assigned to the 821st Air Base Group, US Space Force.

The project is executed under a design-build contract vehicle procured by the US Army Corps of Engineers, New York District, as the contracting agency. The estimated construction value is between $10 million and $25 million, with a 960-calendar-day performance period from notice to proceed. The project number is D3563508 (USACE); the task order is W912DS-23F0086 under indefinite-delivery contract W912DS-21-D-0003.

Pituffik Space Base sits approximately 750 miles north of the Arctic Circle. The operational climate drives every system design decision on the project: extreme winter temperatures, sustained cold periods, blowing snow, and the remoteness of the location require engineering solutions that prioritize freeze protection, energy recovery, and system resilience above all else.

## Scope of Work

The renovation encompasses the full interior of Building 99 across three floors, including demolition of existing finishes and systems, and installation of new mechanical, plumbing, fire protection, electrical, and finishing scopes. The design-build scope includes preparation of full construction documents and construction.

Fifty-four apartment modules are distributed equally across the three floors, each module consisting of a bedroom, bathroom (lavatory, shower, water closet), and connection to shared laundry and kitchen facilities at three locations in the building. Corridor, mechanical, electrical, telecom, and stairwell spaces complete the program.

Mechanical scope covers the full HVAC system: a dedicated outdoor air energy-recovery AHU, dual hydronic heating loops connected to the base district heating plant, per-room hydronic radiators, unit heaters in common and service spaces, ductwork distribution, and a building automation system integrated to the base-wide Trend energy management and control system via BACnet/JACE gateway.

Plumbing scope covers domestic cold and hot water service, domestic hot water production and storage, sanitary drainage from fifty-four apartment units through a crawl-space collection system to the base sewer, and all associated specialties, insulation, and heat tracing.

Fire protection scope includes a wet-pipe automatic sprinkler system throughout, Class I standpipes at each stair, and an addressable fire alarm system on fiber-optic connection to the base network.

## Role on Project

The engineering design team served as MEP subconsultant to the prime architect-engineer joint venture (Jacobs / Ewing Cole) under the design-build structure. Scope of services included mechanical and plumbing engineering design: load calculations, system selection, equipment sizing, design analysis documentation, and production of 100% design drawings (M-series mechanical and P-series plumbing) delivered in December 2025. Drawing revisions were issued in January and February 2026 in response to design review comments.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Arctic climate design.** The defining constraint is the environment. Design outdoor temperatures at Pituffik reach -40°C (-40°F) under extreme conditions — the value used for HVAC equipment sizing consistent with UFC 3-130-07 Arctic construction guidance — against an ASHRAE 99% heating dry-bulb of -29°F. All HVAC equipment selections (pre-heat coil at 171.81 kW, defrost coil at 66.35 kW) account for the most severe expected conditions, not just the statistical 99% value. The dual-loop hydronic system isolates the freeze-sensitive glycol loop (serving AHU coils) from the water-only loop (serving occupied-space radiators), reducing maintenance complexity while maintaining the freeze protection each circuit requires.

**Energy recovery in extreme cold.** At -40°C, bringing outdoor air to habitable conditions requires substantial preheat energy. The Exhausto VEX5080 condensation-rotor energy recovery unit achieves 77.4% temperature recovery efficiency, significantly reducing the load on the pre-heat coil before outdoor air enters occupied spaces. The rotary energy recovery configuration handles the building's ventilation requirement — 80 m³/h supply per bedroom, 75–80 m³/h exhaust per bathroom — while recovering the bulk of the heat already in the exhaust stream.

**Under-building crawl space sanitary piping.** Sanitary drain piping routed through an unheated crawl space in an Arctic building is a freeze failure waiting to happen if not designed specifically for the environment. All crawl-space and exterior sanitary piping is pre-insulated PE pipe with polyurethane foam insulation and a galvanized outer casing, incorporating dual heat trace circuits (one automatic thermostatically controlled, one manual) per UFC 3-130-05. Drain piping is sloped at a minimum of 2% for exterior runs. The 110 mm bathroom stacks and 160 mm mains consolidate to two discharge points at the north face of the building.

**District heating integration.** Rather than installing independent boilers or heat pumps, the design connects to the base district heating plant at 85°C supply / 45°C return, using plate heat exchangers for both space heating loops and domestic hot water production. Three SWEP B85 plate heat exchangers (102 kW combined) provide domestic hot water stored in three 500-liter tanks at 62°C. This approach eliminates on-site combustion equipment and reduces the complexity of the mechanical room at a remote facility where maintenance resources are constrained.

## Outcomes

The 100% design submittal was delivered on 22 December 2025 — mechanical and plumbing drawing sets plus full design analysis documentation including TRACE700 energy analysis and fixture unit calculations. Drawing revisions were issued on 8 January 2026 and 6 February 2026 in response to review comments. Construction has not yet been awarded as of the record date; the project remains in the design phase pending the design-build contractor's procurement process.
