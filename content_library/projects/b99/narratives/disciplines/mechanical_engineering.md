The mechanical engineering scope for Building 99 addressed a complete HVAC system replacement in an Arctic-classified facility at Pituffik Space Base, Greenland, where design outdoor temperatures reach -40°C (-40°F) under extreme conditions.

The centerpiece of the system is a dedicated outdoor air handling unit — an Exhausto VEX5080 — with condensation-rotor energy recovery at 77.4% temperature efficiency, sized to serve the building's full ventilation requirement of 80 m³/h supply per bedroom and 75–80 m³/h exhaust per bathroom. The unit incorporates a 171.81 kW glycol-water pre-heat coil and a 66.35 kW defrost coil to sustain air delivery through the most severe Arctic conditions without freeze damage to the heat exchanger core.

Space heating is provided by a dual hydronic loop system fed from the base district heating plant at 85°C supply / 45°C return. Loop 1 (100% water) distributes heat to per-room hydronic radiators with individual thermostatic control and to unit heaters in corridors, mechanical rooms, and stairwells. Loop 2 (60% propylene glycol / 40% water) serves the AHU pre-heat and defrost coils as a dedicated freeze-protected circuit, preventing loop contamination and simplifying maintenance. Variable-frequency drives serve all distribution pumps and AHU fans.

System controls integrate with the base-wide Trend energy management and control system via a BACnet/JACE gateway, enabling centralized monitoring and scheduling from the base operations center. Arctic-design outdoor air hoods resist snow ingestion, and motorized ultra-low-leakage outdoor air dampers prevent cold infiltration during unoccupied periods. Acoustic silencers (500 mm) are provided at AHU supply and exhaust connections.
