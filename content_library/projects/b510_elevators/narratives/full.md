# Building 510 — Replace Elevators #63, #64 & #67
### Norfolk Naval Shipyard, Portsmouth, Virginia

## Project Overview

Building 510 at Norfolk Naval Shipyard is a six-story, 372,031-SF cast-in-place concrete warehouse constructed in 1956, classified under IBC as a mixed-use Group A-3/B/F-1/S-1 occupancy and designated within NNSY's Controlled Industrial Area. Three geared traction elevators — freight elevator #64, freight elevator #63, and passenger elevator #67 — were original to the building, installed in approximately 1955, and had received only limited safety and controls upgrades in the 1980s. By the early 2020s, the equipment's age, obsolescence, and non-compliance with current federal vertical transportation requirements under UFC 3-490-06 and ASME A17.1–2019 necessitated complete replacement.

The project was executed under NAVFAC MIDLANT IDIQ contract N40085-20-D0005, administered by NAVFAC PWD Portsmouth, as two concurrent work orders: WO 1741405 covering Elevator #64 (estimated construction cost $2.5M) and WO 1741675 covering Elevators #63 and #67 (ECC $4.5M), for a combined ECC of $7.0M. The design team served as prime A-E consultant under firm project number 20-007J, with James Lawrence & Associates LLC as the vertical transportation subconsultant, Broyles & Associates providing structural engineering, and Dills Architects contributing to mechanical and architectural disciplines. The Final Design Submittal was delivered simultaneously for both work orders on December 8, 2023.

## Scope

The project replaced all three elevators in their entirety. Elevator #64 is a 20,000-lb Class C2 freight unit with a Hollister-Whitney #74 overhead traction machine, 75-HP motor, MCE controller, and vertical bi-parting power doors at a 9'-8" × 10'-0" clear opening. It serves seven stops (floors 1 through 6 with a rear entrance at the first floor) with 68'-0" of total travel. Elevator #63 is a 20,000-lb Class C2 freight unit with a Hollister-Whitney #74 machine, 75-HP motor, MCE controller, and vertical bi-parting doors at a 9'-0" × 10'-0" opening, serving eight stops (floors 1 through 6 plus a rear entrance at the first floor and roof access) with 118.75'-0" of travel. Elevator #67 is a 2,500-lb Class A passenger unit with a Hollister-Whitney #54 machine, 25-HP motor, MCE Motion 4000 controller, and two-speed side-sliding doors at a 3'-2" × 7'-0" opening, serving six stops. Elevators #63 and #67 share a single overhead machine room; Elevator #64 occupies a separate overhead machine room.

Supporting systems across all three elevators encompassed the full MEP spectrum. The mechanical scope replaced steam heating in each machine room with ductless split air-conditioning systems maintaining 72°F/50% relative humidity for cooling and 50°F for heating, with estimated machine room heat gains of 20,265 BTU/hr per room; DDC controls connect to NNSY's base-wide building automation system. The electrical scope included 460-V/3-phase power service modifications fed from existing NNSY substations (510-B for Elevator #64; 510-A for Elevators #63/#67), LED lighting upgrades to minimum 19 fc in machine rooms and 10 fc in hoistways, Category 6 copper emergency phone cabling, two-way emergency communications systems in each machine room and at first-floor lobby stations, and a security roll-up door with keypad access at Elevator #63's first-floor interior landing. The plumbing scope provided automatic submersible sump pumps at each elevator pit (50 GPM capacity per UFC 3-490-06) with discharge to the building exterior via Schedule 40 PVC.

The fire protection scope addressed both fire alarm and suppression. Fire alarm modifications — coordinated with concurrent base-wide fire alarm replacement Project P-256 (Mircom FX-MNS system) — included heat detectors at exterior landing locations, and relocation of elevator control modules within three feet of each elevator controller for fire emergency operation. Dedicated sprinkler branches from the sixth floor feed each machine room, with supervised shut-off valves, waterflow switches tied directly to the elevator shunt-trip breakers (instantaneous, no time delay), and guards on machine room sprinkler heads.

The structural scope included reinforcement of existing overhead machine beams and engineering of the new car rail anchorage system. New car rails are welded to horizontal I-beams with 3/4" post-installed anchors, at maximum 3'-0" bracket spacing (with tie brackets required for spans exceeding 12'-2"). Existing horizontal beam spacing in the hoistways varies from 2'-10" to 4'-0".

Hazardous materials abatement addressed confirmed ACM (floor tiles, sealants, and pipe insulation within the hoistways and machine rooms), lead-based paint, PCB ballasts, and mercury-containing lamps. Abatement was designed to sequence with and support the overall construction activities within the continuously operating facility.

Building 510 is subject to State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) review under Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act; machine room window modifications required SHPO coordination. The elevator #63 machine room design also required a custom machine base modification to avoid the existing machine room wall.

## Role

The design team served as prime A-E consultant, responsible for complete multi-discipline design through the Final Design Submittal. The vertical transportation subconsultant (James Lawrence & Associates LLC) led the elevator assessments, performed the 2023 field inspections, and produced the equipment layout drawings (Hollister-Whitney/James Lawrence layouts VT-01 through VT-06 and the six-sheet Elevator #67 package). The prime team coordinated vertical transportation engineering with supporting MEP, structural, hazardous materials, and architectural disciplines, and managed integration with concurrent NNSY base-wide programs, particularly the fire alarm system replacement (Project P-256) and the base-wide DDC infrastructure.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Age and code-compliance gap of original equipment.** The elevators were installed in approximately 1955 and had received only limited controls and safety upgrades in the 1980s. Seventy years of service and the code gap to ASME A17.1–2019 and UFC 3-490-06 required complete equipment replacement rather than modernization. The as-built condition of the hoistways — documented by WadeLynn Geospatial LLC's 3D laser scan (June 2023) — revealed maximum corner plumb deviations of 0'-1-11/32" at Elevator #63 and 0'-1-29/32" at Elevator #64. The nearly two-inch out-of-plumb condition at Elevator #64 required the structural engineer to account for hoistway geometry in rail bracket sizing.

**Shared machine room, constrained geometry.** Elevators #63 and #67 share a single overhead machine room (approximately 24'-0" × 24'-10"), requiring coordination of two traction machines, two controllers, overspeed governors, dead-end assemblies, and HVAC equipment within a confined space. A custom base modification was required for the Elevator #63 Hollister-Whitney #74 machine to avoid the existing machine room wall.

**Hazardous materials in an active industrial facility.** Confirmed ACM, lead, PCB ballasts, and mercury lamps within the hoistway and machine room spaces required abatement design integrated with the construction phasing — all within a continuously operating shipyard facility where interruption to elevator service had operational impacts.

**Security requirements at a Controlled Industrial Area landing.** Elevator #63's first-floor interior landing opens into a restricted area of NNSY's Controlled Industrial Area. The design incorporated a fire-rated roll-up door with a keypad access controller and fire-rated sight glass, coordinating building security requirements with elevator fire emergency operation provisions under ASME A17.1.

**Concurrent infrastructure programs.** The base-wide fire alarm system was being replaced by a concurrent project (P-256) installing a Mircom FX-MNS system. The elevator fire alarm interface — including heat detectors, FEO relay modules, and the elevator shunt-trip waterflow switch — had to be designed to integrate with the incoming system rather than an existing one, adding coordination complexity and timeline dependency to the fire protection scope.

## Outcomes

The Final Design Submittal was delivered on December 8, 2023, covering both work orders. Construction was scheduled to begin approximately May 2024, with Elevator #64 completing approximately May 2025 and Elevators #63 and #67 completing approximately November 2025. The project delivered comprehensive code-compliant replacements for all three elevators in a historically sensitive, security-classified active industrial facility on Norfolk Naval Shipyard, supported by 3D laser scanning for as-built hoistway verification, full hazardous materials abatement design, and multi-system integration with concurrent base-wide infrastructure programs.
