The Seagate Colony Domestic Hot Water System Upgrade Program is a multi-phase mechanical and plumbing engineering study for a 16-story residential condominium tower in Ocean View, Virginia. The project was initiated to address critical end-of-life conditions in a domestic hot water (DHW) distribution system that has been in service since 1975, including a storage tank exhibiting exterior corrosion and failing welds, and original recirculation piping spanning from the 1st-floor mechanical room to the 16th-floor boiler room. Serving approximately 144 to 154 residential units and common areas within the tower, the DHW infrastructure represents an essential building service where failure would directly impact residents.

The engagement is structured as a three-phase program: RFP #1 covers field data collection and system performance monitoring; RFP #2 covers alternatives analysis and option development; and RFP #3 covers final design and construction administration. The consulting team serves as prime consultant, providing mechanical, plumbing, and electrical engineering services. Field monitoring was initiated following a February 2026 on-site kick-off, with the study phase active through spring 2026. Preliminary option development evaluated two replacement configurations for the aging storage tank, providing the building owner with a data-driven basis for capital investment decisions. The multi-phase approach—monitoring before design, design before construction—reflects the complexity of working within a continuously occupied residential tower while managing both scheduling risk and the technical uncertainty inherent in characterizing a multi-decade, multi-vintage mechanical system.
