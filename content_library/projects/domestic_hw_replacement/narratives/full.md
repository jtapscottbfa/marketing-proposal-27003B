# Seagate Colony Domestic Hot Water System Upgrade Program

## Project Overview

The Seagate Colony Domestic Hot Water System Upgrade Program addresses deteriorating conditions in the domestic hot water (DHW) distribution infrastructure serving a 16-story residential condominium tower in Ocean View, Virginia. The original DHW system—including the recirculation loop piping—dates to 1975, placing the principal system components at close to 50 years of service life. The storage tank, originally an electric boiler converted to natural gas service in 1981 and further modified in 2017, exhibits exterior corrosion and failing welds. Gas-fired domestic water heaters and boilers installed in 2017 occupy the 16th-floor boiler room, while domestic water pumps (with the main pump replaced in 2023) and recirculation loop valves have required repeated maintenance interventions. The building's DHW electrical infrastructure includes legacy wiring from the original electric heating configuration and a 600V main electrical switchgear serving the mechanical plant.

The project is owned and operated by the Seagate Colony Condominiums, a privately governed condominium association, with Josh Danford (Maintenance Director) serving as the primary client contact. Source materials reflect a minor discrepancy in the documented number of residential units served, with counts ranging from 144 residential units (tower only, per the client's own presentation) to 154+ units (including possible townhome connections, per consultant scope documents); the tower structure itself comprises 16 floors. The operational risk of a catastrophic DHW system failure affecting the full occupied residential population drove the owner's decision to commission this structured upgrade program.

## Scope of Services

The program is organized as three sequential scope packages (referred to in project documents as RFP #1, RFP #2, and RFP #3):

**RFP #1 — Field Data Collection and System Monitoring.** Comprehensive field investigation to characterize the current DHW system's condition, performance, and failure risk. Activities include on-site assessment, installation of monitoring equipment, data logging of system temperatures, pressures, and flow rates throughout the 16-story tower, and documentation of existing equipment and piping configurations spanning from the 1st-floor mechanical room to the 16th-floor boiler room. This phase establishes the factual baseline required to make defensible recommendations in RFP #2.

**RFP #2 — Alternatives Analysis.** Development and structured comparison of replacement options, including assessment of alternative installation locations, equipment sizing scenarios, capital cost estimates, and operational implications for the building's residents and management. Preliminary option comparison work performed ahead of formal RFP #2 activation evaluated two replacement configurations: a housekeeping room installation and an in-place 1st-floor mechanical room replacement. This work was documented as schematic-level and subject to revision once RFP #1 field data is complete.

**RFP #3 — Final Design and Construction Administration.** Production of construction documents for the selected replacement option, coordination with contractors through a design-bid-build procurement process, and construction administration services through project closeout.

Mechanical, plumbing, and electrical engineering disciplines are all in scope across the program phases, reflecting the interdependency of the gas-fired boiler systems, domestic water distribution piping, pump systems, and electrical service requirements of the replacement equipment.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Aging, multi-vintage system complexity.** The existing DHW system is not a single installation but a series of modifications layered over five decades: original 1975 piping, 1981 gas conversion, 2017 boiler replacements, and 2023 pump replacement. Each retrofit introduced different equipment vintages, connection methods, and performance characteristics across a 16-story vertical loop. Characterizing the system accurately—what is original, what has been modified, and what the current failure modes are—is a central objective of RFP #1. The multi-vintage nature of the system means that replacement scope cannot be defined from drawings or records alone; field investigation is essential.

**Residential occupancy constraints.** The building is a continuously occupied residential condominium tower, meaning investigation, monitoring installation, and eventual construction work must be coordinated to minimize disruption to residents. The multi-phase approach—monitoring before design, design before construction—reflects the need to sequence intrusive activities carefully within an occupied building and to give the owner adequate decision time between phases.

**Scope boundary ambiguity — townhome connections.** Source documents contain a discrepancy regarding whether the building's eight townhomes are connected to the shared DHW distribution loop or served by independent systems. The client's own presentation indicates the townhomes have separate hot water boilers; consultant scope documents indicate the townhomes are tied into the shared distribution loop. This ambiguity affects the sizing boundary for any replacement system and is expected to be resolved through RFP #1 field investigation.

**Early-phase option comparison under data uncertainty.** Prior to completing field monitoring, preliminary option comparison work was performed to give the owner a conceptual framework for capital planning. This required structuring the analysis to be useful for decision-making while being explicit about its schematic-level limitations and the expectation of revision following RFP #1 completion.

## Outcomes

The project is currently in active RFP #1 status. On-site kick-off occurred February 12, 2026, with field monitoring equipment installation and system characterization underway through spring 2026. Status reporting through April 21, 2026, documents ongoing monitoring activities and client coordination. RFP #2 (alternatives analysis) and RFP #3 (final design and construction administration) scope activation dates are not yet established; they are dependent on RFP #1 completion findings. Final project outcomes are pending program completion. This record reflects the project in active early study phase.
