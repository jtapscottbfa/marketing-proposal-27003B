Plumbing engineering is the primary discipline for this project, addressing the domestic water and natural gas distribution infrastructure that is the central subject of the upgrade program. The existing plumbing system is a multi-decade assembly: original recirculation loop piping from 1975 spanning from the 1st-floor mechanical room to the 16th-floor boiler room; a storage tank that originated as an electric boiler, was converted to natural gas service in 1981, and further modified in 2017—now exhibiting exterior corrosion and failing welds; domestic water pumps with the main pump replaced in 2023; and recirculation loop valves that have required regular replacement over the building's service life.

The plumbing engineering scope spans both domestic water (plumbing_domestic_water) and natural gas (plumbing_natural_gas) systems. On the domestic water side, the RFP #1 field investigation documents the existing piping configuration, characterizes corrosion and leak conditions throughout the 16-floor recirculation loop, and establishes flow and pressure baselines for equipment sizing in RFP #2. On the natural gas side, the existing gas supply serving the 16th-floor boilers and the infrastructure associated with the 1981 gas conversion are included in the condition assessment scope.

Preliminary option development compared two replacement configurations for the aging storage tank: installation in a housekeeping room versus in-place replacement in the existing 1st-floor mechanical room. Both options require plumbing rough-in coordination with the building's occupied floors. Final plumbing system design—piping layout, equipment schedules, connection details—will be developed during RFP #3, following the alternatives selection from RFP #2.
