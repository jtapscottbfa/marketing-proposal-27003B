Mechanical engineering scope on this project centers on the gas-fired domestic water heating and distribution systems serving the 16-story residential condominium tower. The 16th-floor boiler room houses gas-fired domestic water heaters and boilers installed in 2017, which supply the building's multi-floor hot water recirculation loop extending from the boiler room down to the 1st-floor mechanical room. The mechanical engineering scope encompasses assessment of the existing gas-fired heating equipment, characterization of hot water distribution system performance across the full vertical extent of the tower, and development of replacement options for end-of-life system components.

The RFP #1 field investigation includes mechanical system monitoring—logging temperatures, pressures, and flow rates throughout the recirculation loop—to establish an empirical baseline for the alternatives analysis in RFP #2. This monitoring-first approach is appropriate given the multi-vintage nature of the system: original 1975 components, a 1981 gas conversion, 2017 boiler replacements, and 2023 pump work have layered different equipment generations across the loop, making performance characterization from records alone insufficient.

Replacement options under development include configurations for both the storage tank and the gas-fired heating equipment. The sub-disciplines engaged under mechanical engineering include HVAC design (in relation to the hot water system's thermal performance and equipment room conditioning requirements) and assessment of the building's hvac_hot_water distribution system as a whole. Detailed mechanical design documents will be developed during RFP #3 following selection of the preferred alternative.
