# Mechanical Engineering — B2185, B2187, B2855 Replace DDC Controls, NAS Patuxent River

Mechanical engineering on this project centered on the design and specification of replacement Direct Digital Control (DDC) systems for the HVAC equipment serving three occupied federal facilities at NAS Patuxent River — totaling approximately 785,000 SF across a research and development facility, and two office buildings.

The mechanical scope required detailed field investigation of three distinct existing HVAC plants before new control sequences could be engineered. Building 2187 alone housed a major central plant: three 650-ton water-cooled centrifugal chillers, three cooling towers, four gas-fired low-pressure hot-water boilers, fifteen variable-speed cooling-only AHUs, twenty-seven constant-volume makeup AHUs, fifty-six computer room air conditioning units, and 442 VAV terminal units. Building 2185 included three chillers, four boilers, twenty-four AHUs, and 225 VAV terminal units. Building 2855 contained three rooftop DX air handling units, two hot-water boilers, and 106 VAV terminal units of various types.

For each building, mechanical engineering designed BACnet-compliant DDC sequences in accordance with UFC 3-410-01, UFC 3-410-02, and UFGS 23 09 23.02, replacing aging or obsolete proprietary platforms with open-protocol controllers. Energy savings control sequences — outdoor-air temperature resets, economizer control, scheduling, and chiller and boiler staging optimization — were developed for Buildings 2187 and 2185. Building 2855 also received a full HVAC testing, adjusting, and balancing scope per NEBB/AABC standards concurrent with the controls upgrade. All work was designed for execution in occupied facilities, requiring careful system-by-system cutover sequencing to maintain building operations.
