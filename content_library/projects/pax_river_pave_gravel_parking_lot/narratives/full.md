# B2829 Pave Gravel Parking Lot — Naval Air Station Patuxent River, MD

## Project Overview

The B2829 Pave Gravel Parking Lot project converted an existing gravel parking area into a paved asphalt parking lot at Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland, adjacent to Building 2829 at 22445 Peary Road. Procured by Naval Facilities Engineering Command (NAVFAC) under a design-build contract (Work Order 1577286), the project was issued via Request for Proposals in May 2020. The resulting facility provides at least 46 paved parking spaces for private motor vehicles across approximately 19,630 square feet of new asphalt pavement, replacing a previously unpaved gravel surface that did not meet the installation's paving standards or current accessibility and stormwater requirements.

## Scope of Work

The design-build scope encompassed full site development of the parking area, including:

**Civil and Site Work:** Design of the asphalt pavement section to replace the existing gravel lot, with associated site grading, drainage, and exterior site improvements including the required number of ADA-compliant handicap parking spaces. Stormwater management was a regulated component: the RFP explicitly noted that "proposed disturbance for this work will exceed 5000 square feet," triggering Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) stormwater permit requirements. Stormwater best management practices (BMPs) were incorporated into the civil design. Site improvements also included exterior landscaping to restore disturbed areas beyond the paved lot boundary.

**Electrical — Site Lighting:** Design of a new exterior LED parking lot lighting system to illuminate the paved lot. The lighting design specified eight (8) pole-mounted LED area luminaires — Lithonia RSX1 LED P3 40K R4, 277V, 110 watts each, on 20-foot anchor-base steel/aluminum poles — distributed across the lot to achieve compliant light levels. A photometric analysis confirmed adequate coverage of the 19,630-square-foot lot area. All lighting was served from existing electrical panels inside Building 2829 (Electrical Room 117), with direct-buried 1-inch Schedule 40 PVC conduit routed from the building to pole foundations, transitioning to galvanized rigid steel conduit above grade at each pole.

**Electrical — Lighting Controls:** A two-zone, two-relay lighting control panel (LCP1) was designed to provide automatic photocell-based switching with manual override capability. The control panel drew power from an existing spare circuit in Panel R1 (208Y/120V, 3-phase). Lighting load circuits were added to existing Panel L1 (480/277V, 3-phase, 400A main circuit breaker) in Building 2829. No new electrical service was introduced; the design integrated fully with the building's existing electrical infrastructure.

## Role on Project

The design team performed services as subconsultant Designer of Record (DOR) to the prime design-build contractor, consistent with NAVFAC design-build delivery conventions in which a licensed architect-engineer firm provides the regulated design under subcontract to the general contractor. The firm held the Maryland-licensed engineer of record responsibility for the electrical drawing set; the PE seal of record was Richard H. Huang (Maryland). The BFA project number assigned was 20-062; NAVFAC issued drawing number 13135488. The design team produced the 100% electrical construction documents, the parking lot photometric analysis, and coordinated the civil scope in the design-build project program.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Design-build coordination within NAVFAC constraints.** NAVFAC design-build projects impose specific roles and responsibilities on the Designer of Record, who functions under the prime contractor rather than directly with the government. The design team navigated the Part 4 minimum performance requirements and Part 3 project program to develop an electrical design that met federal technical criteria (fixture selection, pole heights, controls integration, conduit types) while tying into existing building infrastructure without introducing new service or transformers.

**Stormwater management compliance.** The project crossed the 5,000-square-foot MDE disturbance threshold, requiring compliant stormwater management design. Civil site design incorporated BMPs sized and configured per Maryland regulations, coordinating paved area drainage with the on-site stormwater infrastructure visible on the site plan drawings.

**Integration with existing electrical infrastructure.** Rather than installing new electrical service, the lighting design identified available capacity in two existing panels — one for power (L1, 480/277V) and one for controls (R1, 208Y/120V) — and allocated new dedicated circuits within those panels. This approach avoided the cost and complexity of new service work while fully meeting the lighting load and controls requirements.

## Outcomes

The 100% electrical design drawings were completed and issued in May 2021, approximately twelve months after the RFP date. The design produced a compliant, energy-efficient LED parking lot lighting system and a fully coordinated set of construction documents for the paved lot. The project converted a functionally deficient gravel surface into a formal, ADA-compliant paved parking facility serving Building 2829 at NAS Patuxent River. Contract value and construction completion date are not captured in the available project record; the design documents indicate a complete design deliverable.
