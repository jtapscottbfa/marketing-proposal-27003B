# Solair on the Eastern Shore of Virginia — Tai Dat, LLC Solar PV System

## Project Overview

This project involved the electrical engineering design of a commercial-scale, grid-tied solar photovoltaic system for Tai Dat, LLC at 28261 Turkey Run Road, Bloxom, Virginia 23308, located in Accomack County on Virginia's Eastern Shore. The system is sized at 112.5 kWDC / 133.05 kWAC — a capacity consistent with a large agricultural or commercial property seeking to reduce utility costs through on-site generation. The design was completed in March 2024. The design team served as prime consultant on the project.

The project record is based on a single electrical single-line diagram (S.L.D.) prepared for permitting and interconnection; no basis-of-design narrative, specifications, or contract documents are available in the source record. Financial and schedule details are therefore not recorded.

## Scope

The design scope comprised the full electrical design for a 278-module, three-phase, grid-tied solar PV system. Key scope elements include:

- **PV module and inverter selection and configuration.** 278 Hyperion GH108PB-400W modules, each paired with an Enphase IQ8P-3P-72-E-US microinverter. The microinverter-per-module architecture provides module-level maximum power point tracking, fault isolation, and monitoring capability, which is well-suited to installations where array orientation or shading may vary across the array footprint.
- **Array configuration.** Modules are arranged in 27 strings: 8 strings of 11 modules (8 × 18.13 A per string) and 19 strings of 10 modules (19 × 18.48 A per string), yielding a total AC output current of 458.18 amperes across the three-phase service.
- **Distribution system design.** The three sub-arrays each feed a dedicated Solar Load Center Distribution Panel via 10/3 copper feeder conductors with 4-gauge continuous copper grounding conductors. The three distribution panels aggregate AC output for connection to the utility-owned transformer.
- **Safety disconnect.** An 800-ampere safety switch is specified at the interface between the sub-array distribution panels and the utility transformer, labeled as the solar disconnect next to the meter, in compliance with NEC utility interconnection requirements.
- **Grounding and bonding.** The design addresses grounding and bonding for the PV array, inverters, distribution panels, and safety switch per NEC requirements.
- **Utility coordination.** Scope includes revenue-grade utility metering (meter 164368573, account 2880800), telephony/communications coordination, and back-feed compliance documentation for the interconnection application to the serving utility.
- **Permitting documentation.** The single-line diagram was the primary permitting deliverable, prepared to support both the building permit and the utility interconnection agreement.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Microinverter-per-module architecture at scale.** A 278-microinverter array presents coordination complexity relative to a string-inverter design — each inverter has its own output circuit, and string groupings must be carefully sized to stay within distribution panel and conductor ampacity limits. The design addresses this through a structured three-sub-array configuration, distributing the inverter aggregate across three distribution panels and keeping the feeder sizing tractable.

**Three-phase grid-tied interconnection.** The system is designed for three-phase utility service, requiring balanced current distribution across the sub-arrays and careful coordination with the serving utility's interconnection requirements — including revenue-grade metering, back-feed protection, and the placement and labeling of the solar disconnect adjacent to the utility-owned transformer.
