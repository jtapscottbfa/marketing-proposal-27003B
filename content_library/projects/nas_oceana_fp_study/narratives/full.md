# Study Fire Protection Systems at NSWDG Compound — NAS Oceana, Dam Neck Annex

## Project Overview

This project was a site engineering investigation and fire protection systems study for the Naval Special Warfare Development Group (NSWDG) Compound at Naval Air Station Oceana, Dam Neck Annex, Virginia Beach, Virginia. The NSWDG Compound is a secured military installation encompassing approximately 200 acres with approximately 81 buildings and structures of various sizes, bounded by compound fencing to the south, the Atlantic shoreline to the east, Batt Street to the north, and Lake Redwing to the west. The study area also included the Pass & ID Office (Buildings 383/389), the Building 250 complex west of Lake Redwing, outdoor shooting range facilities, and the EOD compound and magazines north of Batt Street.

The project was commissioned by Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command Mid-Atlantic (NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic) under IQ Contract N40085-15-D-0003 (eProjects Work Order No. 1663960). The Interim Final Submission was delivered in February 2023. There were no construction plans or specifications associated with this task order; the deliverable was an engineering study report.

## Scope

The investigation scope encompassed the full lifecycle of a fire protection infrastructure assessment for a complex, multi-system military installation. The team assessed fire flow and fire water demand requirements for approximately 81 buildings within the compound, then evaluated two distinct and largely independent water distribution systems against UFC 3-600-01 (Fire Protection Engineering for Facilities) requirements.

**High-pressure non-potable fire main.** The dedicated fire main is served by Fire Pump Station 352 and Water Storage Tank 351, a ground-level 200,000-gallon non-potable storage tank. The system serves 14 sprinklered buildings within the compound. Field investigation included hydrant flow testing of the high-pressure system and a valve assessment survey across the system's distribution network.

**Installation domestic water distribution system.** The domestic system is served by Water Storage Tank 531, a 250,000-gallon elevated tank. The system covers a broader set of buildings than the high-pressure fire main. Field investigation included hydrant flow testing of domestic hydrants, a pipe identification survey, and a valve assessment.

Calibrated hydraulic models were developed for both systems using Bentley WaterGEMS software. The models were calibrated against field-collected hydrant flow test data and used to evaluate existing system performance against UFC 3-600-01 fire flow demand thresholds for each building. Eleven categories of deficiency were identified and documented.

From the deficiency inventory, NAVFAC selected five priority areas for detailed recommendations with rough-order-of-magnitude (ROM) cost estimates. Seven discrete recommendations were developed:

- **SCADA reprogramming** to increase domestic system pump station operating pressures, raising fire flow capacity from approximately 1,650 gpm to approximately 1,900 gpm at 20 psi residual — ROM cost $0 (labor only).
- **New 250,000-gallon elevated water storage tank** at the north end of the NSWDG Compound to supplement domestic storage and increase fire flow to approximately 2,500 gpm — ROM cost $2,398,596 (Alternative 2).
- **Replace Water Storage Tank 531** with a new 450,000-gallon elevated tank to increase fire storage reserve — ROM cost $3,351,723 (Alternative 1).
- **Demolish and replace/relocate Pump Station 329**, a 1960s-era pump station, increasing pumping capacity from 240 gpm to 600 gpm and relocating to the north end of the compound — ROM cost $1,364,097.
- **Eliminate the dead-end service main serving the Building 250 compound** by installing a new 8-inch water main via directional drilling under Lake Redwing, connecting the Building 250 distribution loop to the main Installation water system — ROM cost $897,170.
- **Install seven new fire hydrants** with supporting piping to correct the most significant hydrant spacing deficiencies, addressing approximately 44 buildings with deficient hydrant coverage — ROM cost $1,074,933.
- **Disconnect seven high-pressure hydrants** from the non-potable fire main and reconnect to the domestic/potable system, consolidating hydrant coverage and eliminating cross-contamination risk — ROM cost $40,000.

The study also included development of a non-potable water distribution system maintenance program.

The prime A-E contract was held under NAVFAC's indefinite-quantity A-E services contract vehicle (N40085-15-D-0003). Jacobs Engineering, Inc. (Virginia Beach, VA) served as subconsultant, performing all technical investigation, hydraulic modeling, deficiency analysis, recommendation development, and study report preparation. ## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Dual independent water systems.** The compound is served by two entirely separate water distribution systems — a high-pressure non-potable fire main and the Installation domestic system — with overlapping geographic coverage but no interconnection. Each system required an independent field investigation, independent hydraulic modeling, and independent deficiency analysis. Cross-referencing the two models was required to identify buildings where neither system could meet UFC 3-600-01 fire flow demands.

**Limited fire water reserve in domestic storage.** Water Storage Tank 531 (250,000 gallons elevated) serves both the daily domestic demand of the entire Installation and the fire reserve. The available fire reserve was found to be only approximately 50,000 gallons beyond daily domestic usage — substantially less than the tank's nominal capacity. This constraint limited the domestic system's effective fire flow duration for buildings with higher demands, even at flow rates the hydraulic model showed the system could deliver. Storage augmentation recommendations addressed this through two alternatives ($2.4M and $3.4M) depending on the approach selected.

**Dead-end mains and isolated building clusters.** Multiple dead-end service mains were identified throughout the compound, including a high-risk configuration serving the Building 250 complex, which is physically separated from the main compound by Lake Redwing and accessible only via a single dead-end main. The recommended resolution — directional drilling under the lake to create a looped connection — addressed both the dead-end and isolation vulnerabilities simultaneously.

**Restricted facility and document handling requirements.** The NSWDG Compound is a restricted military installation. Investigation materials, hydraulic model outputs, and certain site plans were subject to Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) handling requirements. The ADP Illustrative Site Plans (Appendix F) were marked "CUI//CRIT//FEDCON," requiring appropriate document control throughout the study and in the study report deliverable.

## Outcomes

The Interim Final Submission (February 2023) delivered a complete fire protection systems assessment for the NSWDG Compound: calibrated hydraulic models for both water distribution systems, a documented inventory of eleven deficiency categories evaluated against UFC 3-600-01, and seven actionable recommendations with ROM cost estimates for the five highest-priority deficiency areas. The high-pressure fire main was found to be generally adequate for the 14 sprinklered buildings it serves, delivering approximately 1,900 gpm at 20 psi residual. The domestic system's fire flow capacity (approximately 1,650 gpm at 20 psi residual) and storage limitations were fully quantified, with a range of capital improvement options — totaling approximately $5.77 million in identified infrastructure investment needs — provided to NAVFAC for capital programming and budgeting. A non-potable water distribution system maintenance program was delivered as an additional study output.
