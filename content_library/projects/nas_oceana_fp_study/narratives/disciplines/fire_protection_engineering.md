Fire protection engineering was the primary technical discipline on this project, leading the evaluation of fire water supply adequacy and UFC 3-600-01 compliance for the Naval Special Warfare Development Group (NSWDG) Compound at NAS Oceana, Dam Neck Annex. The discipline scope encompassed establishing fire flow and fire water demand requirements for approximately 81 buildings across the approximately 200-acre compound; assessing the capability of two independent water distribution systems — a dedicated high-pressure non-potable fire main and the Installation domestic water system — to meet those demands; and identifying deficiencies across eleven categories including water storage reserve, dead-end distribution mains, hydrant spacing, and system configuration.

The high-pressure fire main, served by Fire Pump Station 352 and a 200,000-gallon ground-level storage tank, was found generally adequate for the 14 sprinklered buildings it serves, delivering approximately 1,900 gpm at 20 psi residual. The domestic system, at approximately 1,650 gpm at 20 psi residual with only approximately 50,000 gallons of effective fire storage reserve, was identified as deficient for buildings with fire flow demands exceeding system capacity. Sprinkler system coverage, hydrant placement, dead-end distribution main compliance, and cross-system configuration (including seven high-pressure hydrants drawing from the non-potable fire main) were all evaluated as distinct deficiency themes.

Seven recommendations with ROM cost estimates were developed for the five highest-priority deficiency areas identified by NAVFAC, providing a prioritized capital improvement roadmap for the compound's fire protection infrastructure.
