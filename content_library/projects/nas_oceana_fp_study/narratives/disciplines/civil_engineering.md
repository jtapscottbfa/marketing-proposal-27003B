Civil engineering scope on this project focused on site utilities assessment, distribution system configuration analysis, and infrastructure improvement recommendations for the NSWDG Compound's water distribution networks. The field investigation phase included pipe identification surveys and valve assessments across both the high-pressure non-potable fire main and the Installation domestic water distribution system, documenting existing pipe routing, valve locations, and system configuration across approximately 200 acres and 81 buildings. Dead-end service mains — a recurring civil infrastructure deficiency under UFC 3-600-01 — were mapped and evaluated for compliance in both systems.

The most significant civil infrastructure recommendation arising from the study addressed the dead-end service main serving the Building 250 compound. The Building 250 complex is physically separated from the main NSWDG Compound by Lake Redwing, making it dependent on a single feed connection with no redundancy or looping. The recommended solution — installation of a new 8-inch water main via directional drilling under Lake Redwing, connecting the Building 250 distribution loop to the main Installation water system — was developed with a ROM cost estimate of $897,170. Additional civil recommendations included installation of seven new fire hydrants with supporting distribution piping to correct the most critical hydrant spacing deficiencies across approximately 44 deficient buildings (ROM $1,074,933), and the demolition and replacement of 1960s-era Pump Station 329 with a relocated facility at the north end of the compound (ROM $1,364,097).
