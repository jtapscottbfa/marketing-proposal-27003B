Water resources engineering provided the hydraulic modeling and system performance analysis that formed the quantitative foundation of the fire protection systems study. Calibrated hydraulic models were developed using Bentley WaterGEMS for both water distribution systems serving the NSWDG Compound: the dedicated high-pressure non-potable fire main and the Installation domestic water distribution system. Model calibration was based on field hydrant flow test data collected during the site investigation phase, ensuring simulated system performance reflected actual in-service conditions.

The hydraulic models characterized existing performance across both systems — the high-pressure fire main delivering approximately 1,900 gpm at 20 psi residual; the domestic system delivering approximately 1,650 gpm at 20 psi residual — and evaluated whether those capacities were sufficient to meet UFC 3-600-01 fire flow demand thresholds for each of the approximately 81 buildings within the study area. A critical finding from the storage analysis was that Water Storage Tank 531 (250,000-gallon elevated tank) retains only approximately 50,000 gallons available for fire flow beyond daily domestic demand, establishing the quantitative basis for the domestic system storage augmentation recommendations.

Hydraulic simulation of proposed improvements — including domestic system SCADA pressure set-point increases, new storage tank alternatives (250,000-gallon addition at ROM $2.4M; 450,000-gallon replacement at ROM $3.4M), and pump station replacement — allowed ROM cost estimates to be grounded in modeled performance outcomes. A non-potable water distribution system maintenance program was also developed as a companion deliverable to the hydraulic assessment.
