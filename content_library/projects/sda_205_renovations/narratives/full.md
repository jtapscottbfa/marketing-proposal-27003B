# SDA-205 Repairs and Renovation — Full Narrative

## Project Overview

SDA-205 Repairs and Renovation is a comprehensive renovation of Building SDA-205, a single-story steel-framed warehouse constructed in the early 1940s at Naval Support Activity (NSA) Norfolk, Virginia, located near the intersection of Leutze Boulevard and 9th Street. The facility encompasses approximately 118,029 gross square feet organized into five storage bays classified as Occupancy Group S-1 Storage with accessory Business Group B office areas, and is occupied by the DLA Disposition Service for defense logistics and disposition operations. The project addressed decades of deferred maintenance across all building systems, bringing the facility into compliance with current NAVFAC standards, applicable life safety codes, and energy requirements.

The project was executed as Design-Bid-Build under NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic IDIQ Contract No. N40085-17-D-0347, funded in FY2019, with a program amount of $7,322,400 and an estimated construction cost of $6,780,000. Prime A-E was Dills Architects, P.C. The Final Design Submittal was completed 3 March 2021.

## Scope

**Architectural and finishes.** Renovation scope included resurfacing of approximately 116,000 SF of concrete warehouse flooring, demolition of overhead coiling doors, and new construction of restroom and administrative spaces in Bays 1 and 5. New administrative areas include office, customer service, and conference/training functions. An existing Video Teleconferencing system was relocated to the newly constructed conference/training room. Interior finishes were upgraded throughout renovated areas to current NAVFAC standards.

**Mechanical.** All existing mechanical systems were demolished. Replacement equipment included three heat pump split systems serving office and restroom areas in Bays 1 and 5, providing conditioned heating and cooling for administrative and support spaces. Bay 4 received a new radiant heating system suited to the open-storage warehouse environment. A dedicated exhaust system was provided for restroom and storage room ventilation, and motorized two-position outside air dampers were incorporated for code-compliant ventilation control.

**Electrical.** The existing building was served by two separate electrical services — a 300 KVA and a 225 KVA pad-mounted transformer, each with 800A MCB distribution panels. The design consolidated these into a single service by retaining the 300 KVA transformer and 800A MCB, removing the overhead service lateral for the secondary service, and adding a new 60 HP fire pump service lateral from the 300 KVA transformer. New 208Y/120V branch circuit panelboards were provided in the new administrative spaces. All interior and exterior lighting was demolished and replaced with LED fixtures throughout: high-bay and low-bay fixtures for warehouse areas, recessed troffers for office and restroom spaces, and pendant types as applicable. A wireless lighting control system with daylight harvesting sensors and occupancy controls was implemented, with battery backup inverter systems providing emergency egress lighting.

**Plumbing.** All existing plumbing was demolished in the renovated bays. New systems in Bays 1 and 5 included domestic cold and hot water distribution, sanitary DWV systems, restroom fixtures (floor-mounted water closets, wall-mounted lavatories, urinals, and countertop sinks), electric water heaters, thermostatic mixing valves at lavatories set to 110°F for scald protection, and emergency eyewash stations reflecting the industrial warehouse occupancy. A new 8-inch ductile iron fire main was cut into the existing 9th Street water main to serve the fire protection system, representing a new underground utility connection coordinated with NAVFAC base utilities.

**Fire protection.** A fully automatic supervised wet-pipe and dry-pipe sprinkler system was designed throughout all five bays. Dry-pipe configurations were used where freezing conditions preclude wet-pipe. A new 1,250 gpm / 60 psi electric fire pump was provided because available municipal water supply — measured at 69 psi static, 54 psi residual, and 1,130 gpm during a hydrant flow test conducted 1 September 2020 — was insufficient to meet system demand. A new fire pump room with one-hour fire barrier separation was designed per NFPA 20. The fire alarm and mass notification system provides combined voice evacuation and base-wide MNS capability, with a Federal Signal UVRI transceiver interfacing with the installation's base-wide system and a zoned telegraphic transmitter (DET 16) reporting to the central fire alarm station. Manual pull stations were specified at all exits; carbon monoxide detection was provided in rooms with fuel-fired equipment; duct smoke detectors were designed per NFPA 90A.

**Civil and site.** A new 8-inch ductile iron fire main was connected to the existing water main on 9th Street adjacent to the site. Site civil scope included exterior improvements in support of the renovated facility's operational needs.

**Structural.** Structural assessment of the existing steel-framed structure was performed in support of the renovation design.

**Hazardous materials.** Pre-design surveys identified multiple categories requiring abatement: confirmed asbestos-containing 9-inch green floor tile and mastic (504 SF), lead-based paint, and mercury-containing fluorescent lamps. PCB-containing ballasts were assessed and testing confirmed assumed non-PCB status. Abatement was coordinated with the construction sequence preceding demolition and finish work.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Fire pump sizing and water supply.** The 1 September 2020 hydrant flow test measured static pressure of 69 psi, residual pressure of 54 psi, and flow of 1,130 gpm — a supply profile insufficient to support a full automatic sprinkler system in a 118,029 SF facility without pressure boost. A 1,250 gpm / 60 psi electric fire pump was designed to supplement available supply, requiring a new dedicated service lateral from the 300 KVA transformer and a fire pump room with one-hour fire barrier separation meeting NFPA 20 requirements.

**Electrical service consolidation.** The legacy dual-service configuration — two separately derived 11.5 kV transformer services with separate distribution panels — required consolidation for code compliance and long-term operational maintainability. The design retained the higher-capacity 300 KVA service, eliminated the secondary overhead lateral, and integrated the fire pump load on the retained transformer without requiring a new utility service or transformer.

**Base-wide mass notification integration.** The project required integration with NSA Norfolk's base-wide Federal Signal mass notification system, driving the fire alarm system architecture toward a UVRI transceiver and zoned telegraphic transmitter configuration to support both building-level voice evacuation and base-level alerting from a single system.

**Hazardous materials coordination.** Multiple hazmat categories in an occupied warehouse with an active defense logistics mission required careful abatement phasing coordinated with mechanical demolition, electrical fixture replacement, and floor resurfacing operations to avoid cross-contamination and minimize mission disruption.

## Outcomes

The Final Design Submittal for SDA-205 was completed 3 March 2021 under NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic oversight, delivering a fully coordinated multi-discipline design package for a $6.78 million construction project. The renovated facility provides the DLA Disposition Service with updated building systems across all five warehouse bays meeting current life safety, energy efficiency, and operational requirements.
