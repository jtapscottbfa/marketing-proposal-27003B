# Melwood Greenhouses — Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The Melwood Greenhouses project entailed the electrical engineering design for three new commercial greenhouse structures at a horticultural facility in Nanjemoy, Maryland (Charles County). The owner, Melwood, operates the site as an agricultural program. The project involved new-construction electrical infrastructure serving two 30×100 ft greenhouses (GH1 and GH2) and one 30×50 ft greenhouse (GH3), all sited in a row with an existing wood shop and an existing greenhouse structure to the north. The design team served as prime engineering consultant and produced a complete five-sheet signed-and-sealed electrical drawing set (project 23-017, final sealed August 3, 2023), coordinated with the general contractor and electrical subcontractor.

## Scope

The electrical scope encompassed the full power distribution system from the utility point of entry through all equipment branch circuits within the three greenhouse structures:

- **Utility service:** Coordination with SMECO (Southern Maryland Electric Cooperative) for a new pad-mount transformer and metered service. Two 4-inch empty conduits were stubbed for SMECO's future use.
- **Main distribution:** A 175A, 240/120V, 1-phase, 3-wire, 22 kAIC, NEMA 3R main distribution panel (MDP) mounted on a 2×6 pressure-treated lumber backboard on concrete-set posts.
- **Sub-panel distribution:** Three 100A, 240/120V, 1-phase, 3-wire, NEMA 3R sub-panels (GH1, GH2, GH3), each fed by a dedicated feeder from MDP.
- **Emergency standby power:** A 60kW (nominal) propane-fueled standby generator — drawings reference 41kW prime/continuous rating — with a 300A automatic transfer switch (ATS-1), generator battery charger, and generator jacket heater.
- **Agricultural equipment connections:** Unit heaters (160K BTU at 120V in GH1 and GH2; 80K BTU at 120V in GH3), dehumidifiers (240V, 12.4A each), 24-inch and 36-inch exhaust fans, 42-inch exhaust fans (GH1 and GH2), vent motors, HAR circulation fans, intake dampers, and screen motors — each sized and scheduled in the panelboard schedules (sheet E-601).
- **Convenience power:** GFCI weatherproof 20A 125VAC duplex receptacles distributed throughout all three structures.
- **Sump pumps:** Electrical connections for sump pumps (3.0A 120V) in each greenhouse; plumbing by others.
- **Specifications:** A full electrical specification was incorporated directly on drawing sheet E-801, covering submittals, wiring methods, conduit (GRS indoor/outdoor; PVC SCH 40 per plans), receptacle installation, grounding, equipment connections, and field quality control (600V wiring test, GFCI test). Minimum branch circuit conductors: 12 AWG.

## Role on Project

The design team acted as the prime engineering consultant providing full electrical engineering design services. Deliverables included the signed-and-sealed five-sheet electrical drawing set (E-001 legend/riser, E-101 site plan, E-102 floor plan, E-601 panelboard schedules, E-801 specifications), coordination with SMECO for utility service, and supporting documentation for the electrical subcontractor bid process. The project was delivered under a design-bid-build delivery method.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Single-phase agricultural load balancing.** The three greenhouses operate extensive climate-control and agricultural equipment loads simultaneously, all served by a single 1-phase utility service. The panelboard design balanced connected loads across Phase A and Phase B: MDP showed a total connected load of approximately 134.5A (Phase A) and 96.3A (Phase B), with connected ampere totals of 140.5A and 104.6A respectively. Separate 100A sub-panels for each greenhouse permitted independent load management per structure.

**Emergency power continuity for agricultural operations.** Greenhouse climate-control systems — heating, ventilation, dehumidification — must operate continuously to protect crops during utility outages. The design incorporated a propane-fueled standby generator and automatic transfer switch to maintain power to the MDP, ensuring all three greenhouse sub-panels remain energized during grid interruptions. Propane fuel was selected over diesel, presumably for site logistics and availability in a rural Charles County location.

**Weatherproof design for agricultural environment.** All panelboards are NEMA 3R rated for outdoor/damp locations. Receptacles are GFCI weatherproof throughout. The equipment support structure uses pressure-treated lumber on concrete-set posts, appropriate for the outdoor/semi-exposed equipment yard environment.

**Generator rating interpretation.** Drawing sheets E-001 and E-102 reference a "41kW propane generator," while the electrical subcontractor bid specification and equipment proposal describe a 60kW Generac standby unit. This discrepancy likely reflects a prime/continuous rating (41kW) versus nameplate standby rating (60kW) distinction, or a propane derating from the natural-gas nameplate. The drawings were produced as the design-intent documents and the equipment proposal as a procurement document; both refer to the same physical unit.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a complete electrical engineering design for three new greenhouse structures. The signed-and-sealed drawing set was completed August 3, 2023. The electrical subcontractor (Brown Electric Inc.) submitted a bid of $114,500 for the installed electrical scope. The design supports full agricultural climate-control operations — heating, ventilation, humidity control, and continuous power via standby generation — for the owner's horticultural program. The project was delivered as a standalone engagement under a design-bid-build contract vehicle.
