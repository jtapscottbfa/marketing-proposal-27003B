## Project Overview

The HRSD Nansemond Treatment Plant Struvite Recovery Facility Improvements and Digester Capacity Upgrade is a capital improvement project for the Hampton Roads Sanitation District (HRSD) at the Nansemond Treatment Plant in Suffolk, Virginia. The project addressed two interrelated facility enhancement goals: improvements to the struvite recovery facility and expansion of the plant's anaerobic digester capacity. Both components are characteristic of a regional wastewater utility investing in resource recovery capability and long-term treatment throughput at an existing facility.

HRSD is a regional sanitation authority organized under Virginia state law, serving the greater Hampton Roads region. The Nansemond Treatment Plant is one of HRSD's principal wastewater treatment facilities serving the area.

## Scope of Work

The scope documented in available project records covers electrical engineering design for power distribution serving six scrubber exhaust fan motors that form part of the struvite recovery facility improvements. Each of the six fans — designated Fans 1-A, 1-B, 2-A, 2-B, 3-A, and 3-B — required a dedicated 480-volt, three-phase motor feeder sized for 150 amperes. Conductor selection was standardized across all six feeders at 1/0 AWG copper THHN conductors in 2-inch PVC Schedule 80 conduit, conforming to specification section 26 05 19 (low-voltage cable).

Cable-pull and voltage-drop calculations were produced using Southwire SIMpull design tools and sealed as design-phase deliverables. Feeder routing lengths vary with equipment placement across the facility:

| Fan | Feeder Length | Voltage Drop | NEC 3% Limit |
|-----|--------------|--------------|--------------|
| 1-A | 260 ft       | < 3%         | Compliant    |
| 1-B | 260 ft       | < 3%         | Compliant    |
| 2-A | 260 ft       | < 3%         | Compliant    |
| 2-B | 305 ft       | 1.93%        | Compliant    |
| 3-A | 260 ft       | < 3%         | Compliant    |
| 3-B | 320 ft       | 2.02%        | Compliant    |

The two longest feeder runs (Fans 2-B and 3-B) received dedicated voltage-drop calculations documenting the compliance basis. Both returned compliant results and were carried forward at the standard 1/0 AWG conductor size.

## Role on Project

The design team served as engineer of record for the electrical engineering scope, producing and sealing the engineering calculation submittals for the scrubber exhaust fan power distribution. The presence of sealed calculations indicates design-phase completion; the design team's role was that of prime A/E consultant for the electrical scope, with BFA's seal applied to the feeder design submittals.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Variable feeder lengths and voltage drop compliance.** The scrubber exhaust fans are distributed across the facility, with routing distances that range from a standard 260 feet to 320 feet for the most remote equipment. The two longest circuits — Fan 2-B at 305 feet and Fan 3-B at 320 feet — exceeded the typical run length and required explicit voltage-drop verification to confirm compliance with the NEC 3% maximum. Separate Southwire Voltage Drop Calculator analyses were prepared for each of these circuits and submitted alongside the cable-pull calculations as supporting documentation. Both confirmed compliant results (1.93% and 2.02%, respectively), allowing the standard 1/0 AWG conductor size to be maintained throughout the feeder schedule without upsizing.

**Annotation of marginal circuits.** The sealed submittals for Fans 2-B and 3-B include explicit annotated notes referencing the supporting voltage-drop calculations and confirming that the results fall below the NEC limit. This approach — sealing the primary calculation with a cross-reference annotation to the supplemental voltage-drop output — reflects good engineering documentation practice for circuits whose run lengths warrant additional scrutiny.

## Outcomes

Engineering calculations were completed and sealed for all six scrubber exhaust fan feeders. All circuits were designed within the NEC voltage-drop limit, including the two longer runs that required supplemental analysis. Specifications were developed in accordance with section 26 05 19. The submitted design documents support the power distribution requirements for the struvite recovery scrubber exhaust systems at the Nansemond Treatment Plant.
