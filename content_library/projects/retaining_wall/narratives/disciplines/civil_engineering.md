Civil engineering services for this project encompassed site grading, construction access road design, utility coordination, and plan-and-profile documentation across both scope elements — retaining wall replacement and ESR access road. The civil team evaluated two grading configurations for the retaining wall replacement: Option 1 (1:1 slope layback) and Option 2 (2:1 slope layback), examining the trade-offs between construction excavation footprint and slope stability within the constrained corridor between the Hall A berm and adjacent underground structures.

A 15-foot-wide temporary construction access road was designed along the west side of Building 104 to support contractor mobilization and material delivery during retaining wall construction. The civil team also developed the permanent access road alignment between Buildings ESR1 and ESR2, restoring large-vehicle access to an area blocked by surface-routed utility cooling pipes.

Utility coordination was a central component of civil scope. Existing underground infrastructure crossing the retaining wall alignment — including an 8-inch ABS sanitary sewer, a 12-inch DIP waterline, a 6-inch DIP waterline, a 15-inch RCP storm drain, and a 16-inch cold piping main transfer line — required careful depth confirmation, clearance documentation, and management in the site grading and plan-profile drawings. Station-based plan-and-profile drawings (sheet C300) documented critical crossing elevations and stationing for contractor reference. The grading plans also explicitly flagged the Jefferson Lab Radcon requirement that radiation control review and approval be obtained before any excavation of material inside the facility fence line.
