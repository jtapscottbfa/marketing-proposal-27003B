Geotechnical engineering services supported the foundation design and excavation planning for the replacement retaining wall system at the Hall A berm. The geotechnical scope included planning of a subsurface investigation program — proposed soil boring extraction locations are shown on the SC-100 site plan — to characterize soil conditions relevant to retaining wall foundation design and slope stability evaluation for the two grading options (1:1 and 2:1 slope layback) under consideration.

A critical geotechnical coordination item involved confirming the depth and clearance of existing utilities adjacent to the retaining wall alignment. The geotechnical and civil teams confirmed that the 8-inch ABS sanitary sewer and 12-inch DIP waterline west of the existing wooden wall were at least 4 feet below the toe of the slope. Engagement of a private utility locator was recommended to independently confirm the depth, location, and size of all underground utilities prior to construction excavation.

The geotechnical scope also addressed a site constraint unique to the DOE particle accelerator context: soil stockpiling procedures required investigation of material handling and testing protocols in the event of radiation contamination in subsurface fill materials — a condition flagged on the grading plans and subject to Jefferson Lab Radcon review. Coordination with Radcon was identified as a prerequisite for any subsurface work inside the facility fence line. Geotechnical investigation recommendations informed the continuous reinforced spread footing design specified by the structural team, and the discipline provided input on construction sequencing to protect adjacent utility pipe foundations from potential undermining during the retaining wall excavation.
