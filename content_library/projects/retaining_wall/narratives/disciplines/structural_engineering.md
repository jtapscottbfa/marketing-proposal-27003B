Structural engineering services for this project centered on design of a replacement retaining wall system to succeed the over-25-year-old deteriorated wooden wall at the lower end of the Hall A berm. The structural team's assessment of the existing wall informed the determination that full replacement was warranted. The selected design is a cantilevered reinforced concrete retaining wall, founded on a continuous reinforced spread footing approximately 18 inches thick and 6 feet wide. The wall stem was designed at approximately 16 inches in reinforced concrete. Drainage was addressed by specifying a drain board on the soil-side face with drain holes through the footing base to minimize hydrostatic pressure against the wall.

The structural team evaluated the excavation envelope required for wall construction — 8 feet of excavation around the wall perimeter, encompassing a 6-foot-wide concrete footer zone and 6 feet of clear construction access — and identified that foundations supporting adjacent utility piping could be undermined by this excavation. The structural narrative on sheet SC-100 directed the structural engineer to provide input to the geotechnical and civil disciplines regarding protective measures for those utility pipe foundations, establishing a multi-discipline coordination protocol for the construction phase.

Structural scope also addressed coordination with the geotechnical investigation program (soil borings per SC-100) and input on construction sequencing near the underground Hall A structure, where the ground floor elevation and underground truck ramp were in close proximity to the retaining wall construction corridor.
