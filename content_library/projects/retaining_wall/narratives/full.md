# Hall A Retaining Wall and ESR Access Road — Jefferson Lab

## Project Overview

The design team was engaged to provide multi-discipline architect-engineer services for two related site infrastructure improvements at Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab), a U.S. Department of Energy research facility in Newport News, Virginia. The primary scope was replacement of a deteriorated wooden retaining wall at the lower end of the berm on the east side of Hall A, one of the facility's primary experimental halls. "The wall is over 25 years old and has deteriorated, failed in some areas and needs to be replaced." A secondary scope encompassed design of a permanent access road in the area between Buildings ESR1 and ESR2, which had become inaccessible to larger vehicles due to utility cooling pipes routed across the surface.

The engagement was structured as a multi-task-order contract under base contract JSA 21-C0683, spanning Task Order 004 (concept scope, April 2022 RFQ), Task Order 011 (concept design deliverable), and Task Order 012 (detailed design deliverable). The team served as prime design consultant under a federal A-E indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity contract vehicle, with the project executed as design-bid-build. Jefferson Lab's Design Project Manager (SOTR) was Suresh Chandra.

## Scope

The design work encompassed civil site design, structural engineering, geotechnical engineering, and electrical coordination across both scope elements. For the retaining wall, the design team evaluated two slope layback configurations — Option 1 (1:1 slope) and Option 2 (2:1 slope) — to determine the optimal trade-off between construction excavation footprint and cut-slope stability, given the proximity of existing underground utilities and the underground Hall A structure. The selected design replaces the existing wooden wall with a cantilevered reinforced concrete retaining wall system founded on a continuous reinforced spread footing approximately 18 inches thick and 6 feet wide, with drain board on the soil-side face and drainage holes through the base to minimize hydrostatic pressure.

For the access road scope, the team designed a 15-foot-wide construction access road along the west side of Building 104 and a permanent road alignment to restore large-vehicle access between the ESR buildings. Plan-and-profile drawings documented critical crossing elevations and stationing for existing utilities within the construction corridor.

Cost estimates prepared under the RFQ scope carried design contingencies of 10–25% and a construction contingency of 5%, consistent with concept-level design.

## Role on Project

The team served as prime consultant with responsibility for multi-discipline coordination and deliverable production through concept and detailed design phases. Civil, structural, geotechnical, and electrical disciplines were all represented on the design team. Drawing file evidence indicates a civil/structural subconsultant (VHB, project 58665.02) contributed to the concept design deliverable, though the formal teaming structure is not captured in the available source records.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Dense underground utility network.** The site is an active particle accelerator research campus with a complex underground infrastructure including sanitary sewer, domestic water, chilled water, cooling water, cryogenic distribution, deionized water, gas, communications ductbanks, and fire protection lines — all crossing the retaining wall alignment within a constrained corridor. Critical clearance requirements were established: the 8-inch ABS sanitary sewer and 12-inch DIP waterline west of the existing wall were confirmed at least 4 feet below the toe of the slope; the fire line required a minimum 4-foot clearance below the bottom of the new retaining wall. Engagement of a private utility locator was recommended to confirm depth, location, and size of all underground lines prior to construction.

**Radiation control constraints.** Jefferson Lab's Radcon protocols require review and approval of any excavation inside the facility fence line, and soil stockpiling procedures must account for the possibility of radiation contamination in subsurface fill materials. This materially constrained the construction access and staging approach, and the construction documents explicitly flagged the Radcon review requirement on the grading plans.

**Mission-critical facility systems.** Several electrical and mechanical systems adjacent to the work area are mission-critical to accelerator operations. The existing air-cooled chiller in the vicinity required a temporary generator plan to ensure uninterrupted operation during excavation; the existing exhaust fan required continuous operation throughout construction; and a 480V, 3-phase circuit to an air-cooled condensing unit was designed to be temporarily capped at defined limits during excavation and reinstalled upon completion of the retaining wall and access road.

**Foundation protection for adjacent infrastructure.** The structural narrative identified that foundations supporting existing utility piping adjacent to the work area could be undermined by the retaining wall excavation — an 8-foot-deep excavation envelope around the wall perimeter encompassing a 6-foot-wide concrete footer zone and 6 feet of clear construction access. The design called for the structural engineer to provide direct input to the geotechnical and civil disciplines to address the risk to adjacent utility pipe foundations.

## Outcomes

The project advanced from concept scope (RFQ, April 2022) through concept design and detailed design deliverables, with the concept design drawing set completed in August 2023. The team's design replaced a failed and deteriorating wooden retaining wall with a durable reinforced concrete system designed for long-term performance in a constrained, utility-dense, radiation-controlled active research facility. The project is recorded as complete for the design consultant's scope of services.
