The design team provided architect-engineer services for the replacement of a deteriorated wooden retaining wall at the Hall A experimental hall berm at Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab), Newport News, Virginia — a United States Department of Energy research facility. The project also encompassed design of a permanent access road between Buildings ESR1 and ESR2, an area that had become inaccessible to larger service vehicles due to surface-routed utility cooling pipes.

The engagement was structured across three sequential task orders under base contract JSA 21-C0683: Task Order 004 (concept scope), Task Order 011 (concept design deliverable), and Task Order 012 (detailed design deliverable), with the team serving as prime design consultant throughout. The design solution replaced the over-25-year-old, partially failed wooden wall with a cantilevered reinforced concrete retaining wall system on a continuous reinforced spread footing, evaluated in two grading configurations — 1:1 and 2:1 slope layback — to balance excavation footprint against constructability constraints.

The site's active particle accelerator environment introduced significant coordination requirements: a dense underground utility network including cryogenic, chilled water, deionized water, sanitary, and communications infrastructure required careful protection during construction; radiation control (Radcon) protocols governed any excavation inside the facility fence line; and mission-critical mechanical and electrical systems required continuous operation throughout the construction sequence. Civil, structural, geotechnical, and electrical disciplines contributed to the concept and detailed design deliverables.
