Mechanical engineering services for the Cath Lab Room 1 renovation at the Ralph H. Johnson VA Medical Center centered on the replacement and modification of the air handling unit serving the cath lab suite and associated support spaces. AHU-18, a VAV-system air handler, was the primary subject: the mechanical field investigation conducted October 4-5, 2023 documented the unit's ductwork routing, return air fan condition (identified as potentially reusable), chilled and hot water piping connections, supply fan VFD, and relief air damper configuration. Adjacent units AHU-16, AHU-17, and AHU-19 — located in rooms AA237A and AA231 — were documented as part of the existing-conditions survey to establish system boundaries and sequencing constraints.

The mechanical scope required compliance with the VA Surgical and Endovascular Services Design Guide, Section 4.16, which prescribes pressure relationships, air change rates, temperature and humidity tolerances, and HVAC requirements for the Cardiac Catheterization Laboratory room type. Each room within the cath lab suite was required to have separate DDC controls; the specification called for the Johnson Controls DDC/BAS system to govern the VAV distribution. The mechanical team coordinated HVAC interface requirements with the Siemens ARTIS Icono equipment manufacturer reference drawings, which identified ceiling clearances and service access zones affecting supply air duct routing.

Named mechanical personnel included Patrick Propst, PE (Senior Mechanical Engineer), William Wisilosky (Mechanical Design Engineer), and Richard O'Berry, CPD, GPD (Senior Mechanical Designer), all of whom contributed to field investigation documentation and design development for this occupied federal medical facility.
