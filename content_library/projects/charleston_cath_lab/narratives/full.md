# Renovate Cardiac Catheterization Laboratory Room 1
## Ralph H. Johnson VA Medical Center — Charleston, SC

---

### Project Overview

The renovation of Cardiac Catheterization Laboratory Room 1 at the Ralph H. Johnson VA Medical Center in Charleston, South Carolina addressed the comprehensive upgrade of an interventional procedure suite and its associated support spaces within an occupied federal healthcare facility. The project scope encompassed approximately 1,500 square feet on the second floor of the medical center, covering the procedure room (Cath Lab 1, room AA205), the control room (AA219), the cardiology computer room (AA219A), equipment rooms, semi-restricted corridors, and the interconnected mechanical and electrical infrastructure serving these spaces. The programmed construction budget was $1,500,000, with a 271-calendar-day design period of performance measured from Notice to Proceed.

The project was structured as an Architect-Engineer qualifications-based procurement under solicitation 36C24723R0016 (VA project 534-CSI-714), set aside entirely for VA-verified Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Businesses under the VA Veterans First Contracting Program (VAAR 852.219-73). Design scope included a base CLIN for full design services and an optional CLIN for up to five construction period site visits. The design was required to comply with the VA Surgical and Endovascular Services Design Guide, Section 4.16 (April 2016, revised November 2018), which establishes the 850-NSF room template and full room data sheet for the Cardiac Catheterization Laboratory (XCCE1) and Interventional Radiology Laboratory (XACR1) room types.

---

### Scope

The renovation addressed the full building systems serving the cath lab suite. Key scope elements included:

**Mechanical / HVAC.** Replacement and modification of AHU-18, the primary air handling unit serving the Cath Lab area via a VAV distribution system. The mechanical field investigation documented AHU-18's ductwork routing, return air fan (potentially reusable), relief air damper, chilled and hot water piping connections, and the supply fan VFD. The scope required the room to have separate DDC controls, with Johnson Controls DDC/BAS specified. Adjacent air handling units AHU-16, AHU-17, and AHU-19 were documented as part of the existing-conditions survey.

**Electrical.** The electrical field investigations documented an extensive distribution network in the rooms surrounding the cath lab. Key existing equipment included: an automatic transfer switch (ATS-EE, 480/277V) in room AA229; additional automatic transfer switches (ATS-LS, ATS-CC) in room AA230; a 480/277V panelboard (2HEDP1), motor control centers (MCC-NA, MCC-EA), and VFDs for AHU-16 through AHU-19 in adjacent mechanical and electrical rooms; an isolated power panelboard (ISOTROL-2, 120V) in the corridor; and 277V isolated power panelboards (2INP1, 2ICP1, 2ICP2) serving the cath lab suite. The October 5, 2023 investigation of room AA205 (Cath Lab 1), the control room, and the equipment room found no electrical deficiencies in the active equipment serving the procedure space. Decommissioned electrical equipment in room AA203 was documented as offline.

**Plumbing / Medical Gas.** Medical gas systems were required per the VA Design Guide and NFPA 99, with oxygen, medical air, medical vacuum, and nitrogen distributed from a boom at the procedure table. Medical gas shutoff boxes were located in the semi-restricted corridor outside the cath lab per code. Zone Valve Boxes were required in the semi-restricted corridor per NFPA 99 requirements for healthcare facilities.

**Fire Protection.** The existing fire alarm system was a Gamewell E-3 Series installation; the scope required any new fire alarm work to be performed by a Gamewell-authorized contractor. Duct smoke detectors were documented on supply and return ducts for all four air handling units (AHU-16 through AHU-19). The wet sprinkler system was classified as Ordinary Hazard Group 1. Fire Protection design was subcontracted to Poole Fire Protection.

**Construction scope** additionally included demolition of existing finishes, hazmat abatement (flagged in the SOW), drywall and finishes, communications rough-in, and coordination of the Siemens ARTIS Icono cardiac catheterization equipment installation — the latter requiring coordination with manufacturer reference drawings for floor loading, electrical connections, medical gas connections, and ceiling suspension clearances.

### Role on Project

The MEP engineering team participated as subconsultant to prime A/E consultant Moon Tree LLC (Oklahoma City, OK), an SDVOSB holding the prime contract. The MEP scope represented approximately 40 percent of the A/E contract value. The subconsultant team provided mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, plumbing engineering, and QA/QC services. Named MEP personnel included a Senior Mechanical Engineer (PE), a Senior Mechanical Designer and Certified Plumbing Designer (CPD/GPD), a Mechanical Design Engineer, a Senior Electrical Engineer (PE), and an Electrical Designer and project manager (EIT).

Field investigations were conducted in October 2023, with separate visits for electrical systems (October 4 and October 5) and mechanical systems (October 4-5), documenting existing conditions in the rooms directly affected by the renovation and the supporting mechanical and electrical infrastructure. These investigations produced the factual basis for the design approach in an occupied facility where demolition sequencing and system continuity had to be carefully managed.

The team structured around the VA Design Guide Section 4.16 room template, which prescribes architectural dimensions, equipment list (by JSN), MEP requirements, and the room data sheet for the cath lab room type. The medical equipment planning subconsultant (Go Energistics, LLC, Southlake, TX) coordinated the specialized ARTIS Icono equipment interface. Physical security and access control were provided by Force Protect Security Consultants. Cost estimating, constructability review, and scheduling support were provided by O'Connor Construction Management.

### Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Occupied facility with active procedural suite.** The Ralph H. Johnson VAMC cath lab was operational during the pre-design phase. Field investigations were conducted in live rooms where electrical equipment was energized and in service. The scope required phased construction to maintain continuity of care. The SOW called out phased construction and temporary service requirements, and the construction scope included a five-visit construction period services option.

**Complex electrical distribution in adjacent infrastructure.** The electrical field investigations revealed an unusually dense electrical distribution infrastructure in the rooms immediately adjacent to the cath lab (rooms AA229, AA230, AA231, and the corridor rooms). Multiple automatic transfer switches, motor control centers, isolated power panelboards, and VFDs serving not only the cath lab but the broader floor were concentrated in a tight cluster. Coordinating new work with this infrastructure without disrupting adjacent systems required detailed as-found documentation as the design basis.

**Medical equipment interface — ARTIS Icono.** The Siemens ARTIS Icono biplane cardiac catheterization system has demanding physical requirements: structural floor loading, ceiling suspension, specific electrical service provisions (including isolated power), and medical gas connections via procedure boom. Manufacturer reference drawings (labeled "NOT FOR CONSTRUCTION") were provided in the solicitation package as the basis for coordinating these interfaces. The MEP design had to translate manufacturer interface requirements into VA-standard construction documents.

**VA Healthcare Design Guide compliance.** The design was required to comply with the VA Surgical and Endovascular Services Design Guide, Section 4.16. The 850-NSF room template prescribes specific HVAC requirements (pressure relationships, air changes per hour, temperature and humidity ranges), medical gas outlets by type, electrical isolated power requirements, and finishes. The project's 1,500-SF scope (which includes support spaces beyond the procedure room itself) required the VA template to be applied to the procedure room while engineering the support spaces to interface correctly with it.

**Hazmat abatement.** The SOW identified hazardous materials abatement as part of the construction scope for this existing, older building. Abatement scope and sequencing would affect MEP system demolition and replacement timelines; this was flagged as a coordination item in the pre-design phase.

### Outcomes

At the time of the available source documents, the project was in the active design phase following the 2023 field investigations. The source documents reflect a solicitation and early design phase capture; awarded contract values, final design deliverables, construction contract award, and project completion data are not present in the available record. The field investigations confirmed existing conditions, identified no deficiencies in the active electrical equipment directly serving the cath lab suite, and documented the mechanical system inventory as the basis for HVAC replacement and modification design. The procurement was completed as an SDVOSB set-aside with a fully SDVOSB-dominant team.
