## Project Overview

The Baghdad Diplomatic Support Center (BDSC) – Upgrade Fire Protection System project provided multi-discipline engineering design services for fire protection system upgrades at a large U.S. State Department overseas compound in Baghdad, Iraq. The facility is a 147-hectare (364-acre) compound adjacent to Baghdad International Airport (BIAP), originally acquired by the State Department from the U.S. Department of Defense. The project was competitively solicited by the Bureau of Overseas Buildings Operations (OBO) under solicitation number 19AQMM23RBAGHDADFIRE and executed as a design-build contract with a total period of performance of 24 months (8 months design, 7 months procurement and shipping, 9 months construction).

## Scope

The project encompassed fire protection system upgrades across eight existing buildings representing a diverse cross-section of compound facility types: two passenger air movement terminals (Buildings 278 and 279), an office and warehouse building (Building 602 – the Sather Building), two aircraft hangars (Buildings 606 and 610 – the Kirby Hangars), a dining facility (Building 306, DFAC, as an optional CLIN), temporary housing (the Sully Compound), and a modular firing range. The project also included design of a new fire pump house to supplement the existing one.

Core scope elements included:

**Fire alarm replacement.** New fire alarm systems were designed for all eight buildings, with alarm, supervisory, and trouble signals transmitted via fiber optic network to the existing Tactical Operations Center (TOC) in Building 602. The existing Notifier fire alarm system in Building 602 was extended to cover the upgraded scope. Infrared (IR3) flame detectors were specified for the large open-area volumes in Buildings 606 and 610 (aircraft hangars), and smoke detectors were designed for sleeping units in the Sully Compound temporary housing.

**Wet pipe sprinkler systems.** New wet pipe sprinkler systems were designed throughout Buildings 278, 279, 306 (optional CLIN 003), 602, 606, and 610, with replacement of existing upright sprinkler heads as part of the full-building re-installation.

**Fire pump house and underground fire mains.** A new fire pump house was designed with a redundant pump configuration, with the existing pump house retained as a secondary unit. The existing underground fire main from Building 241 to Building 278 was scheduled for replacement; new underground fire mains were designed to extend service to Buildings 306, 606, and 610. New fire hydrants were provided at each new branch connection. Four existing 250,000-gallon water storage tanks were incorporated into an inspection, testing, and maintenance (ITM) program per NFPA 25.

**AFFF removal and foam suppression.** The existing aqueous film-forming foam (AFFF) system in Building 610 was removed under CLIN 005, with PFAS disposal per ITRC guidance. An optional scope addition (CLIN 004) provided new low-level high-expansion foam suppression systems in both Buildings 606 and 610 for aircraft hangar protection per NFPA 409.

**Supporting MEP.** HVAC mini-split air conditioning units were sized for both fire pump houses using TRACE 700 thermal load calculations: approximately 1.2 tons (14.9 MBh) for the existing fire pump house (195 SF) and approximately 1.6 tons (19.0 MBh) for the new PAX Terminal fire pump house (406 SF), based on Baghdad outdoor design conditions of 84°F dry-bulb / 62°F wet-bulb summer and 33°F winter. Electrical design included new connections to existing panelboards for fire alarm control panels and mini-split units, new underground conduit and fiber optic cable for the compound fire alarm network, and panelboard replacements as required. Interior lighting in Building 602's northwest and northeast corridors was replaced with Lithonia 2GTL2 2×2 LED fixtures at 18.39 W, achieving approximately 17.2 and 17.4 footcandles average respectively — associated with ceiling work required for sprinkler installation.

All design conformed to OBO International Building Code (OBO-IBC), OBO International Code Supplements (OBO-ICS), and applicable NFPA standards including 11, 13, 20, 24, 25, 70, 72, and 409.

## Role on Project

The design team served as subconsultant engineer to Futron, the prime Design-Build contractor holding the contract with DOS OBO. Design services were performed under a Statement of Work between Futron and the design firm (operating as FPLS Engineering) dated April 15, 2024. The design team's scope under the subconsultant SOW included production of all design drawings and specifications across fire protection, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing disciplines; preparation and submission of OBO-required design phase deliverables (CD-1, CD-3, and DIFC); QFPE-stamped fire protection submittals; BIM/CAD deliverables per OBO Deliverable Requirements (ODR suite); participation in Integrated Design Reviews (IDR) with OBO Subject Matter Experts; and coordination of the AFFF/PFAS disposal plan per ITRC guidance.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Overseas compound operating environment with security requirements.** The BDSC is an active diplomatic support compound in Baghdad, Iraq, requiring Secret-level personnel clearances, SPOT database registration, Electronic Country Clearance (eCC) processing, and Iraqi visa processing with 30-to-60-day lead times for all personnel traveling to post. DOS/HTSOS security training was mandatory prior to mobilization. The project also contained classified scope elements per the prime SOW, requiring coordination with appropriately cleared personnel for those work packages.

**Design-build delivery under OBO's IDR framework.** DOS OBO's design-build contracting structure required the design subconsultant to participate in Integrated Design Reviews with OBO Subject Matter Experts at each design phase gate. Deliverables required QFPE stamping and OBO-formatted documentation meeting the full ODR suite — a more structured and document-intensive process than standard commercial design delivery. The team produced submittals conforming to these requirements through the CD-3 phase.

**Diverse building typology across a large compound.** The eight in-scope buildings span significantly different construction types — pre-engineered metal buildings, warehouse structures, aircraft hangars, modular units, and passenger terminals — each with different fire protection and detection requirements. Hangar-specific requirements per NFPA 409 drove scope evolution from the baseline prime SOW (April 2023) through additional CLINs in the sub-SOW (April 2024), adding IR3 flame detectors and high-expansion foam suppression systems in Buildings 606 and 610.

**AFFF removal and PFAS regulatory compliance.** Building 610 contained an existing AFFF system with PFAS-containing materials. The project required development and execution of a PFAS disposal plan per ITRC guidance — adding environmental coordination scope to an already complex fire suppression replacement program.

## Outcomes

The design team advanced the project through the CD-3 submittal milestone in April 2025 and was progressing toward the Design Issued for Construction (DIFC) phase within the contractual 8-month design period. The fire protection upgrade program positions the BDSC compound to meet OBO International Building Code standards across all eight buildings, replacing aging or inadequate fire protection systems with code-compliant wet sprinkler, fire alarm, foam suppression, and underground fire main infrastructure. Contract values, award dates, and final construction completion are not documented in the source record available to the extractor.
