# Building 111 Renovations — Full Narrative

## Project Overview

Building 111 at Naval Support Facility (NSF) Indian Head, Maryland is a three-story historic structure — basement, main floor, and second floor — originally constructed in 1899 as the installation's powerhouse. Over its 125-year history the building transitioned through multiple uses before becoming the primary shop and administrative home for NAVFAC Public Works Facilities' in-house workforce. By the time this renovation was initiated, the building carried the full burden of that history: failed and abandoned infrastructure throughout, water intrusion damaging interior finishes from the exterior walls and a leaking roof, outdated HVAC equipment causing repeated flooding, aging electrical systems with obsolete wiring, and suspected asbestos-containing materials (ACM) and lead-based paint (LBP) in multiple areas.

The renovation project — documented under NAVFAC eProjects# 1803740 and BFA Project #24-040C — was structured to address all of these conditions systematically, delivering a safe, modern, and energy-efficient facility for the public works workforce at Indian Head.

## Scope of Work

The project was procured through a Performance Work Statement (PWS) issued by NAVFAC Public Works Department South Potomac (dated February 29, 2024) and structured as a base bid with four independently exercisable options, to be executed in order of precedence over a 212-day period of performance:

- **Base Bid — Building Repair and HAZMAT Remediation:** Prior to any systems work, an AHERA building survey was required, followed by brick repointing and window infill repair on the east front facade, window replacement on both floors, and abatement of asbestos floor tile in six offices and two hallways, asbestos-containing transite wall panels, and any additional ACM or LBP identified during the survey.

- **Option 1 — Electrical Service and Distribution:** Full demolition of the existing obsolete panels and transformers in the basement locker room, shop area, and second floor. Replacement with a new 480V feed and panel for the shop area, a new transformer and 120/208V panel in the shop, a new sub-panel on the second floor, and a new disconnect, transformer, and 120/208V panel in the locker room. New LED lighting throughout the building and new code-compliant switches and receptacles. BX wiring to be replaced where feasible.

- **Option 2 — HVAC Replacement (BFA Design Scope):** Complete removal of all existing HVAC systems and replacement with six new split-system heat pump air handlers and condensing units zoned across the building's occupied areas. Ductless mini-split systems for the conference room, offices, bathrooms, work leader office, second-floor training room and utility room, and basement office and locker room. Four new exhaust systems serving the Men's restroom, Private restroom, future Women's restroom, and basement bathroom.

- **Option 3 — Plumbing:** New fixtures in the first-floor Private restroom; new fixtures (countertops, sinks, faucets, toilets, urinals) and a new shower in the basement locker room; construction of a new Women's restroom on the first floor (two toilet stalls, two lavatories) to support current and future workforce needs in Buildings 111 and 113; and full replacement of exposed waste and water distribution piping in the basement.

- **Option 4 — Room Finishes:** Demolition and replacement of all wood paneling with new drywall, carpet and VCT flooring replaced with new LVT throughout, new 2×2 ceiling grid systems, new interior doors and frames throughout, and painting.

## BFA's Role and Design Approach

BFA served as prime consultant, with direct responsibility for the mechanical engineering design (Option 2). The Prefinal BOD submittal was delivered February 24, 2025, meeting NAVFAC design standards and criteria including UFC 3-410-02N (Heating, Ventilating, Air Conditioning and Dehumidifying Systems), ASHRAE 62.1-2007 (Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality), ASHRAE 55-2008 (Thermal Environmental Conditions for Human Occupancy), IMC-2010, and the SMACNA HVAC Commissioning Manual.

The mechanical design established outdoor design conditions for the Indian Head, Maryland climate (91°F dry bulb / 74°F wet bulb for cooling; 11°F dry bulb for heating) and specified indoor comfort targets of 78°F dry bulb / 50% RH for cooling and 68°F dry bulb for heating in all admin and shop spaces. The six-zone heat pump configuration provided independent control for the kitchen zone, front office zone, conference and private office areas, second-floor spaces, and basement — enabling the building's main HVAC equipment to be serviced with minimal interruption to building functions, with 3-foot minimum access clearance around each piece of mechanical equipment.

Ductwork design followed SMACNA/ASHRAE conventions with galvanized sheet metal as the primary material; aluminum ductwork was specified for restroom exhaust and high-humidity areas to address corrosion. Duct velocities and static pressure loss criteria were defined for low-pressure and medium-pressure systems. The design included a complete testing and balancing (TAB) scope and standalone programmable thermostat controls per zone — appropriate for a facility operating independently of a central building automation system. Building indoor positive pressure (0.02 in.) was maintained to minimize air infiltration. HVAC-generated noise was limited to NC 30 in offices and conference rooms.

## Key Challenges

The building's 125-year history presented several design and construction challenges. The existing HVAC equipment had failed repeatedly, causing flooding in surrounding spaces — a condition the new zoned heat pump systems directly addressed by eliminating water-based failure modes and providing redundancy across zones. Water intrusion from the exterior walls and the roof had caused structural and finish damage including a collapsed ceiling in one office and mold growth; these conditions were prerequisites for the HVAC design because moisture control and envelope integrity are preconditions for HVAC system performance. HAZMAT conditions — asbestos and lead-based paint — were confirmed as suspected throughout and required a certified AHERA survey and regulated abatement sequence before any demolition or renovation could begin.

The multi-option procurement structure was a deliberate sequencing strategy: HAZMAT remediation first, then systems (electrical, HVAC, plumbing), then finishes — ensuring that each phase built on a safe and sound substrate.

## Project Context

The project was executed under NAVFAC contracting with a 212-day period of performance, core working hours of 7:00 AM to 4:30 PM Monday through Friday, and all requirements of EM-385-1-1 Safety and Health Manual for contractor safety. Installation access was controlled through the NSF Indian Head main gate with DBIDS processing. BFA's design scope is documented at the Prefinal submittal level; the project was in active design as of February 2025.
