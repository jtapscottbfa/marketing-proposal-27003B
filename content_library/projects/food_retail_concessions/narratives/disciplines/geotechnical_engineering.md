The geotechnical engineering scope for the NEXCOM Food & Retail Concessions project supported foundation design for a new single-story building at Naval Station Norfolk, Norfolk, Virginia — a low-lying waterfront peninsula with soft compressible soil conditions characteristic of the Hampton Roads tidewater environment.

Subsurface investigation confirmed the need for a deep foundation system. Timber piles were selected as the foundation type, consistent with the soft-soil conditions at the site. Foundation recommendations informed the structural engineer's design of the pile-supported concrete structural slab at the center bay of the building and the overall pile layout required to support the building footprint and superstructure loads.

The geotechnical investigation was conducted in coordination with the environmental engineering discipline given that the project site is located within the CERCLA Site 20 Land Use Control boundary at Naval Station Norfolk, where PFAS-contaminated groundwater has been confirmed. Subsurface investigation activities required consideration of appropriate groundwater sampling and handling protocols, and geotechnical borings were executed within the established environmental monitoring and LUC framework. Dewatering treatment requirements for construction-phase excavation were informed by the combined geotechnical and environmental characterization of the site. Specific boring logs, pile load capacities, and detailed soil profile data are not captured in the available source record for this project.
