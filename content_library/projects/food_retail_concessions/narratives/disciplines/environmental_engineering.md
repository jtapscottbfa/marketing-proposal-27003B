The environmental engineering scope for the NEXCOM Food & Retail Concessions project was driven by the project site's location within the Land Use Control (LUC) boundary of CERCLA Site 20 at Naval Station Norfolk — a contaminated groundwater site with confirmed per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) resulting from historical aqueous film-forming foam (AFFF) use associated with adjacent hangar and airfield operations.

The primary environmental deliverable was the design of a passive air vapor intrusion mitigation system (VIMS) required beneath the entire building slab area to prevent PFAS vapors and other volatile contaminants from migrating into the occupied building. The VIMS design incorporated a spray-applied vapor barrier, an AASHTO #57 stone sub-slab aggregate drainage layer, perforated sub-slab collection piping, plenum boxes, and vertical vent risers extending above the roof — a passive system designed to maintain sub-slab pressure differentials and prevent vapor accumulation beneath the foundation slab.

Contaminated groundwater encountered during construction excavation and dewatering required treatment prior to discharge. Dewatering treatment systems were specified as part of the construction-phase environmental controls, addressing the PFAS-impacted groundwater encountered during utility trenching, foundation preparation, and pile installation. Environmental design was coordinated with the civil and geotechnical disciplines to integrate LUC compliance requirements, dewatering treatment protocols, and VIMS components into the overall construction documents. The environmental complexity represented a defining challenge of this project, materially affecting foundation design, construction sequencing, and project cost for a facility of this scale.
