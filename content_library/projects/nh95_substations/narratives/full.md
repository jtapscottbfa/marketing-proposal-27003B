## Project Overview

The NH-95 Substations Replacement program was a facility-wide electrical infrastructure modernization at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads, Norfolk, Virginia. The project replaced all 12 unit substations (A through L) within Building NH-95, a 1950s-era installation building whose electrical infrastructure had reached the end of its service life. Existing oil-filled transformers were leaking, and original breakers were no longer available as replacement parts. The program was administered by NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic under IDIQ Contract N40085-20-D-0005 and structured as two sequential task orders: Work Order 1581202 (substations A, B, K, and L; BFA Project 20-007A; program amount $8,392,000; estimated construction cost $6,475,000; construction completion estimated September 30, 2022) and Work Order 1687416 / Task Order N4008521F4769 (substations C/D, E/F, G/H, and I/J; estimated construction cost $32,331,000; construction completion estimated May 27, 2024). The Basis of Design was submitted as a Final Submittal on March 30, 2022 (revised April 15, 2022) for the A, B, K, and L scope.

## Scope of Work

Full A-E design services were provided across all disciplines under a Design-Bid-Build delivery method.

**Electrical:** Demolition of existing switchgear and oil-filled unit substations for all 12 substations. New exterior pad-mounted transformers at each substation: 1,000 kVA for substations A and B; 2,500 kVA for substations K and L. New service-entrance-rated double-ended switchboards with automatic transfer switches — 3,000A, 208Y/120V for substations A and B; 4,000A, 480Y/277V for substations K and L. All switchboards used Square D/Schneider QED-2 or QED-6 construction with MasterPact MTZ circuit breakers. Scope also included modification of primary and secondary ductbanks and manholes, arc flash/coordination studies, and circuit trace drawings for all 12 substations.

**Architecture:** Vault expansions to accommodate new switchboard equipment — approximately 42 ft × 19 ft with 14 ft-6 in. ceiling height for substations A and B; approximately 37 ft × 11 ft with new exterior walls and floor finish to match existing construction for substations C/D and F.

**Structural:** Helical pile foundations for vault building additions (30-kip capacity at approximately 25 ft depth). New concrete transformer pads sized at 180 in. × 123 in. for substations A/B and K/L. Structural assessment of existing vault construction to accommodate new loading.

**Mechanical:** Mechanical ventilation design for expanded electrical equipment vaults, with HVAC air distribution systems for the new vault additions.

**Fire Protection:** Modifications to existing fire suppression systems — wet sprinkler and dry/pre-action systems — and fire alarm system updates to accommodate vault expansions and new electrical equipment.

**Civil/Site:** Exterior site improvements for pad-mounted transformer installations, primary and secondary ductbank routing modifications, erosion and sediment control measures.

**Geotechnical:** Subsurface investigation including two SPT borings and one seismic CPT sounding to approximately 100 ft depth; Site Class D seismic classification; liquefaction analysis; settlement calculations; helical pile design recommendations.

**Environmental:** Hazardous materials survey and abatement design covering confirmed lead, cadmium, and chromium paint above laboratory detection levels; asbestos-containing ductbank and manhole materials; transformer oil PCB assessment (PCBs confirmed below the 1 PPM regulatory threshold); and mercury-containing fluorescent lamp disposal provisions.

## Role on Project

The firm served as prime A-E consultant responsible for all design disciplines, deliverable coordination, and client interface across both task orders. Team leads included the DOR Project Manager/Mechanical Lead, DOR BIM Manager, Fire Protection Lead, and Mechanical engineer. NAVFAC MIDLANT's Design Manager served in an Electrical Lead oversight capacity.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Aging infrastructure in an active facility.** Building NH-95 was constructed in the 1950s and had undergone various additions over the decades, creating mixed-vintage construction conditions. Substations remained in service when design commenced, requiring careful sequencing of demolition and replacement to maintain power continuity to the facility throughout the construction period.

**Geotechnical conditions requiring specialized foundations.** Subsurface soils at the site — medium-dense to very loose silty/clayey sands from grade to approximately 30 ft, underlain by clays and silts to approximately 65 ft, with sands below — combined with groundwater at 7–10 ft below grade and a Site Class D seismic classification, precluded conventional spread footings for the vault additions. The geotechnical investigation recommended helical piles with 30-kip capacity at approximately 25 ft depth, which were incorporated into the structural design.

**Hazardous materials in 1950s-era construction.** Laboratory analysis confirmed lead, cadmium, and chromium paint above minimum detection levels on existing structural and MEP components. Asbestos-containing materials were identified in ductbank and manhole components. Transformer oil testing confirmed PCB concentrations below the 1 PPM regulatory threshold. The A-E team conducted the full hazmat survey and prepared abatement specifications integrated into the construction documents.

**Multi-phase IDIQ program coordination.** The full NH-95 substation replacement required two separate task orders under the same IDIQ contract, with different scopes, ECCs, and construction completion milestones. Maintaining continuity of design approach, equipment standards, and documentation conventions across both task orders required deliberate coordination of the A-E team.

## Outcomes

The Basis of Design covering substations A, B, K, and L was delivered as a Final Submittal on March 30, 2022, with a revision on April 15, 2022. Estimated construction completion dates were September 30, 2022 for substations A, B, K, and L, and May 27, 2024 for substations C through J. The completed program replaced all 12 unit substations serving an active naval installation, eliminated leaking oil-filled transformers, and provided a redundant double-ended switchboard configuration with automatic transfer capability at every substation — restoring reliable, code-compliant electrical distribution infrastructure across the entire Building NH-95 complex.
