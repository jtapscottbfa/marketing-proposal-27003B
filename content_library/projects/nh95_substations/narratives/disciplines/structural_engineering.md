Structural engineering on the NH-95 Substations Replacement program at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads, Norfolk, Virginia, addressed two primary scope elements: foundations for the vault building additions and concrete transformer pads for the new exterior pad-mounted transformers.

The vault expansions required to house new double-ended switchboard equipment — approximately 42 ft × 19 ft with 14 ft-6 in. ceilings for substations A and B, and approximately 37 ft × 11 ft for substations C/D and F — could not be supported on conventional spread footings given the site's geotechnical conditions. Subsurface soils consisting of medium-dense to very loose silty/clayey sands to approximately 30 ft depth, groundwater at 7–10 ft below grade, and a Site Class D seismic classification required a deep foundation solution. Based on the project's geotechnical investigation, helical piles with 30-kip axial capacity at approximately 25 ft depth were designed for the vault additions.

New concrete transformer pads were designed for each substation's exterior pad-mounted transformer. For substations A/B and K/L, pad dimensions were 180 in. × 123 in. Structural scope also included assessment of existing vault walls, floors, and ceiling construction to evaluate adequacy for accommodating new equipment loads and the connection of vault additions to the existing 1950s-era building structure.
