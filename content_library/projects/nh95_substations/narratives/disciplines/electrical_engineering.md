Electrical engineering was the primary discipline on the NH-95 Substations Replacement program. The scope addressed a facility-wide end-of-life condition in Building NH-95 at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads, Norfolk, Virginia: existing oil-filled unit substations with leaking transformers and obsolete switchgear breakers that could no longer be sourced for replacement. Design covered all 12 substations (A through L) across two NAVFAC task orders under IDIQ Contract N40085-20-D-0005.

The electrical design replaced each substation with new exterior pad-mounted transformers and service-entrance-rated double-ended switchboards with automatic transfer switches. Substations A and B received 1,000 kVA transformers and 3,000A, 208Y/120V double-ended switchboards; substations K and L received 2,500 kVA transformers and 4,000A, 480Y/277V double-ended switchboards. All switchboards used Square D/Schneider QED-2 or QED-6 construction with MasterPact MTZ circuit breakers, providing redundant double-ended configurations with automatic transfer at every substation. Primary and secondary ductbanks and manholes were modified to accommodate the new exterior transformer placements and revised secondary routing.

Supporting electrical deliverables included arc flash and short-circuit coordination studies for all 12 substations and circuit trace drawings documenting the existing distribution from each substation — essential for an active facility where as-built documentation was limited. The double-ended switchboard configuration restored redundancy at each substation, eliminating the single-point-of-failure condition of the original unit substations.
