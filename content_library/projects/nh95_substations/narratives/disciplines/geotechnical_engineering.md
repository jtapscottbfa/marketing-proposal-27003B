Geotechnical engineering on the NH-95 Substations Replacement program at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads, Norfolk, Virginia, characterized subsurface conditions at the project site and provided foundation recommendations for the new vault building additions. The geotechnical investigation — documented in the Geotechnical Engineering Report (GER 110-7881) included as an appendix to the Basis of Design — included two standard penetration test (SPT) borings and one seismic cone penetration test (SCPTu) sounding to approximately 100 ft depth.

Subsurface conditions consisted of medium-dense to very loose silty/clayey sands from grade to approximately 30 ft, transitioning to clays and silts to approximately 65 ft depth, with sands below. Groundwater was encountered at 7–10 ft below grade. Soil corrosivity was characterized as mildly corrosive. Based on subsurface conditions and the Site Class D seismic classification, liquefaction analysis was performed using CLiq software, and helical piles were recommended for the vault addition foundations — 30-kip axial capacity achievable at approximately 25 ft depth. Settlement analysis used SETTLE3D, and helical pile design used DeepFND software.

The seismic Site Class D classification was confirmed appropriate for the site based on the shear wave velocity profile from the SCPTu sounding and the engineering team's prior experience in the area. No liquefaction potential was identified as a controlling condition for the foundation design. The geotechnical findings directly informed the structural discipline's foundation design and were integrated into the civil and architectural scope for the vault additions.
