Environmental engineering on the NH-95 Substations Replacement program at Naval Support Activity Hampton Roads, Norfolk, Virginia, addressed hazardous materials present in Building NH-95 — a structure originally constructed in the 1950s with various additions over subsequent decades. The environmental scope encompassed a pre-design hazardous materials survey and preparation of abatement specifications integrated into the construction documents, as well as NEPA compliance requirements applicable to work on a federal installation.

The hazmat survey identified three primary concern categories. Lead, cadmium, and chromium were confirmed in paint samples above laboratory minimum detection levels on existing structural and MEP components within the project area, requiring abatement specifications for the demolition contractor. Asbestos-containing materials (ACM) were identified in ductbank and manhole components within the project scope; sampled building materials within the vault areas were confirmed non-ACM. Transformer oil PCB testing confirmed concentrations below the regulatory 1 PPM threshold, so the existing transformers did not require PCB-specific disposal protocols beyond standard used oil handling. Fluorescent light tubes were assumed mercury-containing and included in the hazmat removal scope.

The sub-discipline taxonomy records `environmental_remediation` and `environmental_nepa` as within scope. The hazmat findings were incorporated directly into the demolition and abatement sections of the construction documents, and abatement was sequenced as a precondition to structural demolition activities for the vault additions. The full hazmat analytical laboratory data and sampling results were documented in the Hazardous Materials Report included as an appendix to the Basis of Design.
