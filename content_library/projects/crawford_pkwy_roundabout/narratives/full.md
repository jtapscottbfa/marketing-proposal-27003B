## Project Overview

The Crawford Circle Downtown Gateway Project is a civic infrastructure and public realm improvement for the City of Portsmouth, Virginia. The project is sited at Crawford Circle — the intersection of Crawford Street and Bart Street in downtown Portsmouth — where a traffic roundabout was redesigned as an urban gateway featuring a multi-nozzle decorative fountain and sculptural centerpiece within the central island. Photorealistic renderings produced during design illustrate the installation's daytime and nighttime character as a prominent civic address point.

The design record begins with a topographic survey dated April 9, 2020 (survey firm job number 45136.004). Final design drawings were issued June 28, 2021, with a title-block revision date of August 23, 2021. The project is catalogued under internal number 20-056 and was delivered via design-bid-build procurement for a local government client. The project is recorded as complete.

## Scope

The design scope encompasses plumbing engineering for the fountain system, electrical engineering for site power and lighting, and structural engineering for the installation.

**Plumbing / Water Feature System.** The fountain design is organized around a DBVB-Series Direct Burial Vault System — a fully pre-assembled, temperature-controlled RCF80 PVC vault with stainless-steel top-access grates. The vault contains the sand filter pump, main circulation pump, sump pump, double-check valve backflow preventer, water meter, and water fill piping. Type K copper piping — rigid runs to spray nozzle stations, flexible tubing for inner and outer distribution manifolds — routes water to multiple nozzle locations arranged around the fountain basin.

Two nozzle types serve the display: GEY-Series Geyser Nozzles (machined cast-bronze, multiple units, producing vertical jets to 10 feet) and VEN-Series Vee Nozzles (one-piece brass, water-level-independent, producing thin fan-spray jets to 4 feet). A brass ball-valve manifold at the vault allows operators to independently regulate spray height at each nozzle station. Supporting equipment includes an FAS-08 Anti-Vortex/Diverter Plate and Sump (cast-bronze diverter plate, 2-inch threaded side connections, requiring a 4-inch concrete surround at the sump body), a CWL-002WD Wall-Mounted Overflow/Sensor combining overflow drainage and water-level monitoring behind a concealed face plate, FDF-Series flush-mount in-pool floor drain fittings, FSK-150J front-access skimmers with removable trash baskets for surface debris collection, and wailer-line scuppers. All fountain equipment is specified from Fountain People, Inc. (San Marcos, TX). The construction documents direct the contractor to engage a fountain consultant for whole-system design and equipment sizing per manufacturer recommendations. The domestic water piping system is governed by Specification Section 22 11 16.

**Electrical.** The electrical scope covers LED exterior lighting for the roundabout installation (Specification Section 26 56 19), low-voltage power conductors and cables (26 05 19), grounding and bonding for electrical systems (26 05 26), raceway and boxes (26 05 33), sleeves and sleeve seals for electrical raceways (26 05 44), electrical system identification (26 05 53), and wiring devices including GFCI receptacles (26 27 26). An existing electrical vault shown on the fountain plan was incorporated into the design. Detailed electrical drawings (fixture layout, panel schedule, circuiting) are not captured in the four-sheet document subset on file.

**Structural.** McPherson Design Group served as structural engineer of record, listed on the drawing title blocks alongside the lead firm. The structural scope supported the fountain installation and associated site construction, including the concrete work around buried fountain equipment.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Specialty fountain system integration.** A public decorative fountain requires tight coordination among plumbing, electrical, controls, and civil disciplines within a constrained roundabout island footprint accessible to both maintenance crews and the driving public. The design addresses this complexity by specifying a pre-assembled direct-burial vault that packages pumping, filtration, water metering, and backflow prevention into a single factory-built unit. The stainless-steel top-access grates allow maintenance access without disrupting the surrounding landscape. Separating nozzle types (geyser vs. vee-spray) into independently valved branches via the ball-valve manifold provides operational flexibility for display programming without requiring specialized controls infrastructure.

**Contractor engagement with fountain consultant.** Recognizing that spray fountain systems involve proprietary equipment interactions and startup commissioning beyond standard MEP design, the drawings explicitly require the contractor to engage a fountain consultant for system design and equipment recommendations per Fountain People's specifications. This division of responsibility — design documents set the equipment standards and layout; fountain consultant refines the system for the specific installation — is documented as a construction requirement rather than a design assumption, reducing risk of field-stage coordination failures.

**Partial drawing record.** The project documents on file comprise 4 of a 14-sheet drawing set, covering only the plumbing discipline (sheets P001, P401, P501, P502; positioned as sheets 5, 7, 8, and 9 of the full set). Electrical, civil, site, and landscape sheets are not captured, limiting the completeness of the engineering record available for proposal use.
