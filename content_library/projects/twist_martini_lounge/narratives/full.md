# Twist Martini Lounge — Patio Heater Installation

## Project Overview

The Twist Martini Lounge patio heater installation is a small commercial tenant-improvement project located in Virginia Beach, Virginia. The project addresses the need for supplemental radiant heating in an existing outdoor patio space at a private bar and lounge establishment. The design firm (project number 20-095) served as prime consultant, delivering complete mechanical and electrical engineering design. Construction documents were sealed November 30, 2020.

The project is a focused MEP design engagement. The patio occupies the ground floor of a multi-story building, and all new work connects to the building's existing above-ceiling mechanical and electrical infrastructure. No architectural, structural, or civil work is in scope.

## Scope of Work

**Mechanical.** Three gas-fired infrared tube unit heaters — designated UH-1, UH-2, and UH-3 — were designed for installation in the covered outdoor patio adjacent to Commerce Street. The selected equipment, SunStar Eclipse SIR .35 Series, is rated at 35 MBH high and 25 MBH low input per unit. Heaters are suspended with the bottom edge at approximately 10 feet 5 inches above the finished floor.

Natural gas distribution was designed to tap the building's existing 5 PSIG above-ceiling supply. The system includes a pressure regulator stepping supply pressure down to heater operating range (minimum 5" WC, maximum 14" WC; burner pressure 3.5" WC), a 6-inch dirt leg at each branch, ball valves, Y-connections, and a 24-inch flexible gas connector at each heater connection point. An emergency gas shut-off valve (EGSOV), mounted at 48 inches above the finished floor, is controlled by wall switch through a 24V/120V relay and transformer arrangement.

Each heater is Type B vented with a draft inducer. Vent supports are specified at each column and at the storefront, with a Type B vent manifold at 9 feet 11 inches. Controls provide two-stage (high/low) manual operation at each heater, adjustable by venue staff independent of the emergency shut-off circuit.

**Electrical.** The electrical design extended an existing circuit from Panel A to serve the three heaters at 120V single-phase, 1.2 amperes each. Wiring runs use 1-inch flexible metal conduit (FMC) with 2x#12 and 1x#12 ground THWN conductors to each heater's exhauster junction box and control box, and a separate FMC run with 2x#16 and 1x#16 ground THWN for the common control wiring. Each heater is served by a three-position (Hi/Lo/Off) manual switch (Part #40147010). The Low Fire switch must remain energized during High Fire operation.

The emergency shut-off relay circuit integrates the electrical and mechanical gas systems: wall-switch activation closes the EGSOV via a 24V relay and transformer at each unit. Demolition work included removal of an existing ceiling fan and its associated ceiling fan switch; the freed circuit was refurbished and repurposed for heater power. Switch location for the new controls was identified as a coordination item with the owner.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Integration with existing mechanical infrastructure.** The new heaters connect to a pre-existing 5 PSIG natural gas supply above the ceiling. The design incorporated a pressure regulator and appropriate piping components (dirt leg, ball valves, flexible connectors) to bring supply pressure within the heaters' required operating band while preserving the integrity of the existing system.

**Emergency shut-off coordination across disciplines.** The EGSOV design required coordination between the mechanical gas system and the electrical control circuit. The 24V/120V relay arrangement allows a single wall switch to close the gas supply across all heaters simultaneously, integrating mechanical safety response into the electrical control interface.

**Vent design in a semi-enclosed patio environment.** Gas-fired tube heaters in a covered outdoor space require proper venting. The design specified Type B venting with draft inducers, vent supports at each column and at the storefront, and a manifold arrangement — accommodating the architectural and structural constraints of the existing patio enclosure.

**Operational simplicity for venue staff.** The control scheme — independent two-stage (high/low) manual switches per heater, plus a single-point emergency shut-off — was designed for straightforward operation by non-technical staff, with the zone setpoint adjustable by venue personnel.

## Outcomes

Construction documents were completed and sealed November 30, 2020. The design delivers supplemental radiant heating for the patio through three independently controlled, two-stage gas-fired infrared heaters. Each unit can operate at high or low fire, with the emergency gas shut-off providing a readily accessible safety control.
