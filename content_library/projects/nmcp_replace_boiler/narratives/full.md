## Project Overview

The design team provided mechanical and electrical engineering services for the replacement of Boiler 108 at Portsmouth Naval Medical Center (PNMC) Building 20, the Central Steam Plant, in Portsmouth, Virginia. Building 20 houses the steam generation infrastructure that serves the Naval Medical Center campus — a continuously operated, mission-critical utility function for the military healthcare facility. The project was executed under a NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic A-E IDIQ Design-Bid-Build task order (Contract N40085-19-D-9049, Task Order 9E41P0, FY-21 Project ST 18-0020), with a program amount of $2,800,000 and an estimated construction cost of $2,000,000. The fund type was General Fund, and the work was classified as Maintenance/Repair.

## Scope

The project scope encompassed the complete removal of the existing aging Boiler 108 — a Nebraska "D" type water tube boiler (Cleaver Brooks/Nebraska Model 2D 2056, rated at 20,000 lb/hr) — and its replacement with a new 30,000 lb/hr package water tube boiler. Per the Basis of Design: "This project will remove the aging Boiler #108 from the Portsmouth Naval Medical Center Central Steam plant and replace it with a new lower maintenance and higher efficiency boiler." The replacement required prepping, repairing, or replacing the systems and structures within the boiler room to receive the new equipment, as well as all mechanical and electrical work needed to integrate the new boiler with the existing plant systems.

The new boiler operates at 110 psi delivery pressure and 347°F water temperature. It was designed to function in parallel with the existing Boilers 109 and 110, achieving a true n+1 redundancy configuration for the Central Steam Plant. Electrical scope included modifications to the existing 208Y/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire distribution system; IMC and RMC conduit systems with THHN/THWN copper conductors; and individual motor starters with separate disconnect switches for all motors 1/2 HP and larger. A 240-calendar-day construction duration was specified.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant, responsible for the full mechanical and electrical design. The work was delivered under the Brooks Act qualifications-based selection process through the NAVFAC A-E IDIQ contract vehicle. Design services tracked through NAVFAC's SAES framework, issued in December 2019, with the 100% Final Basis of Design submitted on April 30, 2021. Internal project reference: BFA Project No. 20-007B; NAVFAC eProject Work Order No. 1616699.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

The central design challenge was integrating a replacement boiler into an operating Central Steam Plant serving a continuously operating military medical center. Steam supply to the hospital campus could not be interrupted, requiring careful coordination of the demolition and installation sequence with facility operations. The new boiler's 50% increase in capacity over the unit it replaced (30,000 lb/hr versus 20,000 lb/hr) also required the design team to address interface conditions — structural, mechanical, and electrical — at the existing boiler connections and plant space.

Establishing true n+1 redundancy was a primary design objective. With the three-boiler configuration (new Boiler 108 plus existing Boilers 109 and 110), the plant gains the ability to sustain campus steam demand with any one boiler offline — a critical reliability improvement for a military medical facility.

A minor discrepancy between source documents was resolved during design development: the pre-design SAES (December 2019) described the replacement unit as a "package fire tube boiler," while the 100% Final BOD (April 2021) specified a "package water tube boiler." The BOD is the authoritative design document reflecting the engineering analysis and final equipment selection. Similarly, the SAES listed the existing boiler capacity as 30,000 lb/hr while the BOD documented it at 20,000 lb/hr; the BOD clearly distinguishes existing capacity (20,000 lb/hr) from the new boiler target (30,000 lb/hr), and is treated as authoritative.

## Outcomes

The project produced a 100% Final Basis of Design as of April 30, 2021, providing NAVFAC with a complete design package suitable for competitive Design-Bid-Build procurement. The design delivered a net increase in steam generation capacity from 20,000 lb/hr to 30,000 lb/hr, established n+1 redundancy with Boilers 109 and 110, and addressed the long-term reliability, efficiency, and maintainability objectives of the Portsmouth Naval Medical Center Central Steam Plant. The 240-calendar-day construction duration specification provided a defined execution window for the construction contractor to complete the boiler replacement while managing operational continuity requirements of the active facility.
