## Project Overview

The Dental Clinic Exhaust Fan Installation at Building 1046, Shaw Air Force Base (Shaw AFB), South Carolina, was a targeted mechanical and electrical upgrade to an existing dental clinic on an active Air Force installation. The project originated from a formal finding by the Defense Health Agency (DHA): the DHA Infrastructure Condition Assessment Survey identified pressurization deficiencies in the building's crawl space that required engineered correction. The United States Air Force, as installation owner, and the DHA, as the military health program authority, directed the work through the US Army Corps of Engineers (USACE), which served as the contracting agency for the design engagement.

Building 1046 functions as a dental clinic — an ambulatory healthcare facility within the DHA military medical support structure on Shaw AFB. The crawl space pressurization deficiency represented both a ventilation adequacy problem and a building envelope control concern in a facility operating under military medical facility standards, including UFC 4-510-01.

## Scope

The design scope was focused on two engineering disciplines: mechanical engineering and electrical engineering. No architectural, structural, or other disciplines are recorded in the source documents.

Mechanical scope included the design of an approximately 4,000 CFM inline direct-drive centrifugal cabinet exhaust fan for installation in the crawl space, with all associated systems:

- Fire damper at the floor penetration between the crawl space and the occupied dental clinic above, satisfying life safety requirements applicable to military medical facilities
- Insulated intake and exhaust ductwork routed through the building to roof terminations, providing positive exhaust without recirculation near occupied spaces
- Intake and relief hoods with roof curbs (two hoods total) at the roof penetration points
- Sealed crawl space vents, forming part of the corrective pressurization strategy
- Fan controller integration with the existing building automation system (BAS), enabling coordinated environmental monitoring and control of the crawl space without standalone controls

Electrical scope provided:

- Power connection for exhaust fan EF-1
- THHN/THWN copper conductors sized for the fan load
- Grounding to match existing facility systems per NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code)

The design was governed by the applicable Unified Facilities Criteria suite: UFC 3-400-02 (Engineering Weather Data), UFC 3-401-01 (Mechanical Engineering), UFC 3-410-01 (HVAC Systems), UFC 3-430-01FA (Heating and Cooling Distribution Systems), UFC 3-501-01 (Electrical Engineering), UFC 3-520-01 (Interior Electrical Systems), and UFC 4-510-01 (Design: Military Medical Facilities). Additional references included IMC-2010, ASHRAE 55-2008, ASHRAE 62.1-2007, and applicable ASHRAE Handbooks.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant, responsible for the full design scope across both mechanical and electrical disciplines. The project was delivered using a design-bid-build delivery method. No sub-consultants or subconsultants are recorded in available source documents.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

The central technical challenge was correcting a defined ventilation deficiency within an active dental clinic on a federal military installation, under military medical facility design standards, without disrupting clinic operations.

**Crawl space pressurization correction.** The DHA condition assessment survey established the deficiency to be corrected. The engineering solution addressed the pressurization imbalance through a dedicated exhaust fan system with intake provisions, rather than simply sealing the crawl space passively. Sizing the fan at approximately 4,000 CFM provided the necessary exhaust capacity for the crawl space volume while remaining within the scope of a single direct-drive centrifugal cabinet unit.

**Life safety at the floor penetration.** The floor penetration between the crawl space and the occupied clinic created a fire-separation obligation. A fire damper at this location addressed the life safety requirement under military medical facility criteria, integrating the exhaust path into the existing compartmentation of the building.

**BAS integration in an existing facility.** Integrating the new fan controller with the existing building automation system, rather than deploying standalone controls, kept the crawl space environment within the facility's existing monitoring and control infrastructure. This avoided introducing an isolated control system into an occupied healthcare facility.

**Ductwork routing in an existing building.** Routing insulated ductwork through an existing occupied clinic building to roof terminations required coordination of the exhaust path with existing building conditions. Use of roof-mounted intake and relief hoods with roof curbs provided compliant exterior terminations.

## Outcomes

The project delivered an engineering design correcting the crawl space pressurization deficiency identified by the DHA Infrastructure Condition Assessment Survey at Building 1046, Shaw AFB. The mechanical system — exhaust fan, ductwork, hoods, fire damper, and BAS integration — and electrical power infrastructure were designed in conformance with the applicable UFC standards and ASHRAE criteria for a military medical ambulatory care facility.
