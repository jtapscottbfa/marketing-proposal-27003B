The Dental Clinic Exhaust Fan Installation at Building 1046, Shaw Air Force Base (Shaw AFB), South Carolina, addressed a crawl space pressurization deficiency identified in the Defense Health Agency (DHA) Infrastructure Condition Assessment Survey. The design team served as prime consultant, delivering mechanical and electrical engineering services for the targeted upgrade to an active military dental clinic facility on Shaw AFB.

Mechanical scope centered on an approximately 4,000 CFM inline direct-drive centrifugal cabinet exhaust fan installed in the crawl space, with a fire damper at the floor penetration between the crawl space and occupied clinic space, insulated intake and exhaust ductwork routed through the building to roof terminations, intake and relief hoods with roof curbs, sealed crawl space vents, and integration of the exhaust fan controller with the facility's existing building automation system (BAS). Electrical scope provided the power connection for the exhaust fan (EF-1), THHN/THWN copper conductors, and grounding to match existing systems per NFPA 70. The design was executed to the applicable Unified Facilities Criteria suite — including UFC 3-401-01, UFC 3-410-01, UFC 4-510-01, UFC 3-501-01, and UFC 3-520-01 — along with ASHRAE 62.1-2007 and IMC-2010. The project was delivered under a design-bid-build delivery method for the US Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) on behalf of DHA and the United States Air Force.
