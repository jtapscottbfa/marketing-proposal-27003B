Mechanical engineering scope on the Dental Clinic Exhaust Fan Installation at Shaw Air Force Base addressed a crawl space pressurization deficiency identified by the Defense Health Agency (DHA) Infrastructure Condition Assessment Survey at Building 1046. The mechanical design centered on an approximately 4,000 CFM inline direct-drive centrifugal cabinet exhaust fan installed in the crawl space, sized and configured to correct the identified airflow imbalance under military medical facility design standards.

The system included a fire damper at the floor penetration between the crawl space and the occupied dental clinic, satisfying life safety requirements applicable to the military medical facility context under UFC 4-510-01. Insulated intake and exhaust ductwork was routed through the building and terminated at the roof with intake and relief hoods mounted on roof curbs (two hoods total), providing positive exhaust without recirculation near occupied clinical spaces. Crawl space vents were sealed as part of the corrective pressurization strategy. The exhaust fan controller was integrated with the existing building automation system (BAS), enabling coordinated environmental monitoring and control of the crawl space environment within the facility's existing control infrastructure.

Design was performed to UFC 3-401-01 (Mechanical Engineering), UFC 3-410-01 (HVAC Systems), UFC 3-430-01FA (Heating and Cooling Distribution Systems), UFC 3-400-02 (Engineering Weather Data), and UFC 4-510-01 (Military Medical Facilities), with reference to ASHRAE 62.1-2007, ASHRAE 55-2008, and IMC-2010.
