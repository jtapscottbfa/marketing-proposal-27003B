# P-1127U Composite Shop — Naval Station Norfolk

## Project Overview

The P-1127U Composite Shop is a newly constructed industrial maintenance facility at Naval Station Norfolk (NSN), Norfolk, Virginia, built under a design-build contract to support intermediate maintenance operations for the CMV-22B Osprey aircraft fleet. NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic administered the procurement under an 8(a) Multiple Award Construction Contract (MACC) task order (N40085-23-R-2541). The estimated budget for combined design and construction was approximately $3,870,000, with a contractual task order duration of 515 calendar days from the date of award (inclusive of a 15-day window for bonds and insurance submittals).

The procurement was set aside for SBA-certified 8(a) small disadvantaged businesses, with nine eligible MACC holders competing for the task order under a best-value tradeoff evaluation methodology.

## Scope

The project delivers a new composite repair shop programmed for intermediate maintenance level operations on the CMV-22B, including repair and handling of the aircraft's largest removable components — rotor blades and cargo ramps. The facility incorporates shops, administrative space, a paint mixing room, paint booth space, hazardous material storage rooms, and ancillary support areas.

Structurally, the building consists of a reinforced concrete masonry unit (CMU) wall system with exterior split-faced CMU veneer, a concrete pile foundation system, interior CMU walls, and a standing-seam metal roof. The solicitation called for the facility to be sized to accommodate the largest removable CMV-22B components; explicit gross square footage was not stated in the available source documents.

The PME scope spanned all three engineering systems — mechanical/HVAC, electrical power and lighting, and plumbing/compressed air — with design criteria and system selections fully documented in the Basis of Design.

## Role on Project

The firm participated as the plumbing, mechanical, and electrical (PME) engineering design subconsultant. In this role, the firm developed the full Basis of Design (BOD) for all PME systems — establishing design criteria, system selection rationale, code basis, and equipment parameters — in support of the design-build team's technical proposal submission to NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic. Architecture, structural, civil, and fire protection engineering were outside this firm's scope and were provided by others on the design-build team.

PME design was governed by applicable Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC) and current model codes, including UFC 3-410-01 (HVAC Systems), UFC 3-410-02 (DDC for HVAC), UFC 3-420-01 (Plumbing), UFC 3-501-01 (Electrical Engineering), UFC 3-520-01 (Interior Electrical Systems), UFC 3-530-01 (Interior and Exterior Lighting), and UFC 3-580-01 (Telecommunications Cabling Systems). NFPA 70 NEC 2020 Edition governed electrical design; ICC IMC 2020 and ICC IPC 2021 governed mechanical and plumbing.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Hazardous environment coordination.** The facility's paint mixing room, paint booth space, and hazardous material storage areas required coordinated treatment across all three engineering disciplines. HVAC design followed ACGIH Industrial Ventilation (31st Edition) for exhaust and makeup air requirements in chemically active areas. Electrical design specified explosion-proof and enclosed/gasketed luminaires in NEC-classified hazardous locations, with branch wiring in rigid steel conduit. Plumbing design incorporated specialty floor drains with integral seepage pans and an emergency eyewash and safety shower station compliant with ANSI/ISEA Z358.1 (2014, revised 2020), requiring tempered water supply to serve the hazardous process areas.

**Industrial compressed air as a primary utility.** Unlike typical administrative or office buildings, the composite shop required a dedicated industrial compressed air distribution system. The design specified a duplex factory-packaged, electric-motor-driven compressor unit with an ASME BPVC Section VIII–compliant pressure vessel rated at 200 PSI, providing redundancy and a reliable compressed air supply to aviation maintenance workstations throughout the shop.

**Multi-voltage electrical distribution for mixed equipment loads.** The electrical scope spanned from new utility primary conductors through multiple voltage levels required by the mixed-use facility program. The distribution hierarchy flows from a 480/277V main distribution panel through two 480/277V sub-fed panels, a step-down 480-208/120V transformer, and a 208/120V sub-fed panel — providing voltage levels for HVAC equipment, shop machinery, specialty equipment connections (480V to 120V), lighting, and convenience loads within a single integrated distribution design.

**Mechanical system flexibility for industrial thermal loads.** The composite repair program presents variable internal loads driven by shop operations, occupancy, and process heat. The design addressed this with a 4-pipe fan coil system providing both chilled water and hot water at each coil, supplemented by ductless mini-split systems for zones with distinct occupancy or operational profiles. An independent Dedicated Outdoor Air System (DOAS) — 100% outside air, factory-packaged — decoupled ventilation from sensible load management, enabling energy-efficient operation under the variable load conditions typical of a maintenance shop environment.

## Outcomes

The PME Basis of Design was completed and submitted as part of the design-build team's technical proposal package. The BOD documents a fully coordinated PME systems scope — from outdoor air design conditions (94.1°F DB / 75°F MCWB for cooling; 23.8°F DB for heating) through equipment selections, code compliance basis, and utility connection strategy — establishing the technical and contractual foundation for the PME scope under the task order. Liquidated damages were set at $666.30 per calendar day of delay, consistent with the project's construction value and schedule sensitivity.
