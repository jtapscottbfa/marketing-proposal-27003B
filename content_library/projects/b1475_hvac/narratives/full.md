# Building 1475 – Replace HVAC Systems – Phase II
## Full Project Narrative

### Project Overview

Building 1475 at Norfolk Naval Shipyard (NNSY) is a RADDOACT HAZ HANDLING facility — the Nuclear Refueling/Radcon Facility serving the shipyard's drydock — originally constructed in 1975. The approximately 12,552 SF structure is a steel-frame building with masonry exterior cladding and a steel open web joist roof system. Having operated for decades with aging pneumatic-controlled HVAC equipment, the building required full HVAC system replacement to meet current Unified Facilities Criteria and ensure reliable environmental control for this mission-critical facility.

Bowman, Foster & Associates (BFA) served as prime A-E consultant under NAVFAC IDIQ contract N40085-20-D-0005, with NAVFAC PWD Portsmouth administering the task order. The BFA project number was 20-007K, and the NAVFAC eProjects Work Order number was 1719673. The program amount and estimated construction cost were each $3,816,000. The 100% design submittal was issued September 8, 2025, with construction anticipated in the 2026–2027 timeframe.

### Scope of Design Services

BFA assembled a multi-discipline team covering mechanical, electrical, structural, architectural, fire protection, environmental/hazardous materials, and cost estimating disciplines. The design package was produced under Design-Bid-Build delivery, culminating in a full set of construction documents including drawings, specifications, basis of design, hazardous materials survey report, sustainable design documentation, system safety documentation, construction schedule, and engineering calculations.

**Mechanical.** The heart of the project was the replacement of three rooftop packaged DX air handling units (AHU-1, AHU-2 evaluation, and AHU-3) and three roof-mounted return air fans (RAF-1, RAF-2, RAF-3), along with the complete demolition of the legacy pneumatic control system. New shut-off type VAV terminal boxes with hot water reheat coils serve the first and second floor office areas, fed by rectangular medium-pressure insulated supply and return ductwork. The building's heating hot water system was redesigned with new heat exchangers, an air separator, expansion tank, shot feeder, and in-line circulation pumps. Domestic hot water heat exchanger and circulation pump were similarly replaced. New motorized intake and exhaust louvers with DDC control were provided in the mechanical room. Bathroom, generator room, and mechanical room exhaust fans were replaced. All heating hot water piping was replaced and insulated. The entire pneumatic control system was demolished and replaced with a new DDC system integrated into the NNSY base-wide Siemens DDC network, with HVAC and lighting controls subject to independent commissioning per UFC 1-200-02.

**Electrical.** Electrical scope focused on supporting the new mechanical equipment while upgrading administrative lighting. All conduit, wire, and disconnects associated with the four existing rooftop AHUs and return air fans were demolished, with existing circuit breakers retained for reuse. New NEMA 4X stainless steel disconnect switches and wiring serve the three new rooftop AHUs. New circuits and disconnects were provided for the three new return air fans. The existing 480Y/277V and 208Y/120V electrical services remain, as does the backup generator (currently offline). Existing fluorescent lighting on the first and second floor administrative areas was demolished and replaced with new LED fixtures (4000K, 80+ CRI) with new lighting controls. High-bay and mechanical room LED lighting — already modernized — was retained.

**Structural.** BFA performed a structural assessment of the existing steel roof structure to determine its capacity to support new rooftop equipment. The analysis identified that a portion of the lower roof carries a 50 psf equipment load rating while adjacent areas are rated for 20 psf live load. Modifications included: reinforcement of existing 18" bar joists beneath new post positions; removal and replacement of existing dunnage posts and steel beams to match the footprint of new mechanical equipment; new deck infill at abandoned openings using 1½" Type B metal roof decking; and new L4×4 angle reinforcement at new duct penetration perimeters. New duct support brackets and posts, anchored to existing roof joists, were designed to support the new rooftop ductwork.

**Architectural.** The architectural scope included complete replacement of the lower roof system down to the metal deck, with a new Modified Bitumen roof system over rigid insulation on existing metal deck. All suspended acoustical tile and gypsum board ceilings were demolished and replaced to accommodate the mechanical replacement above. Floor finishes disturbed by mechanical work were repaired and replaced to match adjacent finishes. Roof fall-protection guardrails were repaired and repainted; new guardrails matching the existing design were provided as required by the mechanical layout. Access to restricted areas within the Controlled Industrial Area of NNSY was a persistent coordination challenge. Section 106 SHPO review was required for the historic shipyard structure.

**Fire Protection.** The existing Mircom FX-2000 Addressable Fire Alarm/Mass Notification System was retained with modifications. New duct smoke detectors were provided per NFPA 90A for supply air systems with capacity greater than 2,000 CFM. The existing wet pipe sprinkler system (administration area) and pre-action sprinkler system (two-story industrial area) were modified — sprinklers were removed during ceiling demolition and reinstalled to coordinate with the new reflected ceiling plans. The pre-action system air compressor was resized as required.

**Hazardous Materials.** BFA's hazardous materials team performed a comprehensive building survey, collecting and analyzing bulk samples for asbestos-containing materials (ACM), paint chips for lead, cadmium, and chromium, fluorescent fixtures for PCB ballasts, and transformers for PCB oils. ACM was identified in gypsum board joint compound, floor tiles and mastic, and spray-applied fireproofing on structural steel. Lead, cadmium, and chromium were identified in paint samples throughout. Design documents address removal and disposal of all identified hazardous materials through a dedicated H-series drawing set.

### Key Challenges and Design Approach

The project involved working within a nuclear-controlled, access-restricted facility at an active shipyard, requiring careful coordination of site access, personnel security clearances, and phased construction activities around building operations. The multi-vintage structural system — original 1975 construction with subsequent repair phases (1995, 1996, 2022) — required careful interpretation of as-built drawings and engineering judgment for structural modifications given that final mechanical equipment weights were not available at time of structural design. The transition from steam-heated systems to a new hot-water heating configuration, coordinated with AHU-2 back-up phasing under a separate contract, required careful scope delineation across contract boundaries.

### Outcomes

The 100% design submittal was completed September 8, 2025, completing the A-E design phase. The project program amount of $3,816,000 supported construction anticipated in the 2026–2027 timeframe. Post Construction Award Services (PCAS) were included as a contract option for submittal review, record drawing preparation, field consultation, and design consultation support during construction.
