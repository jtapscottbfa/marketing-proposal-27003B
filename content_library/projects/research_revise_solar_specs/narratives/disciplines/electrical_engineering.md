Electrical engineering expertise was central to the research and revision of UFGS 26 31 00, a federal specification governing facility-scale solar photovoltaic (PV) system components. The electrical engineering scope encompassed both power distribution and renewable energy systems — the two sub-disciplines captured for this project.

The revised specification addresses PV component selection, installation, and integration requirements for DoD facilities and demanded current knowledge of applicable electrical codes and standards. Key updates incorporated into the revision included changes in NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code, 2020 edition) and IEEE 1547-2018, the federal interconnection and interoperability standard for distributed energy resources. These standards have direct technical implications for how facility-scale PV systems are specified: inverter requirements, disconnecting means, grounding configurations, array wiring methods, and utility interconnection conditions all flow from the applicable edition of these codes.

The electrical engineering discipline evaluated how code changes affected PV system design requirements and translated those changes into specification language that facility designers and contractors can apply directly in project construction documents. No physical facility was designed as part of this engagement; the deliverable was the specification document itself, which becomes the technical reference for electrical engineers and contractors designing and installing solar PV systems at federal installations under USACE, NAVFAC, AFCEC, and NASA programs.
