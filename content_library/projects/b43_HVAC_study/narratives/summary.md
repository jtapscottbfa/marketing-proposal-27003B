Building 43 at the Hampton VA Medical Center campus (100 Emancipation Dr, Hampton, Virginia 23667) is an approximately 90-year-old two-story structure housing administrative offices on the first floor and computer repair workshops on the second floor. The building had been served by a nominal 60-ton, two-circuit air-cooled chiller installed over 20 years prior; the 20-ton circuit had failed, leaving the system operating at a reduced effective capacity of 40 tons.

The engineering team was engaged to perform an HVAC load study to assess the building's actual cooling requirements and determine the appropriate replacement strategy for the aging central plant. Using TRACE 700 load calculation software, block load calculations were developed for both floors to establish peak cooling demand. The analysis confirmed that the building's load requirements did not exceed 40 tons — meaning the degraded chiller had not created an active capacity shortfall, but the equipment's age and condition posed ongoing operational and reliability risk.

The study concluded with a recommendation to replace the existing chiller with a new Trane CGAM 40-ton air-cooled unit using R-454B refrigerant, 460V/3-phase power, dual-circuit configuration, four scroll compressors, and BACNet MS/TP communications — achieving ASHRAE 90.1-2019 compliance. The existing chilled water distribution system, including three built-up air handling units, the constant-volume circulator pump, and coil bypass valves, was determined to be serviceable and required no modification. The study was delivered as a final report in May 2025.
