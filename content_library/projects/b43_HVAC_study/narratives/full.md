# Building 43 HVAC Load Study — Hampton VA Medical Center

## Project Overview

Building 43 is an approximately 90-year-old two-story structure on the campus of the Hampton VA Medical Center, located at 100 Emancipation Dr, Hampton, Virginia 23667, owned and operated by the US Department of Veterans Affairs. The building rests on a crawl space with a partial, full-height basement. The first floor houses administrative offices; the second floor is dedicated to computer repair workshops. With construction typical of its vintage — masonry and wood frame, minimal insulation, and legacy window assemblies — the building presented the load modeling characteristics of a historic federal facility undergoing equipment-end-of-life evaluation.

The central cooling plant serving Building 43 had reached a critical point. The nominal 60-ton, two-circuit air-cooled chiller had been in service for over 20 years, and the 20-ton circuit had failed, leaving the system operating on its remaining 40-ton circuit only. Rather than replace the failed equipment at its original nominal capacity, the client sought an independent engineering assessment to determine what the building's actual cooling demand was — and to right-size the replacement accordingly.

## Scope of Work

The engagement was a study-only commission, encompassing:

- Block load analysis of the entire building using TRACE 700 load calculation software, with calculations developed for both floors independently and in aggregate
- Assessment of the existing chilled water system — existing chiller, three built-up air handling units with chilled water coils, single constant-volume circulator pump, and three-way coil bypass valves — to determine whether components beyond the chiller required replacement
- Equipment selection for a replacement chiller, including a Trane CGAM manufacturer's equipment selection report appended to the study
- A final written study report delivered May 1, 2025 (BFA project 25-014)

No design drawings, construction documents, specifications, or construction-phase services were included in the scope.

## Role on Project

The design team served in a study-only capacity, providing independent technical analysis to support the client's capital planning and procurement decision. This included authoring the load calculations, evaluating existing system condition, selecting and documenting replacement equipment, and producing the final study report. The Trane CGAM equipment selection report was generated as a supporting appendix using manufacturer selection software, with the data generation date of May 1, 2025, matching the study's final issuance date.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Right-sizing the replacement against a partially failed existing system.** The core engineering question was whether to replace the 60-ton nominal chiller at its original capacity, or to size the replacement based on the building's actual load. The 20-ton circuit's failure had been present for some period before the study, and the building had apparently continued to function — raising the question of whether the 40-ton remaining capacity was barely adequate, comfortably adequate, or marginal under peak conditions. The TRACE 700 block load analysis resolved this directly: the building's cooling load requirements did not exceed 40 tons, confirming that a 40-ton replacement was the correct capacity. Replacing at 60 tons would have introduced unnecessary capital cost and equipment oversizing.

**Load modeling an aging, mixed-use building.** Building 43's age — approximately 90 years — and its construction characteristics (crawl space, partial basement, legacy envelope) required careful representation of building assemblies in the load model. The report includes building assembly detail sections that characterize the thermal properties of the existing construction. These assembly details and the TRACE 700 zone-level load tables are embedded in the study as formatted calculation output pages; they informed the equipment recommendation and are part of the study record.

**Preserving serviceable existing infrastructure.** A key finding of the study was that the existing chilled water distribution system did not require replacement. The three built-up air handling units with chilled water coils, the single constant-volume circulator pump, and the three-way coil bypass valves were assessed as serviceable. This determination focused the capital project scope narrowly on chiller replacement and avoided unnecessary work on downstream equipment.

## Outcomes

The study concluded with a definitive recommendation: replace the existing failed chiller with a new Trane CGAM 40-ton air-cooled chiller, making no changes to the existing chilled water distribution or pumping system.

The recommended Trane CGAM unit was selected and documented with a manufacturer's equipment selection report. Key specifications of the recommended unit:

- Nominal capacity: 40 tons
- Refrigerant: R-454B (low global-warming-potential A2L refrigerant, replacing R-410A)
- Unit voltage: 460V, 3-phase
- Total power input: 41.35 kW
- Minimum circuit ampacity (MCA): 95A; maximum overcurrent protection (MOP): 125A (single-point connection)
- Configuration: dual-circuit, four scroll compressors
- Controls interface: BACNet MS/TP communications
- Energy compliance: ASHRAE 90.1-2019

The selection of R-454B refrigerant positions the replacement unit for long-term regulatory compliance as the industry transitions away from higher-GWP refrigerants. The BACNet MS/TP interface enables integration with the campus building automation system. The final study was issued May 1, 2025.
