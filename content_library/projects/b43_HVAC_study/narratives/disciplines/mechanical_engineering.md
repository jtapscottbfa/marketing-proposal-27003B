The mechanical engineering scope for this project was the entirety of the engagement: an HVAC load study for Building 43 at the Hampton VA Medical Center campus. The study assessed the cooling load of an approximately 90-year-old, two-story building with crawl space and partial basement, using TRACE 700 block load calculation software to determine peak cooling demand for the first-floor administrative offices and second-floor computer repair workshop spaces.

The existing central cooling plant — a nominal 60-ton, two-circuit air-cooled chiller installed over 20 years prior — had experienced a circuit failure, leaving only the 40-ton circuit operational. The mechanical engineering analysis confirmed that the building's actual cooling load requirements did not exceed 40 tons, supporting a right-sized replacement rather than a like-for-like nominal capacity swap.

The recommended replacement is a Trane CGAM 40-ton air-cooled chiller with R-454B refrigerant, 460V/3-phase power, dual-circuit configuration with four scroll compressors, and BACNet MS/TP communications capability — compliant with ASHRAE 90.1-2019. The existing chilled water distribution system — three built-up air handling units with chilled water coils, a single constant-volume circulator pump, and three-way coil bypass valves maintaining constant flow — was assessed as serviceable and excluded from the replacement scope. The study included a manufacturer's equipment selection report as a supporting appendix. The final study report was issued May 1, 2025.
