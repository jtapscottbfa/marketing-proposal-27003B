The Hall A Berm Retaining Wall Replacement at Jefferson Lab (Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility) in Newport News, Virginia involved replacing a deteriorated wooden retaining wall at the east side of the Hall A berm with a new reinforced concrete cantilevered retaining wall. The existing wall — more than 25 years old and degraded in several areas — protected a critical exhaust vent and associated utility systems serving the Department of Energy particle accelerator facility. The design team served as prime consultant under Task Order #12 of Jefferson Science Associates contract JSA 21-C0683, a qualifications-based federal IDIQ task order, and delivered a complete final design by February 23, 2024.

The replacement wall design features a 12-inch-wide reinforced concrete cantilevered wall on a continuous reinforced concrete spread footing — 12 inches thick and 6 feet wide — with a drain board and corrugated pipe system on the soil side to minimize hydrostatic pressure. Because the wall sits at the lower end of the berm with limited direct equipment access, the design included a temporary gravel access road off CEBAF Boulevard, subsequently filled and restored to original grade and hydraulic capacity following construction. The total area of disturbance is approximately 0.64 acres. Design was governed by VDOT Road and Bridge Standards (2016, rev. 2022), the Virginia Erosion and Sediment Control Handbook (1992), and the Virginia Stormwater Management Program Regulation (2023). The engineer's opinion of probable construction cost at final design was $314,200.
