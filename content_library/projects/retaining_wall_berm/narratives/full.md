# Hall A Berm Retaining Wall Replacement — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The Hall A Berm Retaining Wall Replacement was a civil and structural infrastructure improvement at Jefferson Lab (Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility), a U.S. Department of Energy particle accelerator facility located at 12000 Jefferson Ave, Newport News, Virginia. The project addressed a deteriorated wooden retaining wall at the east side of the Hall A berm — a structure more than 25 years old that had degraded in several areas. The wall is a critical protective element, sheltering an existing exhaust vent and associated utility systems that serve the accelerator facility. Jefferson Lab is operated by Jefferson Science Associates (JSA), a private nonprofit management entity, under a DOE contract; the project was administered under contract JSA 21-C0683.

## Scope

The scope of work was the detailed design for replacement of the existing wooden retaining wall with a new reinforced concrete cantilevered retaining wall at the east side of the Hall A berm. Key design elements included:

- A 12-inch-wide reinforced concrete cantilevered wall
- A continuous reinforced concrete spread footing, 12 inches thick and 6 feet wide
- A drain board with corrugated pipe on the back (soil) face of the wall to minimize hydrostatic pressure and lateral loading
- A temporary gravel access road off CEBAF Boulevard to facilitate construction equipment access to the wall location
- Restoration of the access road area to original grade and hydraulic drainage capacity following construction
- Erosion, sediment, and stormwater management measures per Virginia regulatory requirements

The total area of disturbance associated with the project is approximately 0.64 acres. The design is governed by VDOT Road and Bridge Standards (2016, rev. 2022), the Virginia Erosion and Sediment Control Handbook (1992), and the Virginia Stormwater Management Program (VSMP) Regulation (2023).

## Role on the Project

The design team served as prime consultant under Task Order #12 of Jefferson Science Associates contract JSA 21-C0683, a federally administered IDIQ contract. The project was procured on a qualifications-based basis following a design-bid-build delivery method. The prime consultant led a multidisciplinary team comprising structural engineering (Richard Williams, PE — McPherson Design), geotechnical engineering (Marcos Freeman, PE — GET Solutions), and civil engineering (Jay Wheeler, PG and Matt Mader, PE — VHB). Bruce Bowman of Bowman, Foster & Associates served as Electrical Engineer and Project Manager.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Equipment access to the existing wall.** The existing wooden retaining wall is located at the lower end of the Hall A berm, with no direct access path for heavy construction equipment. The design solution included a temporary gravel access road off CEBAF Boulevard to allow machinery to reach the wall. The road was designed for temporary use only, with a planned restoration sequence to return the disturbed area to original grade and drainage capacity following construction — an approach that also contained the project's environmental and stormwater management footprint.

**Protecting critical utility systems.** The wall to be replaced directly protects an existing exhaust vent and critical utility systems serving the accelerator facility. The design required careful coordination with existing underground and surface utility locations. Geotechnical conditions of the berm — including soil bearing capacity, lateral earth pressures, and drainage characteristics — were characterized through a dedicated geotechnical investigation (Terracon Project No. K4235060, dated December 7, 2023) and informed the retaining wall structural parameters and footing dimensions. A drain board and corrugated pipe drainage system was specified on the soil side of the wall to manage long-term hydrostatic pressure, reducing lateral loading on the structure over its service life.

**Cost growth from program budget to final design.** The Task Order RFP issued in August 2023 stated a project budget of $150,000 to $250,000. The engineer's opinion of probable construction cost at final design completion (February 2024) was $314,200 — approximately 26% above the upper bound of the pre-design budget. The cost difference reflects detailed design development through the task order process, including site-specific conditions such as the access road requirement and subsurface drainage provisions.

**Regulatory compliance on a federal facility.** The project falls under Virginia stormwater management and erosion and sediment control requirements despite its location on a federal DOE facility. The 0.64-acre disturbance area triggered Virginia Stormwater Management Program (VSMP) permitting considerations and erosion and sediment control plan requirements, both of which were incorporated into the design package.

## Outcomes

The Hall A Retaining Wall Replacement Final Basis of Design was completed and signed on February 23, 2024, the formal final design milestone under Task Order #12. This was approximately two to three months after the December 1, 2023 target completion date stated in the original task order solicitation — a schedule slip attributable to routine design development and coordination, within normal range for a final design task order. The design-bid-build construction phase was projected in the original solicitation schedule for a January 2024 award; construction contract award and construction completion data are not available in the source record.
