## Project Overview

Building 9305 at Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia is a two-story masonry structure that functions simultaneously as a military barracks/training facility and an active-duty Preventive, Environmental Medicine and Health (PEMH) outpatient clinic. The embedded clinic component imposed healthcare occupancy code requirements — JCAHO, UFC 4.510.01 (Design Guide for Built Environment of Behavioral Health Facilities), DA PAM 40-50-3 (Army Industrial Hygiene), and WRAMC Construction Safety Policy — alongside standard Army installation standards and NFPA 70. The stated project objective was to provide all labor, materials, and equipment necessary to complete a scoping survey, prepare a Type III work plan, and execute construction and repair work "to ensure the provision of health services can be adequately provided for the next 20 years," while maintaining continuous patient care via transition space throughout construction.

## Scope

Project PB00010-2J encompassed selective renovations at multiple locations within and immediately adjacent to Building 9305:

**First-floor men's restroom and shower room.** Complete demolition of the existing gang shower facility — toilet partitions, plumbing fixtures, tile and substrate down to structural CMU, shower curbs, and accessories — followed by full reconstruction as an ADA-compliant single-sex restroom. New work included new toilet partitions, ADA water closet with grab bars and full transfer clearance, standard water closets, wall-hung vitreous china lavatories, wall-hung urinal, new ADA-accessible shower with stainless steel panel and fixed handheld showerhead, floor-to-ceiling ceramic tile, and new floor drains with concrete slab saw-cut for repositioned plumbing rough-in.

**First-floor women's restroom and shower room.** Same demolition and reconstruction scope as the men's restroom, without urinal. Scale of the women's restroom is somewhat smaller, as indicated by the drawing scale difference between sheets A-101 and A-102.

**First-floor former DFAC area.** Limited demolition — removal of a door, metal frame, and stud wall — to open the space and allow installation of an ADA-accessible interior ramp providing level connectivity through the former dining facility area.

**Second-floor dayroom.** New metal stud partition wall (3-5/8" studs, 5/8" gypsum board, sound batt insulation extending 6" above ceiling grid) to subdivide the existing dayroom, with a new 3'-0"×6'-8" door and metal frame providing a second means of egress and enabling conference room use. New acoustical ceiling tile installed as required.

**B-Wing exterior entrance.** Demolition of existing concrete stair entrance and adjacent sidewalk; replacement with a new ADA-compliant 1:20-slope stair and walkway combination improving accessible approach from the parking area to the B-Wing entry.

**Exterior curb ramp.** Demolition of existing 6-inch concrete curb and sidewalk at the parking lot corner adjacent to the building; new ADA-compliant curb ramp at 1:12 maximum slope, detectable warning surface (truncated dome mat) at the landing, and new concrete curb return to match the existing site edge.

## Role

The design was performed by Bowman, Foster & Assoc. Consulting Engineers, Norfolk, Virginia, serving as engineer of record. The project was delivered under a design-build contract structure, which required the design team to produce PE-sealed design drawings at 65%, 95%, and 100% IFC submittals as part of the contractor's obligations to the government — rather than as a traditional pre-bid design package. Record drawings dated 28 May 2025 document as-built conditions as constructed.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Active healthcare occupancy during construction.** The project's own objective statement acknowledged the constraint directly: "the existing clinic will provide continuing patient care via transition space" throughout the renovation. Maintaining JCAHO-compliant clinical operations in an adjacent occupied building while conducting full restroom gut-and-replacement required careful zone-by-zone construction sequencing and infection control measures consistent with WRAMC Construction Safety Policy. Healthcare occupancy requirements governed site access, dust control, and interim egress paths throughout the project.

**ADA compliance across multiple disciplines and locations.** ADA compliance was not a single design decision but a coordinating thread running through every work area: transfer-clearance water closets with grab bars in both restrooms, ADA-accessible shower systems with fold-down seats, a second egress at the second-floor dayroom, reduced-slope exterior stair approach at B-Wing, and a new detectable-warning curb ramp at the parking lot. Achieving a compliant accessible path of travel from the parking area through the building required close coordination among architectural, plumbing, civil, and electrical disciplines.

**Design-build delivery on a military installation.** The design-build contract structure compressed the typical design review timeline: PE-sealed submittals were produced under construction-contract obligations, subject to Fort Gregg-Adams DPW review, while adhering to the full stack of applicable DoD and federal codes. This required the design team to be responsive to construction sequencing needs while maintaining full compliance documentation.

**Concrete slab saw-cut for plumbing re-routes.** Repositioning shower drains and water closet rough-ins in both first-floor restrooms required saw-cutting the existing concrete slab at new fixture locations, with concrete restoration per Division 03. This added structural coordination and construction sequencing requirements to an otherwise straightforward renovation package.

## Outcomes

The project is complete. Record drawings dated 28 May 2025 document the as-built conditions of all work areas. The renovated first-floor restrooms deliver ADA-compliant gang restroom and shower facilities serving the combined barracks and clinic occupancy. The second-floor dayroom now provides a second means of egress meeting life-safety requirements. The B-Wing exterior approach and the adjacent parking-lot corner now provide accessible paths of travel meeting current federal accessibility standards. Together, the scope upgrades position Building 9305 to support delivery of health services and military occupancy for the 20-year horizon stated in the project objective.
