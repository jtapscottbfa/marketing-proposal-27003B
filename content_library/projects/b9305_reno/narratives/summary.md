Building 9305 at Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia is a two-story masonry structure that serves as both a military barracks/training facility and an active-duty Preventive, Environmental Medicine and Health (PEMH) outpatient clinic. Under project PB00010-2J, the design team delivered a selective multi-location renovation encompassing the full gut-and-replacement of first-floor gang restrooms and shower facilities to current ADA-compliant configurations, removal of former dining facility partitions to accommodate an accessible interior ramp, installation of a new second-floor egress door in the dayroom, and exterior ADA accessibility upgrades including a new curb ramp with detectable warning surfaces and replacement of the B-Wing entrance stair and walkway.

The project was executed under a design-build contract for the US Army Corps of Engineers and the Fort Gregg-Adams Directorate of Public Works. Mechanical scope included new hot-water baseboard heaters in each renovated restroom; electrical scope provided new surface-mounted lighting, exit signs, and occupancy sensors; plumbing scope encompassed complete fixture replacement — wall-hung lavatories, water closets, urinals, and ADA-accessible shower systems — with concrete slab saw-cut to accommodate plumbing re-routes. Civil scope addressed exterior accessible path-of-travel at two locations. The design was governed by JCAHO, UFC 4.510.01, NFPA, IBC, and Army installation standards, with continuous clinic occupancy maintained throughout construction. Record drawings dated May 2025 document project completion.
