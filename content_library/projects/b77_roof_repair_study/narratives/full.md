## Project Overview

This project is a Phase 2 electrical existing-conditions survey at Naval Surface Warfare Center (NSWC) Building 77H West, performed for the Naval Facilities Engineering Command — Mid Atlantic (NAVFAC MIDLANT). The work arose in connection with roof repair construction: before demolition crews could remove the existing gypsum roof deck, the deck's embedded electrical infrastructure had to be inventoried in detail. The survey produced a complete set of field-verified drawings documenting all electrical devices, conduit, panels, and ancillary systems attached to or embedded in the mezzanine-level gypsum deck. The general contractor (ARGUS CJW JV 3 LLC) used these drawings to scope and price the lighting removal scope under a NAVFAC Proposed Change/Request for Proposal issued November 2023. Survey drawings were completed and PE-sealed in February 2024 as BFA project number 23-059.

## Scope

The survey covered the full west mezzanine of Building 77H, spanning column line D/E-10 through D/E-31 — a broader area than the GC's immediate removal scope (D11–D18), providing complete west-zone coverage for Phase 2 planning. The deliverable consisted of four drawing sheets:

- **E-101:** Partial mezzanine plan, west zone (columns D/E-10 through D/E-18) — existing lighting fixtures, junction boxes, conduit, panel references, elevator disconnect, and abandoned mechanical ductwork inventory.
- **E-102:** Partial mezzanine plan, center zone (columns D/E-18 through D/E-26) — same device inventory; HVAC controller and control conduit documented to remain; NMCI fiber pullbox and cabling documented.
- **E-103:** Partial mezzanine plan, east zone (columns D/E-26 through D/E-31) plus mechanical penthouse plan and a two-part lighting one-line diagram.

The one-line diagram on E-103 traced the mezzanine lighting circuit from Panel L-16 (250A, 240/120V, 3-phase, ground floor, column B-13) through parallel timer switches to the mezzanine level, and the elevator circuit from Panel MDP (600A, 480Y/277V, 3-phase, ground floor, column D-12) through the 100A, 3-phase elevator disconnect switch. This circuit documentation confirmed which systems required demolition versus which were to remain.

The survey also inventoried: fluorescent lighting fixtures (1×4 and 1×8 types on steel beam undersides and flush-recessed in gypsum deck), embedded 1/2-inch conduit throughout the deck, 3/4-inch conduit on steel beams, flush-mounted and surface-mounted junction boxes, smoke and CO detectors referenced in the RFP for removal and salvage, NMCI fiber cabling routed in corrugated PVC along steel beams, and abandoned mechanical ductwork of varying cross-sections (20×20 through 44×46-1/2 inches) throughout the mezzanine span.

## Role on Project

The project role was study and investigation only: BFA produced the existing-conditions survey drawings that defined the scope and complexity of electrical demolition required for the roof repair. BFA did not design replacement systems, produce construction documents for new work, or provide construction administration services under this engagement. The electrical engineering team conducted the field survey, processed the findings, and delivered a sealed drawing set suitable for use by the GC in pricing the demolition scope.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Survey scope breadth relative to GC demolition zone.** The NAVFAC RFP defined the immediate work area as "approximately between column line D11 and D18," but a meaningful existing-conditions survey required coverage of the full west mezzanine to understand system boundaries, panel locations, circuit routing, and what connected to or passed through the demolition zone. The survey drawings extended from D/E-10 through D/E-31 — encompassing the full mezzanine — so the GC could see not only what was being removed but what was adjacent and must remain (elevator disconnect, HVAC controller, NMCI infrastructure).

**Multiple device types embedded in a gypsum roof deck.** The gypsum deck presented an inventory challenge because electrical devices were embedded flush in the deck surface rather than exposed on conventional framing — embedded conduit runs, flush junction boxes recessed into the deck underside, and conduit dead-ends were all field-identified and mapped. The one-line diagram confirmed which circuits could be demolished versus which required protection during demolition.

**Abandoned mechanical ductwork coordination.** Abandoned ductwork of significant cross-section (ranging from 20×20 to 44×46.5 inches) ran throughout the mezzanine, crossing the same structural bays as the electrical demolition zone. This infrastructure was inventoried on the survey drawings to inform the GC's demolition sequencing and ensure no conflicts were overlooked.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a complete, PE-sealed existing-conditions drawing set that gave the general contractor field-verified data needed to plan, price, and safely execute the lighting removal scope of the Building 77H West roof repair. The drawings captured all electrical, fire/life-safety, and ancillary infrastructure in the mezzanine ceiling plane, identified circuits and panels serving each zone, and flagged systems to remain. The survey was completed in February 2024 under the federal IDIQ/task-order contract vehicle (contract N4008520D0158, task order N4008523F6427).
