# Jefferson Lab CMSA Yard Expansion — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The Jefferson Lab CMSA Yard Expansion project expanded the Central Material Storage Area (CMSA) gravel laydown yard at Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility in Newport News, Virginia. Jefferson Lab is a U.S. Department of Energy national particle physics accelerator facility operated by Jefferson Science Associates (JSA). The project was contracted under IDIQ Subcontract No. JSA 21-C0683, Task Order 1 (JSA 21-R399547), with JSA as contracting owner and the design team serving as prime A-E consultant.

The project was a site civil infrastructure improvement in direct support of Jefferson Lab's accelerator operations — adding secured, lit gravel laydown capacity on DOE-owned land (Parcel #172000110, approximately 108 acres) immediately east of the existing CMSA yard. The total project budget was $800,000 to $1,000,000, inclusive of design, construction, and construction administration services.

## Scope

The design team developed three site layout alternatives for client review, each presenting a different balance of expansion area, cost, and stormwater management approach:

**Option 1 — 1.85 Acres (approximately 80,586 SF).** Expansion footprint approximately 460 feet by 170 feet, with an Extended Detention Basin (~20,000 SF) to the north and a Level 2 Constructed Wetland (~30,000 SF) to the south separating the expansion from the existing yard. Estimated civil and electrical construction cost: $653,462.

**Option 2 — 2.00 Acres (approximately 87,120 SF).** Expansion footprint approximately 460 feet by 189 feet, contiguous with the existing laydown yard. Level 2 Constructed Wetland with clay liner (~30,000 SF) positioned to the east; Extended Detention Basin (~20,000 SF) to the north. Estimated construction cost: $756,102.

**Option 3 — 2.80 Acres (approximately 121,968 SF).** Expansion footprint approximately 460 feet by 265 feet, with independent stormwater BMPs north and south and a new 1,100-foot outfall ditch and culvert system routing to a receiving ditch north of Building 23. Estimated construction cost: $1,185,346.

All three options shared a common gravel surface specification: 9 inches of crushed stone over a woven geotextile subgrade stabilization layer, with a minimum 1.0% positive drainage slope. Security fencing consisted of 7-foot chain-link with barbed wire matching the existing yard perimeter, with a new powered sliding gate and card-reader access control.

Electrical scope was consistent across all options: 13 new 250-watt LED light poles on 20-foot aluminum standards, with radiation monitors co-located on each pole. Power distribution extended from existing 480/277V panel ME1-H1 (3-phase, 100-amp) in Building 34 via approximately 1,800 linear feet of new 1-inch PVC lighting conduit and approximately 1,700 linear feet of 2-inch PVC communications conduit. Estimated electrical construction cost was $160,088 for all options.

Stormwater design was sized to accommodate approximately 80,000 to 120,000 SF of future impervious surfaces in accordance with Virginia stormwater regulations. Geotechnical investigation (subsurface exploration and geotechnical engineering services) was performed by subconsultant G E T Solutions. Existing site conditions were based on a Jefferson Lab-provided compiled survey, supplemented by limited on-ground verification by VHB.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime A-E consultant to Jefferson Science Associates under a federal IDIQ contract. Subconsultant VHB (South Burlington, VT) led civil engineering design and produced the civil drawing set (sheets C001 through C313 and exhibits EX-1 through EX-3). Geotechnical services were provided by G E T Solutions under a separate subconsultant authorization. The prime team coordinated the multi-discipline conceptual design effort, prepared the preliminary and final conceptual design reports, developed opinions of probable construction cost for each option, and managed client review.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**DOE Facility Safety Requirements.** Jefferson Lab's status as an active DOE accelerator facility imposed safety and security requirements not present on conventional civil site projects. The expansion area was designated for storage of radioactive materials, requiring radiation monitoring infrastructure integrated with every site lighting pole. The design team specified NEMA 3R-rated radiation monitors (120V, less than 500 VA per unit, supplied via 277V-to-120V step-down transformer) co-located at each light pole position, and incorporated Activity Hazard Analysis (AHA) and JLab safety training requirements (SAF 100C, GEN 133) into the design coordination scope.

**Budget Alignment Across Three Options.** The $800,000 to $1,000,000 total project budget (design through construction administration) created a clear evaluation boundary for the three options. Option 1 ($653,462) and Option 2 ($756,102) both fell within budget. Option 3 ($1,185,346) exceeded the upper budget threshold by approximately $185,000, making it viable only if the client prioritized maximum yard area over budget conformance. Presenting all three options with detailed cost estimates gave Jefferson Lab a transparent cost-to-area tradeoff matrix for selection.

**Stormwater Management and Wetland Proximity.** The project site adjoined existing wetland areas, and Virginia stormwater requirements mandated BMPs sized for the full potential impervious load. Options 1 and 2 accommodated stormwater management through basins sited within the disturbed area. Option 3 required a new 1,100-foot outfall ditch crossing existing wetland area, introducing environmental permitting complexity and significant additional earthwork cost compared to the other options. The drainage profile analysis (Sheets C311-C313) showed the outfall slope at approximately 0.30% — functional but resulting in a flat, wet ditch that required careful design to avoid standing water issues.

**Existing Survey Data Reliance.** The design relied on a JLab-provided compiled survey for the existing conditions base plan, with VHB performing only limited on-ground verification to confirm accuracy. This approach was practical for a conceptual design phase but introduced a degree of uncertainty that would need to be resolved through a full topographic survey prior to final design.

## Outcomes

Preliminary conceptual design documents were delivered to Jefferson Lab in October 2021; final conceptual design documents followed in November 2021. Both submittals included full civil drawing sets and opinions of probable construction cost for all three options. The documents established the technical and cost basis for Jefferson Lab to select a preferred option and authorize progression to preliminary (35%) and definite (60%) design phases. Source documents available in the project record capture the conceptual design phase only; records of option selection, final design, or construction are not included in the available project file.
