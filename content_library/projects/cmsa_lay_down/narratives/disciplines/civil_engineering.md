Civil engineering was the primary discipline on the CMSA Yard Expansion, with subconsultant VHB leading civil site design. The scope encompassed site layout development, grading and drainage, stormwater management, utility coordination, security fencing design, and preparation of an existing conditions base plan — all developed across three alternative expansion configurations.

VHB produced three distinct site layout options ranging from 1.85 to 2.80 acres of new gravel laydown area. Each option proposed a crushed stone gravel surface — 9 inches of stone over woven geotextile subgrade stabilization — graded at a minimum 1.0% positive slope for drainage. Grading profiles (Sheets C111, C211, C311–C313) established earthwork quantities and proposed finished grades for each option, accommodating existing site topography and the need to integrate stormwater management structures at workable elevations.

Stormwater design was a central challenge. Virginia stormwater regulations required drainage structures sized for approximately 80,000 to 120,000 SF of future impervious area. All three options incorporated Level 2 Constructed Wetlands and Extended Detention Basins. Option 3 required an additional 1,100-foot outfall ditch and culvert system crossing existing wetland area, which added environmental permitting complexity and significant earthwork relative to Options 1 and 2. Security fencing specified 7-foot chain-link with barbed wire matching the existing CMSA perimeter, with a new powered sliding access gate.

Civil and electrical construction cost estimates (including 20% contingency) were: Option 1, $653,462; Option 2, $756,102; Option 3, $1,185,346. The three-option civil study gave the client a direct cost-to-area comparison for decision-making within the project's $800,000 to $1,000,000 budget.
