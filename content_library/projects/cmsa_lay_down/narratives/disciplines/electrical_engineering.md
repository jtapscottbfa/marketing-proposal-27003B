Electrical engineering on the CMSA Yard Expansion addressed site lighting, radiation monitoring infrastructure, power distribution, and security gate electrical service for the new gravel laydown yard. The electrical scope was developed as part of the conceptual design and was consistent across all three site layout options.

The design called for 13 new 250-watt LED light poles on 20-foot aluminum standards, spaced at approximately 100-foot bays throughout the expansion area. Each pole was equipped with a radiation monitor — a programmatic requirement driven by Jefferson Lab's designation of the expansion area for storage of radioactive materials associated with the facility's particle physics accelerator operations. Radiation monitors were specified at 120V via a 277V-to-120V step-down transformer (NEMA 3R rated, less than 500 VA per unit) co-located at each pole.

Power distribution was designed to extend from existing 480/277V panel ME1-H1, a 3-phase, 100-amp panel in Building 34 (Material Handling Equipment Storage). New 1-inch PVC conduit (approximately 1,800 linear feet) distributed lighting circuits; 2-inch PVC conduit (approximately 1,700 linear feet) served communications; 3/4-inch conduit served the powered sliding security gate card reader. The existing gate power and control was relocated and rerouted as part of the security gate reconfiguration.

Estimated electrical construction cost was $160,088, included in each option's total opinion of probable cost. The integration of radiation monitoring with standard site lighting infrastructure is a notable design feature reflecting the specialized requirements of an active DOE national laboratory facility.
