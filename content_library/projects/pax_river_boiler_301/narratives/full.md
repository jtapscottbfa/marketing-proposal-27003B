## Project Overview

Building 301 at Naval Air Station (NAS) Patuxent River, Maryland is a two-story support facility housing a central boiler plant that provides steam heating to the building. The project, B301 Replace Steam Boiler and Steam Traps, was initiated by Naval Facilities Engineering Command, Washington (NAVFAC Washington) following failure of the building's primary steam boiler. NAVFAC issued a competitive 8(a) Mechanical Multi-Award Construction Contract (MACC) task order solicitation in June 2020 (Solicitation No. N40080-20-R-9100, EPROJECT #1623087), with an estimated construction budget of approximately $480,138 and a required construction duration of 180 calendar days. The engineering design team authored the Basis of Design document (BFA Project #20-053, Final Submittal December 2020) that established the technical scope, equipment basis, and design criteria for the NAVFAC construction solicitation.

## Scope of Work

The project's scope encompassed full replacement of the failed steam boiler and all associated plant equipment and controls, together with a building-wide steam trap replacement program and required asbestos abatement. Key elements:

**Steam Boiler Replacement.** Remove the existing broken steam boiler and install a new high-efficiency gas-fired cast iron steam boiler rated at 5.6 million BTU per hour (MMBH), with auto/manual blowdown capability. The new boiler plant included two boiler feed pumps with variable frequency drives (VFDs), a new deaerator, expansion tank, and hot water chemical treatment unit — a complete, serviceable replacement for the facility's central heating plant.

**Steam Trap Replacement.** Replace approximately 110 steam traps throughout the two-story building: approximately 65 brass thermostatic angle steam traps attached to radiators on the first and second floors, and approximately 45 inverted bucket steam traps on ceiling-hung heaters in Room 101R.

**Asbestos Abatement.** Conduct OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1101-compliant asbestos abatement at approximately 66 existing steam trap locations prior to trap replacement.

**Building Automation and Controls.** Provide a new DDC panel with BACnet open protocol control system, compatible with the facility's existing Niagara building automation software, integrating boiler and steam system controls into NAS Patuxent River's base-wide monitoring infrastructure.

**Electrical Modifications.** Modify the existing 200A, 208Y/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire electrical service to accommodate new mechanical equipment, including VFD power connections for boiler feed pumps and motor connections at 208V or 480V, 3-phase, 60 Hz.

**Gas and Plumbing.** Evaluate and reuse existing 2 psi natural gas supply piping where adequately sized; modify the steam and condensate system as required; insulate domestic hot water supply and recirculation piping.

**Regulatory.** Obtain a Permit to Construct (PTC) from the Maryland Department of the Environment for the new gas-fired boiler installation. Construction performed with the building remaining occupied.

## Role on the Project

The engineering team served as the design engineer authoring the Basis of Design document that defined the technical scope, equipment specifications, and design criteria for all mechanical, electrical, and plumbing disciplines on the project. The BOD served as the government-furnished technical basis enabling NAVFAC Washington to solicit competitive bids from pre-qualified 8(a) Mechanical MACC contractors under FAR 16.505, with award on a lowest-price basis. The engineering role was on the design side; construction was separately contracted through the 8(a) MACC vehicle.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Occupied facility with critical heating system.** Building 301 remained occupied throughout construction, requiring the replacement sequence to maintain heating service continuity. The BOD addressed this by anticipating operating both new and potentially retained boiler equipment during the transition period, providing a phased approach that avoided a hard heating outage.

**Asbestos abatement integrated into steam trap replacement.** With approximately 66 of the building's steam trap locations covered in asbestos insulation, the abatement work had to be sequenced and priced as an integral part of the trap replacement scope. The design explicitly documented the abatement requirement and applicable regulatory standard, ensuring the construction solicitation could be properly scoped and priced by bidders.

**Regulatory permit sequencing.** Installation of a new gas-fired boiler required a Permit to Construct from the Maryland Department of the Environment, a regulatory step with its own lead time that had to be incorporated into the 180-calendar-day construction schedule. The BOD documented this requirement to align contractor planning from the outset.

**Steam trap count and type inventory.** The scope required a complete inventory of existing steam trap types and locations across a two-story building to properly specify replacements. The BOD documented two distinct trap types — thermostatic angle traps on radiators and inverted bucket traps on ceiling-hung heaters — with approximate quantities by floor and area, enabling accurate material take-offs for the construction solicitation.

**BACnet/Niagara integration requirement.** The replacement boiler controls had to be compatible with the installation's existing Niagara building automation system. The BOD specified an open-protocol BACnet DDC panel as the controls basis, ensuring the new plant could be monitored and controlled through the existing base-wide BAS without requiring a parallel or proprietary controls infrastructure.

## Outcomes

The engineering team delivered a complete Basis of Design establishing the technical foundation for a full steam boiler plant replacement and building-wide steam trap program at NAS Patuxent River Building 301. The BOD enabled NAVFAC Washington to issue a competitive 8(a) MACC task order solicitation with a defined construction budget of approximately $480,000 and a 180-day construction window. The design addressed the full MEP scope — including BACnet/Niagara controls integration, asbestos abatement requirements, regulatory permitting, and occupied-building construction constraints — delivering a complete construction procurement package. Given the project timeline (solicitation issued June 2020, BOD finalized December 2020, 180-day construction duration), the project is presumed complete.
