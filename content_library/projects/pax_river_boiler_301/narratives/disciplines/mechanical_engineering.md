The mechanical engineering scope for Building 301 at NAS Patuxent River centered on the complete replacement of the facility's failed steam heating system. The design team specified removal of the existing broken steam boiler and installation of a new high-efficiency gas-fired cast iron steam boiler rated at 5.6 million BTU per hour (MMBH), sized to maintain the building's steam heating capacity. Supporting the new boiler, the design included two boiler feed pumps with variable frequency drives (VFDs), a new deaerator, expansion tank, hot water chemical treatment unit, and auto/manual blowdown system — providing a fully serviceable replacement boiler plant.

A comprehensive steam trap replacement program was a major component of the mechanical scope. Approximately 65 brass thermostatic angle steam traps on first- and second-floor radiators and approximately 45 inverted bucket steam traps on ceiling-hung heaters in Room 101R were replaced throughout the two-story building, totaling approximately 110 steam traps. Asbestos abatement at approximately 66 trap locations — required per OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1101 — was integrated into the replacement sequence.

The mechanical design specified a new DDC panel with BACnet open protocol control system, compatible with the facility's existing Niagara building automation software, integrating the replacement boiler plant into NAS Patuxent River's base-wide monitoring and control infrastructure. A Maryland Department of the Environment Permit to Construct (PTC) was required for the new gas-fired boiler and was documented as a design requirement in the Basis of Design. Construction was planned with the building remaining occupied throughout.
