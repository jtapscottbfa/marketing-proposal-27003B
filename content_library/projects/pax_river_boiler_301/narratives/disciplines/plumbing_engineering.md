The plumbing engineering scope for Building 301 at NAS Patuxent River encompassed natural gas supply evaluation, steam and condensate system modifications associated with the boiler and steam trap replacement program, and domestic hot water piping insulation.

The design team evaluated the existing natural gas supply piping serving the boiler plant. The building's gas supply enters at 2 psi through an existing regulator; the design stipulated reuse of the existing supply piping where found to be adequately sized for the new boiler's demand, avoiding unnecessary piping replacement while ensuring properly sized fuel delivery to the new 5.6 MMBH gas-fired boiler.

Steam and condensate system modifications were required in conjunction with the boiler replacement and the comprehensive building-wide steam trap replacement program. Installation of approximately 110 new steam traps — 65 thermostatic angle traps at radiators on the first and second floors and 45 inverted bucket traps at ceiling-hung heaters in Room 101R — necessitated associated piping work at each trap location throughout the two-story building.

Domestic hot water supply and recirculation piping insulation was included in the plumbing scope, addressing energy conservation and condensation control requirements for the facility's domestic hot water distribution system.
