The engineering design team authored the Basis of Design for the replacement of the failed steam boiler and complete steam heating system in Building 301 at Naval Air Station (NAS) Patuxent River, Maryland, supporting a Naval Facilities Engineering Command, Washington (NAVFAC Washington) 8(a) Mechanical MACC task order solicitation (N40080-20-R-9100) with an estimated construction budget of approximately $480,000 and a 180-calendar-day construction period.

Scope included removal of the existing broken steam boiler and installation of a new high-efficiency gas-fired cast iron steam boiler rated at 5.6 million BTU per hour (MMBH), complete with dual boiler feed pumps with variable frequency drives (VFDs), deaerator, expansion tank, hot water chemical treatment unit, and auto/manual blowdown capability. Approximately 110 steam traps were replaced throughout the two-story building — 65 brass thermostatic angle traps on first- and second-floor radiators and 45 inverted bucket traps on ceiling-hung heaters in Room 101R. Asbestos abatement was required at approximately 66 trap locations prior to replacement. A new DDC panel with BACnet open protocol, fully compatible with the facility's existing Niagara building automation system, integrated the replacement boiler plant into base-wide monitoring and controls infrastructure. Electrical service modifications accommodated new equipment loads, and natural gas supply piping was evaluated and reused where adequately sized. The project required a Maryland Department of the Environment Permit to Construct and was executed with the building remaining occupied throughout construction.
