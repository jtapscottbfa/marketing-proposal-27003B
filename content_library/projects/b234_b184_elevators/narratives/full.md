# Full Narrative — Building 234 / Building 184 Freight Elevator Replacement, Norfolk Naval Shipyard

## Project Overview

Norfolk Naval Shipyard (NNSY) in Portsmouth, Virginia, is one of the oldest and largest naval industrial installations in the United States. Two of its working buildings — Building 234 and Building 184 — housed aging freight elevators that had reached the end of their serviceable lives. Building 234's Elevator 34 was a hydraulic system, 10,000-lb capacity, serving three floors; Building 184's Elevator 60 was a geared traction system, 16,000-lb capacity, serving four floors. Both systems required full replacement to restore operational reliability, bring the facilities into compliance with current UFC and ASME elevator codes, and address a range of accumulated building deficiencies that had developed around the aging elevator shafts and machine rooms.

The design team provided architect-engineer services under NAVFAC contract N40085-17-D-0347, a NAVFAC A-E IDIQ contract administered by Naval Facilities Engineering Command PWD Portsmouth. The project was a FY 2020 task order with combined program funding of approximately $2.68 million across the two buildings ($1,275,461 for Building 234 and $1,400,000 for Building 184), and an estimated construction completion date of August 30, 2023.

## Scope of Services

The design team produced two separate, fully coordinated design-bid-build construction packages — one per building — encompassing drawings, specifications, detailed cost estimates, basis of design reports, site engineering investigation reports, sustainable design reports, and hazardous materials survey documentation.

**Elevator Systems.** Both replacement elevators were specified as Class C1 geared electric traction freight elevators rated between 200 and 250 fpm, compliant with UFC 3-490-06 (Elevators) and the ASME A17.1-2016 Safety Code for Elevators and Escalators. For Building 234, this entailed a full type change: the existing hydraulic system was demolished in its entirety, including hydraulic oil removal and hydraulic piston casing backfill and capping per NNSY requirements, and a new 10,000-lb geared traction unit installed in the existing shaft. For Building 184, the existing geared traction unit was removed and replaced in-kind with a new 16,000-lb geared traction elevator. Both packages required NAVFAC Vertical Transportation Equipment (VTE) Program approval, certified third-party elevator inspection, and emergency communication systems (in-car and machine-room phones).

**Structural.** The design team conducted structural analysis of elevator pits, shaft walls, and machine rooms at both buildings. Existing shaft walls and machine room walls at both locations were found to be cracked and spalled, requiring the team to document conditions through site investigation and design appropriate concrete repairs. At Building 234, the additional complexity of infilling the hydraulic piston hole in the pit floor, waterproofing the pit floor and walls, and repairing crack and spall damage to exterior walls and adjacent stairwell walls was addressed in the design. Structural analysis confirmed that the existing framing could support the new elevator equipment or identified necessary reinforcement.

**Mechanical and HVAC.** Both machine rooms received new HVAC systems designed to current UFC 3-410-01 and ASHRAE 90.1 standards, replacing existing single electric wall-mounted unit heaters that were at the end of their service life. The new systems incorporated direct digital control (DDC) integrated with the NNSY base-wide DDC building automation network, and included testing and balancing. Machine room ventilation and thermal comfort were designed to meet UFC 3-410-01 requirements.

**Electrical.** Both buildings required new electrical service and distribution for the replacement elevators. Building 234's existing 480V three-phase bus duct supply from Unit Substation YA remained in place, with a new plug-in circuit breaker and feeder sized for the new traction elevator, along with a 100A 208Y/120V machine room panel for cab lights and power. Building 184's 208Y/120V switchboard feed was replaced with new circuit breaker, feeder, and panel sized for the new elevator. Both buildings retained existing emergency telephone cables for reconnection to new elevator controllers. Energy Star and low-power-consumption equipment was specified throughout in compliance with UFC 1-200-02 High Performance and Sustainable Building requirements.

**Plumbing.** Existing sump pumps in the elevator pits at both buildings were found to be submerged in mud and inoperable. New sump pumps were designed, with discharge piped to approved drainage points as directed by NAVFAC. At Building 234, hydraulic oil removal from the underground piston casing represented a discrete plumbing/hazmat scope element.

**Fire Protection.** The design included wet sprinkler systems in machine rooms at both buildings, connected to the existing second-floor sprinkler systems, with flow switches and shunt-trip breakers for elevator shutdown on sprinkler activation per UFC 3-490-06. Fire alarm interfaces provided elevator recall and shutdown per NFPA 72, with smoke detectors wall-mounted above elevator doors at all landings. The existing Simplex 4010 fire alarm panel (first floor, Building 234) required coordination. All shaft and machine room penetrations were firestopped.

**Historic Preservation.** Norfolk Naval Shipyard is a historic installation, and both buildings required coordination with the Virginia State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) under Section 106. Windows at both machine rooms were replaced in compliance with SHPO requirements to achieve a finding of No Adverse Effect. The A-E team prepared required documentation including project descriptions, justification for replacement over repair, dimensional records, and photographs of elements to be replaced.

**Hazardous Materials.** A hazardous materials survey and testing program was required at both buildings, consistent with UFC 3-810-01N. Building 234 carried the additional hazmat burden of hydraulic oil in the existing underground piston casing, requiring removal and casing backfill.

**Post-Construction Award Services (PCAS).** The task order included PCAS covering shop drawing review, record drawing preparation, field consultation during construction, design consultation, and attendance at the pre-construction conference.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime A-E consultant under the NAVFAC A-E IDIQ contract, holding direct design responsibility across all disciplines: architecture, structural engineering, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, plumbing engineering, and fire protection engineering. The kickoff meeting and design review meetings were attended by the project manager, architect, structural engineer, mechanical engineer, electrical engineer, cost engineer, and fire protection engineer from the A-E team.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

The project presented several compounding challenges beyond a straightforward elevator replacement. First, Building 234's hydraulic elevator required a fundamental type change to geared traction — converting an existing hydraulic shaft that included an underground piston casing to a traction elevator configuration, with attendant hazmat, structural, and waterproofing implications. Second, the existing infrastructure at both buildings was in poor condition: cracked and spalled shaft walls and machine rooms, inoperable sump pumps submerged in mud, and decades-old electrical service requiring full replacement. Third, the historic installation context introduced SHPO coordination requirements at both buildings, requiring careful documentation and window replacement design to meet the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation. Fourth, Building 234's machine room is located within the NNSY Control Industrial Area (CIA), imposing access restrictions requiring all design and field personnel to be U.S. citizens meeting base access requirements.

The design team addressed these challenges through thorough site engineering investigation at both locations, producing separate investigation reports that documented existing conditions across all disciplines, and through careful coordination between structural, MEP, and elevator disciplines to ensure the new installations fit within existing shaft geometries while meeting current code requirements.

## Outcomes

The project resulted in two fully coordinated design-bid-build construction packages for freight elevator replacement at Buildings 234 and 184 at Norfolk Naval Shipyard, with a combined program value of approximately $2.68 million and an estimated construction completion date of August 30, 2023. The designs brought both facilities into full compliance with UFC 3-490-06, ASME A17.1-2016, and related fire protection, mechanical, and electrical codes, and resolved the accumulated building deficiencies — structural deterioration, mechanical obsolescence, electrical inadequacy, and hazardous material presence — that had developed alongside the aging elevator systems.
