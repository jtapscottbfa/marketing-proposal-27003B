# Building 87 Re-Roof Design — Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility
## Full Project Narrative

### Project Overview

The Building 87 Re-Roof Design project involved producing a complete set of construction documents for the re-roofing of Building 87 at the Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab), a federally owned Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) operated by Jefferson Science Associates, LLC (JSA) on behalf of the United States Department of Energy. Jefferson Lab is a particle-physics accelerator facility located at 12000 Jefferson Avenue, Newport News, Virginia 23606.

Building 87 is a 6,720-square-foot, one-story, pre-engineered metal building constructed in 1996. The structure was later renovated into an office building, housing staff who remained in occupancy throughout the construction period. The original metal roof was coated with an elastomeric coating in 2010, but by the time of this project the coating had deteriorated, the metal roofing was actively rusting, and the entire roof was in substandard condition — posing a risk of interior water damage that necessitated full replacement.

The project was procured as Task Order No. 3 under Purchase Order 21C0683003 issued by JSA/Jefferson Lab, with a design fee of $31,064 and a construction cost budget cap of $130,000. The final submittal drawing set — seven sheets sealed by the architect of record and engineer of record on August 16, 2022 — constitutes the primary project deliverable captured in the source record.

---

### Scope of Work

**Roofing System Replacement.** The design replaced the full roof assembly with a new mechanically adhered EPDM membrane over 1½-inch polyisocyanurate insulation between roof panel flutes, with a ½-inch cover board over the existing coated metal panel. The new system was designed to meet Jefferson Lab's high-performance building standards, incorporating energy conservation, green building principles, and sustainable design as required by the task order. Metal coping at the parapets, and gutters and downspouts at the roof perimeter, were replaced with prefinished metal units. Existing concrete splash blocks were salvaged and reinstalled, with broken units replaced.

**Solatube Daylighting System.** Approximately 13–14 existing tubular daylighting devices distributed across the roof were addressed in the design: existing solatube acrylic domes were removed, disconnected from their original installations, and stored, then reinstalled on new 12-inch prefabricated solatube curbs that integrate cleanly with the new roof assembly. This approach preserved the building's existing daylighting infrastructure without full replacement.

**Plumbing Penetrations.** Existing vent-through-roof (VTR) plumbing penetrations were retained and re-integrated with the new roof assembly using new prefabricated pipe boot flashings.

**Mechanical and Antenna Demolition.** Existing rooftop exhaust fans were demolished and their associated electrical circuits were removed back to the panelboard source. A non-working antenna on the north elevation was removed and disposed of. Existing solatube assemblies were removed, stored, and reinstalled per architectural drawings.

**Occupancy Continuity.** Per Jefferson Lab's requirements, the project was structured during construction to allow building staff to remain in the building without major interruption to their work.

### Technical Scope: Lightning Protection System

A significant portion of the electrical design involved the complete demolition and reinstallation of the building's rooftop lightning protection system — work that was necessitated by the full roof replacement. The existing system consisted of a perimeter conductor installed on the roof's north and south grid lines, 24 typical air terminals at the perimeter, and 4 corner air terminals (28 total), connected to vertical down conductors at the four building corners that penetrate through the roof. The task order specified that the rooftop lightning protection grid be temporarily removed and then reinstalled on the new roof surface; existing vertical down conductors and the balance of the below-roof system were to remain.

Construction documents required that damaged conductors be replaced with No. A24 Class 1 copper conductor (24 strands of 14 AWG) and that damaged air terminals be replaced with ½-inch by 12-inch minimum solid copper equivalents — both consistent with NFPA 780 / UL 96A requirements. A notable Jefferson Lab facility-specific requirement was the prohibition of exothermic welds, which is standard on NFPA 780 installations but was explicitly excluded at this facility. Post-construction, a third-party UL-certified inspection and master label certificate were required.

The lightning protection demolition plan (ED-101) and new-work plan (E-101) provided the complete existing-system inventory and new installation requirements at 1/8-inch scale.

### Design Approach and Standards

The task order required the design to meet Jefferson Lab's high-performance building standards, with explicit inclusion of energy conservation, green building principles, and sustainable design. The selection of a mechanically adhered EPDM system over polyisocyanurate insulation reflects a well-established high-performance roofing approach: EPDM provides long service life with low maintenance requirements, and polyisocyanurate offers among the highest R-values per inch of any roofing insulation, consistent with an energy-conservation objective.

Jefferson Lab imposed specialized training requirements on all design team members performing site visits, including ESH&Q Orientation for Subcontractors (SAF 100C), General Employee Radiation Training (GERT), and Activity Hazard Analysis Preparation (GEN133) — requirements consistent with the accelerator facility's radiation safety program and reflecting the specialized nature of working within a DOE national laboratory environment.

### Delivery

The project was delivered in accordance with Jefferson Lab's standard A-E task order process, including 35% design, 100% design, and final design submittals with JLab review cycles at the 35% and 100% stages. The task order required production of both AutoCAD-native files and PDF deliverables. The final submittal drawing set was sealed on August 16, 2022 — approximately four weeks after the scheduled final design date of July 19, 2022, a slip consistent with the normal review-comment incorporation cycle following the 100% review. Construction support services (submittal reviews, RFI/CCR support, periodic inspections, and as-built drawings) were anticipated to be negotiated under a separate instrument following 100% design completion; no construction-phase documentation is present in the source record.

The design contract value was $31,064. The construction budget cap, including a 5% final design project contingency, was $130,000.
