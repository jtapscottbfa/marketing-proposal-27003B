# Building 87 Re-Roof Design — Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility

The Building 87 Re-Roof Design project delivered construction documents for the complete re-roofing of a 6,720-square-foot, one-story pre-engineered metal building at the Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab), a Department of Energy national laboratory in Newport News, Virginia. The existing metal roof — originally installed when the building was constructed in 1996 and coated with an elastomeric coating in 2010 — had deteriorated to a substandard condition, with active rusting and risk of interior water damage. The project replaced the full roof assembly with a mechanically adhered EPDM membrane over 1½-inch polyisocyanurate insulation, with associated replacement of metal coping, gutters, and downspouts, reinstallation of approximately 13–14 tubular daylighting devices on prefabricated curbs, and patching of the metal deck.

The design also addressed the building's rooftop lightning protection system in its entirety: the existing perimeter grid — comprising 28 air terminals and a continuous conductor on two roof grid lines — was demolished, salvaged, and reinstalled on the new roof surface, with damaged components replaced to NFPA 780/UL 96A standards. Post-construction third-party UL-certified testing with a master label certificate was specified. The project also demolished rooftop exhaust fans and a non-working antenna.

The work was performed under Jefferson Lab's A-E IDIQ program as Task Order No. 3 under Purchase Order 21C0683003, with a design fee of $31,064 and a construction budget cap of $130,000. The final submittal drawing set — comprising seven sheets — was sealed on August 16, 2022. The project was structured to allow Jefferson Lab staff to remain in the building during construction without major interruption.
