# UFGS 23 82 00: Terminal Heating Units — Project Narrative

## Overview

The firm provided professional A-E services to develop an updated, unified tri-service Unified Facilities Guide Specification (UFGS 23 82 00) for Terminal Heating Units. The engagement was administered by the National Institute of Building Sciences (NIBS) on behalf of Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command (NAVFAC) Atlantic under IDIQ contract N6247019D5013, with coordination centered in Norfolk, Virginia. The project reached completion in September 2025.

Terminal heating units — including electric space heaters, gas-fired heating equipment, radiant units, and related controls — are widely used mechanical systems across DoD facilities. The existing specification landscape for these systems consisted of multiple service-branch documents with overlapping, conflicting, and outdated requirements. This A-E task addressed that fragmentation by producing a single unified document that facility designers and contractors can apply consistently across Army, Navy, and Air Force facility programs.

## Scope of Work

The scope required merging three existing service-branch specifications into one:

- **UFGS-23 80 20.00 10** (Navy — Gas-Fired Heating Equipment)
- **UFGS-23 82 00.00 20** (Army — Terminal Heating Units)
- **UFGS-23 83 00.00 20** (Army — Electric Space Heating Equipment)

Work included incorporating current technologies, applicable industry standards and publications, and updating energy and performance requirements across all terminal heating unit types covered by the unified specification. In parallel, the team prepared a cross-reference comparison matrix — an 8-page document cataloging the alignment of all four specification versions (including the base UFGS 23 82 00) — to support merge decisions and document the source of each provision as it carried into or was revised in the unified document.

Outstanding Criteria Change Requests (CCRs) were resolved as part of the engagement, with technical resolutions including updates to reference standards for radiant heating equipment. Facilitated review meetings supported multi-party coordination among Army, Navy, and Air Force criteria stakeholders. The required submittal format was Word/SpecsIntact, consistent with DoD specification development standards, and the final specification was also delivered in PDF form.

## Role

The firm served as prime consultant for the entire scope of work, responsible for specification authoring, cross-reference analysis, CCR resolution, and facilitation of multi-service review coordination. The engagement was procured on a qualifications basis under a federal A-E IDIQ contract vehicle administered through NIBS.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Tri-service consolidation complexity.** Merging three service-branch specifications with independent development lineages required methodical cross-referencing to avoid dropping requirements, creating internal conflicts, or introducing interpretive ambiguity in the unified document. The cross-reference matrix — delivered as an intermediate work product — was the primary tool for managing this complexity, ensuring every provision in each source specification was tracked to an explicit disposition in the unified spec: merged, retained, revised, or superseded.

**Updating technical standards.** Terminal heating unit technology and applicable reference standards had evolved since the source specifications were last revised. CCR resolutions required evaluating current industry standards in the radiant heating equipment category and updating references accordingly, incorporating those changes within the structured UFGS format without disrupting the document's enforceability or inter-section consistency. This required applied mechanical engineering judgment alongside specifications expertise.

**Multi-stakeholder review coordination.** A tri-service criteria document requires consensus from Army, Navy, and Air Force criteria representatives before it can be issued. Facilitated review meetings structured this coordination and provided a managed path to resolving disagreements among the services, allowing the final document to reflect agreed unified requirements rather than service-specific preferences.

## Outcomes

The engagement produced the following deliverables:

- A unified 28-page UFGS 23 82 00 specification for Terminal Heating Units, consolidating all three source documents and resolving outstanding CCRs, delivered in Word/SpecsIntact and PDF formats
- An 8-page cross-reference comparison matrix documenting the alignment of all four specification versions considered in the consolidation
- CCR resolutions with updated reference standards for radiant heating equipment
- Facilitated multi-service review meetings supporting criteria review and approval

The final specification serves as the design and construction reference for terminal heating unit systems across DoD facilities and was delivered in September 2025 under the NAVFAC A-E IDIQ.
