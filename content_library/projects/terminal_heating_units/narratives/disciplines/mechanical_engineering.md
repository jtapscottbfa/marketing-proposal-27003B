Mechanical engineering provided the technical foundation for this specification development engagement. The project centered on terminal heating units — mechanical terminal equipment including gas-fired heaters, electric space heaters, radiant heating units, and associated controls systems — requiring mechanical engineering expertise to evaluate, reconcile, and update technical requirements across three source specifications.

Sub-disciplines engaged included HVAC design and HVAC controls, reflecting the systems coverage of the unified UFGS specification. The mechanical engineering scope involved assessing current industry standards and performance requirements applicable to the full range of terminal heating unit types, incorporating those standards into the revised specification, and resolving outstanding CCRs with updated reference standards for radiant heating equipment. The statement of work specifically required incorporating "current technologies, applicable industry standards and publications, and update energy/performance requirements" — technical work demanding mechanical engineering judgment alongside specifications authorship.

This project produced no constructed facility; the deliverable was a criteria document intended to guide facility designers and contractors on future DoD projects across all three military services. Mechanical engineering expertise was essential to ensuring the unified specification reflects accurate, current, and enforceable technical requirements for terminal heating equipment across the range of building types, climatic conditions, and operational demands encountered in DoD facility programs.
