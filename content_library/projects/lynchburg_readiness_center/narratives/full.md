## Project Overview

The Lynchburg Readiness Center HVAC Upgrades project involved comprehensive mechanical and electrical design services for the replacement of the aging HVAC system at the Virginia National Guard's Lynchburg Readiness Center in Lynchburg, Virginia. The project was commissioned by the Virginia Department of Military Affairs (DMA) under a qualifications-based selection process governed by the Virginia Construction and Professional Services Manual (CPSM), with a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,099,000. The design team was engaged via a request for fee proposal issued in August 2022 and was required to complete design within 210 calendar days of notice to proceed. Design services were delivered as prime consultant under a design-bid-build contract structure, with the final Basis of Design submittal completed in January 2024.

## Scope

The project scope encompassed complete replacement of all rooftop HVAC units, with equipment selection guided by a CPSM life-cycle cost analysis to satisfy Commonwealth of Virginia procurement standards for long-term cost efficiency. Beyond equipment replacement, the design addressed the full distribution system: existing ductwork was analyzed with a determination of replacement or extensive cleaning based on field conditions, and the same evaluation framework was applied to existing piping.

A notable scope element was the incorporation of infectious disease prevention technologies. The client's requirements explicitly called for bipolar ionization, high-efficiency filtration, ultraviolet (UV) lighting, visible lighting, cold-fusion systems, and other emergent air-quality technologies to reduce the potential spread of airborne pathogens within the facility. These technologies were integrated into the replacement system as a unified indoor air-quality package. The Building Automation System (BAS) was upgraded to provide humidity and temperature monitoring and control, and HVAC zoning was redesigned to limit cross-zone migration of airborne particles.

Supporting the mechanical scope, the electrical design included removal of all existing electrical devices, wiring, and conduit associated with HVAC equipment back to the serving panels; installation of new circuits for the replacement HVAC units; temporary support of interior lighting fixtures during ductwork demolition in affected ceiling systems; and reinstallation of those fixtures following ductwork reconstruction. Minor architectural work was included as required to support the HVAC and electrical installations.

## Role

The design team served as prime consultant under a state QBS design-bid-build contract. Disciplines engaged were mechanical engineering and electrical engineering. A site investigation was performed on December 5, 2022, to document existing mechanical and electrical infrastructure and confirm field conditions prior to design development. The investigation produced a Site Environmental/Engineering Investigation Report (SEIR) for the electrical systems and a corresponding field report, both characterizing the existing electrical distribution infrastructure. The mechanical Basis of Design was developed through final submittal in January 2024.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Infectious disease prevention requirements.** The DMA's scope included explicit requirements for post-pandemic indoor air-quality technologies—bipolar ionization, UV lighting, cold-fusion systems, and high-efficiency filtration—representing an expanded HVAC scope beyond a conventional equipment replacement. The design integrated these technologies into the replacement system alongside the BAS zoning and controls upgrades, addressing both comfort and pathogen mitigation objectives within a single coordinated mechanical design.

**Existing conditions complexity.** The site investigation identified approximately 15 locations with displaced ceiling tiles resulting from a prior fire protection system installation, a missing ceiling grille in the kitchen area, and concerns about the accuracy of panel directories for several electrical distribution panels. These conditions informed the design approach and construction sequencing: the electrical scope explicitly addressed temporary lighting support during demolition phases and the need for panel verification before circuit work could proceed.

**BAS integration.** The existing facility had limited humidity and temperature control capability. The design upgraded the BAS to provide comprehensive environmental monitoring and zoning controls aligned with both occupant comfort requirements and the infectious disease mitigation strategy, extending the control system's reach across the new zone configuration.

## Outcomes

Design services were completed through final Basis of Design submittal in January 2024, approximately 14–15 months after the initial request for fee proposal. The project was delivered under a design-bid-build contract structure with a not-to-exceed construction budget of $1,099,000. Construction contract award dates, construction duration, and final construction contract value are not documented in the available source record.
