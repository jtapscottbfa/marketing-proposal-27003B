# Site Eyewash and Safety Shower Stations — Jefferson Lab

## Project Overview

Jefferson Laboratory (Jefferson Lab) in Newport News, Virginia — a Department of Energy national research facility operated by Jefferson Science Associates, LLC (JSA) — undertook a site-wide safety infrastructure upgrade to replace and install ANSI Z358.1-2014-compliant eyewash and safety shower stations across its campus. The campus includes accelerator experimental halls (Hall C, Hall D, LERF) and a broad array of research support buildings. The upgrade addressed approximately 20-plus distinct building and room locations (Buildings 36, 38, 55, 57, 58, 67, 90, 96, 201, and others) spread across the 12000 Jefferson Avenue, Newport News, VA facility.

The design team was engaged as prime consultant under Task Order 7 (TO 07) of IDIQ contract JSA 21-C0683 (Purchase Order 21C0683007, awarded April 19, 2023). The project was structured as a design-only A-E engagement; construction was to be procured and managed separately by the owner under a design-bid-build delivery method.

## Scope of Services

The scope of A-E services encompassed plumbing, mechanical, and electrical engineering design for the full site-wide upgrade. Governing codes and standards included ANSI Z358.1-2014 (the primary standard for emergency eyewash and shower equipment), the International Plumbing Code, NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code), Jefferson Lab's Design Standard, and federal energy requirements under ANSI/ASHRAE/IESNA Standard 90.1-2019, DOE Order 436.1, 10 CFR Part 433, and 10 CFR Part 434.

**Plumbing engineering** covered domestic water supply and sanitary drain, waste, and vent (DWV) systems for each eyewash and safety shower location. Each installation required connections to building domestic water and sanitary systems, and the multi-building character of the project required assessing existing utility conditions in each affected building independently.

**Mechanical engineering** addressed the tepid-water system requirements that ANSI Z358 imposes — the standard mandates that water delivered at emergency fixtures be maintained within a specific temperature range. Mechanical/HVAC design encompassed the systems needed to condition supply water to each location in accordance with those requirements.

**Electrical engineering** covered power distribution for the associated electrical systems at each installation location.

The project was not characterized by a single gross square footage; it was a distributed site-safety infrastructure program spanning multiple independently-occupied buildings across the full Jefferson Lab campus.

The design team served as prime consultant, holding the direct contractual relationship with Jefferson Science Associates/Jefferson Lab under the on-call IDIQ. All three engineering disciplines — plumbing, mechanical, and electrical — were delivered under this prime engagement. The total A-E design fee was $37,211, against the owner's total program budget of $260,000. The contractual design schedule called for delivery in 137 days from Notice to Proceed (NTP).

The procurement vehicle was a federal IDIQ/task-order contract with Jefferson Lab. Task order award was qualifications-based, consistent with Brooks Act A-E procurement practices at DOE facilities. Jefferson Lab personnel and the DOE facility context required adherence to Jefferson Lab's own Design Standard in addition to model codes.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Multi-building coordination.** The project involved approximately 20-plus locations across a variety of building types, construction vintages, and occupancy categories — from large experimental accelerator halls to smaller support and administrative buildings. Each location required independent assessment of existing domestic water, sanitary, and electrical infrastructure conditions to determine the feasibility and configuration of the proposed installations. The Basis of Design document served as the vehicle for consolidating the site-wide analysis into a single design basis.

**Tepid-water compliance.** ANSI Z358.1-2014 imposes requirements on the temperature of water delivered at emergency fixtures. Meeting that standard in a multi-building campus setting — where building temperatures, supply-water temperatures, and available mechanical infrastructure vary — required mechanical engineering engagement beyond standard plumbing fixture design.

**Federal facility standards.** The Jefferson Lab campus is a DOE national research facility operating under federal safety, sustainability, and design-standard requirements. The design had to satisfy both model codes (IPC, NFPA 70) and Jefferson Lab's own Design Standard, as well as DOE energy orders and the 10 CFR regulatory framework. Personnel accessing the site were subject to Jefferson Lab's General Employee Radiation Training (GERT) requirements.

**Design schedule.** The executed PO carried a desired delivery date of September 15, 2023. The Basis of Design was issued as a Final Submittal on March 25, 2024. Source documents do not include a change order or schedule extension record; the actual design completion date is the March 2024 BOD Final Submittal date.
