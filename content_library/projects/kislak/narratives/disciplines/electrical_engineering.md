The electrical engineering scope for the Voices of the Early Americas — Jay I. Kislak Collection exhibit at the Library of Congress covered power distribution and controls engineering for an all-LED exhibit lighting system across a single-floor gallery. The system comprises fourteen fixture types (F2–F14) distributed across a circular Rotunda Gallery and a linear exhibition hall, incorporating 24 VDC low-voltage track and display lighting (LUXAM and Q-TRAN systems), 120 V line-voltage adjustable monopoints (36 W), and a range of casework-integrated cove, display, and wall-mounted fixtures.

Dimming controls span phase-dimming and 0-10 V protocols, organized into lighting control zones with 80 W plug-in LED drivers (Q-Tran iQ-PH-80-120-24VDC-QD1) per fixture group. Cord-and-plug power systems provide display case power provisions integrated with the exhibit casework throughout the gallery. The electrical engineering scope extended into the construction and installation phase: the firm assembled a submittal package for the internal lighting controls within Goppion-manufactured display cases, documenting European specialty components — PowerMe PWM LED dimmers (DLV24A8/P), MeanWell constant-current drivers (XLG-150, HDR-60), Vimar NEMA 2P+T outlets, Lovato TM-series time relays, and ABB M2SS1-10B selector switches — and confirming their suitability for the installation. The project demonstrates electrical engineering capability for high-specification exhibit environments at a federal cultural institution, including construction-phase service integration with a specialist European display case manufacturer.
