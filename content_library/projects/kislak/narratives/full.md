## Project Overview

The Voices of the Early Americas — The Jay I. Kislak Collection is a permanent museum gallery installation at the Library of Congress, 101 Independence Avenue SE, Washington, DC. The exhibit presents the Jay I. Kislak Collection of materials documenting early American history within a gallery occupying a single floor of the Library of Congress building. Two interconnected spaces define the visitor experience: a circular Rotunda Gallery with concentric exhibit cases and freestanding display totems, and a linear exhibition hall accommodating multiple casework zones and display walls. The project was procured under federal solicitation 030ADV23Q0282.

Design documents were produced by Ralph Appelbaum Associates (RAA, Project No. 2212) as the prime exhibit designer contracted by the Library of Congress. The Lighting Workshop (Brooklyn, NY) served as the lighting design subconsultant, producing the lighting floor plan and fixture schedule. Goppion, an Italian specialist exhibit case manufacturer, supplied the custom display cases that anchor the gallery's artifact presentation. The firm served as the electrical engineering subconsultant within the RAA-led design team.

## Scope

The exhibit fit-out scope spans custom casework design, artifact display mounting, visitor-experience elements, and the full electrical and lighting engineering package for the gallery. The electrical and lighting engineering scope included:

- An all-LED exhibit lighting system comprising fourteen fixture types (F2–F14) distributed across both the Rotunda Gallery and the linear exhibition hall, addressing ambient, directional, casework-mounted, and graphic display illumination.
- Low-voltage 24 VDC track and display lighting (LUXAM and Q-TRAN systems) for casework-integrated and display fixtures throughout the exhibition hall.
- 120 V line-voltage adjustable monopoint fixtures (36 W, BK Lighting) for the Rotunda Gallery ambient and directional lighting.
- Phase-dimming and 0-10 V dimming controls organized into lighting control zones per the lighting plan, with 80 W plug-in LED drivers (Q-Tran iQ-PH-80-120-24VDC-QD1) per fixture group.
- Cord-and-plug display case power systems integrated with exhibit casework throughout the gallery.
- Construction-phase submittal documentation for internal lighting controls within Goppion display cases, covering PWM LED dimmers (PowerMe DLV24A8/P), constant-current LED drivers (MeanWell XLG-150, HDR-60), NEMA 2P+T outlets (Vimar), time relays (Lovato TM-series), and selector switches (ABB M2SS1-10B).

A structural engineering scope — addressed in the Final Design Revision 1 Structural Report (RAA, July 2023) — covered structural engineering for exhibit casework, display structures, and mount details. This scope applies to exhibit-specific structures within the gallery, not to the host building's structural system.

## Role

The firm served as the electrical engineering subconsultant to RAA on this project. The primary design responsibilities were the electrical power and controls engineering layer for the exhibit lighting system: power feed design, driver selection and specification, dimming controls coordination, and display case power provisions. Lighting aesthetic design — fixture type selection, luminance levels, and the spatial lighting layout — was the scope of the Lighting Workshop; the firm's role was to engineer the power infrastructure to support that design intent.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**All-LED system with mixed control protocols.** The lighting system incorporates fourteen distinct fixture types across two spatial typologies — the open rotunda and the contained exhibition hall — using a mix of 24 VDC low-voltage track systems, 120 V monopoints, and integrated casework fixtures. Coordinating dimming compatibility across phase-dimming, 0-10 V analog, and PWM protocols for different fixture groups required detailed driver selection and zone mapping. The fixture schedule specifies individual driver types per fixture group and flags power provisions in the lighting plan by zone.

**European component integration for Goppion display cases.** The Goppion display cases incorporate specialty European low-voltage components — PowerMe PWM LED dimmers, Lovato time relays, ABB selector switches, MeanWell power supplies — that required technical review and submittal documentation for conformance with North American installation standards. The firm assembled the submittal package coordinating manufacturer data sheets and confirming each component's suitability for the installation.

**Color temperature coordination for a collection environment.** The lighting system is predominantly 3000 K for artifact and display viewing, with specific fixture types at 6000 K (F10, window graphic lightboxes) and 6500 K (F14, edge-lit reader panels) to support graphic reproduction accuracy. Managing color temperature zoning within a single connected gallery required coordination between the fixture schedule and the lighting zone plan.
