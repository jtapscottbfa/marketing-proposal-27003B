# Building 8221 Addition — NAWCAD Webster Field

## Project Overview

Building 8221 is a pre-engineered metal building addition to an existing industrial laboratory at Naval Air Warfare Center Aircraft Division (NAWCAD) Webster Field, located at 17617 Nesea Way, St. Inigoes, Maryland (St. Mary's County). The addition measures approximately 2,400 SF (40 feet by 60 feet) and attaches to the east endwall of the existing 4,800 SF facility, effectively expanding the laboratory footprint by half. The single-story structure was designed and constructed to Open Storage Secret classification standards, satisfying ICD-705 technical specifications and CNSSAM TEMPEST/1-13 requirements for an accredited secured facility — constraints that governed the engineering approach across all disciplines.

The project was procured as an 8(a) direct award under contract N0042123C0029, solicitation N0042123R0059, awarded June 12, 2023, with a contractual performance period of 476 calendar days and a required delivery date of September 30, 2024. The general contractor of record is The Sanchez Group of Companies, LLC (Callaway, Maryland). The engineering design team served as design subconsultant to the general contractor.

## Scope

The project scope encompassed design and construction of the new pre-engineered metal building addition plus modifications to existing building systems required to serve the combined facility. Key scope elements by discipline:

**Structural / Building Envelope:** A single-story, 40'×60' pre-engineered metal building addition attached to the existing structure's east endwall. Scope included the PEMB superstructure and envelope, roofing, and shallow foundation system. Concrete cast-in-place work supported the foundation. Design criteria referenced UFC 1-200-01, IBC 2021, IEBC 2021, ASCE 7-16, and ACI 318-19.

**Mechanical (HVAC):** New packaged outdoor air conditioning unit serving the addition, supplemented by a packaged outside air handling unit with hot gas bypass. The systems were sized and configured to condition the new addition volume while meeting NAVFAC and UFC 3-410-02N mechanical requirements. The existing building's HVAC systems were not redesigned; the scope is limited to new equipment for the addition.

**Electrical:** The electrical scope was the most extensive system-wide intervention. The existing service — a 208Y/120V, 3-phase, 4-wire system fed from a 150 KVA transformer, with a 600A service fused at 500A — was reconfigured to serve the expanded facility. New work included an 800A main circuit breaker panel (208/120V), an 800A 3R CT cabinet, and relocation of the electrical service entrance to the north wall of the new addition. Wiring infrastructure comprised EMT and rigid metallic conduit with THHN/THWN conductors; an existing cable tray was extended from the original building into the addition. Interior lighting used 1×4 LED fixtures with occupancy sensors; four exterior lighting fixtures were provided. Fourteen tele/data outlets were installed with 1-inch empty conduit for future cable pulls. All electrical design met ICD-705 and CNSSAM TEMPEST/1-13 requirements supporting the facility's accredited secured status.

**Plumbing (Specialty):** Plumbing scope consisted of extending the compressed air distribution system into the addition, with drops and quick disconnects at workstation locations and hose reels to support industrial laboratory operations. Domestic plumbing and sanitary work were not identified in available source documents as part of the addition scope.

**Fire Protection:** The fire protection design extended whole-building wet-pipe sprinkler coverage from the existing building into the addition. A new 6-inch wet sprinkler riser with backflow preventer, flow switch, and tamper switches was provided for the combined system. New fire alarm appliances were integrated into the existing Notifier NFS2-640 addressable fire alarm control unit, with a new notification appliance circuit panel added if required by load calculations. The design incorporated mass notification system integration consistent with UFC 3-600-01, UFC 4-021-01, NFPA 13, NFPA 72, and NFPA 101 requirements.

## Role on Project

The engineering design team served as subconsultant to The Sanchez Group of Companies, LLC, the 8(a) prime contractor. In this capacity, the team authored the full Basis of Design document covering all engineering disciplines (mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, and structural), prepared design documents in support of the construction contract, and provided discipline-specific scope development coordinated with the general contractor and the electrical subcontractor (Brown Electric, Inc.). The delivery structure — design subconsultant working under a GC-prime contract with NAWCAD — reflects the 8(a) direct award vehicle under which the project was procured.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**ICD-705 and TEMPEST compliance.** The addition's requirement to maintain Open Storage Secret classification with ICD-705 / CNSSAM TEMPEST/1-13 compliance imposed constraints on the electrical design that go beyond standard commercial or even typical federal construction practice. Service configuration, conduit types, and electromagnetic shielding considerations shaped the electrical scope materially. The design addressed these requirements by specifying rigid metallic conduit, relocating and upgrading the electrical service entrance, and coordinating with the facility accreditation requirements.

**Whole-building fire protection reconfiguration.** Adding a 2,400 SF bay to a sprinklered existing building required verifying that the existing wet-pipe system could accommodate the expansion. The design provided a dedicated new 6-inch riser for the addition rather than tapping the existing riser, ensuring hydraulic adequacy for the combined coverage area. Integration of new alarm devices into the existing Notifier NFS2-640 FACU required careful device addressing and circuit loading analysis.

**Electrical service adequacy.** The existing 600A service was assessed as insufficient for the combined facility load once the addition was occupied. The design replaced the service arrangement with an 800A main breaker panel and a new CT cabinet, resizing the service entrance for the expanded load while maintaining the existing 150 KVA transformer supply.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a fully commissioned 2,400 SF industrial laboratory addition at NAWCAD Webster Field conforming to Open Storage Secret classification and ICD-705/TEMPEST accreditation requirements, completed within a 476-calendar-day performance period for delivery by September 30, 2024. The addition doubled the usable bay area of the existing facility and upgraded the electrical service and fire protection systems to serve the combined footprint. All engineering disciplines — mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, and structural — were coordinated under a single design subconsulting engagement supporting the 8(a) prime contractor.
