## Project Overview

Bowman, Foster & Associates, Inc. (BFA) conducted a power systems study for the CHEMRES Chemical Plant located in Chesapeake, Virginia. The study—designated BFA Project No. 22-005—was completed on August 3, 2022, and delivered as a final report with an accompanying 12-sheet one-line diagram drawing set. The engagement was a study-only commission: BFA served as the engineer of record for the analysis, with no construction phase. The client, CHEMRES, operates a large-scale chemical manufacturing facility supplied by Dominion Power at 34.5 kV. The plant's electrical distribution system is extensive and complex, supplying production machinery, motor control centers, process equipment, lighting, and support systems across multiple areas of the facility.

## Scope

The scope of the power systems study comprised four interconnected analyses: (1) short circuit analysis to determine available fault current at each bus and equipment location throughout the plant; (2) protective device coordination study to evaluate the selectivity and sequencing of the overcurrent protection hierarchy from the 34.5 kV utility service through the 480V distribution system; (3) equipment interrupting-capacity evaluation to verify that installed equipment—breakers, switches, bus ducts, and motor control centers—was rated for the fault currents present at each location; and (4) arc flash hazard analysis to calculate incident energy levels and arc flash boundaries at each significant equipment location in compliance with applicable industry standards.

The electrical distribution system analyzed includes a Westinghouse Type 345GS1500 power circuit breaker rated 1,200 amperes continuous and 22,000 amperes short circuit at 34.5 kV serving as the main substation switch. Five step-down transformers (four rated 1,151 kVA and one rated 1,500 kVA, all 34.5 kV–480V) with Cutler-Hammer WLI gas switches (600 A, 25 kA interrupting) on each transformer primary distribute power to the 480V system. The 480V distribution includes a 3,200-ampere bus duct (E/W Sub A), a 1,600-ampere bus duct (E/W Sub B), and Bus Duct C with an 800-ampere main circuit breaker feeding MCC #13—along with more than thirteen additional motor control center sections and numerous distribution panels serving production areas, lighting, emergency power, and support systems. Total plant load at the time of study was 2,556.2 kW / 2,091.4 kVAR.

## Role on Project

BFA served as the engineer of record for the power systems study. The firm was tasked directly by CHEMRES with performing all four components of the analysis. BFA's engineers collected equipment data, developed a complete computer model of the electrical distribution system using SKM Power Tools, ran short circuit and load flow calculations, and performed the arc flash evaluation per IEEE Std 1584-2018. BFA produced the final study report, the correlated one-line diagram drawing set, and the arc flash label package for 35 equipment locations throughout the facility.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Complex multi-source, multi-bus industrial distribution system.** The CHEMRES plant electrical system is not a simple radial distribution; it operates through multiple 34.5 kV–480V transformation points, parallel bus systems, and dozens of downstream MCCs and panels serving process equipment ranging from 500-horsepower DC motors to small support panels. Accurately modeling the impedance network and calculating fault currents at each of the 35 labeled locations required comprehensive input data collection across the entire facility. BFA addressed this through systematic data collection embedded in the one-line diagram drawing set and verified through the SKM model.

**High incident energy conditions at maximum trip time buses.** Several buses in the plant—most notably E/W Sub A, Bus Duct C, and E/W Sub B—exhibit extremely high incident energies (64.8, 61.9, and 61.2 cal/cm², respectively) under maximum trip time (2.0-second) conditions. These levels, which require full arc flash suit PPE, arise from the combination of high available fault current and protection settings calibrated for process continuity rather than arc flash minimization. BFA documented these conditions accurately in the arc flash labels and IEEE 1584 Bus Report, providing the plant with the hazard-level data necessary to implement appropriate work practices and PPE requirements.

**Corroboration of transformer ratings.** The study report narrative described all five step-down transformers as 1,151 kVA; however, the study's own input data tables and the one-line diagram drawings showed that Sub E1 is rated 1,500 kVA. BFA's systematic data documentation captured both the narrative claim and the tabular/drawing data, and the study correctly applied the 1,500 kVA rating for Sub E1 in the model. The final one-line diagram drawings serve as the authoritative record of transformer ratings.

## Outcomes

All installed equipment evaluated in the study met applicable interrupting-capacity requirements; no equipment was found to have failed the equipment evaluation. The study produced arc flash labels for 35 equipment locations throughout the plant, each documenting incident energy, arc flash boundary, working distance, and required PPE category in accordance with NFPA 70E-2021. The completed power systems study provided CHEMRES with the quantitative hazard data, labeled equipment documentation, and coordination analysis needed to support a compliant electrical safety program and to make informed decisions about protective device settings and work practices going forward.
