## Project Overview

The Repair Soil Erosion at Reservoir Berms project was a NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic design-build contract (Work Order No. 1618343) located at Norfolk Naval Shipyard in Portsmouth, Virginia. The site occupies the southern area of the installation, north of Elm Avenue and east of Burton's Point Road, directly behind Buildings 385 and 1554. Three 120-foot-diameter above-ground concrete fire protection water storage tanks — Tanks 553, 554, and 555, each holding approximately 2 million gallons — are situated there, constructed in the 1940s on timber pile deep foundations and completely enclosed within a system of earthen berms that both protect and structurally support them.

Record rainfall events in 2016 overtaxed the existing berm slopes, triggering progressive surface erosion in the form of rills, gullies, and localized slope failures along the berms, particularly those surrounding Tanks 553 and 555. The deteriorating berm condition posed a risk to the reliability of the shipyard's fire protection water supply and required engineered remediation to restore structural integrity and long-term performance of the system.

## Scope of Work

The project was procured via best-value design-build under a NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic RFP issued April 28, 2021, with a 365-calendar-day performance period. The scope addressed the berm system across four primary work areas:

**Mechanically stabilized slope repair.** Design and construction of the failed berm areas using a specialty geotechnical slope stabilization approach. Performance requirements were stringent: a minimum global factor of safety of 1.5 against slope failure, a maximum finished slope geometry of 1.5H:1V, a 20-year minimum design life, and zero post-construction global settlement of the existing tanks. The timber pile foundation system beneath the 1940s-era tanks made settlement control a governing design constraint; the RFP required innovative mechanically stabilized methodologies specifically capable of meeting the zero-settlement threshold.

**Slope surface treatment and erosion mitigation.** Engineered surface protection to arrest ongoing erosion and prevent recurrence across the repaired berm faces, integrated with the grading and drainage design.

**Security fencing replacement.** Replacement of existing perimeter security fencing disturbed or affected by the berm repair construction.

**Electrical conversion (optional work items).** Conversion of the existing overhead exterior lighting circuit on poles #280 through #284 to a new direct-buried underground distribution system. The base electrical scope disconnected and removed the overhead conductors while retaining the existing poles and luminaires, then installed a new underground circuit in a minimum 3/4-inch galvanized rigid steel conduit from pole #284 to poles P280, P281, P282, and P283. At pole #284, a new 20A circuit breaker and 30A non-fused disconnect switch in NEMA 4X stainless steel enclosures replaced the existing devices. Option 3 extended the underground circuit further to perimeter poles PPA and LPB. The existing overhead telephone line shared pole #284 and required temporary disconnect and protection in place during underground circuit installation.

Existing site infrastructure — including 8-inch water fill/drain pipes feeding the tanks from Building 385 and 8-inch and 12-inch storm drain pipes to catch basins on top of the berm — was protected in place throughout construction.

## Role on Project

The project was delivered as a design-build, with NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic acting as the government client and design criteria authority. The design team provided engineer-of-record services in support of the design-build prime contractor across the technical disciplines required by the scope. The electrical subconsultant role is documented through an authored electrical design narrative (August 2021) and electrical basis of design (January 2022). The firm's role on the civil and geotechnical scope relative to the design-build contractor is not explicitly documented in the available source records; source documents name the electrical contributions only.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Zero-settlement requirement on 1940s timber pile foundations.** The existing tanks were founded on timber piles characteristic of 1940s construction — a foundation system with limited tolerance for differential loading or disturbance. The NAVFAC design criteria mandated zero post-construction global settlement, effectively ruling out any slope repair approach that would impose surcharge loads or dynamic compaction near the tank perimeter. The mechanically stabilized slope methodology was selected specifically to meet this constraint, controlling load distribution and avoiding settlement-generating techniques.

**Steep berm geometry with high design factor of safety.** The existing berm slopes were steep enough that the 2016 rainfall events triggered failures. The repair had to restore stability at the maximum permitted finished slope of 1.5H:1V while achieving a minimum factor of safety of 1.5 — a combination that left limited margin and required precision in the stabilization system design and execution.

**Overhead electrical infrastructure on failing berm.** The existing overhead lighting circuit traversing poles #280 through #284 ran through the active berm repair area. Retaining the overhead circuit during and after berm reconstruction would have left aerial conductors exposed to disruption from construction activity and from any future slope movement. Converting the distribution to a direct-buried underground system eliminated this vulnerability while preserving the existing pole and luminaire assets.

**Telephony coordination at shared pole.** The overhead telephone line running from pole #284 to pole PPA shared infrastructure with the lighting circuit. The underground circuit installation sequence required temporary disconnect and protection of the telephone line, adding a telecommunications coordination task to the electrical scope.

## Outcomes

The project restored structural integrity and long-term erosion resistance to the earthen berm system protecting the three fire protection water storage tanks, securing the shipyard's fire suppression water supply. The mechanically stabilized repair was designed to satisfy NAVFAC's governing performance criteria: minimum 1.5 factor of safety against slope failure, maximum 1.5H:1V finished slope, 20-year design life, and zero post-construction global settlement of the existing 1940s tank structures. The optional electrical conversion eliminated the overhead lighting circuit exposure on the reconstructed berm. The project was executed from an April 2021 RFP under a 365-day design-build performance period, with electrical design advancing through early 2022.
