For Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command Mid-Atlantic (NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic), the design-build project Repair Soil Erosion at Reservoir Berms addressed progressive slope failures at three 1940s-era, 2-million-gallon above-ground concrete fire protection water storage tanks (Tanks 553, 554, and 555) at Norfolk Naval Shipyard in Portsmouth, Virginia. Record rainfall events in 2016 accelerated erosion along the steep earthen berms enclosing the tanks, producing rills, gullies, and localized slope failures that threatened the structural integrity of the berm system and the fire suppression water supply serving the shipyard.

The project scope encompassed mechanically stabilized slope repair of the failed berm areas using specialty geotechnical methodology; engineered slope surface treatment and erosion mitigation; replacement of security fencing; and an option to convert existing overhead exterior lighting circuits from poles #280 through #284 to a new direct-buried underground electrical system. Design criteria required a minimum factor of safety of 1.5 against slope failure, a maximum finished slope geometry of 1.5H:1V, a 20-year minimum design life, and zero post-construction global settlement to the existing tanks — a demanding threshold driven by the 1940s timber pile foundations beneath the berm. The project was procured under Work Order No. 1618343 via a best-value design-build RFP issued April 28, 2021, with a 365-calendar-day performance period.
