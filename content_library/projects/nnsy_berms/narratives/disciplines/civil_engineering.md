The civil engineering scope on the Repair Soil Erosion at Reservoir Berms project addressed site grading, drainage, and surface erosion control associated with the berm stabilization work at Norfolk Naval Shipyard in Portsmouth, Virginia. The project site is located in the southern area of the installation, behind Buildings 385 and 1554, where three 1940s-era, 2-million-gallon fire protection water storage tanks are enclosed within steep earthen berms. Record rainfall events in 2016 accelerated surface erosion — producing rills, gullies, and localized slope failures — requiring an engineered repair that restored berm geometry within the prescribed maximum 1.5H:1V finished slope while directing stormwater away from the stabilized surfaces.

Civil engineering responsibilities encompassed the engineered slope surface treatment and erosion mitigation program, grading and drainage design consistent with the project's land disturbance of less than one acre, and protection of existing site infrastructure throughout construction. Existing infrastructure requiring protection in place included 8-inch water fill/drain pipes serving the tanks from Building 385 and 8-inch and 12-inch storm drain pipes routed to catch basins on top of the berm. The civil scope was integrated directly with the geotechnical stabilization work: finished grading had to satisfy both the NAVFAC maximum 1.5H:1V slope geometry and the drainage objectives needed to prevent recurrence of the erosion conditions that triggered the 2016 slope failures.
