Geotechnical engineering was the primary technical discipline on the Repair Soil Erosion at Reservoir Berms project at Norfolk Naval Shipyard, Portsmouth, Virginia. The project was triggered by record rainfall in 2016 that caused progressive slope failures — rills, gullies, and localized failures — along the earthen berms surrounding three 1940s-era, 120-foot-diameter, 2-million-gallon concrete fire protection water storage tanks (Tanks 553, 554, and 555). The tanks, founded on timber piles, are completely enclosed within the berm system and their structural condition is directly dependent on berm stability and the absence of post-construction settlement.

The geotechnical challenge was to design and construct a mechanically stabilized slope repair system meeting stringent NAVFAC performance criteria: a minimum global factor of safety of 1.5 against slope failure, a maximum finished slope of 1.5H:1V, a 20-year minimum design life, and zero post-construction global settlement of the existing tank structures. The zero-settlement requirement was the governing constraint; the timber pile foundations of the 1940s tanks imposed strict limits on any load-imposing or dynamic construction technique near the tank perimeter. Mechanically stabilized slope methodologies were specifically selected to satisfy this constraint. The design-build RFP required a specialty geotechnical subcontractor to execute the stabilization construction, reflecting the specialized nature of the repair approach. The design criteria were authored by NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic and carried through the design-build delivery structure.
