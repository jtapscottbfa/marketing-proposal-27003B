# Building 1470 2nd Floor Vault Expansion — Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The Building 1470 2nd Floor Vault Expansion is a new second-floor addition to an existing occupied facility at Naval Support Facility (NSF) Dahlgren, Virginia. The project was commissioned by the Department of the Navy under NAVFAC Washington, South Potomac, and was designed to expand secured laboratory and administrative capacity for Naval Support Facility Dahlgren (NSF Dahlgren), home to the Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren Division (NSWCDD). The addition delivers approximately 4,200 square feet of new Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) space classified under ICD 705 and ICS 705-1 standards, rising above the existing first-floor High Performance Laboratory (HPL) / Special Access Program Facility (SAPF) footprint of Building 1470.

The facility is designed to house a large 2,005-SF HPL space, four dedicated classified workrooms, a corridor, a server room, an electrical room, and an entry vestibule. A new stair connection to the existing first floor was created through selective demolition of a janitor and kitchenette area. The 100% Final Design Submittal was completed April 23, 2021 (BFA Project No. 20-015; NAVFAC Document No. N0017819PX00007; Construction Contract No. N40080-12-C-2542).

## Scope of Work

The project scope encompassed design of a new approximately 76-foot by 55-foot second-floor structural steel and masonry addition atop the existing Building 1470 roof slab. The building addition includes:

- **Architecture and building envelope:** CMU cavity wall construction with 4-inch economy brick veneer matching existing building materials; EPDM single-ply roofing with tapered insulation; a 10-foot by 24-foot canopy at the north entry; and vehicular bollard protection at the north entrance. No exterior glazing was provided anywhere in the facility per ICD/ICS 705 SCIF requirements. A 14-foot by 14-foot roll-up door serves the HPL space on the north elevation.
- **SCIF construction:** All perimeter and partition walls constructed per ICD/ICS 705 Wall A specifications — STC 50/1-hour rated assemblies with 3-inch sound attenuation batt insulation, double-layer 5/8-inch Type X gypsum board on the workroom side, and 3-5/8-inch metal studs. Walls run from slab to underside of deck above at SCIF perimeter. All penetrations (mechanical, electrical, telecommunications) were detailed with ICD/ICS 705-compliant grounding, Z-duct acoustic treatment, and fire-rated through-penetration firestop assemblies.
- **Mechanical systems:** A DX VAV rooftop unit serves the main second-floor addition via VAV boxes with electric reheat at each zone; two dedicated mini-split systems serve the server room for independent cooling; and existing heat pump units were relocated from the lower roof to the new high roof. A new BACnet MSTP building automation system integrates with the existing facility JACE panel. Design conditions: 90°F DB/81°F WB cooling; 12°F DB heating.
- **Electrical systems:** New 500 kVA 480-208/120V dry-type transformer fed from a new 600A circuit breaker in the existing 3,000A MTM switchgear; a new 800A main panelboard distributes power to twelve new panelboards, including a 400A server room panel with emergency power-off (EPO) capability and ten 125A workroom/lab sub-panels. LED lighting at 4,000K/80 CRI is provided throughout, with dual-technology occupancy sensors at all spaces. A new lightning protection system covers the second-floor addition, coordinated with the existing building system.
- **Fire protection and life safety:** The new wet-pipe sprinkler system extends from the existing 4-inch main serving the existing second floor. Light-hazard design applies in offices and corridors; ordinary-hazard design applies in the server room and electrical room. The existing Gamewell FCI E3 addressable fire alarm system is extended with new devices and an existing NAC booster panel circuit. IBC Group B (Business) occupancy classification; Type IIB construction; unlimited area building per sprinklered, two-story configuration.
- **Telecommunications:** Five physically separate network infrastructures — cNMCI, uNMCI, cRDT&E, uRDT&E, CNET, and JWICS — are installed using color-coded CAT6A F/UTP cabling with Panduit Mini-Com faceplates. A new server room (Room 206) is designed per EIA/TIA 569-B and connects to the existing server room (1302O), communications room (1705), and patch panel room (2276) via a new 8-inch EMT conduit riser. RCDD-approved design was required.
- **Electronic security:** A Lenel access control system with 13 HID pivCLASS card readers and keypads, electric strikes, Magnasphere balanced magnetic switches at emergency and primary doors, and Bosch PIR motion detectors provides multi-layer access control at all workroom entries. Blue strobe lights at each workroom door signal controlled access status. The system connects to the existing facility controller in Room 1203 via the NDW 3SP network.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant on the design-build project, with full design responsibility across all disciplines. Joel Andre served as Architect of Record. The team held the designer-of-record role under the NAVFAC design-build procurement vehicle, coordinating design across architecture, structural engineering, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, fire protection engineering, telecommunications engineering, and security engineering disciplines.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**SCIF construction coordination across all disciplines.** Designing a new SCIF addition to ICD/ICS 705 standards requires strict discipline coordination for every penetration through the secure perimeter. Mechanical ducts, electrical conduits, and telecommunications cables all required ICD/ICS 705-compliant grounding, acoustic treatment (Z-ducts for HVAC), and firestop detailing. The design team developed standardized penetration details across disciplines — reflected in dedicated detail sheets for each system — to ensure consistent treatment and constructability for cleared workers.

**Multi-network physical separation.** Supporting five physically separate classified and unclassified networks (NMCI, RDT&E, CNET, JWICS) within the same facility required a color-coded, individually routed cabling infrastructure. Each network type uses a distinct cable jacket color, connector type, and cabinet, with basket-type cable tray in the corridor providing organized routing while maintaining clear physical separation between networks of different classification levels.

**Power distribution upgrade for new load.** The addition of approximately 4,200 SF of laboratory and server space with significant connected electrical load — including a 400A server room panel, ten 125A workroom sub-panels, and HVAC equipment — required a new 500 kVA transformer and 800A main distribution panel served from the existing 3,000A MTM switchgear. The design routed the new 600A feeder from the existing switchgear to maintain service continuity.

**Square footage coordination across documents.** Minor discrepancies appeared in the stated building area across source documents (4,180 SF from dimensional calculations, 4,200 SF in the fire protection section of the BOD, and a 4,720 SF figure appearing in the SOW mission statement). The BOD fire protection section's explicit statement of "approximately 4,200 sq ft" was adopted as the design basis, consistent with the detailed architectural drawings.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a fully equipped and ICD/ICS 705-compliant SCIF addition to Building 1470 at NSF Dahlgren, expanding the facility's classified workspace capacity with a High Performance Laboratory, classified workrooms, a dedicated server room with multi-network infrastructure, and robust electronic security and access control. The 100% Final Design Submittal was completed April 23, 2021.
