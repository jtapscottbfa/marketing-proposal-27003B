Building 23H at the Perry Point VA Medical Center is a 59,054-square-foot, four-story facility built in 1942 that houses outpatient clinics, the Community Living Center, and administrative space. The domestic hot water recirculation system, original to the building's construction and modified over decades of in-house maintenance, had accumulated deficiencies resulting in prolonged hot water wait times at fixtures throughout the building.

The design team served as prime A/E consultant under a qualifications-based Veterans Affairs IDIQ selection to investigate the system and prepare construction documents for corrective action. A multi-day field investigation traced the system from the crawl space through building risers to outlet fixtures and identified the root causes of poor circulation: fixtures located more than 50 feet from their respective hot water recirculation mains, violating Section 607.2 of the 2018 International Plumbing Code, and a suspected cross-connection between cold and hot water lines in the crawl space. The team recommended installation of additional recirculation pumps and balancing devices, piping modifications to bring riser connections within code-compliant distances, and evaluation of residual system pressure. The project scope extended from initial investigation through 30%, 60%, and 100% bid documents and included 3D scan-to-BIM documentation of existing conditions, asbestos survey and abatement design, and design compliance with VHA Directive 1061 for Legionella prevention and scald protection. The estimated construction cost for corrective work fell within the $25,000–$100,000 design magnitude range.
