## Project Overview

Building 23H at the Perry Point VA Medical Center (VAMC Perry Point) is a 59,054-square-foot, four-story building with basement and crawlspace, located on Boiler House Road in Perry Point, Maryland. Originally constructed in 1942 as the campus's main building and surgery/medicine facility, and renovated once in 1999, the building houses outpatient clinics, the Community Living Center, and administrative space. The building's domestic hot water recirculation system dates to the original construction and was modified through subsequent renovations and in-house repairs over the building's lifespan.

By the time of this engagement, the system had developed deficiencies that produced unacceptably long wait times for hot water at fixtures throughout the building, generating complaints from patients and staff. In-house efforts to resolve the issue — including installation of a recirculation pump in 2020 — had reduced complaints but had not corrected the underlying code-compliance and system balance deficiencies.

## Scope

The project was a Department of Veterans Affairs Non-Recurring Maintenance (NRM) A/E study and design engagement (Project No. NRM 512A5-22-002, Solicitation 36C24522R0026). The scope comprised an investigation phase followed by a design phase.

The investigation required a detailed multi-day field survey of the domestic hot water system (DHWS), including tracing all system risers from the crawl space to outlet fixtures, reviewing government-furnished drawings, conversing with VA maintenance staff, and identifying all deficiencies including previously unreported conditions. The A/E was required to determine approximate locations, pipe sizes, invert elevations, and flow directions of all DHWS components.

Following VA review of the investigation findings, the design phase produced 30%, 60%, and 100% bid documents to bring the system into compliance with current codes and standards. Site investigation utilized 3D scanning to BIM as the preferred method for documenting existing conditions. The scope additionally included asbestos identification, abatement design, and industrial hygiene monitoring services for construction, given the building's age and the confined crawl space environment. Design was required to comply with VHA Directive 1061, which governs prevention of healthcare-associated Legionella disease and scald injury from potable water distribution systems.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime A/E consultant, selected through a qualifications-based process from the VA's A/E IDIQ contract holder pool. As prime consultant, the team held direct contractual responsibility to the VA for all investigation, reporting, and design deliverables through final bid documents. The engagement was a Veteran-Owned Small Business (VOSB) set-aside, requiring CVE verification and VetBiz visibility prior to contract award. The study report and bid documents were sealed by a Professional Engineer.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**IPC 607.2 code violation — branch run lengths.** The field investigation confirmed that fixtures throughout the building were located more than 50 feet from their respective hot water recirculation mains, directly violating Section 607.2 of the 2018 International Plumbing Code governing hot or tempered water supply to fixtures. This excessive distance, combined with recirculation deficiencies, caused prolonged hot water wait times for building occupants. The recommended resolution was installation of additional recirculation pumps and balancing devices at the affected risers to restore active circulation through the full branch lengths, with piping modifications to bring connections into compliance.

**Suspected cross-connection in crawl space.** VA maintenance staff and the field investigation identified a suspected cross-connection between cold and hot water lines within the building's crawl space as a contributing factor in circulation inefficiency. This condition required investigation in a confined space environment to document the cross-connection and develop a corrective design.

**Confined crawl space access.** The crawl space presented significant access constraints: confined space designation requiring permits per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146 and the VA Confined Space Program, heat and cold stress conditions, and the possible presence of animals and insects. These constraints shaped investigation methodology and required specific safety planning prior to and during entry.

**System vintage and incremental modifications.** The domestic hot water system was original to the 1942 construction, with modifications accumulated over eight decades of in-house maintenance. The as-installed system conditions diverged significantly from available record drawings, requiring comprehensive 3D scan-to-BIM documentation to establish a reliable existing-conditions basis for design. Cost estimates were required to account for asbestos removal, required phasing, and special field conditions in the confined crawl space.

## Outcomes

The A/E investigation documented system deficiencies and confirmed the IPC 607.2 violation as the primary cause of hot water wait time complaints throughout the building. Based on investigation findings, the team developed recommendations for additional recirculation pumps and balancing devices, riser connection relocations to meet code-required distances, comprehensive piping modifications, and a full assessment of return piping adequacy and residual system pressure. Design documents were produced through the 100% bid document phase, incorporating 3D scan-to-BIM existing conditions, asbestos abatement design, and VHA Directive 1061 water safety compliance. The project design was completed within the VA's $25,000–$100,000 estimated construction cost magnitude for the corrective work.
