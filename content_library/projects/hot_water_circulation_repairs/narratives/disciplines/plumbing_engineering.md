The plumbing engineering scope on this project centered on investigation and design for the domestic hot water recirculation system in a 59,054-square-foot, four-story federal healthcare building originally constructed in 1942. The system, original to the building's construction and modified through decades of in-house maintenance, had accumulated deficiencies causing unacceptably long hot water wait times at fixtures throughout the building.

Field investigation traced the system from water heaters in the basement through two main piping runs in the crawl space to risers serving each floor. The investigation confirmed that fixtures were located more than 50 feet from their respective hot water recirculation mains, violating Section 607.2 of the 2018 International Plumbing Code. A recirculation pump installed by VA maintenance in 2020 had reduced complaints but did not resolve the underlying code-compliance and system balance deficiencies. A suspected cross-connection between cold and hot water lines in the crawl space was identified as a contributing factor.

The plumbing engineer developed recommendations for additional recirculation pumps and balancing devices at affected risers, piping modifications to bring branch connections within IPC code-required distances, and evaluation of existing return piping adequacy and residual system pressure. Design deliverables progressed through 30%, 60%, and 100% bid document phases, with existing conditions documented via 3D scan to BIM. All design incorporated VHA Directive 1061 requirements for Legionella prevention and scald protection, including mixing valve provisions and elimination of dead legs. Asbestos survey and abatement design were included within scope given the building's age and the confined crawl space work environment.
