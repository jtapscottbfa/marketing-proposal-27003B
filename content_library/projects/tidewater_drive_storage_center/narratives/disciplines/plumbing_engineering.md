The plumbing engineering scope for Tidewater Drive Storage Center Phase II encompassed new below-grade drainage infrastructure for a multi-level commercial self-storage facility in Norfolk, Virginia, circa 2020. The engineer of record served as the plumbing engineer of record for Phase II, designing and documenting all new plumbing work on permit-set drawings (sheets P-001 and P-101).

The Phase II plumbing work centered on elevator pit drainage: two submersible sump pumps — SP-1 (Elevator 1) and SP-2 (Elevator 2) — each rated at 55 GPM, 16 feet TDH, 5 HP, 208V/3-phase/60 Hz, discharging via new sanitary waste piping routed through the building interior. An oil/water separator (OWS-1) was incorporated upstream of the sanitary connection to intercept potential hydraulic fluid contamination from the elevator pits, consistent with plumbing code requirements for this application type.

Underground sanitary piping was designed to connect from the oil/water separator to the existing main waste piping established during Phase I, completing the drainage path for both elevator pit systems. The plumbing drawing set includes a pump schedule, sump pit detail, pipe hanger detail, and drainage piping slope schedule governing the new installation. Restroom fixtures visible in the service area were Phase I installations and were explicitly outside the Phase II plumbing scope.

Sub-disciplines engaged include sanitary/DWV (sump pump drainage, waste routing, oil/water separator tie-in) and coordination of sump pump power requirements (208V, 3-phase) as a mechanical-electrical interface item.
