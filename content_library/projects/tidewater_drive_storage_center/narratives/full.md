# Tidewater Drive Storage Center Phase II — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

Tidewater Drive Storage Center Phase II is a commercial self-storage facility located on Tidewater Drive in Norfolk, Virginia (BFA project number 20-009). The project represents the second phase of development at this site, with Phase I having preceded it. The facility is a large-scale, multi-level self-storage building organized into two principal wings or zones, each containing long rows of individual lockable storage bays served by central corridors. Two elevators — one in each major wing — provide vertical access for tenants and goods movement across the building's floors. The facility is a private commercial development consistent with the commercial self-storage building type. Drawings were plotted April 6, 2020.

The available project documentation for Phase II consists of two plumbing discipline drawing sheets: P-001 (Legend, Abbreviations, Schedules, and General Notes) and P-101 (Plumbing Floor Plan — New Work, at 1/8" = 1'-0" scale). No basis-of-design narrative, statement of work, contract documents, or architectural/structural/mechanical/electrical drawing sheets were available in the project record; accordingly, financial details, square footage, floor count, and client identity are not documented here.

## Scope

The engineer of record's scope for Phase II was limited to plumbing engineering. The plumbing work covered new below-grade drainage infrastructure associated with two elevator pit installations:

- **Sump pump systems:** Two submersible sump pumps — SP-1 (serving Elevator 1) and SP-2 (serving Elevator 2) — each rated at 55 gallons per minute (GPM), 16 feet total dynamic head (TDH), 5 horsepower, 1,750 RPM, 208-volt, three-phase, 60 Hz. Both pumps are specified to match the pump schedule shown on sheet P-001.
- **Oil/water separator:** OWS-1, an oil/water separator installed flush with grade near the elevator area in the building's lower section, to intercept and treat elevator pit drainage containing potential hydraulic fluid contamination before the effluent routes to the sanitary waste system.
- **Sanitary waste piping (new):** Underground sanitary piping installed from the elevator sump pits, routed through the building interior, connecting to the existing main waste piping outside the building footprint. Construction notes on P-101 specify that the piping is to be capped and routed underground to the existing main waste line, with a final connection made from the oil/water separator.

Restroom fixtures (water closet and lavatory) visible in the building's service area were confirmed as Phase I installations; their plumbing service connections were not part of the Phase II scope.

The plumbing construction notes on P-101 establish the following sequence of new work: (1) provide sump pump in elevator pit per the P-001 detail; (2) provide interior piping with supports per the hanger detail; (3) provide sump pump flush with grade per oil/water separator requirements; (4) provide oil/water separator flush with grade per product data; (5) route underground piping to the existing building main waste piping and complete the oil/water separator final connection.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Elevator pit drainage and oil/water separation.** Elevator pit sump pump systems in multi-story commercial buildings require careful treatment before discharge to sanitary waste, because hydraulic elevator systems can introduce hydraulic fluid to pit drainage. The design addresses this by routing the elevator pit sump pumps through an oil/water separator (OWS-1) prior to the sanitary connection. This approach conforms to plumbing code requirements for indirect waste connections in applications where contamination is possible.

**Coordination of below-grade infrastructure in Phase II context.** Phase II plumbing work required connecting new below-grade piping to the existing main waste piping established in Phase I. Construction Note 5 on P-101 specifically addresses this: the underground piping is to be routed to the existing building and connected to the existing main waste piping, with the oil/water separator final connection made as part of the same scope. The design drawings use limit-of-demolition and point-of-new-connection symbols to delineate Phase I from Phase II work.

**Restroom scope delineation.** The service area restroom fixtures are visible on the Phase II floor plan but are identified as existing Phase I installations, not new Phase II work. The plumbing drawings clearly limit the new work scope to the elevator pit drainage systems, avoiding scope overlap with the Phase I plumbing base.

## Outcomes

The Phase II plumbing engineering scope delivered a code-compliant elevator pit drainage and waste treatment system for both elevators in a large commercial self-storage facility. The pump schedule on P-001 specifies matched sump pump equipment for both elevators, ensuring consistent performance and maintenance requirements across the two systems. The oil/water separator provides a treatment layer consistent with best practice for hydraulic elevator applications. Drawing sheets were completed and plotted in April 2020. Project status is inferred as complete based on the dated, permit-ready drawing set; this has not been confirmed from contract closeout documentation.
