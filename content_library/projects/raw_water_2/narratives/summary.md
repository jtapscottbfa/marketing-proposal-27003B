The Raw Water No. 2 Treatment Plant Electrical Coordination Study is a technical engineering study completed in May 2024 for a water treatment pump station in Gloucester, Virginia. The engagement focused on the electrical distribution system serving the facility's raw-water pumping equipment — Pumps No. 2, 3, and 4 — and was performed in accordance with ANSI/NEMA MTS-2019 coordination study requirements.

The study comprised a field survey of existing electrical equipment, development of a system model using SKM Power Tools, a short-circuit analysis of the pump distribution system, and a coordination analysis of the overcurrent protective devices protecting the pumping circuits. The existing distribution system consists of three pole-mounted 50 kVA transformers feeding a 480 V low-voltage automatic transfer switch (ATS) equipped with a 200 A main circuit breaker (MCB), with downstream 50 A branch breakers serving Pumps No. 2 and 3 and a 100 A breaker serving Pump No. 4.

The study determined that selective coordination between the 200 A MCB and the pump branch circuit breakers is not achievable with the installed equipment, and that compromised coordination — as defined under ANSI/NEMA MTS-2019 — is the practical objective. No equipment was found to operate outside its rated fault-current capacity. Recommendations were developed to optimize the system under the compromised coordination framework, including repositioning the MCB instantaneous trip setting and a longer-term option for upgrading the MCB to a device with adjustable static trip characteristics. The study provides the client with a clear basis for protective device settings and a documented upgrade path toward a near-coordinated distribution system.
