The engineering team provided electrical engineering services for this engagement as a comprehensive electrical systems study for an industrial welding shop at a private shipyard facility in Norfolk, Virginia (BFA Project No. 21-010, April 2021). The study characterized the facility's 2,000-ampere, 480/277-volt, three-phase distribution system — supplied from a Dominion Energy utility service with 37,302 amperes of available symmetrical fault current — across four integrated analyses: short circuit analysis, ANSI equipment evaluation, overcurrent protective device coordination study, and arc flash hazard evaluation per IEEE 1584-2018 and NFPA 70E.

All evaluated circuit breakers and safety switches — Square D and Eaton/Cutler-Hammer equipment with interrupting ratings ranging from 14 kA to 200 kA — passed ANSI fault current criteria without exception. Fifteen time-current coordination curves confirmed proper cascading protection from the 2,000-ampere main panel through sub-panels and industrial branch circuits serving welding automation trains, motor loads, and support equipment. Arc flash incident energies were calculated at six buses using SKM Power Tools, ranging from 0.43 cal/cm² at low-exposure branch panels to 5.36 cal/cm² at the new 277-volt transformer secondary panel. Level 3 PPE was required at the two 277-volt transformer secondary locations, which reached the maximum arcing duration threshold in the IEEE 1584-2018 model. Six arc flash warning labels and a Virginia PE-stamped one-line diagram were delivered as part of the final submittal package.
