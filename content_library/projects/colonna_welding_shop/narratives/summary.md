The engineering team performed a four-part electrical systems study for an industrial welding shop at Colonna's Shipyard, a private commercial ship repair facility in Norfolk, Virginia (BFA Project No. 21-010, Final Submittal April 9, 2021). The study covered the facility's 2,000-ampere, 480/277-volt, three-phase distribution system — supplied from a Dominion Energy utility service with an available symmetrical fault current of 37,302 amperes — and comprised: a short circuit analysis, an ANSI equipment evaluation, an overcurrent protective device coordination study, and an arc flash hazard evaluation per IEEE 1584-2018 and NFPA 70E, performed using SKM Power Tools.

All circuit breakers and safety switches evaluated under ANSI fault current criteria passed without exception. Fifteen time-current coordination curves confirmed proper cascading protection throughout the distribution hierarchy, from the 2,000-ampere main panel through sub-panels and branch circuits serving welding automation trains, motor loads, hydraulic equipment, and step-down transformer secondaries. The arc flash evaluation characterized six electrical buses across the system, producing incident energy values ranging from 0.43 to 5.36 cal/cm², PPE level assignments, arc flash boundary distances, and six arc flash warning labels for field installation. The final submittal package provides the client with the technical basis for NFPA 70E-compliant electrical safety program implementation at the welding shop.
