# Colonna Welding Shop 2000A Service — Electrical Systems Study

## Project Overview

The engineering team delivered a comprehensive electrical systems study for an active industrial welding shop at Colonna's Shipyard, a private commercial ship repair facility in Norfolk, Virginia. The project (BFA No. 21-010) was issued as a final submittal on April 9, 2021. The engagement was a study-only scope — no construction documents or construction-phase services were included.

The facility's electrical distribution is a 2,000-ampere, 480/277-volt, three-phase, four-wire system fed from a Dominion Energy utility service, with an available symmetrical fault current of 37,302 amperes at the service entrance. From the 2,000-ampere main circuit breaker panel, the system branches to multiple sub-panels and branch circuits serving heavy industrial loads: three welding automation train circuits (60 amperes each), a sub-arc train, a 10-horsepower ironworker motor load, hydraulic mechanical equipment, and lighting and general power panels supplied through step-down transformers (a 50 kVA existing unit and a 75 kVA new unit, both stepping 480V to 277V).

## Scope

The study comprised four integrated analyses delivered as a single final submittal package:

**Short circuit analysis.** Calculated symmetrical fault currents at each bus in the distribution system, using the Dominion utility available fault current (37,302A symmetrical) as the upstream boundary condition.

**Equipment evaluation (ANSI).** Compared calculated fault currents at each device location against the rated interrupting capacity of each circuit breaker and safety switch in the system, evaluating adequacy per ANSI standards. The evaluation covered fourteen-plus devices, including Square D PC-series, PA-series, LA-series, ED-series, FA-series, FD-series, and KC-series breakers, as well as Eaton/Cutler-Hammer DH series heavy-duty safety switches.

**Overcurrent protective device coordination study.** Evaluated the time-current characteristics of each protective device relative to its upstream and downstream neighbors, producing 15 time-current characteristic (TCC) curves to document coordination throughout the distribution hierarchy from the main panel to branch circuits.

**Arc flash hazard evaluation (IEEE 1584-2018 / NFPA 70E).** Performed incident energy calculations at six electrical buses using SKM Power Tools, assigned PPE levels, calculated arc flash boundaries, and produced arc flash warning labels for each analyzed location. Buses analyzed included the 2,000-ampere main panel (480V), the 400-ampere sub-panel (480V), the 600-ampere sub-panel (480V), the existing 3-phase 200-ampere service panel (480V), the existing 200-ampere transformer secondary panel (277V), and the new 200-ampere transformer secondary panel (277V).

The one-line diagram (Drawing E-601) was produced as part of the study deliverable package, documenting the complete distribution system topology, circuit breaker ratings, transformer specifications, wire sizes, and load identifications. The drawing was stamped by a Virginia-licensed Professional Engineer.

## Role on Project

The engineering team served as the sole engineering provider for this study, responsible for all four analyses, the one-line diagram, and the full final submittal package. BFA role classification: study-only. No prime/sub teaming structure is documented in the available source records.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Transformer secondary bus incident energies.** Two 277-volt transformer secondary buses — the existing 200-ampere panel (EX PNL 200A, fed by the 50 kVA T1 transformer) and the new 200-ampere panel (NEW 200A PNL, fed by the 75 kVA TNEW transformer) — reached the maximum arcing duration threshold in the IEEE 1584-2018 model (flag *N9). These locations produced the highest incident energy results in the study: 4.49 cal/cm² at the existing 277V panel (41-inch arc flash boundary) and 5.36 cal/cm² at the new 277V panel (46-inch arc flash boundary), both requiring Level 3 PPE. The study characterizes these conditions through completed incident energy calculations and provides arc flash labels enabling personnel to work safely under the applicable NFPA 70E PPE requirements.

**Mixed existing and new equipment.** The distribution system contains both existing equipment (EX SERVICE 3-phase 200A panel, EX PNL 200A fed by the original T1 50 kVA transformer) and newly installed equipment (the TNEW 75 kVA transformer and NEW 200A PNL). Both sets were incorporated into the one-line diagram, analyzed in the short circuit study, evaluated in the equipment evaluation, included in the coordination study, and given arc flash labels — confirming that new and existing overcurrent devices are properly coordinated with one another.

## Outcomes

- **Equipment evaluation:** All circuit breakers and safety switches passed ANSI fault current criteria. Per the study report: "After completion of the equipment evaluation, no pieces of equipment were found to have failed the evaluation."
- **Coordination study:** All circuit breakers were confirmed to be properly coordinated with the next upstream or downstream device throughout the system. Fifteen TCC curves were produced documenting coordination.
- **Arc flash evaluation:** Six buses characterized; incident energies ranged from 0.43 cal/cm² (Level 0 PPE, 10-inch boundary, 400A sub-panel) to 5.36 cal/cm² (Level 3 PPE, 46-inch boundary, new 277V panel). Six arc flash warning labels produced for field installation.
- **Deliverable package:** One-line diagram (Drawing E-601, Virginia PE-stamped), equipment evaluation report, coordination study with 15 TCC curves, arc flash evaluation with bus results and labels — complete final submittal April 9, 2021.

The final study package provides Colonna's Shipyard with the technical basis for NFPA 70E-compliant electrical safety program implementation at the welding shop, including documented PPE requirements and arc flash boundaries for all energized work locations in the distribution system.
