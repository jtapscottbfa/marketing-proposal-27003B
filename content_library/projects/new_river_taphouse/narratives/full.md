# New River Tap House Renovation

## Project Overview

The New River Tap House Renovation is a commercial interior renovation of a two-story taphouse and bar facility in Virginia Beach, Virginia. Permit drawings were completed in March 2022 (BFA project no. 22-012). The design team served as prime consultant, delivering architecture, mechanical engineering, plumbing engineering, and electrical engineering services for the permit set. The building is a two-story structure; renovation scope was concentrated on the second floor, with the first floor documented as existing-to-remain.

The project falls within the private commercial food-and-beverage sector. No client name or contract value information is available in the source record; all facts are drawn from the permit drawing set.

## Scope

The renovation scope encompassed selective demolition and full new-work construction on the second floor. Demolition scope included removal of an existing wall and covering of existing second-floor skylights per owner direction. The first floor was retained in its existing configuration, with coordination items noted for MEP disciplines.

New construction on the second floor included two primary program areas:

**ADA Uni-Sex Restroom.** A new uni-sex restroom was designed to full ADA accessibility standards. The restroom layout provides required clearances for the toilet (54-inch minimum), lavatory (42-inch minimum), and grab bar configurations (60-inch floor space). Interior finishes include tile wainscoting at 8-inch height, painted gypsum board above. Accessories include stainless steel toilet paper holder, paper towel dispenser, soap dispenser, sanitary napkin dispenser, and waste paper receptacle. A 2×2 drop-in acoustic ceiling grid was installed, with mechanical exhaust and electrical lighting fixtures coordinated with the engineering disciplines.

**Bar Counter Areas.** Two bar counter zones were laid out on the second floor. Each features a low partition wall at 4 feet, 0 inches in height and knee walls with countertops at 44 inches above finished floor. A 12×12-inch bar sink and a three-compartment sink serve the food-and-beverage program, confirming commercial kitchen/bar fixture requirements under applicable plumbing code. An attic space exists above the second floor, accessible via the construction framing condition.

Interior partitions throughout the new-work areas are framed with 6-inch metal studs at 16 inches on center with 5/8-inch gypsum board and 6-inch acoustical batt insulation, set with slotted deflection track at the top to accommodate structural movement.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant, responsible for the full permit drawing package. Architecture led the project from design through permit submission. Mechanical, plumbing, and electrical engineering were provided as supporting disciplines. The permit set, dated March 3, 2022, includes architectural sheets (G-001, A-001, AD101, A-101, A-102, A-501) and cross-references to mechanical (M-501), plumbing (P-101), and electrical (E-101) sheets. Architectural drawings explicitly direct the contractor to refer to the mechanical, plumbing, and electrical drawings for MEP scope; the full MEP drawing sheets are indexed in the permit set but were not available in the source file reviewed during extraction.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**ADA Compliance in a Confined Restroom Footprint.** The second-floor restroom was designed as a uni-sex ADA-compliant facility within a constrained floor area. The design resolved required clearances for toilet, lavatory, and grab bar placement — 54-inch minimum, 42-inch minimum, and 60-inch floor space respectively — within the available building footprint. ADA clearance details were provided on sheet A-501 as a dedicated reference for the contractor.

**Acoustical Separation.** The partition wall assembly specification — 6-inch metal studs, 5/8-inch gypsum board, and 6-inch acoustical batt insulation — indicates deliberate attention to sound control appropriate for a bar environment where noise transmission between spaces is a design consideration.

**Coordination with Existing Structure.** General construction note 14 on sheet A-001 directs temporary shoring where required for demolition of ceiling-mounted items, indicating the team anticipated coordination requirements with the existing building structure during the demolition phase.

**Incomplete Drawing Set.** The permit set as issued references MEP sheets (P-101, M-501, E-101) that were not available in the source document reviewed. The architectural documents provide cross-references to those sheets but the full MEP scope inventory cannot be reconstructed from architectural drawings alone.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a permitted commercial renovation package for a Virginia Beach taphouse, advancing the space to a permit-ready state for second-floor improvements including a code-compliant ADA restroom and a reconfigured bar service area.
