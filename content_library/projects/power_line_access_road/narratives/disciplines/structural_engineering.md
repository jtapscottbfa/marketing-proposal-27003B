Structural engineering design for the HPDTA Power Line Access Road project centered on three bridge structures required to carry the access road across wetland areas along the alignment at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA) in Hertford, North Carolina. The bridge scope was incorporated as a selected design option in lieu of culverts or conventional fill across the wetland crossings.

The three bridges span 35 feet, 175 feet, and 315 feet respectively, with a consistent 24-foot deck width matching the roadway corridor. All bridge structures were designed for AASHTO HL-93 loading, consistent with the project's governing vehicle load requirement of 91,000 pounds per the RFP. The structural sub-disciplines engaged include structural concrete design and foundation engineering; the construction scope incorporates both cast-in-place and precast concrete elements.

The structural design accounts for the wetland site conditions, which require foundations suited to the soft or variable subsurface conditions typical of coastal North Carolina wetland environments. The 532.5 linear feet of bridge crossing constitutes approximately 45 percent of the total 1,190-linear-foot infrastructure corridor, making bridge structural design the dominant structural challenge on the project. All three structures were designed under the single governing load criterion, with the largest span — 315 feet — representing the primary wetland crossing. The final structural design was incorporated in the issued-for-construction drawing package included with the December 8, 2025 Final Submittal basis of design.
