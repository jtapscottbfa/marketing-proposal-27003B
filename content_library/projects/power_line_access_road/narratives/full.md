# HPDTA Power Line Access Road Design

## Project Overview

The HPDTA Power Line Access Road Design project (BFA Project #24-068B) encompassed the full A/E design of a new gravel access road and associated infrastructure at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA), a Department of Defense facility located at 2835 Harvey Point Road, Hertford, North Carolina. The project was initiated by Boeing Aerospace Operations (BAO), the facility operator, to provide vehicular access along a power line corridor on the HPDTA installation. The alignment crosses multiple wetland areas, requiring bridge structures over each crossing in lieu of conventional fill or culvert solutions. The firm served as prime consultant, delivering all civil, structural, and electrical design disciplines under a single contract.

## Scope of Work

Civil design encompassed approximately 1,190 linear feet of new infrastructure, broken into 657.5 linear feet of 20-inch-thick gravel roadway and 532.5 linear feet of bridge structures. The road width is 20 feet, and the alignment was designed to connect to an existing asphalt road apron on Towers Boulevard. The roadway was designed to support minimum 91,000-pound vehicle loads to accommodate heavy equipment access. Three bridge structures were designed to span wetland areas, with spans of 35 feet, 175 feet, and 315 feet, each 24 feet wide, sized to AASHTO HL-93 loading. Bridge structures were designed as a selected design option replacing the base-bid culvert approach.

Running parallel to the roadway for its full length, the electrical design included a concrete-encased ductbank of four 4-inch Schedule 40 PVC conduits, approximately 1,400 linear feet, providing a dedicated infrastructure corridor for future fiber cable installation. NCDOT traffic-rated pull boxes were designed at regular intervals along the ductbank.

Contract services were structured in three tiers: Base Bid (IFC design documents, specifications, calculations, construction cost estimate, and permit support); Option 1 (structural bridges over wetland areas, incorporated into the final design); and Option 2 (Title II construction-phase services including bidding support, submittal review, construction observation, and project closeout). The period of performance was 180 calendar days from notice to proceed.

## Role on the Project

The firm served as prime consultant responsible for the full scope of design services. Civil, structural, and electrical engineering disciplines were coordinated under a single contract and basis of design. The Final Submittal basis of design was issued December 8, 2025. Services included preparation of issued-for-construction documents suitable for public bid under Davis-Bacon prevailing wage requirements applicable to this DoD facility contract.

Permit coordination included National Wetland Permit No. 14 (NWP 14) requirements applicable to the wetland crossings. Stormwater permitting was also referenced in the contract scope. The design team coordinated with existing 2012-vintage Koth Consulting civil drawings and 2024 Boeing-prepared area-of-work drawings establishing the design basis for the corridor alignment and existing conditions.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Wetland crossings.** The power line access road alignment traverses wetland areas, which required bridge structures rather than conventional earthen fill or culverts. This constraint drove the structural scope: three bridges with spans of 35, 175, and 315 feet were designed to cross the wetland areas while minimizing wetland impact consistent with NWP 14 thresholds. The structural bridge option was incorporated as a selected scope option.

**Heavy-vehicle load requirements.** The road design requirement of 91,000-pound minimum vehicle load — explicitly stated in the RFP — drove both the gravel roadway section design (20-inch thickness) and the bridge structural design (AASHTO HL-93 loading). All infrastructure elements were coordinated to meet this single governing load requirement.

**Ductbank coordination.** A concrete-encased PVC ductbank runs parallel to the roadway for the full corridor length, requiring coordination between civil roadway grading and electrical ductbank placement at 18-inch minimum depth below grade. Pull box locations were distributed along the corridor at intervals accommodated by the road alignment, with all pull boxes rated for NCDOT traffic loading to allow installation within the roadway-adjacent zone.

**Design scope evolution.** The solicitation estimated the road length at approximately 1,500 linear feet (verify-in-field). The final civil engineering design resolved to 1,190 linear feet — 657.5 LF gravel roadway and 532.5 LF bridge — reflecting the engineered alignment and actual wetland crossing lengths established in final design.

## Outcomes

The Final Submittal basis of design was completed and issued December 8, 2025. The design package included IFC drawings, specifications, and a construction cost estimate for the base bid scope with structural bridge option incorporated. Construction contract value and construction schedule are not captured in available project records.
