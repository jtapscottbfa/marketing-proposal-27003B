# Full Narrative — Renovate for Human Performance Program, Building 3855E, JEBLCFS

## Project Overview

The Renovate for Human Performance Program project converted Building 3855E at Joint Expeditionary Base Little Creek – Fort Story (JEBLCFS), Virginia Beach, Virginia, from existing warehouse space into a dedicated training and gymnasium facility supporting the Navy's Human Performance Program. The renovation was driven by an operational capacity need: Building 3855A, the installation's primary Human Performance Program facility, required spillover space to accommodate physical readiness and athletic training demand. Building 3855E was selected for conversion to meet that need.

The project was procured by Naval Facilities Engineering Command, Mid-Atlantic (NAVFAC MIDLANT) under solicitation RFP N40085-21-R-8721, issued 8 July 2021. The contract vehicle was a firm fixed-price task order under an 8(a) design-build/design-bid-build Multiple Award Construction Contract (MACC). The solicitation was restricted to the five MACC holder firms (or their joint ventures) holding contract numbers N4008518D1140 through N4008218D1145. Award was made on a low-price basis per FAR Part 15. The estimated construction value at solicitation was between $500,000 and $1,000,000, with a contractual performance period of 200 calendar days from award.

## Scope

The project scope encompassed the reconfiguration and renovation of the Building 3855E warehouse interior to function as a human performance training and gymnasium facility. Construction scope included:

- **Selective demolition** of existing warehouse interior elements to prepare the space for its new use
- **HVAC systems installation** to condition the space for high-occupancy athletic training, addressing the substantial sensible and latent loads associated with a human performance program environment
- **Electrical power distribution** upgrades to serve new mechanical equipment, building infrastructure, and training facility loads
- **Lighting** designed to meet illumination requirements for gymnasium and training operations
- **Finishes** appropriate for an athletic training environment

The project was structured as design-build, with the design team responsible for developing the mechanical and electrical basis of design within the contractor's execution framework. ## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Warehouse-to-training-facility conversion loads.** Converting warehouse space to a high-occupancy human performance training environment is a substantive MEP design challenge. Warehouses are typically conditioned minimally — warehouse HVAC is often limited to basic ventilation — while athletic training facilities carry significant cooling and ventilation loads driven by occupant density and physical exertion. Electrical infrastructure in warehouse buildings is similarly limited compared to the power distribution requirements of a conditioning facility. The renovation required a comprehensive MEP systems upgrade within the existing building envelope and structural grid.

**Accelerated design-build delivery under MACC task order.** The 8(a) MACC task order structure, combined with the 200-calendar-day performance period, required the design team to develop construction documents on a compressed schedule coordinated directly with the construction contractor. Design-build delivery on a NAVFAC MACC task order imposes specific coordination requirements, including early alignment with NAVFAC design review milestones and the contractor's construction sequencing.

**Federal facility HVAC and electrical standards.** Military facilities must meet Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC) and other DON/NAVFAC design standards for HVAC and electrical systems, which impose additional requirements beyond commercial building codes. The renovation's HVAC and electrical design was required to comply with applicable UFC standards for the facility type.

## Outcomes

The project delivered a renovated training and gymnasium facility at JEBLCFS, expanding the installation's Human Performance Program capacity. The facility provides the spillover training space the Navy identified as needed to support the physical readiness requirements of personnel at JEBLCFS. Project status is assessed as complete based on elapsed time since the July 2021 procurement and the 200-calendar-day construction duration.
