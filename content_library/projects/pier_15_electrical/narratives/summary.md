This project involved the design of a medium-voltage electrical power distribution system modification at Pier 15, a naval pier facility in Virginia. The scope encompassed a ship-to-shore electrical infrastructure upgrade — delivering 23 kV medium-voltage service through a new underground cable run and SF6 switching station at the pier head, then stepping down to 277/480V through three pad-mounted transformer banks serving vessel berthing positions along both the west and east sides of the pier.

The electrical design comprised three stainless-steel pad-mounted transformer banks totaling 8,500 KVA capacity: two 3,000 KVA units on the west side serving ship-to-shore power positions via turtle-back connection points, and one 2,500 KVA unit on the east side for utility service. Each transformer bank feeds a dedicated low-voltage switchboard — 4,000A units on the west (each with twelve 400A branch breakers and one 1,200A breaker for temporary generator connection) and a 3,000A unit on the east (with eight 400A branch breakers). The medium-voltage feed originates from an existing 12.3/23 kV WYE overhead service at a deadend pole, transitioning to 35 kV-rated shielded underground cable at the pier entry.

The design was carried to Final Submittal in March 2023 under project number 20-035. The project is classified as complete. Source documentation is limited to the electrical one-line diagram; no contract value, schedule, or client identification is available in the project record.
