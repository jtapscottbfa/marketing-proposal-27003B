# Pier 15 — Electrical Pier Modifications

## Project Overview

The Pier 15 Electrical project (project number 20-035) involved the design of a medium-voltage electrical power distribution system modification for a naval pier facility in Virginia. The project client is a federal naval facilities entity (NAVFAC), consistent with the pier's location and the file-path context of the source drawings. The design was brought to Final Submittal in March 2023 and the project is recorded as complete. No program budget or contract value data is available in the current project record.

The facility is a naval pier — not a building — and the scope is limited to electrical infrastructure serving multiple ship berthing positions along the pier. The project does not involve building construction, renovation, or interior systems. It is a discrete electrical infrastructure modification: replacing or installing the medium-voltage distribution backbone, transformer banks, and low-voltage switchboards that deliver shore power to vessels berthed at Pier 15.

## Scope

The electrical scope encompassed the full medium-voltage-to-low-voltage distribution chain for Pier 15, from the existing overhead utility feed to the vessel connection points:

**Medium-voltage service and switching.** The existing 12.3/23 kV WYE overhead service at a deadend pole (equipped with an S&C dropout-rupter switch, 34.5 kV rated, 600A, with 27 kV surge arrestors) feeds the pier via 35 kV-rated 500 kcmil copper shielded underground cables in 6-inch conduit. At the pier head, an above-grade junction box transitions the underground cable to a 6-way 25 kV SF6 ground-mounted switch, which distributes service to each of the three transformer banks.

**West side — ship-to-shore power (TB1 and TB2).** Two 3,000 KVA stainless-steel pad-mounted transformers (23,000/277/480V, FR3 fluid, 15% fans, bayonet fusing) each feed a 4,000A 277/480V switchboard. Each switchboard is equipped with a 4,000A main breaker, twelve 400A three-pole branch breakers providing individual ship-to-shore circuits, and one 1,200A three-pole branch breaker reserved for temporary generator connection. Conductors from each transformer to its switchboard consist of twelve sets of 3×600 kcmil copper in wireway. Ship-to-shore connections are made via turtle-back connection points on the west side of the pier (positions H#1 and H#2).

**East side — utility power (TB3).** One 2,500 KVA stainless-steel pad-mounted transformer (23,000/277/480V, FR3 fluid, bayonet fusing) feeds a 3,000A 277/480V switchboard equipped with a 3,000A main breaker and eight 400A three-pole branch breakers. Conductors consist of eight sets of 3×600 kcmil copper in wireway. Ship-to-shore connections are made via the east-side turtle-back connection point (position N). Shore power cables from the switchboards to vessels are furnished by others and are outside the design scope.

Total transformer capacity installed under this project: 8,500 KVA (three banks combined).

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Medium-voltage system integration on existing pier infrastructure.** The project required integrating new medium-voltage switching and transformer equipment into an existing naval pier without disrupting ongoing pier operations. The design addressed this through the use of a compact 6-way SF6 ground-mounted switch at the pier head — providing six discrete outgoing feeder positions in a single, minimal-footprint enclosure — and pad-mounted transformer configurations that minimize civil construction requirements on the pier deck.

**Stainless-steel equipment specification for marine environment.** All three transformer banks and their associated switchboards are specified in stainless-steel construction, appropriate for the corrosive marine environment of a naval pier. FR3 (natural ester) fluid is specified for the transformers, offering improved fire safety performance relative to mineral oil in the confined, high-traffic environment of a working pier.

**Temporary generator connection provision.** The west-side switchboards (TB1 and TB2) each incorporate a dedicated 1,200A three-pole branch breaker for temporary generator connection, providing operational continuity for ship-to-shore power in the event of a utility supply interruption. This provision was integrated into the switchboard design without requiring additional switchboard sections.

**LSIG trip provisions.** The west-side 4,000A switchboards include provisions for Long-Short-Instantaneous-Ground (LSIG) trip functionality, providing selective coordination capability for the complex multi-branch shore power distribution network.

## Outcomes

The design produced a modernized medium-voltage electrical distribution system for Pier 15, delivering 8,500 KVA of step-down transformer capacity and a fully coordinated 277/480V shore power distribution network across west and east pier sides. The system provides dedicated ship-to-shore power circuits (twelve on each west-side switchboard, eight on the east-side switchboard) with integrated temporary generator connection capability and marine-grade equipment enclosures. The project reached Final Submittal in March 2023 and is recorded as complete.
