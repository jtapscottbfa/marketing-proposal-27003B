The electrical engineering scope for the Pier 15 project encompassed the complete design of a medium-voltage power distribution system modification for a naval pier, from the existing overhead utility feed through to the vessel shore-power connection points.

At the medium-voltage level, the design addressed the feed from an existing 12.3/23 kV WYE overhead service via a 35 kV-rated 500 kcmil copper shielded underground cable run, transitioning at the pier head to a 6-way 25 kV SF6 ground-mounted switch for distribution to three transformer banks. At the low-voltage level, three stainless-steel pad-mounted transformers (two 3,000 KVA and one 2,500 KVA, all 23,000/277/480V with FR3 fluid) step down to 277/480V for distribution through dedicated switchboards — 4,000A units on the west side (each with twelve 400A ship-to-shore branch breakers and a 1,200A temporary generator connection breaker, with LSIG trip provisions) and a 3,000A unit on the east side (eight 400A branch breakers). Ship-to-shore connections are made via turtle-back connection points; shore power cables to vessels are by others.

Total installed transformer capacity is 8,500 KVA across the three banks. All equipment is specified in stainless-steel construction and FR3 fluid for the marine environment. The sub-disciplines engaged include medium-voltage power distribution, protective device coordination, and shore power system design. The electrical design was carried to Final Submittal in March 2023.
