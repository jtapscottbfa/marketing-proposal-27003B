# Mechanical Engineering — Building 340 Replace Closed Circuit Evaporative Cooler

BFA's mechanical engineering scope encompassed the full design of a new closed-circuit evaporative cooler system at Naval Air Station Oceana Building 340, serving as the heat rejection component for the building's existing water-source heat pump (WSHP) geothermal system.

The primary equipment selection was a new EVAPCO ESW4 9-43G18-SP-R induced-draft, vertical-discharge closed-circuit cooler rated at 524.8 GPM with 25% propylene glycol, entering water temperature of 100°F and leaving water temperature of 90°F at an 80°F ambient wet-bulb — achieving approximately 168 tons (2,524 MBH) of heat rejection capacity. The unit features all-stainless-steel construction, two 5-HP inverter-duty fan motors with VFD control, electric sump heaters, a low-sound fan, electric water level control, and an external service platform with ladder and motor davit. BFA specified compliance with NFPA 214 and Cooling Technology Institute (CTI) ATC-128 certification requirements per NAVFAC RFP.

The mechanical design also addressed DDC controls integration, requiring modification of the existing Trane Tracer building automation system via BACnet to accept the new cooler's fan, spray pump, VFD, and temperature monitoring points. A new chemical water treatment system with Neptune filter feeder was specified for spray water quality management. Interior mechanical room work included replacement of the existing floor drain with a new 12" × 36" trench drain discharging to sanitary sewer in Room 166, and new condenser water piping with insulation, metallic jacket, and heat tracing for freeze protection. All design work was executed per UFC 3-410-02N and ASHRAE standards, with NAVFAC design conditions of 91°F dry-bulb / 80°F wet-bulb for outdoor cooling.
