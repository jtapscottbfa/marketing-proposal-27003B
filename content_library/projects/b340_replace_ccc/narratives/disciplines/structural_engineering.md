# Structural Engineering — Building 340 Replace Closed Circuit Evaporative Cooler

BFA's structural engineering scope for the Building 340 project addressed the design of a new support system for the EVAPCO ESW4 9-43G18-SP-R closed-circuit evaporative cooler — an approximately 26,500-lb operating-weight unit with an 18'-5.75" height and 8'-5.5" × 18'-0" plan footprint.

The structural design consisted of two primary elements: a new reinforced concrete mat foundation and a structural steel support frame. The mat foundation is 2'-6" thick, reinforced with #6 bars at 12" o.c. (top mat) and #8 bars at 12" o.c. (bottom mat) in each direction. The foundation was positioned approximately 3 feet from the existing building wall to avoid imposing bearing loads on existing building foundations — a critical consideration given the absence of geotechnical borings required by the RFP; allowable bearing capacity was assumed at 1,500 PSF pending contractor-provided geotechnical confirmation prior to concrete placement. The foundation is elevated 6 inches above surrounding grade to achieve positive drainage away from the building.

A two-level structural steel support frame of W12 sections raises the cooler bottom to +2'-0" above the foundation, providing clearance for drain and piping connections and meeting the RFP requirement that the cooler discharge elevation be at or above the adjacent building wall. Three-quarter-inch diameter anchor bolts in the concrete foundation resist lateral forces from wind and seismic loading.

Design loads included: Dead load — 26,450 lbs (cooler) + 1,020 lbs (external service platform); Wind — 124 mph Vult (Exposure Category C, Risk Category II), producing base shears of Vx = 13.2 kips and Vy = 6.5 kips; Seismic — Sms = 0.124g, Design Base Shear 3.38 kips per equivalent lateral force method. The design was executed per UFC 3-301-01, IBC 2018, ASCE 7-16, AISC 360, and AISC 341. Special inspections were specified and assigned to Broyles and Associates, P.C.
