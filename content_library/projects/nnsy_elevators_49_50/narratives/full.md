# Building 276 — Replace Elevator #49 and Elevator #50, Norfolk Naval Shipyard

## Project Overview

The design team provided full architect-engineer services for the replacement of two freight elevators — Elevator #49 (eProjects Work Order 1362120) and Elevator #50 (eProjects Work Order 1681187) — in Building 276 at the Norfolk Naval Shipyard (NNSY), Portsmouth, Virginia. Building 276 is an approximately 544,000-square-foot industrial warehouse originally constructed in 1942 and designated a contributing historic property within the NNSY historic district. The project was executed under a NAVFAC IDIQ A-E contract (N40085-20-D0005) via qualifications-based selection, administered by NAVFAC PWD Portsmouth. Final Design Submittals for both elevators were delivered in August 2022. The combined estimated construction cost (ECC) across both work orders is approximately $3,220,000, with estimated construction completion in December 2023. Delivery was design-bid-build.

## Scope of Work

The project replaced two four-stop freight elevators and all systems associated with their machine rooms, hoistways, and lobbies. Each elevator received a complete system replacement — conveying equipment, controls, cab, and guide rails — alongside targeted upgrades to the mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, and finish systems serving each machine room and hoistway.

**Architecture** led design of elevator lobby modifications and interior finishes, coordinated hazardous materials abatement phasing, and managed SHPO documentation for Elevator #49. SHPO consultation addressed exterior alterations — a stair addition and window replacement — required in connection with the elevator replacement scope on the contributing historic property.

**Structural** services centered on assessment of the existing hoistway structure. A 3D laser scan survey of all four hoistway wall faces (drawings A101–A104) documented as-built plumb deviations at each floor level from the pit through the machine room. Measured deviations were small at lower floors but increased at upper levels — a pattern consistent with the thermal and settlement behavior of 1942 concrete construction. A deviation of approximately 3-5/16 inches on the north wall at the upper machine room level was flagged as significant for guide rail bracket design. Structural scope included existing structure and foundation assessment to verify adequacy for new equipment loads.

**Mechanical (HVAC)** provided climate control for each machine room via a new ductless split air conditioning system — outdoor condensing unit and wall/ceiling fan coil — within the approximately 570-square-foot space. Existing exhaust fans and louvers were retained and reconfigured with thermostat control interlocked with intake louvers. A key requirement was integration of the HVAC controls with the NNSY base-wide DDC building automation system per NAVFAC facility management standards, requiring controls sequence development and DDC points list coordination with the base facilities management team.

**Electrical** provided new power distribution infrastructure for each elevator: a new 3-phase/200-ampere circuit breaker in existing Switchboard CL (480Y/277V) for Elevator #49, a new 30 kVA dry-type transformer, and a new 100A, 208Y/120V three-phase panelboard in the machine room. Elevator #50 reused the existing 200-ampere breaker for its feeder. Fused elevator control switches with integral shunt trip relays were provided for both elevators, with shunt trip actuation coordinated with fire protection waterflow switches. LED lighting was installed throughout each machine room, hoistway, and cab. GFCI duplex receptacles were provided at machine room, pit, and top-of-hoistway locations. Two-way intercom emergency communications — master station in the machine room, lobby station at the first floor — were provided per code requirements.

**Plumbing** addressed drainage in each elevator pit via a new submersible sump pump with oil minder system, with Schedule 40 steel discharge piping routed to the building exterior, reflecting NAVFAC environmental protection requirements for elevator pit drainage at active shipyard facilities.

**Fire Protection** modified the existing wet pipe sprinkler system to serve each elevator shaft and machine room. New sprinkler piping was extended from the fourth floor to each machine room, with supervised shut-off valve, check valve, waterflow switch, and test and drain valve. Waterflow switches were wired to shunt trip breakers on the elevator power feeds. The fire alarm system was modified to interface with each elevator controller for Firefighters' Emergency Operation (FEO), with smoke detectors at all elevator landings and in each machine room.

**Hazardous Materials** services encompassed assessment and abatement design for hazardous building materials present in the 1942-era construction within the work areas — a precondition for demolition and construction activities in the hoistways, machine rooms, and lobbies.

## Role

The firm served as prime consultant, providing all architect-engineer design services across architecture, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, and hazardous materials disciplines under a NAVFAC qualifications-based IDIQ task order.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Historic property context.** Building 276 is a contributing historic property, requiring SHPO coordination for exterior alterations associated with Elevator #49. Design solutions balanced operational and code requirements with the historic character of the installation.

**As-built hoistway geometry in aging concrete construction.** The 1942 concrete hoistways were not perfectly plumb, with deviations that increase toward the upper floors. Rather than proceeding from assumed dimensions, the team performed a 3D laser scan survey of all four hoistway wall faces at all floor levels before design commenced. The resulting verified as-built geometry informed elevator equipment selection, guide rail bracket design, and clearance calculations — avoiding potential fit problems during elevator installation.

**Multi-system integration in constrained machine rooms.** Each approximately 570-square-foot machine room required integration of new elevator controls, HVAC, electrical power and panelboard, fire suppression, and fire alarm systems, with clearance maintained to all components per UFC 3-490-06 and elevator equipment manufacturer requirements.

**Base-wide BAS/DDC integration.** NAVFAC's requirement for machine room HVAC controls to connect to the NNSY base-wide DDC network added a coordination layer beyond a standard machine room HVAC design, requiring development of controls sequences and a DDC points list aligned with base facility management standards.

## Outcomes

Final Design Submittals for Elevator #49 and Elevator #50 were delivered in August 2022. The project had an estimated construction completion of December 2023. The design delivered a complete lifecycle replacement of both freight elevators — including all supporting systems — within the constraints of an active WWII-era shipyard building on a contributing historic property.
