Hazardous materials services were provided as part of the design scope for elevator replacement in Building 276 at the Norfolk Naval Shipyard. Building 276, constructed in 1942, is a WWII-era industrial warehouse in which hazardous building materials associated with mid-twentieth-century construction are present. These materials required assessment and management before demolition and construction could proceed in the elevator hoistways, machine rooms, and lobbies.

Hazardous materials assessment established the presence and extent of hazardous building materials within the project work areas. Abatement design documents were developed to govern contractor handling, removal, and disposal of hazardous materials encountered during demolition — establishing work procedures, personal protective equipment requirements, air monitoring protocols, and disposal requirements consistent with applicable federal and Virginia environmental regulations governing work at an active naval installation.

Hazardous materials abatement was sequenced as a precondition for construction activities within the affected areas, ensuring that demolition of existing elevator equipment, hoistway finishes, and related building elements proceeded safely. The hazardous materials scope is captured under the existing hazmat abatement construction scope and represents a standard but essential precursor to renovation work in WWII-era federal facilities at Norfolk Naval Shipyard. Specific materials identified and the extent of abatement are documented in the project design documents.
