Mechanical engineering services provided climate control design for the elevator machine rooms serving Elevator #49 and Elevator #50 in Building 276 at the Norfolk Naval Shipyard. Each machine room — approximately 570 square feet — received a new ductless split air conditioning system, comprising an outdoor condensing unit paired with a wall or ceiling fan coil unit, sized to maintain code-compliant temperatures for elevator control equipment in an active shipyard environment.

Existing exhaust fans and louvers were assessed for reuse and reconfigured as needed, with thermostat control sequences interlocked with intake louvers to provide controlled ventilation. Where relocation was required to accommodate new elevator equipment or clearances, fans and louvers were repositioned and recommissioned as part of the mechanical scope.

A significant requirement for both machine rooms was integration of the HVAC control systems with the NNSY base-wide DDC building automation system per NAVFAC facility management standards. This requirement extended the scope beyond standard machine room HVAC design to include development of a controls sequence of operations and a DDC points list, coordinated with the base facilities management team. The integrated control approach ensures the machine room environments are monitored and managed within the NNSY facility management infrastructure, consistent with NAVFAC requirements for building systems on the installation.
