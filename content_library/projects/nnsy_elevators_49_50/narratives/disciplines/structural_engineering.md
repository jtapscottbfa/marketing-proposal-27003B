Structural engineering services on this project focused on assessment of the existing elevator hoistway structure in Building 276, a 1942-era reinforced concrete industrial warehouse at the Norfolk Naval Shipyard. The age and construction method of the building introduced uncertainty about as-built hoistway geometry — a critical parameter for elevator equipment selection, guide rail alignment, and hoistway clearance compliance.

To establish verified as-built conditions before design proceeded, the structural team coordinated a 3D laser scan survey of all four hoistway wall faces across all floor levels, from the pit through the machine room levels. The survey produced four drawing sheets (A101–A104) documenting wall plumb deviations at each floor in fractional-inch measurements. Deviations were generally within acceptable tolerances at lower floors but increased at upper levels, consistent with the long-term behavior of 1942 concrete construction. A deviation of approximately 3-5/16 inches was documented on the north wall at the upper machine room level — flagged as potentially significant for elevator guide rail bracket design.

The dimensional survey data directly informed equipment selection, rail bracket sizing, and clearance calculations, reducing the risk of field fit problems during elevator installation. Structural scope also included assessment of the existing hoistway structure and foundations to verify adequacy for new elevator equipment loads.
