Plumbing engineering services on this project addressed pit drainage requirements for both elevator hoistways in Building 276 at the Norfolk Naval Shipyard. Water and fluid infiltration into elevator pits is a common concern in older industrial facilities, and NAVFAC environmental protection requirements for active shipyard facilities add further constraints on how pit drainage is managed.

Each elevator pit received a new submersible sump pump equipped with an oil minder system to detect and manage petroleum-based fluids that may enter the pit from elevator hydraulic equipment or other sources — an environmental protection measure appropriate for an active shipyard setting. Discharge piping was designed in Schedule 40 steel, routed from the pit sump to the building exterior in compliance with applicable plumbing and environmental requirements.

The plumbing scope was limited to pit drainage systems; no domestic water supply, sanitary waste, or other plumbing systems were included within the elevator replacement scope for either elevator. The oil minder provision and Schedule 40 steel discharge piping reflect NAVFAC standards for elevator pit drainage at Norfolk Naval Shipyard, where petroleum management in building drainage is a facility-wide requirement.
