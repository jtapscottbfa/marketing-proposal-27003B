# B101A, B2641 & B2865 Replace Boilers — NAS Patuxent River

## Project Overview

Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland (NAWC/NAS PAX River), is a major Navy installation and test and evaluation complex. Three of its buildings — B2641, B2865, and B101A — were served by aging boiler plants requiring full replacement. The project was procured by NAVFAC as a single design-build solicitation (ACQR#5768527, Work Order 1639149) with three line items, one per building. The design team served as Designer of Record (DOR) on the design-build team, producing a Basis of Design (BFA Project #20-018, Final Level Submittal dated August 13, 2020) that governed the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing design across all three facilities.

## Scope

Each building was addressed as a discrete scope under a single contract vehicle:

**Building B2641** — The existing boiler plant consisted of two Smith 19A-51W-03 cast-iron fuel oil-fired boilers, each rated at 810 MBH. Replacement scope included removal of the existing boilers and all associated equipment, installation of new oil-fired packaged non-condensing hot water boilers (minimum two units, each sized to carry 67% of building load), new hydronic pumps and piping, a shot-type chemical feeder, and integration with the building's existing DDC system. Crane access was required for boiler removal and installation.

**Building B2865** — The existing boiler consisted of a single Fulton Gas-Fired Pulse Combustion Boiler, Model PHw-1000, rated at 1,000,000 Btu/hr. Replacement scope included installation of two new gas-fired cast-iron modular condensing boilers, each rated at 650 MBH and meeting a minimum thermal efficiency of 92.7%, with new pumps and DDC controls integration. A Permit to Construct (PTC) from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) was required prior to installation.

**Building B101A** — The boiler room serves Hangar 101 (H101) and houses two Weil McLain BGL-988-SF-PF-LO-UL gas-fired hot water boilers. The existing plant also includes hot water pumps and a steam converter. Replacement scope included installation of two new high-efficiency gas-fired condensing hot water boilers, replacement of all associated pumps and piping, removal of the steam converter, and a new control panel interfaced with the building DDC system. An MDE Permit to Construct was required. The existing equipment is listed under the NAS Patuxent River Title V Operating Permit, requiring permit coordination as part of the replacement.

Across all three buildings, the electrical power distribution serving the new mechanical equipment was modified, including adjustments to existing 480Y/277V and 208Y/120V three-phase panels, new individual motor starters with separate disconnect switches for each motor, and new conduit (IMC/rigid metallic) and copper THHN/THWN conductors. Plumbing scope in each building included replacement of the air separator, make-up water assembly, and expansion tank, as well as insulation of domestic hot water supply and recirculation piping. Building B2641 required installation of dams around floor drains to contain potential fuel leaks.

## Role on Project

The design team served as Designer of Record under the design-build project structure. As DOR, the team was responsible for the engineering design across mechanical, electrical, and plumbing disciplines, production of construction documents, and coordination of regulatory permit applications for the two gas-fired buildings. The Basis of Design document represents the final-level design submittal for the project.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Occupied facilities and temporary heating.** All three buildings were occupied or in active use during construction. Temporary heating provisions were required to maintain building operations throughout boiler removal and replacement, which occurred during periods when the permanent heating system was offline.

**FOD protocols at B101A.** Building B101A's boiler room is located adjacent to an active taxiway at NAS Patuxent River. All construction activities required compliance with Foreign Object Debris (FOD) protocols to protect flight operations, adding coordination and procedural requirements to the construction-phase work.

**Asbestos abatement at B101A.** Asbestos-containing materials may be present in Building B101A. Pre-construction testing was required, and abatement scope was identified as a contingent element of the project depending on testing results.

**Regulatory permit coordination.** Buildings B2865 and B101A required Permits to Construct from the Maryland MDE prior to boiler installation. B101A's existing equipment is covered under the NAS Patuxent River Title V Operating Permit. Permit coordination was a required pre-construction deliverable, with design documents structured to support the PTC applications.

**Equipment identification discrepancy at B101A.** Source documents contained a minor inconsistency regarding the existing boilers in B101A: the RFP's air quality section references "Hurst 4.2-MMBH gas-fired units" (consistent with the Title V permit at time of permit issuance), while the Basis of Design and RFP Statement of Work both identify the installed equipment as two Weil McLain BGL-988-SF-PF-LO-UL units. The BOD's field-verified identification was treated as authoritative for design purposes.

**Multi-building procurement.** The three buildings vary in fuel type (oil vs. gas), boiler configuration, building use, and environmental permit status. Managing these differences under a single solicitation with three CLINs required the design to address each building independently while maintaining a coherent project-level approach to controls, electrical, and plumbing standards.

## Outcomes

The project is recorded as complete. The Final Level Submittal of the Basis of Design (August 2020) reflects completed design documentation for all three buildings, with the design criteria, equipment selections, controls integration approach, and regulatory permit requirements fully established for each CLIN. Contract value and construction completion date are not captured in the available source documents.
