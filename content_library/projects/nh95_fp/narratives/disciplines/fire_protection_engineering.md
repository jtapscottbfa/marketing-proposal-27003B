Fire protection engineering was the primary technical discipline on this project. Building NH95, a 275,400 SF, three-story operations center constructed in 1959, had accumulated a patchwork of fire suppression systems in varying states of repair across its service life. The design scope directed replacement of existing sprinkler heads throughout the older sections of the building, repair and replacement of standpipes, risers, and fire pump systems, and conversion of existing dry-pipe and preaction systems to wet-pipe construction throughout. In areas where no sprinkler coverage existed, the project provided new fire suppression systems or evaluated equivalent suppression alternatives, including repair of the substrates and finishes disturbed by system installation.

A new fire detection and alarm system was designed to replace the existing system across the full building. The new mass notification capability was integrated with the base-wide Federal Signal Giant Voice system, requiring new annunciator panels, software, and system integration across the building's floor plates.

Jensen Hughes, Inc. (Virginia Beach, VA) served as the fire protection engineering subconsultant, authoring the 35% Basis of Design submittal. The BOD established the fire protection design basis in accordance with applicable NFPA standards for the building's construction type, occupancy groups, and actual height. Design accounted for the building's MDI of 99 and the requirement that fire suppression coverage remain operational in all occupied zones throughout the phased construction sequence — a significant coordination constraint given that the building remained fully occupied by active Navy personnel during construction.
