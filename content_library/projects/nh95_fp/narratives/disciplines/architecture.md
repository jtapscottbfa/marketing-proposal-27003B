Architectural scope on this project encompassed interior renovations throughout the 275,400 SF operations center at Building NH95 and the design of a temporary SCIF-capable modular office facility at the adjacent NH5 site. Within the main building, the architectural work addressed common space interior finish repairs and replacements required in areas affected by fire protection system installation and hazardous materials abatement: floor finishes, wall finishes (including drywall and plaster repair and replacement), acoustical ceilings, interior doors and frames, and painting and wallcovering throughout the impacted zones. The SAES explicitly directed that finish repairs accompany the fire protection system work — making the architectural scope an integral part of the project's overall construction sequence rather than an independent renovation effort.

The architectural design of the temporary SCIF modular office facility on the NH5 site required compliance with SCIF construction criteria as defined by building users, including construction standards for the classified envelope and provision of NIPR, SIPR, and other classified network access per user requirements. This scope was architecturally distinct from the main building renovation, involving modular construction rather than renovation of existing space.

A key constraint on the architectural scope was the continuous occupancy requirement: all building tenants remained in place throughout construction, and the phasing plan required architectural work to be sequenced so that fire-rated assembly continuity was maintained in occupied areas at all times. The building's mission-critical status — MDI of 99 — and secure operational environment informed material selections, finish standards, and sequencing decisions throughout.
