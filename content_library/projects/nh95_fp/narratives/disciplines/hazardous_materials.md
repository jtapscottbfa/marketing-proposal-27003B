Hazardous materials assessment and abatement design was a significant enabling scope element for the renovation work at Building NH95. The 1959-vintage facility was found to contain asbestos-containing materials (ACM), lead-based paint, and polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) — all of which required identification, assessment, and abatement prior to or concurrent with the fire protection system replacement and finish renovation activities. A HAZMAT survey was conducted and documented as part of the 35% Basis of Design submittal, establishing the scope and location of abatement required by material type and area of the building. Reference drawings HM101 through HM122 were identified in the BOD as the hazmat drawing series depicting material locations, though these drawings were not available in the project's source document set.

The presence of hazardous materials throughout the building added complexity to the phased construction approach. Abatement sequencing had to be tightly coordinated with the fire protection system work and finish repairs in each construction zone — abatement of disturbed materials had to precede invasive construction, while the occupied building environment required strict containment and industrial hygiene protocols to protect building users and staff who remained in place throughout construction. The limited work-hour windows (after-hours and weekends only) compressed the available time for abatement activities within each zone.

The hazardous materials scope was governed by the applicable abatement specifications within the project's outline specification set, and by the HAZMAT survey findings documented in the Basis of Design.
