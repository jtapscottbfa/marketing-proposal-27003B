# FY22 Fire Protection Repairs, Ops Center, Building NH95  
Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia | NAVFAC MIDLANT

---

## Project Overview

Building NH95 is the headquarters operations center at Naval Station Norfolk (NSA Norfolk), Virginia — a 275,400 SF, three-story facility (including an unoccupied basement) constructed in 1959. The building carries a Mission Dependency Index of 99, placing it among the highest rated facilities in service to the Atlantic Fleet. At that criticality level, continuous occupancy and mission availability are non-negotiable constraints on any construction activity.

By FY22, the building's fire protection infrastructure had reached a state requiring comprehensive repair and replacement. Original and patchwork systems installed across decades of building history — wet-pipe zones in varying states of disrepair, dry-pipe and preaction systems no longer appropriate for the occupancy, gaps in sprinkler coverage in older sections, and an aging fire detection and alarm system — no longer met current NFPA standards or NAVFAC requirements. The project scope was authorized under Operation and Maintenance, Navy (O&MN) appropriations, with a program budget of $31,645,000 and an estimated construction cost of $26,637,000 (Design-To ECC of $23,973,000).

---

## Scope

The project directed the design team to address the full fire protection system infrastructure of Building NH95 through repair, replacement, conversion, and new installation:

**Fire Suppression.** Replace existing sprinkler heads throughout older sections of the building; repair and replace standpipes, risers, and fire pump systems; convert existing dry-pipe and preaction systems to wet-pipe construction; and provide new sprinkler coverage or other fire suppression systems in previously unsprinklered areas. All suppression system work included repair of the substrates and finishes disturbed by the construction.

**Fire Alarm and Mass Notification.** Repair the existing fire detection system and provide a new detection system throughout the building. Link the building's annunciation to the base-wide Federal Signal Giant Voice mass notification system, including new panels, software, and system integration.

**Hazardous Materials Abatement.** The 1959-vintage building was found to contain asbestos-containing materials (ACM), lead-based paint, and polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs). A HAZMAT survey established the extent of contamination; abatement was designed to precede or accompany the fire protection system work and finish repairs in each construction zone.

**Interior Finish Repairs.** Common space interior finishes — floors, walls (including drywall and plaster), acoustical ceilings, interior doors and frames, and painting and wallcovering — were repaired or replaced throughout the building in the areas affected by fire protection system installation and HAZMAT abatement.

**Emergency Lighting.** Emergency egress lighting systems were replaced throughout the building.

**SCIF Modular Offices (NH5 Site).** Temporary SCIF-capable modular office facilities were designed for installation on the adjacent NH5 site to accommodate building users displaced during phased construction. The modular facilities required SCIF construction per user specifications and provision of NIPR, SIPR, and other classified network access as directed by users.

The outline specification set covered Divisions 22 (Plumbing), 23 (HVAC), 25 (Integrated Automation), 26 (Electrical), 27 (Communications), and 33 (Utilities) alongside the fire protection and architectural finish scopes.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant to NAVFAC MIDLANT, responsible for the full multi-discipline design under a task order issued against AEDC IDIQ contract N4008517D0347 (eProjects Work Order 1687420, client project number ST 19-0641). Fire protection engineering was performed through Jensen Hughes, Inc. (Virginia Beach, VA) as subconsultant — Jensen Hughes authored the 35% Basis of Design submittal for the fire protection scope. The prime team encompassed fire protection, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, structural, civil, architectural, and hazardous materials disciplines, with design-bid-build delivery and competitive firm-fixed-price construction procurement.

Design submittals were structured as Design Development (35–50%), Pre-Final (100%), and Final Design. Construction administration services (PCAS) were included as an optional task order item, subject to Government discretion after construction contract award.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Continuous Occupancy of a Mission-Critical Facility.** Building NH95 hosts active Navy command and operations staff who could not vacate during construction. The SAES directed that "all building tenants must be able to continue work during construction" and that "complete construction after working hours and on weekends to prevent disrupting work." The design team resolved this by developing a comprehensive phasing plan that divided the building into discrete construction zones, sequencing fire protection system turnover so that operable life-safety coverage was maintained in occupied areas at all times. Work hours were limited to off-peak periods to protect mission operations.

**SCIF Construction Continuity.** Certain areas of Building NH95 required SCIF construction classification, and the users needed equivalent classified workspace during the construction period. The design solution provided temporary SCIF-capable modular offices on the adjacent NH5 site, meeting SCIF construction requirements per user definition and incorporating NIPR, SIPR, and other classified network access — a technically complex scope element requiring coordination between the architectural, civil, electrical, and telecommunications disciplines.

**Hazardous Materials Management in an Occupied Building.** Abatement of ACM, lead-based paint, and PCBs in a fully occupied, mission-critical building required strict sequencing, containment, and industrial hygiene protocols. HAZMAT abatement had to be coordinated with fire protection system construction and finish repair sequencing in each zone to prevent cross-contamination and to keep building users safe during active abatement operations.

**Aging Infrastructure in a 1959-Vintage Building.** The building's age introduced structural and systems conditions that could not be fully anticipated without investigation. The design included existing conditions assessment by the structural engineering discipline and HAZMAT survey to establish existing conditions prior to design, ensuring the project's scope was grounded in verified field conditions rather than assumed.

## Outcomes

The project was in the design phase at the time of the available source documents (35% BOD submittal). Construction contract award and construction completion data are not available in the project record, as the task order design fee had not yet been established at the SAES stage (to be negotiated at task order issue) and construction contracting had not occurred. The design-to ECC of $23,973,000 established the budget constraint governing the design team's scope decisions through final design.

The project represents a significant life-safety infrastructure investment in one of the Navy's highest-priority shore facilities, addressing decades of deferred fire protection system maintenance under a single coordinated multi-discipline renovation scope.
