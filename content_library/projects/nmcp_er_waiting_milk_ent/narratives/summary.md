This project involved multi-discipline design criteria development for HVAC replacement and interior renewal in the Emergency Department waiting room at Naval Medical Center Portsmouth (NMCP), Building 2 — a 1,062,644-square-foot, five-level federal military medical center in Portsmouth, Virginia. The core work replaced a 20-year-old Buffalo Forge air handling unit (AHU) serving the full 29,000-square-foot Emergency Department with a new unit providing variable air volume (VAV) fan operation, economizer controls, hydronic heating and cooling coils, UV lighting, and direct digital controls integrated to the existing Metasys BACnet building automation system. The 1,863-square-foot ER waiting room was designed for 12 air changes per hour under negative pressurization with 100% exhaust, per UFC 4-510-01 waiting room classification WRC02, reflecting the infection-control requirements for an active federal hospital environment. Interior renewal of the waiting room included new VCT flooring, 2×2 acoustic tile ceiling with insulation, LED lighting, paint, three new interior doors, and new seating furniture. The design covered mechanical, electrical, fire protection, architectural, and commissioning disciplines; testing, adjusting, and balancing by a certified independent firm and full commissioning by an independent commissioning authority were specified requirements. The project was procured through the US Army Engineering and Support Center, Huntsville (CEHNC) Medical Repair/Renewal (MRR) program as a firm-fixed-price task order to a MATOC-holder construction contractor, with a disclosed project magnitude of $500,000 to $1,000,000.
