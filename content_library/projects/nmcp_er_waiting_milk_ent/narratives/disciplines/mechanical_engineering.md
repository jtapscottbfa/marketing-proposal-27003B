The mechanical engineering scope centered on replacing a 20-year-old Buffalo Forge air handling unit (AHU) that served the entire 29,000-square-foot Emergency Department at Naval Medical Center Portsmouth, Building 2. The replacement AHU was specified with variable air volume (VAV) fan operation, an economizer, hydronic heating and cooling coils, integrated UV lighting, and double-wall internally insulated construction — meeting current energy performance and infection-control standards for a federal military medical facility.

The ER waiting room was designed to operate at 12 air changes per hour under negative pressurization with 100% exhaust and no return air, per UFC 4-510-01 waiting room classification WRC02 — a condition requiring precise coordination of air distribution between the waiting room and the broader Emergency Department. New variable air volume terminal units with hydronic reheat were provided; existing VAV boxes elsewhere in the ER were rebalanced. Complete ductwork replacement was performed within the mechanical room; louvers and plenum boxes were replaced. The hydronic distribution piping serving the AHU — including valves, strainers, gauges, and isolation valves — was replaced in full.

Direct digital controls (DDC) for the new AHU, replacing all controllers, valves, dampers, and sensors, were integrated to the existing Metasys BACnet building automation system. Variable frequency drives were specified on all fan motors; NEMA premium efficient motors were required. Testing, adjusting, and balancing (TAB) by a certified independent firm (AABC/NEBB/TABB) was required at project completion, coordinated with the independent commissioning authority.
