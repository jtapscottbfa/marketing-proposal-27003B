## Project Overview

Naval Medical Center Portsmouth (NMCP) — a Navy military treatment facility and major medical center located at 620 John Paul Jones Circle, Portsmouth, Virginia — occupies Building 2, a 1,062,644-square-foot, five-level facility completed in 1998. The Emergency Department comprises approximately 29,000 square feet of that building. By fiscal year 2020, the existing Buffalo Forge air handling unit (AHU) serving the entire Emergency Department had reached 20 years of service life and required replacement to restore adequate climate control, meet current energy and infection-control code requirements, and maintain reliable operation within an active federal healthcare environment.

This project — "FY 20 ER Waiting Room HVAC and Interior Renewal, Naval Medical Center Portsmouth, Building 2, Portsmouth, VA" — addressed both the mechanical infrastructure serving the Emergency Department and the interior condition of the 1,863-square-foot ER waiting room through a single, coordinated effort. Procurement was managed by the US Army Engineering and Support Center, Huntsville (CEHNC) under the USACE Medical Repair/Renewal (MRR) program as a firm-fixed-price task order competition among four qualified MATOC-holder construction contractors. The disclosed project magnitude ranged from $500,000 to $1,000,000. Solicitation number MRR-20-P-0000 008658; base contract W912DY-1x-D-00__.

## Scope

The base scope comprised two closely integrated components — AHU replacement and waiting room interior renewal — executed within an operational military hospital.

**HVAC Replacement.** The existing Buffalo Forge AHU was removed and replaced with a new air handling unit providing variable air volume (VAV) fan operation, economizer controls, hydronic heating and cooling coils, integrated UV lighting, and double-wall internally insulated construction. The replacement unit continued to serve the full 29,000-square-foot Emergency Department. New VAV terminal units with hydronic reheat were provided, and existing VAV boxes in the broader ER were rebalanced. Ductwork within the mechanical room was replaced in full, as were louvers and plenum boxes. Hydronic distribution piping to the AHU — including valves, strainers, gauges, and isolation valves — was replaced entirely.

The ER waiting room was designed to 12 air changes per hour under negative pressurization with 100% exhaust and no return air, per UFC 4-510-01 waiting room classification WRC02 — a specification driven by infection-control requirements in a space serving potentially symptomatic patients. Direct digital controls (DDC), replacing all controllers, valves, dampers, and sensors, were integrated to the existing Metasys BACnet building automation system. Variable frequency drives were provided on all fan motors; NEMA premium efficient motors specified. Independent testing, adjusting, and balancing (TAB) by an AABC/NEBB/TABB-certified firm and full commissioning by an independent commissioning authority were required at project completion.

**ER Waiting Room Interior Renewal.** The 1,863-square-foot waiting room was renewed from the floor up. Demolition included existing ceramic tile and carpet flooring, the full acoustic tile ceiling system (grid, tile, and 6-inch fiberglass batt insulation), three doors (one 3-foot single-leaf, two 9-foot automatic sliding units), and all wall-mounted items. New work included vinyl composition tile (VCT) resilient flooring with 4-inch resilient base; new 2×2 acoustic ceiling tile grid with insulation; 5/8-inch Type X gypsum board on 20-gauge steel studs at 16 inches on center for new partitions; paint (prime plus two coats); one 4-foot 0-inch by 7-foot 0-inch wood interior door at triage; two trackless automatic aluminum sliding glass doors; and new seating furniture. LED lighting — 2×4 recessed troffers, downlights, exit signs, and 2×4 fixtures with battery backup — replaced the existing luminaires. Lighting levels were designed to 30 foot-candles in the waiting room and 50 foot-candles in staff areas per IES and UFC 3-530-01.

**Electrical.** AHU electrical service was shut down and demolished; the new unit was connected through the motor control center. Temporary power via the MCC maintained ER continuity during transition. Wiring specified as EMT conduit, copper THHN/THWN conductors (#12 AWG power minimum, #14 AWG control minimum), with voltage drop limits of 2% feeders, 3% branch circuits, 5% overall.

**Fire Protection.** The existing wet pipe sprinkler system (Simplex 4100ES fire alarm; Aurora 481BF fire pump) was retained in service throughout. Temporary fire protection measures and formal reinstatement procedures governed the ceiling demolition phase. Sprinkler heads were reinstated to match existing manufacturer, type, finish, and temperature rating. Fire alarm smoke detectors and notification appliances were temporarily removed and reinstalled at existing locations.

**Options (Not Exercised).** Three priced options were incorporated via Amendment 0001 but were not exercised: Option 1 (Repair Milk Technology Room — new partition, sheet rubber flooring, ACT ceiling, plumbing, MEP, sprinkler layout modification); Option 2 (GME Building 3 space rejuvenation — carpet and VCT replacement over approximately 1,385 square yards of carpet and 167 square yards of VCT, 22,548 square feet of drywall refinish and paint, 2,503 linear feet of cove base, six phases after hours); Option 3 (ENT Clinic patient room refresh, Building 2 second deck — approximately 9,733 square feet of patch/paint, wall protection, 25 doors, ACT, VCT). The base scope — ER waiting room HVAC replacement and interior renewal — constitutes the executed work.

## Role on Project

The design team produced a multi-discipline Basis of Design (BOD) document establishing the design criteria for the project under the USACE MRR program delivery model. The BOD covered mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, architectural, and commissioning requirements across all base scope and option items. Under the MRR program structure, the BOD served as government-furnished design criteria; the MATOC construction contractor then developed a "Work Plan" (final construction documents) and executed both design completion and construction under a single firm-fixed-price task order. The design team's role was as A-E for the design criteria phase; construction-phase A-E services were not part of this scope under this delivery model.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Active hospital HVAC replacement with infection-control requirements.** Replacing the AHU serving an active Emergency Department required meeting UFC 4-510-01 performance parameters — 12 ACH, negative pressure, 100% exhaust — in a space where HVAC system behavior directly affects patient safety and infection risk. The design specified these parameters explicitly and required commissioning verification of the air distribution system against the design intent, with TAB documentation.

**20-year-old equipment requiring replacement without Emergency Department shutdown.** The Buffalo Forge AHU had exceeded service life in a continuously occupied, mission-critical environment. The design specified a temporary power connection via the motor control center to maintain electrical service to the ER during AHU transition, and required coordination of mechanical system outages to minimize operational impact on the Emergency Department.

**BACnet BAS integration with existing Metasys infrastructure.** Rather than installing a standalone controls network, the new DDC was designed to integrate to the existing Metasys BACnet building automation system. This required replacing all controllers, valves, dampers, and sensors while preserving the existing network topology, reducing the BAS disruption footprint.

**Fire protection continuity during ceiling demolition in an occupied hospital.** Ceiling replacement in the ER waiting room required temporary removal of sprinkler heads and fire alarm devices in a continuously occupied healthcare environment. The design specified formal temporary protection measures and reinstatement procedures to ensure no gap in fire suppression coverage during demolition.

## Outcomes

The project is recorded as complete. The base scope — AHU replacement serving the Emergency Department and ER waiting room interior renewal — was executed. No executed contract value, final completion date, TAB report, or commissioning closeout documentation was available in the source documents reviewed; financial and schedule outcomes are not confirmed in the project record. The disclosed pre-award magnitude was $500,000 to $1,000,000. The three options were not exercised.
