# ORF Parking Administration Building — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The ORF Parking Administration Building is a new, single-story building of approximately 3,895 square feet located at 2200 Norview Ave on the campus of Norfolk International Airport (ORF), Norfolk, Virginia. Commissioned by the Norfolk Airport Authority (NAA) under client project number 25-F-02, the facility provides dedicated operational space for the airport's parking management program. The building houses administrative offices, a parking operations suite, a break room, restrooms, an IT closet, and a mechanical/electrical room. The design approach reflects the compact, operationally efficient character of an airport support building: a single-story footprint organized for staff workflow, with all-electric mechanical and plumbing systems.

The project is delivered under a design-bid-build procurement. Andre + Marquez Architects (AV ARCH) serves as the architect of record. MEP engineering services were provided as a subconsultant to the architect. Design documentation advanced from a concept layout (July 2025) through 65% submittal drawings for the electrical and plumbing disciplines (October 13, 2025) and 100% submittal-stage mechanical design and equipment selection (October 15, 2025).

## Scope of Services

MEP engineering services encompass the full mechanical, electrical, and plumbing scope for this new single-story facility.

**Mechanical.** The HVAC design centers on a built-up air handling unit (AHU-1) with an electric heating coil and variable-air-volume (VAV) terminal distribution, serving the building's primary occupied spaces — administrative offices, the parking operations suite, a break room, sign storage, and corridor areas, corresponding to approximately 3,272 square feet of conditioned floor area per thermal load modeling. Six or more VAV terminal boxes with baseboard electric heating provide zone-level temperature control throughout these spaces.

Two supplemental mini-split systems serve the heat-gain-sensitive equipment spaces: a dedicated 0.6-ton mini-split (MSI-1/MSO-1) for the IT closet and a 1.2-ton mini-split (MSI-2/MSO-2, supported by condensing unit ACCU-1) for the MEP room, each providing independent operation. An exhaust fan (EF-1) serves the restroom zone. Total installed cooling capacity across the three HVAC subsystems is approximately 9.5 tons. Thermal load analysis was performed using TRACE 700 v6.3.5 against Norfolk, Virginia outdoor design conditions: 22°F heating design dry-bulb and 90–91°F cooling design dry-bulb. The building is entirely all-electric — no natural gas service — with electric heating coils in the AHU and electric domestic water heating covering all thermal loads.

**Electrical.** Interior lighting is provided by LED fixtures from a Lithonia Lighting product schedule, including recessed luminaires in the administrative offices and admin suite, surface-mounted fixtures in the break room and storage areas, and downlights in the corridor. Fixture lumen outputs range from approximately 3,000 to 6,000 lumens across the fixture types. Three panelboards (PB-36, PB-38, PB-40) distribute power across the building's zones. Emergency and exit lighting with integral battery backup units is installed at egress paths and the building entrance. Occupancy sensor switching is provided at office doorways to support energy performance. Site lighting consists of pole-mounted LED area luminaires (Lithonia DSX2, 135W, MVOLT, 4000K) distributed around the building perimeter and adjacent surface parking area, consistent with the facility's role within a parking operations environment at the airport campus.

**Plumbing.** Domestic water service enters at a 2½-inch main and distributes cold water throughout the building. Hot water is generated by a 30-gallon, 4,500-watt, 208V/3-phase electric water heater (EWH-1). A Bell & Gossett ECOICIRC XL recirculation pump (RCP-1, 3 GPM, 15 ft head) maintains hot water availability at fixtures throughout the building. A master thermostatic mixing valve (TMV-1) tempers the 140°F storage temperature to 105°F at distribution, and a 2-inch reduced-pressure zone backflow preventer (BFP-1) protects the potable water supply. The plumbing fixture complement includes flush-valve water closets, lavatories, a two-compartment kitchen sink, an ice maker connection, a bi-level drinking fountain, a flush-valve urinal, and a mop sink. Exterior frost-proof wall hydrants are provided at multiple building perimeter locations. All plumbing fixtures are selected to meet ADA/ABAAG accessibility requirements.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**All-electric mechanical and plumbing design.** The building is designed without natural gas service, relying entirely on electrical energy for space conditioning and domestic water heating. This approach simplifies utility infrastructure at an airport campus and eliminates gas permitting requirements. The HVAC system is configured with an electric heating coil in the primary AHU and supplemental electric baseboard heat at VAV terminal zones. Domestic hot water is generated electrically, with a recirculation pump and thermostatic mixing valve maintaining safe and consistent delivery temperatures. The all-electric strategy is reflected consistently across the mechanical and plumbing documentation.

**Multi-system HVAC zoning within a compact footprint.** At approximately 3,895 square feet, the building serves a diverse range of occupancy types — administrative offices, operational staff areas, a break room, restrooms, an IT closet, and a mechanical equipment room — each with distinct thermal and ventilation requirements. The design addresses this by organizing the building around three independent HVAC subsystems: the primary VAV system for the main occupied areas, and dedicated mini-split systems for the IT closet and MEP room. This configuration allows independent environmental control for heat-gain-sensitive equipment rooms without imposing their loads on the primary air handling system.

**Design-phase progression across disciplines.** The project source documents reflect a design in active progression, with mechanical design reaching the 100% submittal stage while electrical and plumbing drawings remained at the 65% submittal stage as of October 2025. Equipment selections were prepared in parallel with the design drawing packages, enabling early procurement coordination. The design team coordinated load calculations and equipment selections through multiple submittal cycles across the MEP disciplines.
