## Project Overview

The Enthalpy Analytics Labs – Lab Admin Area Renovations is a commercial tenant build-out located at 1470 East Parham Road in Richmond, Virginia. The project involved converting an existing warehouse area within the Enthalpy Analytics Labs facility into additional laboratory and administrative space. The client is a private commercial entity engaged in analytical research. The project was structured as a design-bid-build engagement with the design team contracted directly by the owner for engineering design services. The design team served as prime consultant and engineer of record across the mechanical and electrical disciplines.

## Scope of Work

Engineering scope was limited to mechanical and electrical disciplines. No architectural, structural, civil, or construction management services were included in the engineering contract. Laboratory fume hoods were specified as furnished and installed by others; the engineering scope addressed the mechanical and electrical infrastructure required to support their operation.

**Mechanical scope** covered the full HVAC design for the converted spaces:

- Five (5) split-system heat pumps with controls to condition the new lab and admin areas
- One (1) dedicated make-up air split system (MAHU) supplying 1,250 CFM to offset exhaust from laboratory fume hoods provided by others, maintaining positive building pressurization during hood operation
- Variable volume supply and return ductwork distribution system
- Volume dampers at all duct branch connections; manual balancing dampers and motorized control dampers throughout
- BAS/DDC controls for thermostats and sensors, placed at a minimum of 48 inches above finished floor
- Test-and-balance (TAB) requirements specified for the completed system

**Electrical scope** covered power and lighting design for the renovated space:

- Electrical power distribution supporting the new split-system heat pumps and mechanical equipment
- Lighting design including LED pendant low-bay fixtures, IFD emergency and exit lighting with 90-minute battery backup, and occupancy/dimming controls using ceiling-mounted dual-technology (PIR/ultrasonic) sensors
- Receptacle circuits including 20A 120V standard and emergency-power circuits, quadruplex receptacles, 230V NEMA 6-15R receptacles on emergency power for lab equipment, and exterior weather-proof GFCI duplex receptacles
- 208Y/120V and 480Y/277V panelboard design, including corrected schedules for existing panels supporting the project
- Dry-type transformer(s) per the distribution requirements
- Arc flash hazard warning labels on all panelboards per NEC 2020 and NFPA 70E

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant and engineer of record, responsible for the full mechanical and electrical design from initial scope development through construction document issuance. Design drawings were issued as a coordinated set including legend and abbreviation sheets, floor plan and distribution sheets, equipment schedules (mechanical and lighting fixture), and panelboard schedules. Mechanical and electrical construction documents were dated October 2021.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Laboratory pressurization and hood coordination.** The project's defining technical requirement was maintaining positive building pressurization when laboratory fume hoods are operating. Because the hoods were furnished by others outside the engineering contract, the design team could not control the exhaust devices directly. The resolution was a dedicated make-up air split system (MAHU) sized to offset precisely the 1,250 CFM exhausted by the lab hoods — an approach that keeps the HVAC design independent of the hood vendor while satisfying laboratory pressurization and indoor air quality requirements.

**480V system compliance in a lab renovation.** The electrical design incorporates a 480Y/277V distribution system alongside the lower-voltage 208Y/120V system. In a laboratory renovation environment, ensuring proper arc flash hazard analysis and labeling on all panelboards per NEC 2020 and NFPA 70E is a deliberate code compliance step, particularly where panels serve both high-voltage lab equipment loads and general building systems simultaneously.

**Warehouse-to-lab adaptive reuse constraints.** Converting existing warehouse structure to code-compliant laboratory and administrative use introduces common adaptive reuse constraints: mechanical and electrical penetrations required coordination with the general contractor; equipment placement constraints prohibited mechanical equipment above electrical panels; duct clearances (minimum 24 inches maintained for access) and thermostat/sensor placement met ASHRAE, ADAAG, OSHA, and applicable state and municipal code requirements.

## Outcomes

The project produced a complete mechanical and electrical design package for the warehouse-to-lab conversion, including a coordinated HVAC system with five split-system heat pumps and a dedicated make-up air unit, and a fully designed electrical distribution, lighting, and receptacle system for the new laboratory and administrative spaces. Design documents were completed and issued in October 2021. Specific contract value, construction schedule, and facility square footage are not captured in the available project record.
