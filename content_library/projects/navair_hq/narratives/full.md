# NAVAIR HQ Engagement — Building 2272 Interior Renovation

## Project Overview

Building 2272 at Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland — known as the Moffett Building — is a five-story, Type IB cast-in-place concrete-framed headquarters office building originally constructed in 1996. Its precast concrete panel and glass exterior encloses approximately 58,000 gross square feet per floor, housing multiple U.S. Naval Air Systems Command (NAVAIR) programmatic user groups: procurement groups, weapon systems program executives, and related DoD headquarters functions. As NAVAIR pursued a strategic workplace consolidation initiative to bring dispersed groups into updated, activity-based environments within a single building, the command undertook a sustained, multi-phase interior renovation program that continues into 2026.

The design team served as prime MEP consultant under the contracting authority of Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command (NAVFAC), delivering mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, telecommunications, and architectural engineering services. The engagement spans at minimum Phases 2b through 6, with each phase addressing a distinct set of suites through separate contract actions.

## Scope

The renovation program encompasses suite-by-suite demolition and full interior fit-out across all five floors of Building 2272. Architectural scope per phase includes new interior partition systems, doors and hardware, acoustical ceiling systems, flooring, paint, wall coverings, architectural woodwork, and furnishings reconfigured to support open collaborative zones alongside enclosed private offices and conference rooms consistent with activity-based workplace standards.

MEP scope across phases is executed against the building's original 1996 infrastructure. Mechanical scope includes VAV terminal unit additions and replacements, duct extensions and modifications for supply and return air distribution, Z-bend sound-attenuating return air transfer ducts at full-height partitions, and BAS/DDC integration for all new terminal equipment — all served from the existing rooftop air handling units and basement centrifugal chiller plant. Electrical scope includes LED 2×2 troffer lighting retrofits with occupancy sensor controls, new branch circuit wiring using EMT conduit with THHN/THWN copper conductors, dedicated power for printers, monitors, and breakroom equipment, furniture-integrated power systems, and sound masking systems throughout renovated areas. Fire protection scope includes sprinkler head relocations and additions for each new partition layout, and fire alarm appliance (strobe and speaker) relocations and additions integrated into the building's Simplex 4100ES addressable fire alarm and combined mass notification system. Telecommunications scope provides new structured cabling for data and voice to serve each reconfigured suite layout.

Suite 345 represents a distinct scope stream within the program. That suite was designed to Intelligence Community Directive (ICD) 705 standards for Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) construction and procured via a separate 100% small-business set-aside solicitation (N4008024R2402), with a performance period of 365 calendar days.

Phase 2b, the program's earliest documented phase, included schematic design services (at 15% Design Intent Document level) in support of a GSA design-build request for proposals for NAVAIR's Procurement Group suites, with BFA providing MEP engineering beneath a prime design team. Subsequent phases (Phase 3 onward) show BFA authoring Basis of Design documents independently, delivering phased 95% BOD submittals directly to NAVFAC.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime MEP consultant across the majority of program phases, providing engineering authorship of Basis of Design documents at 35%, 65%, and 95% submittal levels, construction documents, and scope of work documentation. Disciplines on the design team include mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, plumbing engineering, fire protection engineering, telecommunications engineering, and architecture/interior design. No key personnel names are captured in the source record.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Sustained delivery against aging 1996 infrastructure.** All MEP disciplines worked around building systems original to a 1996 construction — rooftop VAV air handling units, basement centrifugal chiller, hydronic heating, existing electrical distribution, and the combined sprinkler/standpipe system. Each phase required careful coordination to integrate new terminal equipment and branch wiring without triggering upgrades to the base building infrastructure.

**Multi-phase sequencing in an occupied facility.** The renovation proceeded suite-by-suite while the building remained operationally occupied by active-duty NAVAIR commands. Phased BOD submittals tracked separate construction contracts for different suites, requiring consistent design standards and details across many independent work packages.

**ICD-705 SCIF construction as a parallel scope stream.** Suite 345's classified-facility construction requirements differ materially from the open-office renovation scope applied to other suites. The SCIF procurement was separated from the general office renovation contracts, with distinct solicitation, security construction standards, and contractor qualification requirements.

**Activity-based workplace reconfiguration of traditional office stock.** The program's driving objective — converting traditional closed-office federal workspace into collaborative, activity-based environments — required careful architectural planning and MEP coordination to support the shift from enclosed private offices to open workstation clusters, collaboration zones, and shared conference rooms within the constraints of an existing concrete-framed building.

## Outcomes

The engagement has delivered modernized, activity-based workplace environments for multiple NAVAIR programmatic user groups across Building 2272 through Phases 2b, 3 (multiple sub-phases), 4 (multiple contracts), and 5. As of April/May 2026, active design work continues on Phase 6 suites and additional suite areas including Suite 446. The program demonstrates sustained delivery capacity across a complex, multi-phase federal renovation engagement with concurrent design and construction phases, multiple contract vehicles, and an ongoing client relationship spanning at minimum four years.
