Mechanical engineering scope on the Building 2272 renovation program addressed HVAC modifications and extensions across all phases, working within the constraints of the building's original 1996 infrastructure: rooftop variable-air-volume (VAV) air handling units, a basement centrifugal chiller plant, and a hydronic hot water heating system. Each suite phase required new VAV terminal units with reheat coils in reconfigured conference rooms and office areas, along with new supply ductwork serving linear diffusers at perimeter zones and 2×2 ceiling diffusers at interior zones.

A recurring design detail across phases was the Z-bend sound-attenuating return air transfer duct installed at full-height partition walls, allowing return air to pass from occupied zones to the ceiling plenum without compromising acoustic separation — an approach suited to the activity-based workplace layouts with enclosed private offices adjacent to open collaboration areas.

All new VAV terminal equipment was integrated into the building's existing Building Automation System (BAS) and DDC controls infrastructure, maintaining centralized monitoring and scheduling consistent with the building-wide controls platform. The mechanical design was delivered through phased 95% Basis of Design submittals per phase and suite grouping, each coordinated with architectural partition layouts, electrical, and fire protection discipline drawings.
