## Project Overview

Replace Oil Switches 117, 129 & 143 was a medium-voltage utility infrastructure replacement project at Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia, executed under a NAVFAC Mid-Atlantic (NAVFAC MIDLANT) indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity design contract (Contract N40085-20-D-0005, NAVFAC Special Project ST17-0551, eProject WO 1582361). The program budget was approximately $3.85 million, with an estimated construction cost of $3.5 million. The 100% final design submittal was delivered in February 2023; construction completion was estimated for September 2023.

The project replaced legacy oil-filled medium-voltage switches — equipment carrying environmental liability due to potential oil spills — with modern SF6 gas-insulated switches at six discrete field sites distributed across the north-central portion of the installation: Sites 1, 2, and 3 on Towway Drive and Sites 4, 5, and 6 on Fifth Avenue and Breezy Point Drive. Work was procured under a design-bid-build delivery method following the 100% design completion.

---

## Scope

**Electrical distribution.** Three existing hazardous oil-filled medium-voltage switches (117, 129, and 143) were replaced with new SF6 gas switches. Oil Switch 196, at the intersection of Bellinger Boulevard and Fifth Avenue, was demolished without replacement. Deteriorated pad-mounted transformers serving Buildings V-66, SP-368, and SP-62 were replaced with new units providing 11.5 kV primary service and 208V three-phase secondary service. Loads were sectionalized to VFI protected switch ways to improve fault isolation and system reliability. Failing medium-voltage cabling in the SP and V areas of the base — operating at 11.5 kV and 4160V — was replaced with new conductors routed through new ductbanks. New pad-mounted junction boxes, including four-way and five-way configurations, were integrated into the distribution network.

**Ductbank and civil site work.** New concrete-encased ductbanks were constructed to route replacement cabling. Transformer pads were constructed in reinforced concrete, positioned 2 feet above grade. Civil scope included topographic survey and underground utility marking, erosion and sediment control measures (silt fence and inlet protection), pavement patching at road and sidewalk crossings, and tree replacement per the request for proposal.

**Structural foundations.** New reinforced concrete equipment pads — 8 inches thick, positioned 2 feet above grade — were designed to support the SF6 gas switches and junction boxes at each site. Anchor bolt systems (3/4-inch diameter anchors) were designed to resist lateral overturning forces on the switch equipment.

**Hazardous material abatement.** Three categories of hazardous materials were addressed: lead-jacketed medium-voltage cables scheduled for replacement, the oil-filled switches being decommissioned (Switches 117, 129, 143, and 196), and asbestos fireproofing materials in the underground manholes through which existing cabling was routed. Abatement was sequenced as a prerequisite to demolition and new construction activities.

**Architectural.** A deteriorated exterior door on Building SP-62 — a 3 feet 4 inches by 7 feet 2.5 inches steel hollow metal unit with new hardware — was replaced as part of the project package, addressing a deficient building enclosure condition at the utility access point.

## Role on Project

The firm served as prime consultant, holding the NAVFAC IDIQ design contract and providing overall project management, electrical engineering lead, and architectural design services. A subconsultant team was assembled and coordinated across civil site engineering (Hoggard-Eure Associates, PC), structural engineering (Broyles & Associates, PC), geotechnical engineering (GeoEnvironmental Resources, Inc.), and construction cost estimating (Osmundson & Associates Cost Consultants). Project management was provided by Ian Peralta; Mike Dixon served as electrical lead. The NAVFAC MIDLANT Facilities Engineering and Acquisition Division (FEAD) design manager was Frances Peppers.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Hazardous material sequencing.** The coexistence of lead cables, oil-filled switches, and asbestos fireproofing within the same underground manhole network required abatement to be fully defined and sequenced before demolition and ductbank construction could proceed. The design package addressed this through an Activity Hazard Analysis (AHA) and Accident Prevention Plan (APP), establishing clear work sequencing and containment requirements for the construction solicitation.

**Maintaining base utility continuity.** Replacing active medium-voltage distribution equipment on an operating naval installation required load sectionalization design that allowed individual circuit segments to be de-energized for construction without interrupting base-wide supply. The design's VFI protected switch way configuration provided the fault isolation architecture necessary to support phased construction sequences without service disruption.

**Multi-site coordination.** Work was distributed across six field sites at different locations within the installation, each requiring site-specific transformer pad elevations, ductbank routing, and switch configurations. Civil, structural, and electrical design elements were coordinated per-site to ensure compatible elevations, drainage, and clearances among disciplines.

---

## Outcomes

The project delivered a complete 100% design package in February 2023, replacing hazardous oil-filled switch infrastructure with modern SF6-insulated equipment across the installation's medium-voltage distribution network. The design resolved both the environmental liability of the legacy oil switches and the reliability risk posed by deteriorated cabling and aging transformers. The load sectionalization approach improved fault isolation capability beyond what the replaced infrastructure provided. Construction completion was estimated for September 2023.
