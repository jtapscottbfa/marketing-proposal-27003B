Environmental engineering scope on this project centered on the hazardous material abatement program required for removal and disposal of legacy utility equipment containing regulated materials. The project identified and addressed three categories of hazardous materials: lead-jacketed medium-voltage cables scheduled for replacement, the oil-filled switches being decommissioned (Switches 117, 129, 143, and 196), and asbestos fireproofing materials in the underground manholes through which existing cabling was routed.

Abatement of these materials was sequenced as a prerequisite to ductbank construction, cable pulling, and switch equipment demolition, ensuring that demolition and new construction activities were not initiated in areas with unmitigated hazardous material exposure. The environmental scope was coordinated with the electrical demolition sequence to address hazmat characterization, containment, and disposal requirements before general construction disturbed potentially contaminated materials.

NAVFAC environmental and safety requirements governed the abatement approach: the Basis of Design package included an Activity Hazard Analysis (AHA) and Accident Prevention Plan (APP) that documented hazmat handling procedures and established work sequencing requirements for the construction solicitation. The project's environmental work reflects the regulatory complexity typical of infrastructure replacement on active naval installations where legacy materials from earlier construction eras require systematic identification and management before new utility systems can be installed.
