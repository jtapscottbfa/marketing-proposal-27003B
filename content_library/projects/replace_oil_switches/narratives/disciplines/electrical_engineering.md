Electrical engineering was the primary design discipline on this project. The scope centered on replacement of three hazardous oil-filled medium-voltage switches — Switch 117, 129, and 143 — at Naval Station Norfolk with new SF6 gas switches, and demolition of a fourth oil switch (Switch 196) at the intersection of Bellinger Boulevard and Fifth Avenue. The electrical design also addressed replacement of deteriorated pad-mounted transformers serving Buildings V-66, SP-368, and SP-62 with new units providing 11.5 kV primary service and 208V three-phase secondary service with metering.

To improve system reliability and fault isolation, loads were sectionalized to vacuum fault interrupter (VFI) protected switch ways. Failing medium-voltage cabling in the SP and V areas of the base — operating at both 11.5 kV and 4160V — was replaced with new conductors routed through new concrete-encased ductbanks. New pad-mounted junction boxes, including four-way and five-way configurations, were designed to complete the rebuilt distribution network. Mike Dixon served as electrical lead for the project.

Work was distributed across six discrete field sites: Sites 1, 2, and 3 on Towway Drive, and Sites 4, 5, and 6 on Fifth Avenue and Breezy Point Drive. Electrical design was coordinated with civil ductbank routing, structural equipment pad design, geotechnical foundation input, and the hazardous material abatement program required to safely remove the legacy oil-filled equipment and lead-jacketed cabling before new infrastructure was installed.
