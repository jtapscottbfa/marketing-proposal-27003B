# ERC Inspection Station #2 Replacement — Full Project Narrative

## Project Overview

Inspection Station #2 serves as a vehicle inspection and access control point at the Portsmouth entrance of Elizabeth River Crossings' Downtown Tunnel complex, located across from Portsmouth Plaza in Portsmouth, Virginia. Elizabeth River Crossings OpCo, LLC (ERC), the private operator of the tunnel system under a comprehensive agreement with VDOT, initiated the replacement of the aging existing inspection station with a modern, code-compliant facility. Bowman, Foster & Associates, Inc. served as prime consultant and engineer of record for the replacement design, leading a multi-disciplinary team through design and delivery of a complete construction bid package for competitive design-bid-build procurement.

## Scope

The project scope included demolition of the existing inspection station building and all associated site utilities, followed by construction of a replacement facility on the same general site. The replacement building is a pre-manufactured Porta-King Model RR-PC167SW structure — approximately 304 square feet, single story — mounted on a new cast-in-place concrete slab-on-grade. The foundation consists of a 24-foot by 15-foot (out-to-out) slab, 6 inches thick, reinforced with #4 bars at 12 inches on center each way, with a turned-down perimeter edge footing. Civil site work encompassed grading, drainage, and relocation of existing utilities serving the new building footprint.

MEP systems include a new 100-ampere, three-phase, four-wire electrical panel (Panel A) fed from the existing ERC tunnel electrical infrastructure at Cabinet 264-13, via two 2-inch conduits. Interior electrical systems include fluorescent and emergency lighting, convenience receptacles, LAN system outlets, and relocated ERC CCTV cameras with weatherproof exterior junction boxes. VDOT signage was repositioned on the new building exterior. The HVAC scope provides a wall-mounted HVAC unit, chase heater, and wall-mounted heater for year-round climate control. The plumbing scope introduced domestic water and sanitary drainage — a new capability not present in the existing inspection station — including a 1-HP submersible pump lift station for sanitary service, with a below-grade pump circuit run from the existing electrical manhole.

The project was delivered as a design-bid-build construction bid package including drawings, specifications, and project manual, produced for competitive procurement by ERC.

## Role on Project

Bowman, Foster & Associates, Inc. served as prime consultant and engineer of record, responsible for civil, structural (in coordination with subconsultant Broyles and Associates, PC), electrical, mechanical, and plumbing design across the full project scope. Hoggard/Eure Associates, P.C. provided survey services. Bowman, Foster & Associates led all design coordination across disciplines and produced the complete construction bid package issued for ERC's competitive procurement.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Operational continuity through construction.** A non-negotiable requirement was that the existing VDOT inspection station remain fully operational throughout construction until all utilities were transferred and commissioned in the new building. This constraint shaped electrical transition sequencing — the existing 100A load center and associated systems remained live until Panel A in the new building was energized and utility transfer was completed. The site electrical plan explicitly notes this requirement, and the demolition scope for the existing electrical systems was sequenced accordingly.

**Interface with existing tunnel electrical infrastructure.** The new building connects to Cabinet 264-13, an existing electrical manhole within the ERC tunnel electrical system, via two new 2-inch conduits (one for power and control wiring, one spare). Coordinating the interface between the pre-manufactured building's factory-installed systems and the site-fed electrical service required careful specification of panel ratings, feeder sizing, conduit routing, and control wiring integration.

**Pre-engineered building structural coordination.** The Porta-King building unit is provided with a C4x5.4 steel channel base frame and manufacturer-supplied anchor tabs. The structural design by subconsultant Broyles and Associates, PC resolved the interface between the manufacturer's base frame and the cast-in-place concrete foundation, specifying half-inch galvanized adhesive anchors (Hilti HY200 or equal) at 4-inch embedment and requiring special inspections per IBC 2021 §1704/1705 for earthwork, concrete reinforcing and placement, and post-installed adhesive anchors. Design loads followed the 2021 Virginia Construction Code: 60 PSF floor live load, 20 PSF roof live load, 35 PSF ground snow, and 120 MPH ultimate wind speed (Exposure C).

**Introduction of new plumbing service.** The existing inspection station had no plumbing; the replacement facility introduced domestic water supply and sanitary drainage as a programmatic upgrade. Because the site's topography did not support gravity sanitary drainage, a 1-HP submersible pump lift station was incorporated, requiring coordination between the plumbing and electrical disciplines to route and size the below-grade pump circuit from the existing electrical manhole.

## Outcomes

Design was completed through a final construction bid submittal dated March 2026, delivering a complete design-bid-build construction package for competitive procurement. The replacement facility provides improved operational capability relative to the predecessor — modern HVAC, new plumbing service, updated electrical systems, and relocated technology infrastructure (CCTV, LAN) — within a compact pre-engineered footprint suited to the spatial and operational constraints of the Downtown Tunnel approach at Portsmouth.
