# Civil Engineering — HPDTA 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade

BFA's civil engineering scope on the HPDTA 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade was dominated by the design of approximately 19,000 linear feet of underground duct bank along the installation's primary access road corridor—a gravel road approximately 18 feet wide extending 3.6 miles (19,000 feet) through the HPDTA property along the security fence line.

The duct bank design called for concrete-encased Schedule 40 PVC conduit run near or within the road surface, with special engineering attention to culvert crossings throughout the corridor. Roadway surface restoration to existing material and grades following duct bank installation was incorporated into the civil design. Manholes are spaced every 400 feet to facilitate future fiber cable pulling operations.

Civil scope also included geotechnical investigation of subsurface conditions—including soil core data to determine soil composition, density, and water table depth—to verify site suitability for both the duct bank corridor and the new building foundations. Topographic survey covered both pre- and post-development conditions, with grading and erosion control plans included in the design package.

Erosion and sediment control measures (silt fence, temporary and permanent seeding) were designed consistent with North Carolina requirements. Formal NCDEQ ESC approval was not required because total land disturbance remains below the one-acre threshold.

Design was executed in accordance with UFC 3-201-01, Civil Engineering. The civil team coordinated utility location services (including ground-penetrating radar where tracer wire was absent) to document all existing embedded utilities before finalizing the duct bank layout.
