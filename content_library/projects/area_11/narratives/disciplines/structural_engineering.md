# Structural Engineering — HPDTA 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade

BFA's structural engineering scope on the HPDTA 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade covered the Area 11 substation PEMB and the four distributed Electrical Control Buildings.

For the Area 11 substation, the structural team designed the pre-engineered metal building (PEMB) for dead, live, wind, and seismic forces in accordance with UFC 3-301-01, Structural Engineering. The PEMB is supported on a conventional concrete foundation with slab-on-grade. A thickened foundation was designed to carry the concentrated load of the new generator and belly tank, which weigh approximately 52,750 pounds. Access stairs and platforms on both sides of the generator are included in the structural package.

For the four Electrical Control Buildings, the structural design employs exterior 8-inch CMU bearing walls on conventional concrete spread footings with interior concrete slab-on-grade. Roof framing consists of wood-framed (conventionally framed) construction. An exterior generator is located adjacent to each building, enclosed within a screen wall on a raised concrete equipment pad. Roof structure selection—wood framing versus concrete slab—was subject to verification with HPDTA and the end user based on ATFP (anti-terrorism/force protection) requirements, reflecting the iterative nature of the design process on this DoD installation project.

Geotechnical investigation data provided by the civil engineer informed foundation design at all building locations.
