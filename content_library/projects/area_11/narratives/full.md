# HPDTA 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade — Full Narrative

## Project Overview

Bowman Foster & Associates (BFA) provided prime A/E design services for the HPDTA 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade at Harvey Point Defense Testing Activity (HPDTA), a U.S. Department of Defense facility located at 2835 Harvey Point Road in Hertford, North Carolina. BFA's internal project number is 24-064D. The 100% Release for Construction Basis of Design was submitted on February 13, 2026, within a 180-calendar-day period of performance established by the Statement of Work dated March 14, 2025.

HPDTA is engaged in an ongoing recapitalization of its critical mission security systems. The 11 Area Electrical Utility Upgrade is part of that broader effort, specifically targeting the base's southside perimeter camera surveillance infrastructure, which suffered from several identified security deficiencies: its electrical supply was derived from a medium-voltage utility system with no backup power capability, and its communications cabling ran overhead on utility poles—an arrangement both operationally vulnerable and inconsistent with contemporary base security standards.

## Scope of Work

BFA's scope encompassed full architectural, civil, structural, mechanical, and electrical design, executed across a phased deliverable sequence: onsite design survey, site findings report, two rounds of conceptual design, 35%, 90%, and 100% design submittals, and an in-person presentation of the 100% package at the HPDTA site. Title II construction administration services—including pre-bid support, RFI response, submittal review, and periodic onsite construction monitoring visits—were included as Bid Option #1.

**Facility Design — Area 11 Substation.** The centerpiece of the design is an approximately 1,250-square-foot pre-engineered metal building (PEMB) to house the Area 11 generator, switchgear, and transformers. The PEMB is supported on a concrete foundation and slab-on-grade, with a thickened foundation section to carry the new generator and belly tank weighing approximately 52,750 pounds. Access stairs and platforms are designed on both sides of the generator. The galvanized steel PEMB structure was designed to resist dead, live, wind, and seismic forces in accordance with UFC 3-301-01, Structural Engineering. The substation is intended to support the future Visitor's Control Center (VCC) on the installation.

**Facility Design — Electrical Control Buildings.** Four 12-foot by 12-foot (144 SF each) Electrical Control Buildings were designed for distributed locations along the perimeter fence line. Each building houses fiber-optic camera equipment, UPS systems, surveillance, and communication gear. Buildings are constructed with exterior split-face CMU walls (painted) on conventional concrete foundations with concrete slab-on-grade, and wood-framed roofs covered with asphalt fiberglass composite shingles (minimum 30-year) and R-30 insulation. Interiors are conditioned (heated and cooled) to protect sensitive electronics, with interior finishes of painted water-resistant GWB over 2-inch rigid foam insulation and vapor barrier against the masonry walls (R-19 rated walls). Each building has interior ductback access (hand hole). An aluminum screen wall with an exterior generator and holding tank on a raised concrete equipment pad is provided adjacent to each control building.

**Electrical and Communications Infrastructure.** The electrical scope is focused on replacing the existing overhead aerial camera support with a fully underground raceway system. BFA designed approximately 19,000 linear feet of concrete-encased Schedule 40 PVC conduit duct bank running the full length of the installation's approximately 18-foot-wide gravel access road (3.6 miles), with manholes placed every 400 feet for fiber cable installation. The conduit design also integrates with bridge structures along the roadway corridor. The duct bank carries empty conduit only—no conductors—as the fiber cable installation is a separate follow-on activity. A new electrical panelboard at the VCC substation will serve the camera system with a dedicated power circuit for each camera. All 40 existing pole-mounted cameras along the perimeter fence (covering Goose Neck Road, Osprey Road, and Harvey Neck Road, a combined 12,160-foot perimeter) will be re-supported from the new underground system. All existing aerial electrical and communication cabling will be removed in its entirety. Grounding design includes a separate green insulated equipment-grounding conductor in all feeder and branch circuits.

**Civil Engineering.** The civil scope addressed duct bank corridor design along the gravel roadway, with special attention to culvert crossings and restoration of road surface to existing material and grades following duct bank installation. Erosion and sediment control measures (silt fence, temporary and permanent seeding) were designed to meet North Carolina requirements; formal NCDEQ ESC approval was not required because land disturbance is less than one acre. Geotechnical investigation was performed to characterize subsurface conditions along the duct bank corridor and at new building locations.

**Mechanical Engineering.** Mechanical scope was focused on engine generator fuel venting systems for the Area 11 substation generator. HVAC conditioning for the four Electrical Control Buildings was also required to protect heat-sensitive electrical and communications equipment housed within each building.

**Design Standards.** Design was executed to applicable UFC and NAVFAC standards, including UFC 3-501-01 (Electrical Engineering Design and Analysis), UFC 3-520-01 (Interior Electrical Systems), UFC 3-201-01 (Civil Engineering), UFC 3-301-01 (Structural Engineering), UFC-1-300-09N (Navy and Marine Corps Design Procedures), and UFC 3-101-01 (Architecture), in addition to NFPA 70, ANSI C2, NCBC 2018, IBC 2018, and ABA 2015.

## Role on Project

BFA served as prime consultant under a firm-fixed-price design contract with the USG Contracting Office, providing all five engineering disciplines in-house or under BFA's coordination. The architectural lead was responsible for cross-discipline coordination, meeting documentation, and coherent package assembly across all submittal phases. No existing site drawings were available for the 11 Area—BFA developed all existing-condition drawings from field survey.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

The absence of any existing site drawings for the 11 Area required BFA to develop a complete set of existing-condition documentation from scratch, including underground utility location (ground-penetrating radar was identified as potentially necessary where tracer wire was absent), topographic survey, and geotechnical investigation. This front-loaded survey burden was explicitly scoped and sequenced in the SOW's phased deliverable structure.

The ~19,000 linear foot duct bank through a gravel road corridor presented civil coordination challenges around culvert crossings and roadway restoration, which the civil design addressed through site-specific attention to grades and surface material matching.

The four Electrical Control Buildings required ATFP-compliant design (anti-terrorism/force protection), with roof structure selection—wood-framed versus concrete slab—to be verified with HPDTA and end users based on ATFP requirements, reflecting the iterative, stakeholder-driven design process embedded in the two-conceptual-design-round structure of the SOW.

## Outcomes

BFA delivered the 100% Release for Construction package within the 180-calendar-day period of performance, including in-person presentation at HPDTA. The design replaces a security-deficient, overhead-utility-supported perimeter camera system with a hardened, underground, backup-power-capable electrical and communications infrastructure serving all 40 perimeter cameras along 12,160 feet of fence line, consolidating support at the upgraded Area 11 VCC substation.
