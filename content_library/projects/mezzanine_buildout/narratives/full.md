# CBP Advanced Training Center Mezzanine Expansion — Project Narrative

## Project Overview

The CBP Advanced Training Center Mezzanine Expansion is a Design-Build renovation and expansion at the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Advanced Training Center (ATC), located in Harpers Ferry, West Virginia. The CBP ATC is a federal law enforcement training campus; the project scope was confined to Building 6, identified in project documents as the Tactical Training Building. The existing structure is a pre-engineered metal building (PEMB) on a reinforced concrete slab on grade. The project was executed under a Design-Build contract with a contractual period of performance of 180 days following Notice to Proceed.

## Scope

The project encompassed two parallel spatial expansions within the Tactical Training Building. On the first floor, the existing gym was enlarged by converting the footprint of the adjacent restroom and locker room into gym space. The restroom facility was removed in its entirety, and a new laundry room with domestic water supply and sanitary drainage was constructed on the first floor to relocate the displaced laundry function. On the second floor, the mezzanine was extended outward with new structural steel framing and a lightweight concrete floor system, creating additional office and storage area within the building's existing envelope.

The full construction scope included selective demolition of existing partitions and plumbing fixtures; cast-in-place concrete for new foundation elements; new structural steel members extending the mezzanine framing; interior partitions, drywall, flooring, acoustical ceilings, painting, and doors and frames throughout the affected areas; complete new HVAC systems serving the expanded spaces; new electrical power distribution and interior lighting; domestic water and sanitary DWV plumbing for the relocated laundry; extension of wet-pipe fire suppression branches; and fire alarm system expansion to cover new occupied areas. Electronic safety and security systems were also included in the construction scope.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant, authoring the multi-discipline Basis of Design (BOD) that defined the scope, existing conditions analysis, design criteria, applicable codes, and discipline-by-discipline design intent for all six engineering disciplines: architecture, structural engineering, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, plumbing engineering, and fire protection engineering. The BOD functioned as the governing technical reference for the Design-Build contractor's procurement and execution of the work, providing the bridging document between owner requirements and the contractor's design development and construction drawings. The prime contract value is not recorded in the available source documents; an electrical subcontract base bid of $78,068 (I.B. Abel Inc.) is documented in the project record.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Expansion within a pre-engineered metal building.** The Tactical Training Building's PEMB framing established fixed column lines, bay spacing, and structural load limitations that governed the approach to the mezzanine expansion. New structural steel was integrated into the existing frame, with shallow foundations sized to transfer new mezzanine loads to the slab on grade. Expanding within a PEMB requires careful coordination with the original building system: the existing column grid and framing geometry constrain where new members can connect, and new load cases must be reconciled against the original manufacturer's design basis.

**Design-Build single-contract delivery.** The SOW defined a two-NTP Design-Build structure with a Design NTP and a separate Construction NTP, but a single prime contract encompassing both phases. The Basis of Design served as the primary deliverable under the design phase, establishing the technical framework the Design-Build contractor would develop into full construction documents. This required the BOD to be both technically complete and usable as a procurement basis — a balance that shaped the level of detail and resolution provided in each discipline section.

**Federal security requirements and access constraints.** All contractor employees and subcontractors were required to hold a favorably adjudicated Background Investigation. The facility restricted access to U.S. citizens and legal U.S. residents; CAC/PIV credentials or equivalent were required for site access. These requirements applied to design-phase site investigation and construction-phase personnel alike, constraining subcontractor eligibility and influencing schedule sequencing.

## Outcomes

The project delivered expanded training support facilities for the CBP Advanced Training Center: enlarged first-floor gym space, new first-floor laundry room, and additional second-floor office and storage capacity created by the mezzanine extension. All six engineering disciplines were addressed with new construction work within and adjacent to the existing pre-engineered metal building envelope, within the 180-day contractual period of performance.
