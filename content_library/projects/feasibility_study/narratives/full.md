# Melwood Recreation Center Feasibility Study — New Construction

## Project Overview

This project is a construction feasibility study for proposed new buildings at the Melwood Recreation Center (MRC), a rural therapeutic camp and recreation campus located at 9035 Ironsides Rd, Nanjemoy, MD 20662 in Charles County. The campus is operated by Melwood Horticultural Training Center, Inc., a private nonprofit organization that provides therapeutic recreation and habilitative services to individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities, in collaboration with a children's hospital partner. The feasibility study was procured under RFP 25-RFP-MRC01, issued April 2025, with a six-month contract term from Notice to Proceed.

The program evaluated three new buildings: a Multipurpose Activity Building, a Classroom Building, and a Medical Cabin. The design team reached Concept C at the time of the ROM estimate (April 2026), with architectural floor plans prepared for the Multipurpose and Classroom buildings. Combined program area is approximately 22,661 gross square feet. The ROM estimated construction cost (ECC) is approximately $13,534,290, with a total project cost of approximately $15,023,062 (approximately $663/SF), including design contingency, escalation, furniture and equipment, and projected design fees.

## Scope

The feasibility study scope encompassed three buildings with distinct programs:

**Multipurpose Activity Building (approximately 11,810 SF, single-story):** A large recreation facility organized around a full basketball court with two half-court configurations, a stage with prop and costume storage, an indoor rock wall, men's and women's restrooms, and general storage. The program is designed to serve campers with physical and developmental disabilities, including provisions for accessible restrooms and lobby entry.

**Classroom Building (approximately 4,492 SF, single-story):** An arts and educational facility housing a Music Studio, Arts and Crafts Studio, Photography Studio, Pottery Studio with storage and kiln room, and associated bathrooms and general storage. This building serves as the creative and therapeutic arts program hub.

**Medical Cabin (gross square footage not determined in available drawings, two stories):** A medical support facility housing a single treatment area, two sick bays, and a medication administration station on Level 1, with additional program on Level 2. No architectural floor plan was available at the time of extraction; the Medical Cabin was described in the fire protection engineering analysis and RFP scope documents.

All three buildings will be served by new on-site infrastructure: private well water supply, new septic systems, and a dedicated fire protection water supply system.

The feasibility study was structured as a design-bid-build standalone engagement. Procurement was through commercial competitive best-value award.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime consultant on the feasibility study, responsible for coordinating architectural concept design, engineering discipline feasibility analysis (mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, and civil), and production of the ROM cost estimate. The fire protection engineering analysis was performed by a subconsultant (Jensen Hughes). Architectural concept drawings were produced in collaboration with a co-architect.

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Rural site without public utilities.** The MRC campus has no access to public water supply or municipal sanitary sewer. New construction at this scale on a rural site requires new private well infrastructure for potable water and new on-site septic systems for each building. The septic infrastructure challenge was compounded by the need to site three new buildings across a dispersed campus (Multipurpose Building in an open field near the Caretaker's House; Medical Cabin in the existing cabin area), requiring separate septic system designs for each structure.

**Fire protection water supply.** Absence of public water supply is the defining constraint for fire protection on this project. Providing code-compliant wet-pipe sprinkler systems in all three buildings required a dedicated fire protection water supply infrastructure consisting of a 70,000-gallon above-ground water storage tank, a 500-gpm fire pump at 100 psi in a dedicated detached pump house, and 6-inch or 8-inch distribution piping to the buildings. Emergency generator backup for the fire pump was evaluated and determined unnecessary based on reliable primary power availability, a decision recorded in the fire protection engineering analysis.

**Occupancy classification complexity.** The Medical Cabin's combined sleeping, treatment, and medication administration functions required careful occupancy classification analysis. The Classroom Building, due to the nature of the camper population and its program under a children's hospital partnership, was classified under IBC Occupancy Group I-4 (Day Care). These occupancy determinations drove differentiated fire alarm system requirements across the three buildings: an emergency voice alarm system with visible signals (strobes) in the Multipurpose Building; a manual fire alarm and smoke detection system in the Classroom Building; and horn/strobe alarms plus smoke alarms in sleeping rooms in the Medical Cabin.

**Standby power for medication administration.** The Medical Cabin's medication administration function requires standby generator power as part of the building program. This requirement was captured in the feasibility study scope and reflected in the ROM cost estimate.

**Program evolution between RFP and concept design.** The RFP (April 2025) described two buildings: a Medical Cabin and an Activity/Multipurpose Building. By the time Concept C drawings were produced (February 2026), the program had evolved to three buildings, with the original Activity/Multipurpose program split into a dedicated Gymnasium/Activity Building (SDA-001) and a separate Classroom/Arts Building (SDA-002). The ROM cost estimate (April 2026) reflects the three-building scope. An earlier schematic bubble diagram shows a combined program that preceded the split; the Foam Pit/Obstacle Course Room shown in the bubble diagram does not appear in the Concept C drawings, indicating continued program refinement.

## Outcomes

At the conclusion of the feasibility study engagement, the design team had produced Concept C architectural floor plans for two of three buildings, a ROM cost estimate of approximately $15 million for the full three-building program, and a fire protection engineering analysis establishing occupancy classifications and life safety system requirements for all three buildings. The ROM estimate provides the owner with a project cost basis for funding, grant applications, and capital planning. The feasibility study established the technical framework — site infrastructure strategy, fire protection water supply approach, MEP system concepts, and occupancy-driven code compliance path — needed to proceed to full design. Construction phase status and contract values are not yet established; the project was in active feasibility and early design at the time of record extraction.
