Plumbing engineering addressed one of the defining site constraints of the Melwood Recreation Center feasibility study: the campus has no access to public water supply or municipal sanitary sewer. All three proposed buildings — the Multipurpose Activity Building, the Classroom Building, and the Medical Cabin — require new on-site plumbing infrastructure to support domestic water, sanitary waste, and fire protection water supply.

For domestic water and sanitary systems, the feasibility study established that new private wells will serve the campus potable water needs and new on-site septic systems will be required for each new building. Existing septic systems are present on the MRC campus, and the preferred approach for new construction is dedicated new septic systems rather than connection to existing infrastructure. Sewage easement plats and septic location plans were produced during the feasibility study period and are included in the source document set.

The fire protection water supply requirement drove the most significant plumbing infrastructure decision: a 70,000-gallon above-ground water storage tank was established as the basis for fire suppression water supply to all three buildings, delivered via 6-inch or 8-inch distribution piping to a 500-gpm fire pump in a dedicated detached pump house. This infrastructure is a consequence of the absence of public water at this rural site and the requirement for wet-pipe sprinkler systems in all three buildings.

No plumbing drawings or fixture schedules were produced at the feasibility study stage. Plumbing domestic water and sanitary sub-disciplines are reflected in the ROM cost estimate; specific system configurations and fixture counts are not captured in the available record.
