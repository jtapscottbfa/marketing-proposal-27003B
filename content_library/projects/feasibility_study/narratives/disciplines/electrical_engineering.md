Electrical engineering was included in the feasibility study scope for the Melwood Recreation Center new construction program, covering power distribution planning for all three proposed buildings. The ROM cost estimate reflects electrical systems costs across the Multipurpose Activity Building, Classroom Building, and Medical Cabin.

The primary electrical engineering determination at the feasibility study stage involved standby power requirements: the Medical Cabin's medication administration function requires a standby generator as part of the building program. This requirement was captured in the feasibility study scope and reflected in the ROM cost estimate. A separate question — whether an emergency generator was needed to support the fire pump — was evaluated and resolved negatively, with the fire protection engineering analysis concluding that reliable primary power to the campus is sufficient to support the fire pump without emergency generator backup.

No electrical drawings or single-line diagrams were produced at the feasibility study stage; MEP drawings were not part of the source document set available for this project. Specific electrical scope details — service entrance sizing, distribution panel configuration, lighting systems, and low-voltage systems — are not captured in the available record and represent a Pass 1 gap to be addressed when electrical design documents are produced in subsequent phases. Electrical power distribution and electronic safety and security systems are both included in the construction scope taxonomy for this project, indicating these disciplines are expected in the full design effort.
