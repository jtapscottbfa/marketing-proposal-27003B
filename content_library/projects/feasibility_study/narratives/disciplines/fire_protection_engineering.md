Fire protection engineering was a central and technically demanding element of the Melwood Recreation Center feasibility study, performed by Jensen Hughes as subconsultant. The discipline's scope encompassed occupancy classification analysis, fire suppression system design parameters, fire alarm system requirements, and fire protection water supply infrastructure sizing for all three proposed buildings.

The absence of public water supply at this rural campus was the governing constraint. The fire protection engineering analysis established a dedicated water supply infrastructure: a 70,000-gallon above-ground storage tank supplying a 500-gpm fire pump at 100 psi, housed in a dedicated detached pump house, with 6-inch or 8-inch distribution piping to the buildings. Wet-pipe sprinkler systems are required in all three buildings under IBC and NFPA 101.

Occupancy classification determinations were a key deliverable. The Classroom Building, serving a population that includes individuals with disabilities under a children's hospital program partnership, was classified under IBC Occupancy Group I-4 (Day Care). The Medical Cabin's combined sleeping, treatment, and medication administration functions informed its classification and drove differentiated life safety system requirements. The Multipurpose Activity Building's large assembly occupancy and its population of users with sensory and mobility disabilities drove the requirement for an emergency voice alarm system with visible signals (strobes) throughout — a higher-tier fire alarm system than would be required for a standard gymnasium.

Fire alarm requirements by building: emergency voice alarm with strobes in the Multipurpose Building; manual fire alarm plus smoke detection in the Classroom Building; horn/strobe alarms plus smoke alarms in sleeping rooms in the Medical Cabin. Emergency generator backup for the fire pump was evaluated and determined unnecessary based on reliable primary power availability.
