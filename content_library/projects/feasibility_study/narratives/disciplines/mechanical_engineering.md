Mechanical engineering was included in the feasibility study scope for the Melwood Recreation Center new construction program, covering HVAC systems planning for all three proposed buildings: the Multipurpose Activity Building (approximately 11,810 SF), the Classroom Building (approximately 4,492 SF), and the two-story Medical Cabin. The feasibility study established the framework for HVAC system selection and sizing that would inform the ROM cost estimate and subsequent design phases.

Source documents available at the time of extraction did not include MEP drawings — no mechanical floor plans or equipment schedules were produced at the feasibility study stage. HVAC systems are reflected in the ROM cost estimate cost line items but specific equipment selections, system types, and capacities are not captured in the available record. The rural site's lack of public utilities does not directly constrain HVAC system selection, though the site's remote location and the population served (individuals with disabilities, including a medical program) may influence system redundancy and reliability requirements. The Medical Cabin's medication administration function and sick bay program suggest ventilation and environmental control requirements beyond a standard recreational facility.

Specific HVAC scope detail — equipment inventory, system types (e.g., packaged rooftop units, split systems, air handling units), and distribution strategies — is not captured in the source record and represents a Pass 1 gap to be addressed when MEP design documents become available in subsequent design phases.
