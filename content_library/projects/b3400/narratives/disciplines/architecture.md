Architecture led the planning, space design, and construction documentation for the reconfiguration of Building 3400 Suite 117 at Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia — an approximately 3,700 SF first-floor interior renovation converting a former medical/clinical suite into administrative office space for the Public Affairs Office (PAO) and Trial Defense Services (TDS).

The architectural scope began with selective demolition of the existing clinical suite, which had housed exam rooms, optometry, audiology, immunization, and soiled linen spaces. The new layout accommodates a complex mix of occupancy types: private attorney offices, a paralegal area, conference room, recording/photo studio, director's office, open cubicle and workstation areas, reception, break rooms, dressing rooms, file/storage, and copy area — distributed across the suite footprint and organized to serve two distinct organizational tenants with different functional and acoustic requirements.

New construction used metal stud framing with 5/8" gypsum wallboard and sound batt insulation throughout. For acoustically sensitive rooms — attorney offices, conference room, and the recording/studio — partitions extend full height to the structural deck above to provide continuous sound separation. Interior finishes include 24-oz, 2×2 carpet tile; 2×2 acoustical ceiling tile at 9'-0" finished ceiling height; solid core wood doors (3'-0" × 7'-0"); Roppe 700 series wall base; and Sherwin Williams SW6119 Antique White paint throughout. A 6'-0" × 7'-0" cased opening was introduced to improve the spatial connection between reception and the open office. Break room locations received new casework and sinks. Design was delivered through 95% submittal in April 2025, with active military occupancy maintained throughout the anticipated construction period.
