# Building 3400 — Construct New Offices, Suite 117
## Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia | PB00002-4J

---

## Project Overview

Building 3400 Suite 117 is an approximately 3,700 SF interior renovation and reconfiguration project located on the first floor of an existing building at Fort Gregg-Adams, Virginia, an active United States Army installation. The project was issued by the Fort Gregg-Adams Directorate of Public Works (DPW), Contract Management Branch (CMB) under client project number PB00002-4J. The design team served as prime consultant across all design disciplines (internal reference: project 24-081C).

The project represents a consequential program change: Suite 117 had previously operated as a medical and clinical suite, housing exam rooms, optometry, audiology, immunization, soiled linen, and associated support spaces. The scope converted this clinical suite entirely into administrative office space for two distinct Army organizational tenants — the Public Affairs Office (PAO) and Trial Defense Services (TDS) — whose operational profiles differ substantially from each other and from the prior clinical use. The new design accommodates open office areas, private attorney and director offices, conference space, a recording/photo studio, paralegal workstations, reception, file/storage rooms, break rooms, dressing rooms, and copy areas across the reconfigured suite footprint.

---

## Scope of Work

The project scope encompassed selective demolition of all existing interior elements within Suite 117, followed by complete new interior construction across architectural and MEP disciplines.

**Demolition** included removal of existing non-structural partitions, ceiling systems, flooring, interior doors and hardware, casework, medical-grade plumbing fixtures and associated piping (mop sink, wall-mounted clinical sinks, restroom fixtures and accessories, toilet partitions), and miscellaneous signage and soffits.

**Architectural new work** established the full interior environment. New metal stud framing with 5/8" gypsum wallboard and sound batt insulation defines office boundaries throughout; for acoustically sensitive spaces — attorney offices, conference room, and the recording/studio — partitions extend from floor to the structural deck above to provide full sound isolation. Finishes include 24-oz, 2×2 carpet tile throughout the reconfigured office area, Roppe 700 series wall base, Sherwin Williams SW6119 Antique White paint on all walls, and a 2×2 acoustical ceiling tile system at 9'-0" finished ceiling height. Interior doors are solid core wood, 3'-0" × 7'-0". One 6'-0" × 7'-0" cased opening was introduced at the reception/open office transition to improve spatial continuity. Break room spaces received new casework and sinks.

**Mechanical (HVAC)** scope reconfigured the suite-level air distribution system to serve the new partition layout. New single-duct variable air volume (VAV) boxes replaced the demolished distribution, with controls integrated into the existing building automation system (BAS). New low-pressure supply air ductwork was routed throughout the reconfigured space, with new diffusers and return air grilles appropriately located and sized for the new room arrangement. The existing central air handling and chiller plant infrastructure serving Building 3400 was retained in service; the mechanical scope was limited to the suite-level distribution system.

**Electrical** scope provided new interior lighting and power infrastructure for the reconfigured space. New LED fixtures throughout are specified at 4000K and 5000K color temperatures, minimum 80 CRI, with integral emergency capability to maintain code-compliant egress illumination. Conduit is EMT/IMC/RMC, minimum 3/4". New power and data outlets were distributed across all office areas, conference room, studio, and support spaces. The electrical design connects to the existing building service, which provides both 480Y/277V three-phase and 208Y/120V three-phase service; no changes to main building electrical service were required.

**Plumbing** scope addressed the conversion of the former clinical plumbing infrastructure to a simple administrative office plumbing layout. New construction provides four break room sinks at two break room locations (two sinks each), served by new domestic cold and hot water supply (Type L copper above grade, Type K copper below grade) and Schedule 40 PVC sanitary waste and vent piping. The existing building hot water system was retained and extended to serve the new fixtures. Floor drains are provided at mechanical equipment and condensate areas. The overall plumbing fixture count is substantially reduced from the demolished clinical configuration.

**Fire protection engineering** was included in the prime consultant scope, coordinated with the architectural and MEP design. Specific fire protection system documentation is not available in the source records reviewed for this project.

## Role on Project

The design team served as prime design consultant, self-performing full multi-discipline architectural and engineering design across all project disciplines: architecture, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, plumbing engineering, and fire protection engineering. Services were delivered from schematic design through 95% construction document submittal. The construction statement of work — issued by Fort Gregg-Adams DPW CMB and included in the procurement package for the separate construction contract — was prepared as part of the prime consultant's design services scope.

The project was delivered under a design-bid-build framework, with design services and construction contracted separately.

---

## Key Challenges and Resolutions

**Active Military Occupancy During Construction.** The defining operational constraint on this project was that the building remained in active use by military personnel throughout the construction period. The statement of work explicitly required: "Soldiers will continue training in the building during construction." This condition placed significant demands on construction phasing, sequencing, and coordination — all of which were addressed in the design documents and SOW to provide clear requirements to the construction contractor for maintaining building access, egress, and operations continuity while renovation work proceeded in Suite 117.

**Program Transition from Medical/Clinical to Administrative Use.** The conversion of a purpose-built clinical suite to administrative office use required the design to address a substantially heavier demolition scope than a typical same-use renovation. All clinical-grade casework, medical plumbing fixtures, specialized room configurations (audiology booth, optometry lane, immunization area), and associated MEP infrastructure required removal. The new design had to introduce appropriate administrative office infrastructure — sound-isolated attorney offices, open workstation areas, conference and collaboration space, and a specialized recording/photo studio — within the same floor area and structural shell, while coordinating with the retained central MEP infrastructure.

**Sound Isolation for Legal and Communications Functions.** Trial Defense Services (TDS) requires a high level of acoustic privacy for attorney-client communications, legal proceedings preparation, and paralegal work. The Public Affairs Office (PAO) includes a recording/photo studio with its own acoustic requirements. The design addressed these requirements through sound batt insulation in all new partitions and full deck-height partition construction at all private office, conference, and studio boundaries.

---

## Outcomes

Design was completed through 95% construction document submittal in April 2025. The completed design package provides a full construction procurement set for the conversion of approximately 3,700 SF of former medical/clinical space into purpose-configured administrative offices serving PAO and TDS at Fort Gregg-Adams. The design addressed the dual-tenant program with distinct spatial types for legal, communications, and administrative functions, while managing the constraints of active occupancy and the legacy clinical MEP infrastructure. No construction contract value data was available in the source records reviewed for this project.
